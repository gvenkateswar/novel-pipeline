# Genre map — Romance (novella and novel)

Defaults for the story architect, plot architect and sample reader. A book's `book-brief.md` may override any line in writing; the architect follows the override and notes it in the outline. Built from the October 2026 postmortem, the two books' reader notes, and `docs/ai-fiction-tells.md` 4.10. Covers clean through open-door; the heat lines are set per book.

## Positions (as % of total length)

| Beat | Position | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Leads meet with spark (friction plus attraction) | Ch 1, inside the first 5% | Both on the page in the first scene they share |
| Inciting incident and the romantic hook both on the page | by the sample mark (12%) | A buyer who finishes the sample knows what the book is and who the couple is |
| Act 1 ends (point of no return for the premise) | ≤ 20% | Book 1 ran Act 1 to about 30% and lost the listener at 29% |
| First kiss or equivalent charged contact | ≤ 35% | Book 1: 48%. Book 2: Ch 4 of 10, which was late for an open-door book |
| Midpoint reversal | 45–55% | Reverses direction, does not add a complication |
| First intimate scene (open-door books) | 40–55% | At the heat level the brief sets, at or above the floor |
| Rupture | 65–78% | |
| Repair, four beats in order | 75–88% | Accountability, verification, condition, ordinary beat. No intimate scene in the rupture chapter |
| The ordinary scene (just them, no plot) | before the declaration | Mandatory; both books lacked it |
| Declaration | 88–95% | Specific, in words, in that character's voice; never a recap |
| HEA / HFN | last 5% | A scene, never a summary. No epilogue that only reports |
| Point of no return for the relationship | ≥ 60% unless the sub-genre dictates earlier | tells doc 4.10 |

## Every chapter

- **A turn:** something irreversible.
- **Longing:** attraction on the page in every chapter from Ch 1, in a form the reader would call attraction (a touch that lasts, a look held, a line said too close, a thought about the other that is not about the plot). Business between the leads does not count.
- **A laugh:** one line a specific person would say at a specific target. Humor that goes literal is a finding.
- **Tonal contrast** with the previous chapter.

## Heat (set per book in the brief)

- Clean: closed door; kisses as the brief caps them; no swearing. "Clean" is a content rule and never a register. The market research says readers call clean books bland; the longing line above is how this map answers that.
- Open door: the brief sets the level (1–5) and the number of scenes. Each intimate scene changes the relationship, is written in close POV from that character's history, and carries a word floor (800). No scene works as standalone erotica; no scene is cut to aftermath to save words. The label on the blurb ("steamy," "open-door," "slow-burn") is decided from the finished draft, never from the brief.

## Texture

One or two pieces of trade, sport, money or procedure per scene, each because it reveals character or moves the romance. A run of codes, figures or all-caps strings is a finding (the ear check flags it). In Book 1, the chapter every reader liked had 3 codes; the opening chapters had 34.

## The leads

- Each wants something apart from the other (tells 4.10, item 74).
- Each has a syntax profile in the bible, and the dialogue is distinguishable with the tags stripped.
- The heroine's choices follow from what she knows; her blind spots are emotionally credible; her mistakes have costs. "Never a stupid choice" is not a rule here; it removed the useful mistakes on Book 2.
- At least one choice by a lead that a reasonable reader would condemn.

## Series

A series opener leaves one clear, legible hook (who the next couple is, or what the next case is) without cheating the HEA. The hook is stated plainly enough that a reader can repeat it.

## Sample reader persona (default; the brief or the research may replace it)

A woman in her forties to sixties who reads on her phone in bed, mostly KU, three or four books a month in this sub-genre, who abandons a sample when the couple has not been in a room together by the end of it or when the opening is all setup. She is tired of: heroines with no job, instant love, generic small towns, and books that feel like they were written to a list.
