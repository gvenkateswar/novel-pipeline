## THE CORE

**Premise:**
Beth Calloway, 33, has worked the deck of the Cutter Island ferry for eleven years. On a sleet-blown November night freight run she puts her palm on a lobster truck's silent reefer, finds vacuum-sealed bricks of pills under the top layer of a sprung crate, and is seen seeing them by two men, one of whom walks back onto her island. She reports it by landline at 1 a.m. By dawn the driver is in custody and a Deputy US Marshal, Dane Reyes, is on the first boat to take her to a mainland safe house for a week. A nor'easter shuts the ferry down that afternoon and wrecks the slip that night. For ten days the island is cut off, and the safe house is her own cottage with a stranger on the couch. Someone keeps finding her. Very few people could be telling them where she is, and one of them is the man sleeping between her and the door.

**Emotional question:**
Can a woman who has survived by trusting only what she can check with her own hands trust a man she cannot check, at the exact moment that being wrong about him could kill her?

**Tone:**
Taut, physical, cold-weather. Working-class and unsentimental, with dry humor that comes from people who are good at hard jobs. The romance burns under pressure, not in spite of it.

**This story is NOT:**
- **Not a rescue fantasy.** Dane does not save a helpless woman. Beth makes every danger call the water, the weather or the island decides, and Dane's skill is recognizing that and holding the position she gives him.
- **Not a cozy small town.** There are no bakeries, bookshops, inns, festivals or quirky ensemble. Cutter Island is a working island of about 240 winter people who owe money to banks and fix their own generators.
- **Not erotica with a plot attached.** Three sex scenes, each tied to a trust decision. If a scene could be cut without changing what Beth knows or risks, it is wrong.
- **Not a twist-for-twist's-sake mystery.** The leak is planted from Ch 01 and solved fairly. Nobody is introduced late to be guilty.
- **Not a dark romance.** Dane never menaces Beth, never overrides her consent and never "takes" anything. The danger comes from the plot, not the hero.
- **Not a villain parade.** Tess is a frightened, indebted friend, not a monster. Ned is a man who looked away to save a boat. Even Doucette goes into the water and gets the ring buoy.

**Target prose register:**
Close third person, past tense. Beth's sections stay strictly in her head; Dane's sections (Ch 02 opening, Ch 08 opening, Ch 09 middle, Ch 10 coda) strictly in his. Short to mid-length sentences, concrete nouns and working verbs ("she sweated the line", not "she pulled hard on the rope"). Incident over banter: a scene earns its words with something happening. Dialogue is clipped and functional; people on boats say the least that will work. Metaphor comes from the POV character's world. Beth thinks in tides, loads, weather, sequence and feel. Dane thinks in sightlines, distances, exits, timelines and what people do with their hands. At most one figure of speech per paragraph, and many paragraphs have none. Enter scenes late and leave early. Never summarize the previous chapter at the top of a new one. Name the specific thing: "a 3/8-inch chain binder", "the Icom handheld", "a Thermo King on the nose of the trailer", not "a strap", "the radio", "the cooling unit". Fear shows in the body and in what Beth chooses to do next, not in named emotions.

## CHARACTERS

### Beth Calloway (POV, ~90%)

- **Age, body:** 33. Five foot seven, built by the work: broad through the shoulders, forearms roped from line handling, calloused palms, a white seam of scar across her left palm from a hawser that ran when she was 23. Dark blond hair in one braid, usually under a black watch cap. Gray eyes. Windburn across the cheekbones from October to April. Wears Grundéns bib pants and an orange float coat on deck, XtraTufs everywhere. **Vary how her body appears in the prose**: never describe the same feature twice in a chapter, and never let her size, shape or "curves" become a recurring topic (a documented comp complaint).
- **Voice (how she sounds out loud):** Low, unhurried, a little hoarse in cold weather. She talks in statements of fact about wind, water and loads, and they turn out to be true. Mild Maine flatness, but **no phonetic dialect**: no dropped g's on the page, no "ayuh", no "wicked" more than once in the book.
- **Background:** Born and raised on Cutter. Her father, Hollis Calloway, fished a 38-foot lobster boat, the *Ruthie C*, until the bank took it eight years ago; he died of a heart attack two years after that, at 61. Her mother left for the mainland when Beth was nine and lives in Bangor; they talk at Christmas. Beth went to high school on the mainland (boarding weekdays, home weekends), did one year at Maine Maritime Academy and left when her father got sick the first time. She has worked the ferry since she was 22. She holds a Merchant Mariner Credential with an able seaman endorsement and, since 30, a 100-ton master's license she has never used, because asking Ned for the wheel would feel like asking him to be old. She lives alone in her father's cottage above the harbor and still owns his fish house on the cove. Nobody has used it in years.
- **Wound:** She watched the bank take her father's boat on paper while he did everything right on the water. She learned that what you can check with your own hands (a binder, a barometer, a tide) is true, and that what people tell you about money and safety mostly isn't.
- **Want:** To testify, get her life back and go to work on Day 10 as if none of this happened.
- **Need:** To let someone in without checking him first, and to learn that trust given with full knowledge is stronger than trust she never had to test.
- **What she fears:** Being made helpless: on someone else's boat, in someone else's plan, in a room without an exit. Underneath that, being a fool about a person, the way her father was about the bank.
- **Greatest strength / how it is also her flaw:** Competence and self-reliance. She reads weather, boats, people and the island better than anyone sent to protect her. The flaw: she trusts her island by default and strangers not at all. That is exactly backwards in this book. She explains Tess away three times on the page because Tess is "hers."
- **How she talks:**
  - States conclusions before reasons; gives the reasons only if asked, and they are always specific ("Wind's backed northeast and the glass is down four since breakfast").
  - When she is frightened she gets quieter and more exact, and she counts things: steps, minutes to the flood, the boats on moorings.
  - Answers emotional questions with a task ("Hand me the light"). Answers direct ones directly, once she has decided to.
  - Swears like a deckhand under load: rarely, and short (see Language Rules). Never at Dane in anger; at objects and weather.
  - Calls Ned "Ned" to his face and "the captain" to strangers. Calls Dane "Reyes" until Ch 04, "Dane" after the first kiss, and "Deputy" once, in Ch 07, like a slap.
- **Sample dialogue lines:**
  - "It won't run. Pack if you want. You'll be unpacking by two."
  - "Lid's sprung. You'll lose your count."
  - "That latch sticks. Whoever that was has never been in this house."
  - "Hiding gets noticed out here. Being seen is normal. I'm going to the store."
  - "I know what you are. I'm choosing anyway."
- **Behavioral tics:**
  1. Puts her palm flat on things to read them: a reefer box, a wall in a storm, a man's chest when she wants to know if he's lying.
  2. Checks the barometer by tapping it with one knuckle every time she passes it.
  3. Keeps an exit in her head in every room: she knows which door, which window and how many steps. In scene 2 she notices she has stopped doing it.
- **What she knows, and when:**
  - Day 0: what she saw (crate, bricks, two men, the hooded man walking off). The fuel float was lit at 9 p.m. with the *Margaret Ann* alongside (she notices it and files it as odd).
  - Day 1: the driver is in custody; she is a federal witness; the grand jury is Day 11 (from Dane, Ch 02); the ferry is out for "ten days if the state gets a barge out" (Ned, VHF). She believes only Dane knew she stayed home. **She does not count Tess.**
  - Day 2: overhears "No. She doesn't know. ... Daily. Understood." Does not know what it refers to. Learns the *Margaret Ann* is storm-bound. Gets the Ashby key from Ned.
  - Day 3: identifies the hooded man's walk on the *Margaret Ann*'s skiff. Sees Dane close the notebook.
  - Day 4: after scene 1, realizes the check-in log widened the circle. Decides to hunt.
  - Day 5–6: sees the fuel log gap and explains it away. Learns Russ is cousin to Arsenault's ex-wife. Hears "coordinated with local emergency management." Learns about the witness Dane lost.
  - Day 7: reads the notebook (the $9,000 deposit, the credibility review, the extraction time). **Has never heard of the $9,000 until this moment.**
  - Day 8: Ned's confession; "Nobody but Tess. Tess doesn't count." Puts it together. Dane's full account.
  - Day 8 night: Tess's "water side" warning confirms it in Tess's own voice.
- **The one thing the prose-writer must never make her do:** **Make a senseless choice in danger.** She never goes toward a sound unarmed when an armed professional is present, never goes somewhere alone without a reason the reader can see and defend, never freezes, never hides somewhere stupid, never confronts a dangerous person without an exit and a witness, and never "forgets" something she would know. Every danger choice must match the outline's "Danger beats" table: she makes the smart call with the information she has. Fear is allowed. Mistakes about *people* (Tess) are allowed. Mistakes about the water, the boat or the island are not.

### Deputy US Marshal Dane Reyes (POV, ~10%)

- **Age, body:** 38. Six foot, lean, a runner's build rather than a gym one. Dark hair cut short, gray coming in at the temples. Brown eyes that settle on hands before faces. A clean-shaven face that is stubbled by Day 3 and stays that way. A knot of old scar tissue on his right knuckles from a door in 2019. Wears a dark gray soft-shell over a fleece, jeans, waterproof hiking boots that are wrong for a boat deck and he knows it. Glock 22 on his right hip, a spare magazine, cuffs, a small trauma kit, an Iridium sat phone and a Rite in the Rain notebook in his inside pocket.
- **Voice:** Quiet, level, a little lower than you expect. He doesn't raise it. When he wants you to listen he slows down.
- **Background:** Grew up in Lowell, Massachusetts. His father, from Ponce, Puerto Rico, drove a city bus for thirty years; his mother was a dispatcher for the Lowell police. He speaks Spanish the way kids of immigrants do: well enough with his father, badly on the phone. Four years Army military police, then the Lowell PD, then the Marshals Service at 29. Assigned to the District of Massachusetts (Boston). Divorced, no children; the marriage ended quietly when he was 34 and he takes the blame for it. **The lost witness:** four years ago, a 24-year-old cooperating witness in a Boston gang case, Marisol Duarte, asked to stop and see her sister on the way to a safe site. It was against the plan. He allowed it for ten minutes, and she was shot in the parking lot. The review cleared him. He has not bent a rule since, until this book.
- **Why he is alone:** He was on temporary duty in Portland (District of Maine short on deputies that month) and given what was meant to be a one-day job: take a witness off an island on the morning boat and deliver her to a mainland safe site until the grand jury. A second deputy was meant to meet him at the mainland terminal for the drive. The storm came twelve hours early. Nobody else could get out.
- **Want:** To deliver Beth alive and credible to the grand jury on Day 11, by the book, with nothing on the record anyone can use.
- **Need:** To learn that following every rule is not the same as doing right by a person, and that being believed requires being known, which costs him something.
- **What he fears:** Bending a rule for a woman and getting her killed. Again.
- **Greatest strength / how it is also his flaw:** Discipline and patience. He reads people, waits, and gathers. The flaw: he thinks silence is protection. He withholds things "for her own good" and does not count what finding out will cost her.
- **How he talks:**
  - Few words. Asks more than he tells. Questions that start "Walk me through" or "Tell me about."
  - Repeats back what he heard to confirm it, in fewer words ("Northeast. Four millibars. Two o'clock's dead").
  - Never says "trust me." Never says "calm down." Never says "I've got you."
  - Explains his refusals. A "no" from Dane always comes with the reason and with something she can do instead.
  - Dry humor at his own expense, never at hers.
  - Uses "ma'am" once, on Day 1, and she shuts it down. Calls her "Ms. Calloway" through Day 1, "Beth" after the back door.
- **Sample dialogue lines:**
  - "Walk me through it. Slower than you want to."
  - "You were right about the boat. I'm going to stop arguing with you about water."
  - "I can't keep you alive with my hands on you. That's not a no. It's a not yet I can't afford."
  - "When this is over, I report this. I want you to hear that before, not after."
  - "I'd rather you get asked than have it found."
- **Behavioral tics:**
  1. Counts exits and sightlines on entering any space; in his POV, every room arrives as distances and doors.
  2. Writes in the notebook at the end of each day, standing, on the porch, so he can see the road.
  3. Sits with his back to a wall and his right side clear. Beth notices by Ch 03 and takes the other chair without being asked.
- **What he knows, and when:**
  - Day 1 morning: the case basics from Quist and his supervisor; Pelletier in custody; one-day job. Sees the bank bag handed over and thinks nothing of it. Sees the dragger on the guest mooring.
  - Day 1 evening: the grand jury date (Day 11) from his supervisor on the sat phone. Doesn't know about Beth's phone check-in until Ch 05; she told him only that she'd "taken care of it."
  - Day 2: Quist's credibility order: $9,000 cash deposited with Beth's paycheck through the island bank bag on Day 1. Keep a daily log, report nightly, don't tell her.
  - Day 3: his money questions; by that night his log says "account consistent; no indication." He believes her from Day 3.
  - Day 4: learns about the phone check-in and the open log (after scene 1).
  - Day 6: arranges the extraction with Quist and the Coast Guard; the town's emergency management director is asked to clear the landing.
  - Day 8, 4 a.m.: finds the notebook open. On the call, learns who outside the Marshals had the landing time: the Coast Guard and the island EMD, Teresa Marr. Connects it to the Day 1 phone call, the log and the fuel float.
- **The one thing the prose-writer must never make him do:** **Coldness mistaken for restraint.** His restraint must always read as care. Every time he steps back, refuses or withholds, the reader must see what it costs him and why (in Beth's POV, through a visible tell: a hand that stops, a reason given, a practical kindness done right after). He is never curt for effect, never contemptuous, never possessive, never "growls," never orders her around about things she knows better. He never uses the notebook against her. In Beth's POV he is unreadable; he is never unkind.

### Teresa "Tess" Marr (the leak)

- **Age, body:** 46. Short, solid, quick on her feet. Cropped salt-and-pepper hair she cuts herself. Reading glasses on her head, a pencil in the glasses. Red Carhartt vest over whatever sweater is clean, fingerless gloves in the store because she won't heat it above 58. A laugh you can hear from the fuel float.
- **Voice:** Fast, warm, a little raspy (quit smoking at 40 and still talks like a smoker). Fills silence. Tells you what things cost.
- **Background:** Came to Cutter at 22 to sternman for a summer and stayed. Has run Marr's General Store and fuel dock for nineteen years: groceries, hardware, propane, the post office window, gas and diesel at the float. Does the islanders' banking by collecting deposits in a canvas zip bag that rides the morning boat to the credit union on the mainland. Keeps net control for the storm check-in net. Has been the town's emergency management director "since town meeting," because nobody else put their hand up. Fourteen years older than Beth; babysat her; was the one who drove her to the mainland the day her mother left (they don't talk about that). Ned's partner for twelve years; they keep separate houses "so we stay friends."
- **The debt:** State rules forced her to replace the store's two underground fuel tanks four years ago. She borrowed $180,000 from the bank to do it. She is behind.
- **Motive:** For two years the *Margaret Ann* and boats from two other harbors have fueled at her float after hours, off the books, for cash (Arsenault's offer: no questions, no log, twice the pump price). That cash kept the store and the tank loan alive. She knew it was wrong and decided it was small. She guessed the boats carried something; she chose to think "pills, the kind people's doctors give them."
- **What she tells herself (in order):**
  1. Day 0: "They only want to scare her off testifying. Nobody gets hurt. If I don't help, they burn the store and me in it, and then who looks out for Bethie?"
  2. Day 1–4, after the back door: "They were just trying the door. If I stop now, they know I'll talk." She knows better and keeps going because she is afraid.
  3. Day 6–7, after the ambush: no story left. Only fear, and the thought that if she confesses now she has killed them both.
  4. Day 8: she hears Beth's voice on channel 9 and understands that Beth trusts her completely. That is what breaks her.
- **The exact moments she passes information (by cell phone to Arsenault, from the store's back office, the only place on the island with signal; see World Rules):**
  1. **Day 0, about 9:30 p.m.** Arsenault, at the float, tells her the deckhand who saw was Beth. Tess confirms who Beth is and where she lives (everyone knows). Before the morning boat she adds $9,000 cash, in hundreds, under Beth's paycheck in the bank bag, on Arsenault's instruction ("make her look bought").
  2. **Day 1, about 9:30 p.m.** After Ned announces the slip is wrecked on VHF, Tess calls Arsenault: "She didn't go. She's home." Her source: Beth's landline check-in call at 5:40 p.m. and her own log entry "Calloway — home (phone)." The back door is tried at 11:40.
  3. **Day 4, about 2 p.m.** Ned told her about the Ashby key on Day 3 over supper (he tells her everything). She passes it on. The generator shed burns at 10 p.m.
  4. **Day 6, about 7 p.m.** As emergency management director she was asked that afternoon to keep the town landing clear and the fire company on standby at 0600 Day 7. She passes the time and place. The float lights are cut overnight.
  5. **Day 8, about 5:30 p.m.** Beth, on channel 9, tells her she is alone on the ferry tonight. Tess passes it on, after a pause.
  6. **Day 8, 11:00 p.m.** On channel 9, with Arsenault in the store: "Bethie. Water side." He pulls the radio away. This is her one act for Beth.
- **What she does not know:**
  - That the pills are fentanyl, or what that means for the people at the end of the line. (She learns it from Quist on Day 9.)
  - That the back door, the fire and the landing were meant to kill, not frighten. She learns the landing involved a shot from Russ's radio report on Day 7.
  - That the feds would see the $9,000 within a day. She thought it would come up, if ever, at trial.
  - The fish house. It never crosses her mind; she has not thought about Hollis's fish house in years.
  - Dane's notebook, or the credibility review.
  - Exactly what Ned looked away from on the freight. She suspected and never asked.
- **How she talks:**
  - Nicknames: "Bethie," "hon," "Captain" for Ned when she's teasing him.
  - Money in every third sentence: prices, the loan, what things cost before.
  - Jokes about her titles to deflect from them.
  - Under stress she gets brighter and busier, not quieter: rings up, restocks, talks.
  - Tells the truth about small things constantly, which is why nobody checks the big ones.
- **Sample dialogue lines:**
  - "Emergency management director. Since town meeting. I was slow putting my hand down."
  - "That tank loan's going to bury me before the cold does. Eighteen hundred a month, Bethie. For dirt with steel in it."
  - "Channel 9 if the phones go. Like the old days."
  - "Okay, Bethie."
  - "They said they'd only scare you."
- **Behavioral tics:**
  1. Straightens the check-in log and the fuel log so the edges line up with the counter, every time someone looks at them.
  2. Holds her phone face-down on the counter. Picks it up and goes into the back office when it buzzes, always smiling.
  3. Touches Beth when she talks to her: a hand on the arm, a tug on the braid. In Ch 06 she stops doing it, and Beth doesn't notice until Ch 08.
- **How to make the reader like her:** She is the funniest person on the page in Ch 01 and Ch 03. She gives credit to people who can't pay, keeps the store open in the storm when she could shut it, carries soup to old Mrs. Dyer, and is visibly, specifically kind to Beth (she remembers Beth's father's coffee order). Her fear must show in small physical moments the reader can reread: a dropped coin, a laugh a beat too late, the pause before "Okay, Bethie." She never gloats, never explains a plan, never threatens anyone, and is never shown plotting on the page.
- **The one thing never:** **Never a cartoon.** She does not sneer, monologue or enjoy any of it. She has no on-page scene in which she reveals her guilt before Ch 08. Her guilt is assembled by Beth.
- **Fate:** Arrested Day 9. Tells Quist where the ledger is (under the floor of the back office, in a gallon freezer bag). Cooperating, charged. Her story continues off-page into Book 2 as a witness.

### Ned Haskell (captain, red herring)

- **Age, body:** 64. Big, stooped, slow-moving on land and quick in a wheelhouse. White beard kept short, red face, hearing aid in the left ear he turns off when he doesn't want to hear something. Green wool Johnson Woolen Mills jacket older than Beth. Reading glasses on a cord. Bad hip.
- **Voice:** Deep, slow, gravelly, full of long pauses. On the radio he is clipped, formal and correct, a different man.
- **Background:** Has run the Cutter Island ferry for 31 years and is the ferry district's longest employee. Hired Beth at 22 because her father asked him. Caretakes six summer houses, including the Ashby place on the north point. Widowed at 50; Tess's partner for twelve years. **His smaller crime:** for about a year he has known that one lobster dealer's reefer on the Thursday night freight run is wrong: unit off, wrong weights, a sternman from away loading it. He took no money. He looked away because that dealer's contract is the night freight run, and without it the ferry district loses the subsidy that keeps the boat running in winter, and without the boat the island dies.
- **Want:** To keep the boat running and the island alive. To keep Beth off that run without telling her why.
- **What he fears:** Being the man who killed the island, and being the man who got Beth hurt. Now he is afraid he is both.
- **What he knows, and when:** Knows the reefer is wrong (for a year). Day 0: tries to send Beth home ("I'll work it short"). Day 2: lies to Dane about the reefer ("he always runs his unit"). Day 2: gives Beth the Ashby key. Day 3: tells Tess about it at supper. Day 7: living aboard the ferry to tend lines and pumps. Day 8: confesses.
- **How he talks:** Answers questions with weather or work. Never apologizes in words. Short declaratives, long silences. His tell for lying: he gives more detail than anyone asked for.
- **Sample dialogue lines:**
  - "I'll work it short. Go home."
  - "Nobody but me and God."
  - "He always runs his unit. Always has. Thermo King, it's a good one, they run all night on those." (the lie, over-detailed)
  - "Where's your marshal?"
  - "Nobody but Tess. Tess doesn't count."
- **On the radio (formal):** "All stations, all stations, this is the Cutter Island ferry *North Reach*. The fourteen hundred departure is cancelled. Next departure to be announced. *North Reach* standing by one-six and one-three."
- **Behavioral tics:**
  1. Turns his hearing aid off with one finger when he wants a conversation over.
  2. Wipes the wheelhouse windows with a rag that makes them worse.
  3. Brings coffee as an apology. He brings Beth coffee three times in the book.
- **The one thing never:** **Never a speech.** His confession in Ch 08 is short, angry and ashamed, ten lines at most, pulled out of him by Beth with the mic in her hand.
- **Fate:** Cooperates with HSI, keeps his license, and loses the freight contract. Captains the first boat on Day 10.

### Russ Whitcomb (constable, red herring, Book 2 lead)

- **Age, body:** 44. Middle height, wide, red-bearded with gray at the chin. Lobsterman's hands, a missing tip on his left ring finger (a winch). Orange Grundéns in his boat, a faded brown Carhartt and a Red Sox cap on land; a brass constable's badge on his belt that he sometimes forgets. Carries a Smith & Wesson M&P on duty.
- **Voice:** Wry, slow, amused by everything, including himself.
- **Background:** Fishes 400 traps out of Cutter on his boat *Second Wind* four days a week; constable the rest. Completed the state's part-time law enforcement training so he can make arrests on the island (see Research notes). Divorced six years from Jolene, who moved to the mainland; she is Lyle Arsenault's cousin. Russ and Beth were in the island school together (he was four grades ahead). He drinks one beer a night and has never been drunk in public.
- **Why he looks guilty:** He asks Beth where she'll sleep (Ch 03). He turns up on the woods road near Lookout Hill (Ch 04). He is related by marriage to Arsenault (Ch 06).
- **Why he isn't:** He asks where she'll sleep because on an island someone will, and he wants to know who asks. He is on the woods road checking summer camps for frozen pipes, which is his actual winter job. The feds told him to stay clear of the *Margaret Ann* and he did, and he tells Beth so plainly.
- **Want:** To be taken seriously as a lawman by people who think he's a lobsterman with a badge.
- **What he fears:** That the island's trouble is family trouble, and that he'll be the last to know again (he was, with his marriage).
- **How he talks:** Asks questions in a deadpan that sounds like jokes. Answers his own questions. Uses "somebody" for gossip. On the VHF he is grimly formal (no profanity on air; see World Rules).
- **Sample dialogue lines:**
  - "Where you sleeping tonight? Not asking for me. Asking because somebody will."
  - "Checking camps. Somebody's got to make sure the summer people's pipes freeze in an orderly way."
  - "Feds told me stay off that boat. So I stayed off that boat. I'm not a complicated man."
  - On the VHF: "*North Reach*, Whitcomb. One in custody at the store. Tess Marr is unhurt. Over."
- **Behavioral tics:**
  1. Takes his cap off and puts it back on to buy time.
  2. Leans on whatever's nearest (truck, counter, door frame) as if standing is optional.
- **The one thing never:** **Never just comic relief.** He is competent, armed and trained, and in Ch 09 he walks into the store alone to take Arsenault. Book 2 depends on the reader respecting him.

### Lyle Arsenault (antagonist, skipper of the *Margaret Ann*)

- **Age, body:** 52. Tall, gone soft in the middle, handsome in a worn way. Clean-shaven, which no one else on a dragger is. A good Musto jacket. Smokes Marlboro Golds.
- **Background:** Skipper and owner of the *Margaret Ann*, a 62-foot steel dragger hailing from a harbor down the coast (out-of-state hailing port painted on the stern: Gloucester, Mass., because he bought her there and never repainted it). Two years running pills by offshore transfer: product comes off a larger vessel offshore, rides in the fish hold, is landed at Cutter and two other islands, packed into a dealer's crates and trucked off on the night freight. Shelters at Cutter in the storm because it's where his fuel is.
- **What he wants:** Beth unable to testify, and his own name out of Pelletier's mouth.
- **How he talks:** Easy, friendly, generous: buys strangers coffee, compliments the store. Threats delivered as advice ("Wouldn't want anything to happen to this place, Tess. It's the heart of the island"). Never raises his voice.
- **On the page:** Ch 03, buying cigarettes at the store (one line to Tess, one look at Dane). Otherwise off-page or a voice on a radio until Ch 09, when Russ arrests him in the store. Ch 10 walked down in cuffs.
- **Sample lines:** "Pack of Golds, and whatever the gentleman's having." / "Rough night to be from away."

### Shane Doucette (the hooded man)

## WORLD & SETTING

**Cutter Island, Maine (fictional).** An island of about 3,100 acres in an unnamed bay on the midcoast (Penobscot Bay-type waters, never named as Penobscot Bay), 7 nautical miles and a 45-minute ferry crossing from the fictional mainland town of **Pruett Harbor**, which sits a few miles off Route 1. Year-round population about 240; summer about 1,100. One ferry, one store with a fuel dock, the island school (eleven children K–8), the Union Church, a town office open Tuesdays and Thursdays, a volunteer fire company with one engine and one tanker, a part-time constable, a fish co-op pier, a town landing, about 35 working lobster boats. **No airstrip, no bridge, no doctor** (a nurse practitioner flies in by LifeFlight in emergencies only, and the island has an EMT on the fire company). Power comes from the mainland by submarine cable; phone lines and fiber come by a second cable. Most houses have generators; everyone has propane.

**Season and weather:** mid-November. The water is about 48–50°F. Sunrise about 6:30 a.m., sunset about 4:10 p.m. (see Research notes). The book has two systems: a nor'easter from the afternoon of Day 1 through Day 3, with seas still too high to land until Day 6; a short window on Day 6 night and Day 7 morning; then a second, faster system arriving Day 7 evening and clearing with a northwest wind on the evening of Day 8. By Day 9 noon the harbor is flat and bright.

The wheelhouse sits on top of the deckhouse, reached by an outside steel ladder and an inside companionway; its door locks. ### Research notes (verified facts and write-arounds)

*Status key: **Verified** = confirmed against a source located in this session (named). **Confirm** = could not verify in this session; check before drafting the chapter that uses it. Search tools returned page titles and links, not full text, so "Verified" here means an authoritative page on the point was located and the stated fact is the standard rule it sets out. Treat anything with a number in it as worth a second look before publication.*

**Ferry**
1. **Ownership.** Maine's island ferries are a mix: the state-run Maine State Ferry Service serves several Penobscot Bay islands; others are run by private companies or island transit districts. *Verified* (Maine DOT ferry service pages, maine.gov/dot). **Write-around:** Cutter is served by the fictional Cutter Island Ferry District, not the state service. That keeps Ned's authority over holds and freight contracts local and believable and avoids real state staffing rules.
2. **Vessel and crew size.** A 128-foot bow-loader carrying about 18 cars and a winter crew of three fits the scale of small Maine island ferries. *Confirm* (exact certificated numbers are fictional; check that 149 passengers and three crew are consistent with a Coast Guard certificate of inspection for a vessel of this size).
3. **Security rules and inspection.** Vessel security plans and TWIC rules under 33 CFR Part 104 apply to certain vessels, including passenger vessels certificated for more than 150 passengers. *Verified* (33 CFR 104.105, Cornell LII). **Write-around:** the *North Reach* is certificated for **149** passengers, so it has no MTSA vessel security plan, and freight is checked against a paper manifest, not inspected. That is what makes the smuggling plausible. Beth carries a TWIC anyway because she relieves on a larger vessel in summer (optional texture).
4. **Beth's credential.** An MMC with an able seaman endorsement and a 100-ton master's license are real credentials. *Confirm* whether a small inspected ferry's COI would require the deckhand to hold an MMC (it may only require a certain number of credentialed crew). Doesn't change the plot.
5. **Transfer bridge damage and repair.** Maine ferry service has been interrupted by storm damage to terminals and transfer bridges. *Verified* (MaineDOT ferry service interruption notices; NEWS CENTER Maine on Swan's Island ferry disruption after winter storm damage). Ten days to bring a crane barge, rig a temporary repair and restore landings is *Confirm*; a real repair could take longer. **Write-around if needed:** on Day 10 the ferry runs passengers and light freight only, using a temporary ramp; trucks wait for a full repair. Ned says "foot traffic and pickups only" in Ch 10.
6. **Reefer units on live lobster.** Live lobster moves by road in refrigerated trucks kept cold and damp. *Verified in principle* (Truck News, "Lobster longhaul"). Whether a reefer unit would be silent is the issue: modern units cycle off in start-stop mode. **Write-around (binding):** Beth's tell is not just the silence. The unit's **display panel is dark** (switched off, not cycling), and the box's door seal is not cold under her palm. She knows the dealer runs continuous mode for live product because she has heard that unit drone across the deck every Thursday for six years. The prose should give all three cues.
7. **Lobster crates.** A standard wire or plastic lobster crate holds roughly 90 pounds of lobster. *Confirm.* For the story: the crates in the reefer are the dealer's wax-box style shipping cartons stacked on pallets, with a top layer of live lobsters in wet seaweed and newspaper over vacuum-sealed bricks of pressed pills. One sprung lid during tie-down is enough.
8. **Chain binders and tie-down.** Trucks on Maine ferries are secured with chains and lever or ratchet binders to deck D-rings, and the deckhand checks them by feel. *Confirm* (standard practice; check whether the district would use ratchet or lever binders. Default: ratchet binders, which Beth checks by the handle angle and the tension).
9. **Captain living aboard in a storm.** A captain or crew tending lines and pumps aboard a moored ferry during a storm is plausible. *Confirm.*
10. **Boarding over the stern from a skiff.** With low freeboard aft and a recessed quarter ladder, plausible. *Confirm.* Note: in Ch 09 Doucette's skiff outboard cuts out some distance off and he paddles in the last 100 feet, which is what Dane hears.

**VHF**
11. **Channel 16** is the international distress, safety and calling channel; **channel 9** is the US secondary calling channel for recreational boaters; channel 13 is bridge-to-bridge navigation safety (commercial vessels monitor it); channel **22A** is the Coast Guard's main working channel for safety broadcasts after announcement on 16; channels 68, 69, 71, 72 and 78A are recreational working channels. *Verified* (USCG Navigation Center, "U.S. VHF Channel Information"; Connecticut DEEP, "Marine VHF Radio: The Basics"; BoatUS, "VHF Radio Protocol"). NOAA weather broadcasts run on separate WX channels. *Verified* (weather.gov/marine).
12. **Profanity on VHF** is prohibited by FCC rules. *Verified in principle* (BoatUS VHF protocol; the general FCC rule against obscene or profane language on radio). Binding in the book.
13. **Procedure words**: Mayday (life in grave and imminent danger), Pan-Pan (urgent, not life-threatening), Sécurité (safety, navigation and weather). *Verified* (BoatUS). Ned's cancellation is a plain all-stations announcement, not a Sécurité call.
14. **Handheld range** is short (a few miles at 5 watts, less behind terrain); anyone tuned to the channel can hear. *Verified in principle* (CT DEEP basics). **Binding:** Beth's handheld reaches the harbor and the store from the ferry; it does not reach across Lookout Hill to the north point.
15. **DSC**: fixed VHF sets have a red distress button that sends position on channel 70. *Verified* (USCG NAVCEN). Not used in the plot; don't add it.
16. **Island storm check-in nets** on VHF: plausible and in keeping with island practice, but no source located. *Confirm.* Book practice is set in World Rules.

**US Marshals and federal procedure**
17. **Who protects a pre-indictment witness.** The formal federal Witness Security Program (WITSEC) is run by the US Marshals Service for authorized witnesses, and the Justice Manual sets out how witnesses are admitted, including emergency procedures. *Verified* (Justice Manual 9-21.000, justice.gov; usmarshals.gov/witsec). A non-WITSEC witness is more often protected by the case agency or local police. **Write-around (binding):** the AUSA requested **emergency protective services** for Beth through the Marshals while an emergency WITSEC application is considered, because HSI Portland had no agents free and the threat was immediate. Dane's job on Day 1 was a protective **transport** to a mainland safe site pending that decision. Dane says it in one line in Ch 02 ("You're not in the program. You're in the waiting room for the program. I'm the waiting room"). Nothing in the plot changes.
18. **Solo detail.** Unusual. **Write-around:** a second deputy was to meet him at the Pruett Harbor terminal; the storm came early. Pike says on Day 1 she can't get anyone out. *Confirm* that this reads as plausible to a reader with law enforcement knowledge (it is a fiction convention).
19. **The credibility log.** A protecting deputy would not normally assess a witness's credibility; that belongs to the case agent and the AUSA. An HSI agent cannot order a deputy marshal. **Write-around (binding):** on Day 2 **Quist requests** that Dane keep an observational log (movements, contacts, anything Beth says about the freight or money), because he is the only federal officer with eyes on the witness and the AUSA won't put a possibly paid witness in the grand jury room without it. **Pike approves** the request. "Daily. Understood." is Dane to Pike. The instruction not to tell Beth comes from both. The outline's "Quist orders" becomes "Quist asks and Pike orders." Nothing else changes.
20. **Giglio and Brady.** Prosecutors must disclose to the defense information that could impeach a government witness, which includes a witness's relationship with an investigating or protecting officer and a credibility review of the witness. *Verified in principle* (ABA, "Giglio v. United States"; Massachusetts and Vermont Giglio procedure documents). Dane's line "I'd rather you get asked than have it found" is accurate.
21. **Relationship with a protectee** is serious misconduct and would trigger a referral to the Marshals' internal affairs. *Confirm* the exact office name; on the page just say "a referral," "OPR" or "internal affairs" once. His outcome (suspension, then transfer) is plausible fiction.
22. **Rule 5.1 preliminary hearing:** a magistrate judge must hold a preliminary hearing within 14 days of initial appearance if the defendant is in custody, unless the defendant is indicted first or waives. *Verified* (Fed. R. Crim. P. 5.1, Cornell LII). **Calendar logic (binding):** Pelletier's initial appearance is Friday, November 13 (Day 1). The 14-day deadline is Friday, November 27, the day after Thanksgiving. The grand jury does not sit Thanksgiving week after Monday, so **Monday, November 23 (Day 11) is the last session** at which the government can indict and avoid the hearing. The defense will not agree to extend the deadline. *Confirm* Portland's actual sitting schedule; the logic holds regardless.
23. **Grand jury subpoena.** A subpoena can be served by a marshal, deputy marshal or any nonparty adult. *Verified* (Fed. R. Crim. P. 17(d), via ruledex.com). **Binding:** Dane serves Beth her grand jury subpoena on Day 1 morning, a single sheet she pins under the barometer. In the grand jury room: no judge, no defense lawyer; the AUSA questions her and jurors may ask questions; she may step outside to consult a lawyer; witnesses are not bound by grand jury secrecy about their own testimony. *Verified in principle* (Rule 6, standard practice). Witness fees and travel are paid. *Verified* (28 U.S.C. § 1821).
24. **The $9,000 deposit.** Cash deposits over $10,000 trigger a Currency Transaction Report; deliberately keeping under that to avoid reporting is structuring, which can prompt a Suspicious Activity Report, filed within 30 days. *Verified* (FinCEN, "Suspicious Activity Reporting: Structuring" ruling; FinCEN report reference). A SAR would **not** reach Quist on Day 2. **Write-around (binding):** Quist requests Beth's account records as routine witness vetting on the afternoon of Day 1, by grand jury subpoena, and the credit union's BSA officer calls her on Day 2 morning because the island bag held $9,000 in hundreds under a deckhand's paycheck, which the teller flagged. Day 2 stays. *Confirm* turnaround as plausible.
25. **Rural credit union and the bank bag.** An island store collecting neighbors' deposits in a bag that rides the morning boat is plausible island practice. *Confirm* (no source located); keep it informal and old-fashioned on the page: deposit slips, a zip bag, Tess's initials on the seal tag.
26. **HSI** investigates cross-border and maritime smuggling, including drugs. **Maine Marine Patrol** officers are the state's marine law enforcement with patrol boats. The **Coast Guard** can assist other federal agencies with transport. *Confirm* specific inter-agency details; on the page keep it to Quist's one line: "Coast Guard will lift you; Marine Patrol brings the relief."
27. **Federal courthouse.** The District of Maine sits in Portland and Bangor; the Portland courthouse is the Edward T. Gignoux U.S. Courthouse. *Confirm* the address before using it on the page (prefer "the federal courthouse on Federal Street").

**Island emergency practice**
28. **Emergency management director.** Maine law requires each municipality to have an emergency management agency director, appointed locally; small towns often appoint a volunteer. *Verified* (37-B M.R.S. § 782, Maine Legislature). **Write-around for the leak:** it is not standard for the Coast Guard or Marshals to "notify" a town EMD of a witness landing. **Binding version:** because the island is under a storm emergency and the EMD is the town's single point of contact, Pike's office calls the EMD on Day 6 to ask that the town landing be kept clear and the fire company's EMT stand by at 0600 Day 7 ("a federal transfer," no name given). That is how Tess learns the time and place. Dane's overheard line stays: "coordinated with local emergency management for the landing."
29. **Constables.** Maine municipal constables may serve process and, if they have completed the required law enforcement training, may exercise arrest powers within the municipality. *Verified in principle* (30-A M.R.S. § 2671, Maine Legislature). *Confirm* firearm and training details. **Binding:** Russ has completed the part-time officer training and has arrest powers on Cutter; he can hold a prisoner in the town office until Marine Patrol arrives.
30. **Volunteer fire company in storms; generators and propane.** Plausible as written. *Confirm* local practice. Binding: the fire company knocks on the doors of anyone who misses the check-in.
31. **Phones and cell in the outage.** Cell sites typically have battery backup for hours and may have generators; backup can run out in long outages. *Verified in principle* (Fuel Logic and Generator Source pages on cell tower backup power). A **signal booster** amplifies a weak outside signal from a distant tower; it needs power, and coverage is limited to the area around its inside antenna. *Confirm* (standard product behavior; no source located). See World Rules for the binding version.
32. **LifeFlight of Maine** is the state's air medical service and lands at designated landing zones, including on islands. *Verified* (lifeflightmaine.org, landing zone update). It is a medevac service and would not move a federal witness. Cutter's only landing zone is the ballfield behind the school. **No airstrip.**
