# Revision brief — Ten Days Out, third pass (final pipeline pass)

*October 27, 2026. Built from the second-draft panels (`reviews/external/draft2/synthesis.md`) and three outside reviews of the proposed plan. Binding for this pass. Where it conflicts with `revision-brief.md`, `outline.md` or `story-bible.md`, this brief wins. After this pass the pipeline stops and the book goes to human readers.*

## Settled before the pass

- **No heat expansion.** The three scenes stay where they are, at their current length. No word quota on any scene. An intimate scene may change only where an item below touches it.
- **Label:** the front note keeps "steamy, open-door." The blurb will say the same.
- **No new plot.** Who does what, who wins, and the order of events in `outline.md` stay. What changes is why characters choose what they choose, and what they know when they choose it.
- **About 1,200 words net across the book** is a scope estimate, not a target. Ch 06 gets shorter; Ch 07, 08 and 10 may grow where the items need it.

## Completion criteria (the pass is done only when a fresh reader can answer these from the page)

1. Why does Beth make her Ch 07 choice, given what she knows?
2. Have both lovers named what they hid, and does their behavior afterward show they learned from it?
3. Why must the Ch 09 confrontation happen, given the safer options Ch 08 names?

## C1. Ch 07 — Beth's decision (change the actions, not just the explanation)

Problems the panels found: she switches off his alarm (so he misses his 11:15 check-in with Pike, which has no consequence on the page); she leaves with no plan for how she will be heard; she goes to Ned despite knowing Ned lied about the reefer and eats at Tess's table.

Required:
- **She does not switch off his alarm.** She leaves at 10:58 knowing the alarm gives her seventeen minutes, and that is why she goes by the shore and not the road. She leaves the notebook open on the bench at the money page so he will know why she left. That is her message.
- **Her objective is the ferry's fixed VHF and the wheelhouse door that locks, not Ned.** Her plan: lock herself in, and at first light call the Coast Guard on 16 herself and ask for the marshals in Portland by name from the notebook (Pike's name is in it). She does not call in the dark because 16 is open to every boat in the harbor, including the *Margaret Ann*, and a woman alone on a lit boat at night who just told the harbor where she is would be bait. She chooses to wait for daylight and witnesses. Her reasoning stays in her short sentences.
- **Ned is a risk she accepts on purpose.** She knows the reefer lie and his supper at Tess's. She decides the locked door covers it: Ned can't get into the wheelhouse unless she opens it. That is why she turns the key on him (already on the page; keep it and make the reason visible).
- **Her radio:** she keeps the Icom on, volume down, on scan, so she can hear, and does not transmit. One line.
- Keep "If it was him, it wasn't her." Keep the church, the heel on the road and the voice talking him along. Shorten the second retelling of the notebook clues in this chapter by about half; the reader has just read them.

## C2. Ch 08 — Beth's admission, Dane's call to Pike, and why the trap

Order in the crew-mess repair stays (his ownership, her check of the notebook, her condition, the license and Lowell exchange). Add:

- **Beth's admission, after her condition and before Lowell.** She tells him that on Wednesday, when he said "local emergency management," she knew it meant Tess, and she said nothing. She owns it in her plain register, without taking the bullet on herself and without making it cancel what he did ("That's mine. Yours is still yours."). Dane's response changes how they act next: he says that from here she hears everything he hears, and he asks her to say every name that comes into her head, even the ones she doesn't want to. She does, right then: Tess, Ned, Russ. (Russ knew the Ashby house; this is where the narrow suspect list adds him.) Four to eight lines total.
- **The emergency-management gap (one or two lines, Dane):** Portland notified county emergency management of the landing window, as procedure required; the county passes it to the island's local coordinator; Dane did not know until this morning that on Cutter that coordinator is one woman at the store, and when he said "only us" after the ambush he meant only the detail. He owns not asking.
- **Why the trap: make it necessary before they choose it.** Before the 3:30 scene, put on the page specific things Beth sees and hears that make waiting fatal for Tess:
  - Through the binoculars at about three: the *Margaret Ann* taking fuel at the store float, deck lights on in daylight, her crew stowing gear for a passage. She will sail on the evening slack when the sea lays down, hours before relief.
  - On the three o'clock net on 9, Tess reads the roll in a voice Beth has never heard her use, and skips the Calloway name she has never once skipped in eleven years. Beth knows that sound: Tess is reading with someone beside her.
  - So: tonight the boat sails, and the one person left who can put Arsenault at the store is Tess. Then "Waiting kills her" is a conclusion from evidence, not a guess.
- **Dane's call to Pike happens on the page, before the plan is agreed, and Pike's answer closes the safer options.** In two or three lines of Dane's report: the Coast Guard boat cannot put anyone on the Cutter pier before first light (bar and swell at the pier, not the open sea); state police will not cross until the boat does; a blue-light approach to the store would send Arsenault to sea with Tess. Pike authorizes him to hold the boat and to let them come to him; she does not authorize bait. Dane tells Beth exactly what Pike said (per the condition she just set). Beth's broadcast is then the decision they make together, inside what Pike allowed: they do not go to Arsenault; they tell him where she is and make him come to the place they control. Dane agrees because her plan turns his "hold the boat" into a place where Arsenault has to cross thirty-four feet of dark steel she knows and he does not. Show his judgment and hers both improving the plan: he adds the brow chained and the floods on a switch only she can reach; she adds the stern chain and the buoy.
- Fix the "Nobody shoots the ferry captain" exchange: Dane still objects; Ned goes anyway; Dane makes him carry Russ's scanner frequency and walk in daylight on the open road. One more line of reason, not reassurance.

## C4. Ch 10 — Night 9, the two of them

- After the scene in her bed, add the ordinary exchange the panels asked for, in place of most of the Pike and Moreau talk (keep at most two lines of that). Something they enjoy or want together that is not the case: she asks what Christmas in Lowell is like; he describes his father's bus route and his mother's ham and says he would like to put her on the bus; she says she'll need a reason to miss two ferry runs; he gives her one. Warm and a little funny, like the trauma-kit joke. It must not become another orderly exchange about reporting and disclosure.
- Optional: one line where she tells him, in her own house, what her silence on Wednesday cost her to carry. Only if Ch 08's admission leaves room; do not repeat it.
- The "I love you" on the car deck then echoes something begun that night. Do not add a declaration on Night 9.

## C5. Voice pass, whole book (voice-editor)

- **Gesture then explanation.** Where a gesture (a hand stops, waits, closes the inch, stays) already shows the meaning, cut the sentence that explains it. Chapters 06–08 first, then the rest. Cap "stopped short / an inch short / and waited" at 2 in the book.
- **Dane's spoken dialogue** in his longer, level register throughout, as his narration already is. No short aphoristic trades at the kitchen table. Beth is the one who talks like a logbook.
- Clue reconstructions: when narration retells clues the reader saw in the last chapter, cut to one line.
- No new tics: watch for "in order," watching hands, ticking stoves.

## C6. Cross-chapter check (continuity-checker, after the prose changes)

Read Ch 05–10 together and check every commitment the changes create: the alarm and check-in timeline, the tide clock from 10:05 low water through the church at one and the ferry at two (agree with the bible's tide table), Russ's knowledge of Ashby, who knew the landing window and how, the relief timing (why Saturday patrol boats come but the pier was not usable earlier), Ned's walk, and anything a new line in Ch 07 or 08 promises later.

## Process

2. Continuity-checker: C6, saving `reviews/v4-continuity-check.md`. Prose-writer fixes.

4. Repetition audit on v4.
5. A fresh cold read that answers the three completion questions from the manuscript alone.
6. Promote v4, reassemble `manuscript.md`, stop.
