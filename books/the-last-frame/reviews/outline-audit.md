## Part B. Findings

**No blocking findings.** Every setback, deadline and decision has an answer on the page to questions 2, 3 and 4.

### Non-blocking (ordered by cost to the reader)

**Finding 1.** Ch 2 ("Danny's signature putting the store behind it. His father put the store up once, in 1974… Ray has never asked it of the store, and won't ask twice"); Ch 6 ending ("a cashier's check drawn on the store's credit-union account"; Ray and Danny at the adding machine).
**Fails:** Q5 (consistency). Act 1 is irreversible because Ray won't ask the store twice. Eight weeks later he draws $48,000 on the store's account, with Danny at the tape. A reader who remembers Lucien's 1974 will see the vow break, and the five o'clock turn will look arbitrary in hindsight.
**The architect should:** draw the check on Ray's own savings or his house, and say so in the Ch 6 ending line. Or make the check the second ask, have Danny name it as such, and let Ray pay that cost on the page.

**Finding 2.** Ch 7 (in her head: "she's four short"); Ch 9 ("about eleven hundred dollars under… with one day left"); Ch 10 s3 ("twenty-eight thousand nine hundred"; "most of her savings go on the note"); Author decision 19 ("a paydown of about $15,000").
**Fails:** Q5 (what she knows at each moment).
**The architect should** reconcile three things:
- (a) On Feb 2 the Open, with Linda's name, is booked and projected to clear, so "four short" is the stale January figure. Have her think it as the number without the Open.
- (b) The Ch 9 figure should already include Sunday's league income. Otherwise Sunday adds nothing and the Ch 10 count can't land exactly where Saturday left it.
- (c) At eight to one, a $1,100 gap needs about $8,800, not $15,000. Either the paydown is "about nine thousand, most of what she has", or her savings are smaller. Bible-builder sets the figures, but the outline's "about $15,000" will be carried forward as written.

**Finding 3.** Ch 4 money fact ("his league costs her seven hundred dollars every week it bowls"); Ch 7 cost ("Two teams"); Ch 8 s1 (Ray wins them back).
**Fails:** Q5 (what the reader can work out from the page). By the book's own Ch 4 fact, league bowlers lose her money. Two teams walking out reduces her weekly loss, and Ray winning them back increases it. A reader who holds the $700 fact will notice.
**The architect should** say in one line what the two teams cost her: their Open entries, their places in the Memorial, or the town's opinion. Or have Ellen note dryly that their leaving saves her money and costs her the town.

**Finding 4.** Ch 8 s5 ("The league bowls its own Memorial on Saturday afternoon, on four lanes, the way it always has"); Ch 3 ("Linda's Memorial stays as Linda bowled it"); `story-bible-v1.md` World rule 4 (the championship is on all ten lanes, top teams from both divisions).
**Fails:** a fact conflict, not a causal question.
**The architect should** pick one format for the Memorial (four lanes or ten) and use it in Ch 3, Ch 8 and Ch 9. The Ch 9 schedule (Memorial at four, Open final at seven) depends on how many lanes the Memorial takes.

**Finding 5.** Ch 1 ("He says he wanted her to hear it from him before the hardware store said it"); F1 row ("It's the argument, not news").
**Fails:** Q1 (wording).
**The architect should:** since Ellen signed the contract, change the line to what he wants her to hear first: that the town already knows she signed, that the Bench has the date, and that he won't pretend he isn't waiting. As written, the beat sheet will read it as a disclosure.

**Finding 6.** Ch 2 ("the only other person who came to look was a man from Concord who wanted the lot for condos"); Ch 2 turn ("there's no one else").
**Fails:** Q4 (a third buyer exists on the page).
**The architect should:** add one clause from Ray saying the condo man offered land value, under the note, so selling to him wouldn't pay Arlene or make Ellen whole. Then "there's no one else" holds, and the man stays in place for Arlene's Ch 8 foreclosure line.

**Finding 7 (moral weight, Ellen's signature).** Ch 8 s5 ("She signs, for her own reason"); Condemnable choices (lists Ray's request as #3, not Ellen's signature).
**Fails:** none of questions 1–5. This is about framing.
Ellen co-signs a release that puts an 84-year-old's place at The Pines on her own number, for a reason about her relationship with Ray. A reasonable reader may condemn her signature as much as his request. The outline should list it among the condemnable choices so the beats don't write it as a grace note. The book already meets the requirement for at least one condemnable choice several times over.
