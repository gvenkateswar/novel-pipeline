## Part A. Event ledger (questions 1–5)

### A1. The backup purchase agreement and the March 1 deadline (Open for the author 1; Ch 1)
1. **Who knew?** Arlene (her condition for carrying the note, set before Dec 1). Ray (signed as backup). Ellen: the outline doesn't say. Ch 1 has Ray tell her "so that she hears it from him and not from the hardware store", which treats it as news to her.
2. **Channel.** Ray, in the doorway. The outline doesn't say who signed the agreement.
3. **Mechanism.** On March 1 the building belongs to Ellen. Arlene can't contract to sell what she no longer owns, and the bible's research notes say NH foreclosure takes weeks of notice. A backup sale "that afternoon" works only if **Ellen herself** signed, at the Dec 1 closing, a contract to sell to Ray on March 1 if her refinance doesn't fund. If she signed it, Ray's doorway line isn't news. Ch 8's release then needs her signature too, so she would learn of it on Feb 6, not from Ray on Feb 16. If she didn't sign, the backup can't deliver the building on March 1, and the deadline goes back to binding no one. → **Finding 1 (blocking).**
4. **Safer options for Arlene:** thirty days (refused: the Pines); the $140,000 (closed: "The Pines doesn't take half, dear"). Pass, once Finding 1 is answered.
5. **Follows?** Ellen accepting a backup clause as the price of the note is credible. The outline needs to say she did.

### A2. The bank's $30,000 bar (Ch 1)
1. **Who knew?** The bank, Ellen, and then the town.
2. **Channel.** Ellen, out loud, in her own vestibule, to Ray, in front of Denise and bowlers. Named. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Her choice not to keep it private, made in anger, and it costs her the pool. Pass.
4. **Safer options.** Not applicable.
5. **Follows?** A private woman boasting her loan terms is out of character, but it is the mistake of someone being needled, and it has a cost. Pass.

### A3. The five o'clock buyout (Ch 2, Act 1 end)
1. **Who knew?** Ray, Ellen, Kate (on the phone), the Bench (at 4:58).
2. **Channel.** In person. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Why it expires.** "His lender holds that number until the bank closes at five tomorrow; after that it's the backup price or nothing." A commercial lender doesn't hold a larger acquisition loan for one day and then reduce it permanently. The building also has an appraisal (Judy "has had the appraisal since Christmas", Ch 7), and a lender that sized Ray's backup loan to a failing building has no reason to approve the at-cost figure even for a day. The outline doesn't say why the at-cost number can never come back, or why no third buyer exists. The Act 1 point of no return rests on a lender rule a reader won't believe. → **Finding 2 (blocking).**
5. **Follows?** Ellen at his counter at 4:58, watching the clock go past five, follows from what she wants and is a strong turn. The expiry under it is what fails.

### A4. Storm night (Ch 3)
1. **Who knew?** The 3 p.m. forecast (three to five inches, ending by nine). The Highway Department captain (3:10 call: the forecast is wrong). Ellen.
2. **Channel.** Phone and forecast. Named. Pass.
3. **Closable?** She could cancel league at 3:10. She didn't, it was her call, and it is on the page. Pass.
4. **Safer options:** cancel at 3:10 (refused on the page, with a cost); send everyone home at 7:40 when the band stalls; hold everyone at 8:55. The 8:55 hold has its reason ("thirty-one cars on Kettle Road in that is worse"). The 7:40 window gets no line. Minor.
5. **Follows?** Yes. A controller trusting the official forecast over a plow boss is a credible mistake with a cost.

### A5. The promise and "no readback" (Ch 3)
1. **Who knew?** Ellen, from the moment it happened. On Dec 17 she already knows league nights lose about $700 a week (she sees it in her first week; Ch 4), so she knows the condition is likely to bite.
2. **Channel.** None. The strip, the box and the log are cut. Pass.
3. **Closable?** She can repeat it at any point before Ch 6. Her reason not to at the moment (he is turning the pin, looking at her "as if she's handed him something") is credible. Pass.
4. **Safer option:** say it again. Refused, on purpose. That is the design.
5. **Follows?** Yes, and it is more culpable than in v1 because she knows the numbers. Pass.

### A6. The rate vote (Ch 4 to Ch 5, the midpoint)
1. **Who knew?** Ellen (the $700 loss, from week one). Ray (told in Ch 4; he says he'll stand against it). The league (at the Jan 5 meeting).
2. **Channel.** Ch 4 argument; the meeting. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Safer option for Ellen:** raise her own price. Lineage is the house's price; a proprietor doesn't usually need the customers' vote to set it. The outline says "The bylaws set rates only at the September and January meetings", but league bylaws bind the league, not the owner. The outline doesn't say what binds Ellen: a season contract with the house, Arlene's sale terms, or the sanction. Without that, a reader asks why she lets her customers vote on her prices. Raising it anyway risks teams walking, which happens in Ch 7 regardless. → **Finding 3 (blocking).**
5. **Follows?** Ray standing against her five days after the kiss follows from Ch 4, where he told her he would. Pass.

### A7. Taking Linda's night (Ch 5 to Ch 6)
1. **Who knew?** Ellen, Denise (Ch 5: "Who did you say that to?"), Ray (bob-house), Hollis and the committee (Jan 16), the league (Jan 18–19).
2. **Channel.** The Bench's board, Jan 18; "by Tuesday the league knows". Named. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Safer option:** an Open on championship night with its own cup, leaving Linda's name and trophy alone. The outline's answer to the earlier finding: "The derby committee will move its weekend crowd indoors only for a final its own people can enter… The numbers really do say otherwise." But the committee's condition is about who can **enter**, not about Linda's name. In Ch 8, Ray's condition strips Linda's name off the Open, and Hollis answers for the committee: "We voted on an Open, not on a name." The outline's own Ch 8 shows the safer option worked all along. The only difference is nine entries, which were her "cushion". So the numbers didn't say otherwise. She took Linda's name for margin. → **Finding 4 (blocking).** That may be the stronger book, but the outline claims the opposite, and Denise's Ch 5 beat and the F8 row are built on that claim.
5. **Follows?** It can't be judged until Finding 4 is resolved.

### A8. "Did you know I didn't hear it?" / "I said it." (Ch 6)
1. **Who knew?** Ellen. Ray suspects, and asks.
2. **Channel.** In person. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Safer option:** the true sentence. Refused, on the page. That is the design.
5. **Follows?** Yes. The condemnable choice is explicit, to his face. Pass.

### A9. The walkout and the captain's charge (Ch 6 to Ch 7)
1. **Who knew about the storm-night promise?** Ellen and Ray, in the pit behind lane 5, under a crowd's roar. Denise (inferred, Ch 5). Possibly Hollis (outside the bob-house).
2. **Channel.** In Ch 7 the captain says publicly that "she gave Ray her word and broke it". The outline doesn't say how the captain learned of a private promise made in the pit. Ray refused to sign his petition, so Ray didn't tell him. → **Finding 5 (blocking).** This line triggers Ray's public defense, which forces her confession, so the rupture rests on it.
3. **Closable?** It can't be judged until the channel is named.
4. **Safer options for the teams:** stay. Closed: the Highway Department has the storm-night grievance, and the Legion goes out of loyalty because half of them plow for him. Pass.
5. **Follows?** Ray's "That's mine" follows from his character. Pass.

### A10. Ray's $48,000 check (Ch 7)
1. **Who knew?** Ray, Danny.
2. **Channel.** In person. The check is drawn on the store's credit-union account, and the lender is in Concord, so Granite Lakes holds neither. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Safer option for Ellen:** take it. Closed: "If I take it I'll never know which of us carried it", plus the unspoken reason in her head. Pass.
5. **Follows?** Yes. Pass.

### A11. The confession and the cruel line (Ch 7)
All five questions pass. The confession comes from her own mouth, made necessary by his defense of her. "You sounded fine on the radio that night too, I bet" is credible and has a cost (Ch 8).

### A12. The side match (Ch 8 s1)
1–3. Pass (Gil, Denise as scorer, Denise tells Ellen).
4. **Stakes.** If Ray loses, he bowls in Gil's league. That is a stake for Gil, and only weakly one for the defectors.
5. **Continuity.** "If he wins, both teams come back **at the new rate**." The rate was voted down on Jan 5, so there is no new rate. → **Finding 9.**

### A13. Arlene lets Ray out of the backup (Ch 8 s2)
1. **Who knew?** Ray, Arlene. Ellen, only if she is a party to the agreement (Finding 1).
2. **Channel.** Arlene's kitchen; Concord by phone. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Arlene's safer option is to say no, and every fact the outline gives her points to no.** She refused thirty days in Ch 1. She would carry the note "only if someone was contracted to buy the building on March 1… so that Arlene is paid that day either way". On Feb 6 Ellen has been projected short since Jan 5, and her cushion depends on an Open that hasn't run. Arlene's answer ("she bet on Ellen in November and hasn't changed her bet") contradicts the hedge she demanded in November. An 84-year-old giving up her place at The Pines, which took four years on a list, three weeks before the balloon, needs a reason a reader accepts. → **Finding 6 (blocking).**
5. **Ray's choice.** It puts Arlene's housing at risk, and makes Ellen's failure worse for her (no buyer, so default and foreclosure instead of a sale), in order to make a romantic gesture. The outline gives Ellen "one breath" of anger and moves on. A reader may condemn this, and the outline doesn't treat it as a choice to condemn. See Finding 6 and Finding 13.

### A14. Ray's condition: Linda's name off the Open (Ch 8 s5)
1–3. Pass (Hollis by phone for the committee; Ellen phones the entrants herself).
4. **What it does to the Memorial.** Linda's trophy "goes back in the case". The outline doesn't say whether the league's own Memorial runs this year. If it doesn't, Ray's condition cancels Linda's night, which is the opposite of what he asked for on Dec 17. → **Finding 10.**
5. **Follows?** Protecting her name from sale is credible for Ray. The outline needs to say what happens to the league's final.

### A15. Storm day: the refunds (Ch 9)
1. **Who knew?** Hollis (radio), Ellen, Ray, Denise, the treasurer.
2. **Channel.** Radio. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Safer options:** keep everyone (closed: 150 people with children, and the boiler). Rain checks (closed: her printed terms promise cash, and the entrants are driving back to Massachusetts). The treasurer's standing is closed (the raffle draw). Pass.
5. **Follows?** Yes. Keeping her printed word, after breaking her spoken one, is a good design. Pass.

### A16. The savings paydown (Ch 9 to Ch 10)
1. **Who knew savings existed?** Ellen. Kate (Ch 2: "The house. Not the rest. I swear."). The reader, from Ch 2, with no amount.
2. **Who knew a paydown lowers the bar?** Ellen, from Ch 7 ("The money pays the note down far enough that the bank's number drops"). Yet in Ch 10 s1 she asks Judy "whether paying the note down at closing changes the committee's number", a question she has known the answer to since Feb 2. Minor; see Finding 11.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Safer source for the paydown: the winter's own cash.** The alley has just earned about $28,900 of operating income, and it sits in the business account. On the page, the obvious first source for a $15,000 paydown is that account, not the emergency savings she swore to Kate she'd never touch. In the bible, what stops this is the bank's liquidity requirement (cash on hand at closing). The outline has moved that "offstage". With the reason off the page, the savings sacrifice looks chosen because the plot needs a loss. → **Finding 7 (blocking).**
5. **Does the savings, available all along, undercut earlier stakes?** **Not in the arithmetic. On the page, it does, until the ratio is shown.** By the bible's own figures, $1 of principal moves the bar by about 12 cents. $48,000 moves it about $5,700, which matches v1's $29,600 to $23,950. So $15,000 moves it about $1,800. That covers the $1,100 storm-day hole, but not the $4,000 January gap (about $34,000 needed) or anything before it. The stakes hold. But the outline puts the ratio on the page only in Ch 10 s3 ("The four thousand… would have taken more than she had"). A reader who has learned in Ch 2 that "the rest" exists, and in Ch 7 that paydowns work, will spend Ch 7 to Ch 9 believing she has a way out she isn't using. → **Finding 8.**

### A17. Sunday, the final and the count (Ch 10)
1–5. Pass. The derby's Sunday is cancelled for slush and water, a stated measurement, so no recovery comes through the door. The final runs on fees already paid. The count is still under. Ray "doesn't offer his money", consistent with Ch 7. Judy is an entrant in a borrower's Open, so her entry fee counts toward the income she is underwriting. Minor; see Finding 11.

## Part B. Findings

### Blocking

**Finding 1.** Open for the author 1 ("At the Dec 1 closing, Arlene would carry the $250,000 only if someone was contracted to buy the building on March 1"); Ch 1 ("He tells her he's Arlene's backup buyer so that she hears it from him and not from the hardware store"); Ch 8 s2 ("She signs").
**Fails:** Q2 (who is party to the agreement, and how the building moves on March 1).
**The architect must answer:** Did Ellen sign the backup at the Dec 1 closing? Only a sale contract signed by the owner can move her building to Ray on March 1; Arlene can't sell what she sold, and foreclosure takes weeks. If Ellen signed it: Ray's doorway line must be an argument about something she already knows, not a disclosure. Releasing Ray in Ch 8 then needs her consent too, so she learns of it on Feb 6, and the Ch 8 s5 reveal and Arlene's private signing both have to change. If she didn't sign it: say what legal mechanism delivers the building on March 1. One sentence in Ch 1 settles which.

**Finding 2.** Shape ("It cannot come back (his lender only backs the backup price after that)"); Ch 2 ("His lender holds that number until the bank closes at five tomorrow").
**Fails:** Q4 (the irreversibility rests on a lender rule that doesn't exist in practice).
**The architect must answer:** Make five o'clock **Ray's** deadline, for a reason in his character. For example: he'll make the at-cost offer once, because past Thursday he'd be paying $390,000 for a building he can have for the backup price, and he won't ask her twice. Or give it a real external trigger the reader recognises, such as a rate lock that expires, or Danny's signature on the store as collateral, good for one day only. Then add one line on why no other buyer exists, for example Arlene had the place listed for two years and only two people ever came to see it.

**Finding 3.** Ch 4 ("she's putting a raise to the league at the January meeting under Article Four"); F7 row ("The bylaws set rates only at the September and January meetings"); Ch 5 ("The vote is no").
**Fails:** Q4.
**The architect must answer:** What binds the owner to the league's vote on her own price? Name the instrument in one line, in Ch 4: for example, the season agreement Arlene signed with the league in September, which Ellen inherited at closing and which fixes lineage until the January meeting votes. Without it, a reader will ask why she doesn't just raise it, and that question lands on the midpoint itself.

**Finding 4.** Ch 5 ("The only night in the season that could make it up is the championship, if the town could enter it"); F8 row ("The numbers really do say otherwise"); Ch 6 (Hollis: "Our people come in off the ice to bowl. Not to watch your league"); Ch 8 s5 (Hollis: "We voted on an Open, not on a name"; "Nine entrants take their money back. Her cushion is gone").
**Fails:** Q4. The outline's own Ch 8 shows the safer option (an Open on championship night with its own cup) works, and that it costs only the cushion.
**The architect must answer:** Choose one, and rewrite the F8 row and Denise's Ch 5 beat to match. (a) She takes Linda's name for margin, knowing an unnamed Open would just clear, and the page says so in her own head in Ch 5. This sharpens the condemnable choice. Or (b) give a January fact that makes the name necessary then, and that has stopped being true by Feb 16, for example entries were selling only on Linda's name until the derby crowd committed. As written, a reader will reach Hollis's Ch 8 line and conclude she never needed to break her word.

**Finding 5.** Ch 7 ("The captain says she gave Ray her word and broke it").
**Fails:** Q2.
**The architect must answer:** How does the Highway Department captain know about a promise made in the pit under a crowd's roar, when Ray refused his petition? Name the carrier: for example Denise, who asked "Who did you say that to?" in Ch 5, or Ray himself, saying in the store on Jan 19, in front of the Bench, that she'd given her word. The carrier has to be someone whose telling costs them something, or it becomes a convenience.

**Finding 6.** Ch 8 s2 ("Arlene: she bet on Ellen in November and hasn't changed her bet. She signs"); Open for the author 1 ("so that Arlene is paid that day either way"); Ch 1 ("The Pines doesn't take half, dear").
**Fails:** Q4 (Arlene's safer option is no) and Q5 (her yes contradicts the hedge she demanded and the deadline she refused to move).
**The architect must answer:** Why does a creditor who wouldn't give thirty days, and who carried the note only against a backup buyer, give up the backup three weeks before the balloon, while Ellen is projected short? Give Arlene a reason that costs her, in her own terms. For example: she releases him because she won't hold Raymond to buying a building he has stopped wanting, and she tells him exactly what she is risking. Then the page has to treat Ray's request as a choice with victims. Ellen's "one breath" of anger is too little for a man who has put an 84-year-old's housing on her number without asking her.

**Finding 7.** Ch 10 money fact ("About fifteen thousand of her own savings, paid into the note at closing"); The money, on the page ("Not on the page at all: Judy's cash test").
**Fails:** Q4 (the safer source, the winter's own operating cash, isn't closed on the page).
**The architect must answer:** Why can't the paydown come out of the business account that the winter just filled? Put the reason on the page once: for example, in Ch 10 s1 Judy says the committee wants the winter's cash left in the account at closing as working capital, so any paydown has to be new money. Otherwise the savings sacrifice reads as invented to give Act 3 a loss.

### Non-blocking (answer before beats)

**Finding 8.** Ch 2 ("The house. Not the rest. I swear"); Ch 7 (the paydown mechanism); Ch 10 s3 ("The four thousand she was short in January would have taken more than she had").
**Fails:** Q9-adjacent (the reader holds a solution the page hasn't ruled out).
**The architect must answer:** Move the ratio forward to Ch 7, inside Ray's offer, in a line the reader can carry: for example, it takes forty-eight thousand to move the number four. Then "the rest" can't look like a way out in Ch 5 to Ch 9, and the Ch 9 shortfall is the first time her savings could reach. The arithmetic holds: $15,000 moves the bar about $1,800.

**Finding 9.** Ch 8 s1 ("both teams come back at the new rate").
**The architect must answer:** The rate was voted down on Jan 5, so there is no new rate. Change the line to the actual terms of their return, and give the defectors something they want if Ray loses, not only something Gil wants.

**Finding 10.** Ch 8 s5 ("Linda's trophy goes back in the case"); Ch 10 s2 (Ray restores Linda's name; Denise wins her trophy).
**The architect must answer:** Between Feb 16 and Feb 28, does the league's own Memorial run this year? If Ray's condition cancels it, he has asked for the opposite of what he asked on Dec 17, and the page has to show he knows it. Say whether the Sunday final is the league's or the Open's.

**Finding 11.** Ch 10 s1 (Ellen asks Judy "whether paying the note down at closing changes the committee's number"; "Judy bowls her Open flight").
**The architect must answer:** Ellen has known the mechanism since Ch 7, so make it a request to Judy, not a question. Judy entering the Open pays money into the income she is underwriting. Either cut her entry, or have her say she paid at the door like anyone and the fee is excluded, in one line.

**Finding 12.** Open for the author 4 ("Nothing is withheld from either lead for longer than the chapter it happens in").
**The architect must answer:** This claim is false. Ellen keeps from Ray that she watched him not hear, from Ch 3 (Dec 17) to Ch 7 (Feb 2), four chapters, and lies about it in Ch 6. That is the design and it is dramatic irony, so the reader isn't cheated. But the author is being told the brief is obeyed. Correct the line and ask the author to approve the exception explicitly.

**Finding 13 (moral weight, Ray).** Ch 8 s2 and s5.
**The architect must answer:** Ray's release puts Arlene's place, and a worse failure outcome for Ellen (default, not sale), on Ellen's number without asking either of them first. That is a second choice a reasonable reader may condemn, and the outline presents it as a gift. Decide which it is and write the cost.

**Finding 14 (ending).** Ch 10 ("hers, paid for twice").
**The architect must answer:** The winter doesn't clear $30,000. She buys the bar down. The chosen angle's promise is that she makes the alley pay. Say once, in Ellen's or Judy's voice, what the winter actually made against the old bar, so the reader judges the win knowingly, not as a financing trick revealed at the end. This is an author call, not a logic hole.

**Finding 15.** Ray's backup "at a price that pays Arlene and leaves Ellen little" against the bible's "Profit from someone else's mistake on purpose" (Ray never would).
**The architect must answer:** One line from Ray on why the backup price isn't profiting from her failure: for example, it's what Arlene is owed and not a dollar under it.

## Part C. Questions 6–9

### 6. Stakes curve
| Act | Ellen's cost | Ray's cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (Ch 1–2) | Public humiliation; night one in the red; the pool (her own boast); the clean exit, seen expiring (subject to Finding 2); her oath to Kate on the record | His key; standing as her backup buyer |
| 2a (Ch 3–5) | Integrity (the promise left unconfirmed); the captain's goodwill; voted down in public by the man she kissed; $4,000 short | A renewal fee; voting against the woman he loves |
| 2b (Ch 6–7) | A lie told to his face; Ray; two teams; the key; the 2 a.m. night used as a weapon | Her; the man who said that line |
| 3 (Ch 8–10) | A public confession; Arlene's place on her number; nine refunds; the storm refunds, permanent; her savings and her oath, permanent | The building, for good; Linda's ball out of the locker; Linda's name given to the town |

The costs climb, and Act 3's are the first that stay lost. **Pass**, subject to Findings 6 and 7, since two of Act 3's costs rest on them.

### 7. Subplots the protagonist doesn't resolve personally
Denise and Linda's trophy (Denise). The two teams (Ray). The derby (Hollis). Kate is now resolved through Ellen's ask, so it is no longer an unresolved thread, but the first three suffice. **Pass.** (Finding 9 still applies to the teams' return terms.)

### 8. Moral weight
Ellen in Ch 6 ("I said it", refusing the true sentence when asked directly) will be condemned. Ray's Ch 7 line will be condemned. Ellen running league against the captain's warning, then charging the people she stranded, is a lesser but real one. **Pass.** See Finding 13 for a fourth that the outline doesn't treat as one.

### 9. Reader knowledge
Nothing is withheld from the reader. Suspense runs on the vote (guessable from Ch 4, where Ray says he'll stand against it: one chapter early, acceptable), on whether Ray learns what she knew (Ch 3 to Ch 7, dramatic irony), on the weather, and on the number. **Pass**, subject to Finding 8: the reader must not think she has an unused solution.
