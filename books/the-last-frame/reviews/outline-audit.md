# Outline audit — The Last Frame, outline v2

*Plot-auditor, Mode 1. Read: `outline.md` (v2 rebuild, Oct 28), `research/chosen-angle.md`, `book-brief.md`, `genre-maps/romance.md`, and `story-bible-v1.md` (money table and research notes only, for established facts). Standard applied: would a reader with only the page accept it? "The outline says so" does not count as an answer.*

## Part A. Event ledger (questions 1–5)

Each event is run through the five causal questions. Where an answer fails, it points to a Finding in Part B.

### A1. The March 1 deadline and Arlene's "No" (Ch 1)
1. **Who knew?** Arlene (the Pines rule, for four years). Ellen, at the snack bar on Dec 1. The town presumably knows the balloon date, since the pool sheet is built on it.
2. **Channel.** Arlene's mouth, to Ellen, at the snack bar. Named. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Safer options for Arlene:** (a) give thirty days; (b) put the $140,000 she received at the Dec 1 closing toward the Pines fee; (c) set up Ray as a backup buyer who closes on March 1 if the refinance fails. The outline closes only (a). The outline's own dates break the failure case: Ray's letter is extended "through March 31" (Ch 4), so a sale to Ray happens in March, after the Pines has given her unit away. So if Ellen misses, Arlene does not get paid on March 1 either, and her "No" gains her nothing. → **Finding 1 (blocking).**
5. **Follows from knowledge?** Ellen's question does. Arlene's refusal doesn't, until Finding 1 is answered.

### A2. The bank's $30,000 bar (Ch 1)
1. **Who knew?** Granite Lakes Savings, Judy, Ellen. In Ch 1, Denise and "the town" know the exact terms, closely enough that Ellen "corrects one number".
2. **Channel.** "Denise explains the terms the way the town tells them." The outline doesn't say how a bank's private underwriting condition got from Judy's office to the hardware store's pool sheet. Judy works to the rules, and Ellen is not shown telling anyone. → **Finding 2 (blocking).**
3. **Closable?** It can't be judged until the channel is named.
4. **Safer options.** Not applicable.
5. **Follows?** Ellen correcting the number is in character, but it confirms the leak is accurate, which makes the missing channel worse.

### A3. The buyout refusal (Ch 2, the declared Act 1 point of no return)
1. **Who knew?** Ray (offer and financing), Ellen, and Kate (on the phone mid-offer).
2. **Channel.** In person; Kate by phone. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Safer options for Ellen:** (a) take it and walk away whole; (b) ask for a week; (c) counter. Her reason, "the one thing she's picked", is credible for Dec 3. But the outline never withdraws the offer. Ray stays a "ready buyer through March 31" (Ch 4) and then offers a buy-in (Ch 7). On Jan 1, when Ellen learns she "bought half-blind", taking Ray's at-cost offer would save her house money **and** keep the Memorial as Ray heard it. The outline doesn't say whether that offer still stands, or, if it does, why she doesn't take it on Jan 1. Without that answer, the refusal isn't a point of no return. → **Finding 3 (blocking).**
5. **Follows?** On Dec 3, yes. On Jan 1, not answered.

### A4. Storm night: 31 stranded (Ch 3)
1. **Who knew a nor'easter was coming?** Anyone with a radio. Hollis (28 years of weather calls, a plow route). Ellen (31 years of ground stops).
2. **Channel.** A forecast, a weather radio, Hollis. The outline doesn't mention any forecast.
3. **Closable?** Yes. A controller cancels a Thursday league night that afternoon. Hollis tells everyone to go home at seven.
4. **Safer options:** cancel league that afternoon; send people home at the first heavy band; Hollis plows the drive early. None is closed. The heroine's defining skill is acting on weather before it arrives, so a reader will notice. → **Finding 4 (blocking).**
5. **Follows?** It can't follow until the outline says why the storm caught both safety professionals out.

### A5. The promise and NO READBACK (Ch 3)
1. **Who knew?** Ellen, from the moment Denise's strike covered "unless the numbers say otherwise". Ray never knew. The reader knows.
2. **Channel.** The strip, filed "in the box with the night's strips". That box is the after-hours box Ray must write in on **every visit** (Ch 2: "every visit goes on a strip in the box by the office door").
3. **Closable?** Yes, for seven weeks (Dec 17 to Feb 2). She can repeat the condition at dawn, in Ch 4, or in the bob-house. She can also move the strip out of the one box her rival-lover is required to open. In Ch 5 the box is "at her elbow" and she doesn't open it. In Ch 6 she chooses "I said it" and leaves out the rest, so she knows the strip is now evidence against a lie she has just told. The outline gives a reason for not repeating the condition ("he has just told her about Linda"). It gives no reason for leaving the proof in Ray's box after Jan 2. → **Finding 5 (blocking).**
4. **Safer options:** repeat the condition at dawn; tell him in the bob-house; file the strip in the office or the safe. Only the first is addressed.
5. **Follows?** Not saying it again on Dec 17 is a credible, costly mistake. Pass. But a reader may not condemn it at all; see Finding 16.

### A6. The midpoint: league nights lose money (Ch 5)
1. **Who knew?** Arlene, for twenty years. Ray ("knows the league doesn't pay its way"). Denise, who works the counter since 2004 and says "Now do a Tuesday" in Ch 4, so she knows. The bank's underwriter, who saw Arlene's books. **And Ellen**, who by Jan 1 has run nine league nights from her own counter: taking lineage, paying staff, paying the house prize contribution, and invoicing Ray's calls. In Ch 2 she says Ray's "free labor is half of why Arlene's numbers looked better than they were".
2. **Channel.** The outline says Arlene, at her kitchen table. But the faster channel is Ellen's own register, plus Denise, and the outline doesn't close either one.
3. **Closable?** It doesn't need closing. It needs explaining: why did a woman who labels 41 things buy with $140,000 of house money "on the summary statements", and then not see a $700-a-week loss in her own drawer for a month?
4. **Safer option for Arlene:** she "wants to be paid", so she would tell Ellen on Dec 1, at the snack bar, where she has already spoken up about the deadline. That would give Ellen four more weeks at the corrected rate. The outline's stated motive argues for telling her earlier. → **Finding 6 (blocking).**
5. **Follows?** Ellen's surprise ("You sold me a building that loses money two nights a week") contradicts her own Ch 2 line.

### A7. The rate rise and the Open (Ch 6–7)
1. **Who knew?** Ray (Jan 2, bob-house). Hollis and the whole derby committee (Jan 16, by public vote). Danny (Feb 1). The league is told formally only on Feb 2.
2. **Channel.** The committee vote on Jan 16 makes the Open public, and its final is the league's own Memorial. The outline doesn't say how the league avoids hearing about it for 17 days in a town where the pool sheet sits in the hardware store. It also doesn't say why the rate rise Ellen calls urgent ("next September is too late: close is short") waits a month, at about $400 a week. → **Finding 7 (blocking).**
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Safer options for Ellen:** (a) run the Open around the league's final, which is exactly Ray's Ch 8 condition; (b) hold the Open on a different weekend; (c) raise lineage only. Ch 8 shows (a) works: "Without a paid gate on the final, she's projected just over the bank's number." The outline doesn't show that in January the numbers really required the paid gate on **Linda's** final. If they didn't, the numbers never "said otherwise", and she broke the promise for margin, not survival. → **Finding 8 (blocking).**
5. **Follows?** It can't be judged until Finding 8 is answered.

### A8. The two teams walk out (Ch 7) and come back (Ch 8)
1. **Who knew?** The Highway Department captain (charged on storm night), the Legion, Gil, Ray, and Denise (who keeps score at Pine Ridge and tells Ellen).
2. **Channel.** The meeting; Denise. Named. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Safer options for the teams:** pay and grumble; finish the season and leave in September. Driving forty minutes in February to join someone else's mid-season league is a large protest. It is acceptable for the Highway Department (Ch 3 grievance plus the rate). **The Legion has no grievance on the page except the rate.**
5. **The return bet.** "If he wins, both teams go back … at twelve-fifty." The outline doesn't say what the teams win if Ray loses, so there is no reason for them to accept the bet. → **Finding 9.**

### A9. Ray's $48,000 check and Ellen's refusal (Ch 6–7)
1. **Who knew?** Ray and Danny (adding machine, Feb 1). Granite Lakes, which issues the cashier's check and is also Ellen's refinancing bank.
2. **Channel.** In person, on the hood. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Safer option for Ellen:** take it. Her stated reason: "She can't take his money for a promise she knows he never heard." But the check's purpose is that "the league stays as it is and so does the Memorial". Taking it would **make the promise true as he heard it**. The stated reason doesn't follow from the facts. Her real reasons would be control, or "on my own", and the outline needs to put the real one on the page. → **Finding 10.**
5. **The bank.** The outline doesn't say whether Ray's lender is Granite Lakes. If it is, Judy's bank is underwriting both the buyer-in-waiting and the refinance, which is a conflict and a leak channel nobody addresses. → **Finding 11.**

### A10. The rupture over the strip (Ch 7)
1. **Who knew?** Ellen (since Dec 17); the reader. Ray, from Feb 2, by reading it.
2. **Channel.** The strip box. Named. Pass, given Finding 5.
3. **Closable?** See Finding 5.
4. **Ray's trigger:** he pulls the Dec 17 strips "out of decency" after the Highway captain's complaint. Plausible.
5. **Follows?** "He reads NO READBACK and understands it." That depends on Ray knowing what a readback is. The Ch 2 plant ("She writes his answer down and reads it back once") supports it. Pass. The cruel line is credible and costly. Pass.

### A11. Ray's release and its timing (Ch 8 s2, revealed Ch 10)
1. **Who knew?** Ray and Leclerc. Arlene is Leclerc's client, and Ray visits her across the street the same morning. The reader knows.
2. **Channel.** A signed release held by Leclerc. Named. Not recording it closes the registry leak, which answers v1 audit F6.
3. **Closable?** Partly. Leclerc is Arlene's lawyer, holding a document about a right Arlene created, and the outline doesn't say whether Arlene is told.
4. **The mechanism doesn't work as written.** "Record it with Ms. Daly's closing on March 1, not before." If the refinance fails, there is no closing, so a release keyed to the closing does nothing in the only world where the right of first refusal matters. In the world where she succeeds, the right was never going to be used. As written, Ray gives up nothing. The Ch 10 meaning ("he signed before she asked") depends on the release binding whatever the outcome. The outline also doesn't say what the release changes if she fails: she still has to sell, and Ray is still the only buyer with financing. → **Finding 12 (blocking).**
5. **Follows?** "I don't want it on her mind" is consistent with Ray's character. It also means he lets her make the D-side ask believing he profits if she fails. That is a second withheld fact, held for three chapters, and the brief's "no withheld secrets" applies (see Finding 18).

### A12. Storm day: the refunds (Ch 9)
1. **Who knew?** Hollis (4:10 a.m. call; 1:40 p.m. travel advisory), Ellen, Ray, Denise, and the derby treasurer.
2. **Channel.** Radio. Named. Pass.
3. **Closable?** Not applicable.
4. **Safer options:** (a) keep everyone, as in December. Closed on the page: 150 people including children, and the boiler quits when the power does. Pass. (b) Send them home but move the evening flights to Sunday, or issue rain checks, which keeps the money. **Not closed.** She is already moving the final to Sunday noon. The outline never says why the evening entrants can't be moved too. (c) Send them home without refunding, if the Open's terms allow it. Not addressed. Whose money is being refunded is also unclear: "The derby treasurer objects out loud," but the derby owns its raffle and the alley earns only a venue fee. → **Finding 13 (blocking).**
5. **Follows?** Sending them home: yes. The cash refund: not shown.

### A13. Sunday: the per-day derby fee recovers the money (Ch 10)
1. **Who knew?** Ellen (she set the fee on Jan 16), the committee, Hollis.
2. **Channel.** Hollis by radio: "At 10:30 Hollis calls it: the road's open. The derby's Sunday … comes indoors."
3. **The logic is inverted.** Dock Road is where the derby trucks park and the public access to the ice (bible). If the road is open, the derby can go back **out on the ice**. The outline uses the road opening as the reason the events come indoors and gives no ice condition (slush, flooding, snow load) that keeps the derby off the lake. Without one, the recovery depends on a call that should go the other way. → **Finding 14 (blocking).**
4. **Safer option for the derby:** go back outdoors on Sunday. Not closed.
5. **Ellen's knowledge.** On Saturday she knows the per-day fee exists, so her "eleven hundred under" should already account for a possible Sunday fee. The outline needs to say whether she counts it.

### A14. Ray's condition: no paid gate on the final (Ch 8 s5)
1. **Who knew?** Ray, Ellen.
2. **Channel.** In person. Pass.
3. **Problem.** The derby committee voted on Jan 16 for an Open whose final is the Memorial with "paid entries, spectators". Changing the final privately on Feb 16 changes a public, booked event. The outline doesn't say whether the committee or the ticket buyers have a say. → **Finding 15** (non-blocking, one line).

### A15. The same-day refinance (Ch 10)
The outline's "Get right" field says pre-staging needs "one offstage line in Ch 4 or 6 if the bible needs it", but no chapter is given the line. A reader who sees Sunday's receipts turn into a Monday wire will doubt it. → **Finding 17** (non-blocking).

## Part B. Findings

### Blocking (questions 2, 3 or 4 unanswered)

**Finding 1.** Ch 1 key events (Arlene: "The Pines gives her unit to the next name on the list if she isn't at the door with the check on March 1… No"); Ch 4 ("He is a ready buyer through March 31"); premise fact 3 ("If it doesn't, she has to sell, and Ray gets first crack").
**Fails:** Q4 (Arlene's safer options) and Q2 (how a forced sale reaches Arlene by March 1).
**The architect must answer:** On March 2, if the bank says no, what happens to Arlene's money and to the building, and when? A right-of-first-refusal sale to a buyer whose letter runs through March 31 can't pay Arlene on March 1, so her refusal to extend gains her nothing unless something else does. Also: why can't the $140,000 she received on Dec 1 go toward the Pines fee, and why has no one (Arlene, Ray or Ellen) set up a backup sale to close March 1? The answer has to reach the page in Ch 1 or 2. Otherwise the deadline the whole book counts down to turns out not to bind anyone.

**Finding 2.** Ch 1 money fact ("Denise says it; Ellen corrects one number"); "What changed" table ("The bank's terms arrive in Ch 1 as town talk and one line from Arlene").
**Fails:** Q2.
**The architect must answer:** Who carried the bank's exact condition from Judy's office to the hardware-store pool? Name the person and the moment, for example Ellen telling Arlene, who repeated it at the snack bar, or Ellen telling the Bench herself during purchase-and-sale week. One line in Ch 1.

**Finding 3.** Ch 2 turn ("She refuses the buyout… Point of no return (~15%)"); Ch 4; Ch 5 ("she bought half-blind").
**Fails:** Q4.
**The architect must answer:** Does Ray's offer to buy at what she paid still stand after Dec 3? If not, say when and how it is withdrawn, and make that the irreversible step. If it does, Ch 5 must give the reason Ellen doesn't take it on Jan 1. That day, accepting would return her house money and keep the promise as Ray heard it. Without that, a reader will see a free exit she ignores.

**Finding 4.** Ch 3 opening ("Hollis's plow sideways at the bottom of the Kettle Lanes drive… snow coming faster than he can push it") and key events ("A nor'easter strands 31 bowlers").
**Fails:** Q4 and Q3.
**The architect must answer:** Why didn't a retired controller, who issued ground stops on forecasts for thirty-one years, cancel Thursday league that afternoon, and why didn't Hollis send everyone home at seven? Give the forecast that failed: what was called for, what came, and when it turned. Ellen should make the call to hold everyone visibly, as a decision with a cost, not have the storm make it for her.

**Finding 5.** Ch 2 ("every visit goes on a strip in the box by the office door"); Ch 3 ("She files it in the box with the night's strips"); Ch 5 ("She doesn't open it"); Ch 6 ("She does not say she watched him miss it"); Ch 7 ("He finds the other one").
**Fails:** Q3.
**The architect must answer:** Why does a woman who is now, in Ch 6, actively hiding that she watched him miss it leave the written proof for seven weeks in the one box her lover is required to open on every visit? If the reason is that controllers never destroy or move a record, the page must say so before Ch 7, in her head, as a rule she won't break. Then finding the strip becomes the price of her own integrity, not an accident of filing.

**Finding 6.** Ch 2 (Ellen invoices Ray "because his free labor is half of why Arlene's numbers looked better than they were"); Ch 4 (Denise: "Now do a Tuesday"); Ch 5 ("Ellen bought on the summary statements Arlene's lawyer sent… Ellen: 'You sold me a building that loses money two nights a week'"); "What changed" table ("Arlene raises the league herself on Jan 1… because she wants to be paid").
**Fails:** Q2 (a faster channel, her own register and Denise, is open and not closed) and Q4 (for Arlene, telling her on Dec 1 is the safer option).
**The architect must answer:** (a) Why do nine league nights at her own counter not show Ellen a $700-a-week loss? For example, the house prize contribution and banquet accrual might be paid in a lump at season's end, so the drawer looks fine. (b) Why doesn't Denise, who knows, tell her? (c) Why does a creditor who "wants my money" wait from Dec 1 to Jan 1 to say it? (d) Why did this particular buyer accept summary statements at all? Without (a), the midpoint is information the heroine already had. Without (c), Arlene's stated motive argues against her own timing.

**Finding 7.** Ch 6 ("Raising it next September is too late"; Jan 16 committee vote, "public and booked"); Ch 7 (Feb 2 meeting, "The room turns").
**Fails:** Q2.
**The architect must answer:** (a) How does the league not hear for 17 days that its Memorial has become the final of a public Open? (b) Why does a rate rise Ellen calls urgent wait a month, at about $400 a week? If the bylaws require the scheduled February meeting, say so once in Ch 6. If the town does hear, the walkout has to start building before Feb 2.

**Finding 8.** Ch 5 ("the way to the bank runs through raising the league and turning Linda's Memorial into the final of something the town will pay to watch… The numbers just did"); Ch 8 money fact ("Without a paid gate on the final, she's projected just over the bank's number").
**Fails:** Q4.
**The architect must answer:** What January number shows she needs the paid gate on **Linda's final** in particular, and not an Open on the same weekend that runs around the league's final (the compromise that works in Ch 8)? If the Open without the Memorial gate already clears the bar, then the numbers never "said otherwise", and the moral design changes. She broke the promise for margin. That may be the stronger book, but the outline has to choose and say which.

**Finding 12.** Ch 8 s2 ("record it with Ms. Daly's closing on March 1, not before"); Ch 10 s4 ("recording tomorrow with her closing… he signed before she asked").
**Fails:** Q4 (the mechanism as written doesn't do what the scene says it does).
**The architect must answer:** Does Leclerc record the release on March 1 whether or not the refinance closes? If yes, Ray's instruction must say so in those words. If no, the release only works in the world where it doesn't matter, and Ray gives up nothing. Also state what the release changes for Ellen if she fails. She still has to sell, and Ray is still the only financed buyer. So what exactly did he give away: the building, or only the head start? The Ch 10 reveal lands only if a reader can say what was surrendered.

**Finding 13.** Ch 9 s4 ("She refunds the evening flights at the counter, envelope by envelope, and moves the league final to Sunday noon. The derby treasurer objects out loud").
**Fails:** Q4.
**The architect must answer:** Why can't the evening entrants be moved to Sunday, or given rain checks, when the final is being moved anyway? Give the specific reason, for example Sunday's lanes are full with the kids' division and the final, or the Open's entry terms promised a cash refund if cancelled, so the refunds are the cost of keeping her word. Also say whose money it is, and why the derby treasurer has standing to object.

**Finding 14.** Ch 10 s1 ("At 10:30 Hollis calls it: the road's open. The derby's Sunday, the kids' division and the awards, comes indoors under the per-day fee").
**Fails:** Q4 (the derby's safer option, going back onto the ice, isn't closed) and the trigger runs the wrong way.
**The architect must answer:** What ice condition after Saturday's storm (slush, flooding, snow load, pressure ridges) keeps the derby off the lake on Sunday even though Dock Road is open? Hollis says it in a measurement. The open road should be what lets people reach the alley, and the bad ice what sends them there.

### Non-blocking (must still be answered before beats)

**Finding 9.** Ch 8 s1 ("If he wins, both teams go back to Kettle Lanes at twelve-fifty").
**Fails:** Q5 (for the teams).
**The architect must answer:** What do the defectors get if Ray loses? Without a stake on their side there is no reason for them to accept the bet. Also give the Legion a grievance of its own beyond the rate, or have it leave out of loyalty to the Highway Department, said in one line.

**Finding 10.** Ch 7 ("She can't take his money for a promise she knows he never heard").
**Fails:** Q5.
**The architect must answer:** Taking the check keeps the Memorial as Ray heard it, so it would make her promise true, not exploit it. Her stated reason doesn't follow. Put her real reason on the page: she won't share control, she wants "on my own", or she can't let him buy in while he believes something false about her.

**Finding 11.** Ch 6 ending ("a cashier's check from Granite Lakes"); Ch 2/4 (Ray's lender, unnamed).
**Fails:** Q1.
**The architect must answer:** Is Ray's lender Granite Lakes? If so, Judy's bank holds both the buyer-in-waiting's commitment and Ellen's refinance, and the outline must say why that information doesn't cross. If not, name the lender.

**Finding 15.** Ch 8 s5 ("the final stays the league's… No paid gate").
**Fails:** Q4 (other parties).
**The architect must answer:** The derby committee voted on an Open with a paid-gate final on Jan 16. Who agrees to the change, and does anyone who already bought a spectator ticket get a refund? One line.

**Finding 16 (moral weight, Q8).** Ch 3 condemnable choice 1; Ch 3 condemnable choice 3.
On Dec 17 Ellen has no reason to think the numbers will ever bite; the league loss is revealed only on Jan 1. A reader may see the NO READBACK strip as scruple rather than fault, and may cheer her for charging the stranded bowlers ("Sleeping is free; strings are not"). The choice a reader will condemn is Ch 6: "She says she said it. She does not say she watched him miss it." The outline lists Ch 3 as the condemnable act.
**The architect must answer:** Which moment is the condemnable one? Make sure the Ch 6 omission is written as a choice made in front of him, with the true sentence available and refused. Ray's Ch 7 line will be condemned as written. The book meets the map's requirement either way.

**Finding 17.** Ch 10 "Get right" ("the appraisal ordered in December; one offstage line in Ch 4 or 6 if the bible needs it").
**Fails:** Q2 (how Sunday's receipts become Monday's wire).
**The architect must answer:** Assign the pre-staging line to a specific chapter and speaker. Without it, a same-day underwriting, closing and wire after a Sunday count looks like the plot needs it.

**Finding 18 (withheld facts versus the brief).** `research/chosen-angle.md` must-have 6 ("Complete ending, no cliffhanger, no withheld secrets") and must-have intro ("resolve every thread that book 1 raises"); outline Subplots ("Kate… Left open… She never comes round on the page").
**Fails:** a brief conflict, not a causal question.
**The architect must answer:** The release is withheld from Ellen for three chapters (Feb 6 to Feb 28). The reader knows, so it is dramatic irony, but the D-side ask is made under a false belief Ray chose to leave her in. The architect should say once that this is allowed under the brief. Kate left unconvinced conflicts with "resolve every thread". Either the author approves the override in `book-brief.md`, or Kate's last text has to count as a resolution. Author decision 14 records the default but doesn't cite the conflict.

## Part C. Questions 6–9

### 6. Stakes curve: cost per act
| Act | Ellen's cost | Ray's cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (Ch 1–2) | Public humiliation (the lob), night one in the red, the pool, the clean exit (see Finding 3: possibly not gone), Kate's disapproval | His key, his free mechanic's role (now invoiced) |
| 2a (Ch 3–5) | Integrity (the strip), the Highway Department's goodwill, her innocence about the purchase, the version of the alley that keeps Ray | A fee to stay a ready buyer; he loves the woman he's waiting to buy out |
| 2b (Ch 6–7) | Ray's trust, two teams, the key, her 2 a.m. secret used against her | Ellen; he has become the man who said that line |
| 3 (Ch 8–10) | A public confession in the minutes, the paid-gate margin, ~$1,100 under after the refunds, "on my own" | The right of first refusal (see Finding 12), Linda's ball out of the locker, Linda's trophy opened to the town |

Acts 1 through 2b climb. **Act 3 costs Ellen less than Act 2b on the page.** Her biggest Act 3 loss is a number, and her own Ch 6 plan wins it back within 24 hours. Of what she gives up in Act 3, only the confession and "on my own" stay given up. Ch 4's listed cost to Ellen ("she has to answer it in front of him") ends with her "pleased", so in effect it costs her nothing, in a chapter the map requires to cost something. Not blocking. The architect should name one Act 3 loss Ellen does not get back, and one real loss for Ellen in Ch 4.

### 7. Subplots the protagonist doesn't resolve personally
Denise and the Memorial (Denise wins it). The two teams (Ray, at Pine Ridge). The derby through the storm (Hollis). Kate (left open). **Pass**, subject to Finding 9 (the side-match stakes) and Finding 18 (Kate versus the brief).

### 8. Moral weight
Ray's Ch 7 line ("You sounded fine on the radio that night too, I bet") is a choice a reasonable reader will condemn, and it has a cost on the page in Ch 8. Arlene's sale behind summary statements will be condemned. **Pass.** See Finding 16 on which of Ellen's moments carries the weight.

### 9. Reader knowledge: why Arlene sold to Ellen
The outline claims the league loss is guessable "by the end of Ch 4, one chapter before the reveal", and that "you'll charge them; he never would" stays unguessed until Ch 5. The outline's own plants don't support either claim:
- **League loss, guessable by the end of Ch 2, three chapters before the reveal.** Two Ch 2 lines give it away. Ray: "Arlene never charged the league for a lot of things," said with pride. Ellen invoices him "because his free labor is half of why Arlene's numbers looked better than they were". The Ch 4 "Now do a Tuesday" confirms it. **More than two chapters: flagged.**
- **Arlene's reason, guessable by the end of Ch 3, two chapters before.** Ch 1: "He'd have kept it nice." Ch 2: Ellen invoices what Ray gave free. Ch 3: she charges stranded bowlers, and the Highway Department captain says "nobody's ever charged him for a snowstorm". By the end of Ch 3, an attentive reader has been shown three times that Ellen charges and Ray and Arlene don't. That is exactly at the limit. The clue that gives it away is the Highway captain's line, set beside the Ch 2 invoice.

The architect must either cut or blunt one Ch 2 plant (the "numbers looked better" reason is the most explicit), or move the reveal earlier. The Ch 5 reveal also has to survive Finding 6: if Ellen has seen her own drawer, the information isn't a reveal to her even if it is one to the reader.

## Verdict

**BLOCKED: 11 blocking items** (Findings 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 12, 13, 14). Seven non-blocking items (Findings 9, 10, 11, 15, 16, 17, 18) also need answers before beats.
