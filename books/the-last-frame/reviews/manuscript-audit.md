# Manuscript audit: The Last Frame

**Draft read:** `books/the-last-frame/manuscript.md`, the ten finals assembled (31,227 words per the orchestrator), October 29, 2026.
**Order of reading:** the whole manuscript cold, then `docs/ai-fiction-tells.md` Part 7, then `outline.md` and `story-bible.md` to check intent against the page, then `chapters/chapter-10/reader-notes.md` last.
**Line numbers** refer to `manuscript.md`. I had no shell, so the frequency observations below come from the read, not from a count; `tools/audit.py` owns exact numbers.

Problems only. Each finding gives the chapter, the line, the question it fails, and what a fix needs (a line, a scene, or the outline).

## Findings, ordered by what they cost the reader

### Finding 1. The notary condition in Ch 10 isn't planted, contradicts itself, and costs nothing
**Where:** Ch 10 s4, l.2198: *"The paper you signed in February to let go of your contract with Ellen was never notarized, so you sign it again at this desk in front of me before four, or the wire doesn't go. I like you, Ray. The underwriter has never met you."*
**Questions failed:** who knew (2), how it got through (3), whether it was earned (9), stakes (6).

- **Nothing before it plants it.** No earlier page mentions a notary, an acknowledgment, an underwriter, or the bank reviewing the release. The release is introduced at l.1732 (*"one sheet on Arlene's lawyer's letterhead ... with Arlene's signature at the top, and his under hers, and a line left at the bottom"*) and signed at a bowling-alley counter at night (l.1748), with no witness.
- **The page doesn't say why Arlene's lawyer missed it.** He drew the paper (l.1682–1684) and had it for about three weeks (from Arlene's call around Feb 10 to Mar 1). Granite Lakes had it for thirteen days (*"There are thirteen days,"* l.1752). Neither caught it, and the page offers no reason. The closest ingredient is Arlene's instruction, *"Raymond will carry it to her himself, and he'll ask her"* (l.1684), which would explain why the paper never went back through the lawyer's office. The page never connects the two.
- **It indicts Ellen as well, and the page doesn't notice.** *"Ellen read any paper in her building"* (l.2082), and she *"read it once, top to bottom"* (l.1736). A woman who wrote a weather contingency into her Open terms signed a release of a real-estate contract with no notary block and didn't catch it.
- **As written, it doesn't hold together.** If the paper was "never notarized", all three signatures carry the same defect. Only Ray re-signs (l.2226). Ellen sits at the same desk and isn't asked to. Arlene stands on the sidewalk Ray walks past (l.2204–2214), and nobody asks for hers either.
- **The page never says why the bank cares about the paper at all.** A reader has to supply that a lender won't fund a building someone else holds a contract to buy.
- **It costs nobody anything.** Ray walks two blocks and signs. The van leaves on time. The cold reader's verdict, *"I could see the author's hand"*, is what the page earns.
- **The outline didn't intend it.** Outline Ch 10 s5 has Ellen tell Ray it funded, up the ladder, and Arlene count the wire confirmation with a licked thumb. The notary snag was added below the outline and never went past the domain-checker.

**What a fix needs (outline level, then a plant):** cut it, or rebuild it as a planted step that goes wrong and costs something.
- *If it stays,* plant the notarization in Ch 8 s3 (the lawyer on the phone says the signatures must be acknowledged, and Arlene says Ray will carry it). Name the reason the bank needs the release in one clause. Make the defect fall on a signature that hurts. Arlene's is the obvious one: she leaves at four, and Ray, who "doesn't ask twice", would have to ask her again. Account for all three signatures.
- *If it goes,* Ch 10 needs some other thing that is actually in doubt (see Finding 2).

### Finding 2. Act 3 costs less than Act 2, and the bed the book stakes is never at risk on the page
**Where:** Ch 8 s4 to Ch 10.
**Questions failed:** stakes curve (6), choice and cost (5).

- **The highest cost in the book is Ch 7** (~69%): the public confession, two teams gone, Ray's radio line, the key on the counter.
- **Act 3 is a run of repairs.** The Bench pours her coffee (l.1624), the release scene ends warm on *"Not yet"* (l.1762), there's supper (Ch 9 s1), and the D-side ask is read back *"correct"* (l.1871).
- **The storm takes sixteen hundred dollars in Ch 9, and that loss is undone eighteen lines of story later** by Kate's *"Then do it"* (l.2038). Kate pushes back once (*"I did the subtraction anyway ... It's harder than it sounds,"* l.2034).
- **The only cost the page states for the savings is one line:** *"What's left heats the camp through one bad winter"* (l.2232).
- **Arlene never learns her bed is at risk.** It is the stake the book names at l.1650 (*"I go on the bottom of that list at eighty-four, and I may not live to the top of it"*) and l.1943 (*"Arlene's room is on it."*). After the storm nobody tells her. Ellen, a woman who *"wrote it up herself before anyone asked"* (l.517), doesn't report a $1,715 shortfall to her creditor. Arlene appears again only to load a van and make a joke (l.2214).
- **The two condemned choices in Ch 8 are never paid for:** Ray asking for the release, and Ellen signing it knowing she was already under (Finding 3). The outline promised *"every dollar after Feb 16 is Arlene's bed as well as her alley"*. On the page, that is a sentence Ellen says, and nothing follows from it.
- **Ch 10's only open question,** the Memorial, is settled in summary (*"He bowled ordinary strings with it,"* l.2102).

**What a fix needs (scene):** one Act 3 scene where the cost reaches someone who didn't choose it, Arlene above all. It needs Arlene told, on the page, by Ellen, after the storm. The page has to show what that costs Ellen. Then Ch 10 needs one decision whose outcome the reader doesn't already know.

### Finding 3. Ch 8's figure puts Ellen under the bar in the best case, and the page talks as if she had a margin
**Where:** Ch 8 s4, l.1722: *"That puts me a hundred and fifteen dollars under thirty thousand, with every lane of the Open full. The Open is my whole margin, and the weather owns the Open. Page two refunds every entry in cash if weather stops play, so a hundred and fifteen under with every lane full means one storm takes all of it."*
**Questions failed:** whether the choice follows from what she knows (5), reader knowledge (9).

- **"Under with every lane full" means she is short with perfect weather.** "One storm takes all of it" reads as if there were something left to take.
- **The Ch 5 baseline doesn't match.** There, the unnamed Open *"closes it with nothing over"* (l.1019). Nothing on the page moves her from "nothing over" to "$115 under" except the walkout weeks. Denise refuses to name that cost (l.1455), and the teams came back.
- **The outline intended her to be $100 over here** (outline figures table, Feb 16), so that the storm, not the refunds, puts her under. On the page she signs Arlene's release two paragraphs later (l.1748) knowing she is already short, with her savings sworn off.
- That makes the signature reckless rather than risky. Ray hears her say she is short and *"offered her nothing"* (l.1724). The page doesn't let either of them say that out loud.

**What a fix needs (line):** either restore the outline's figure ("a hundred over, with every lane full; one storm takes the hundred and the rest"), or keep the $115 and give her one sentence that says she is signing a certain shortfall and how she means to cover it.

### Finding 4. Ray's $48,000, Ch 6–10: the safer offer isn't closed once Arlene's bed is on the table
**Where:** Ch 6 s4 (l.1314–1340), Ch 7 s1 (l.1363–1391), Ch 8 s4 (l.1724), Ch 9 s3 (l.1945–1951), Ch 10 s4 (l.2200).
**Questions failed:** safer options (4), whether the choice follows from knowledge (5).

- **Ch 7: closed.** Ellen refuses, *"If I take it I'll never know which of us carried it"* (l.1385), and her interior adds the unreported silence (l.1387). A reader accepts that.
- **Ch 6–7, a cost the page doesn't name.** Ray tells Danny, *"If she takes it, you can't buy it March first"* (l.1318–1320), so the check also removes Arlene's backstop. Nobody asks Arlene. Neither Ray nor Ellen, the planner, raises it at the hood.
- **Ch 8: not closed.** Ray now knows she is under (l.1722), knows Arlene's bed rides on it, and has the check in his jacket. The page gives his silence one reason, Kate's oath (l.1724), which binds Ellen's money, not his.
- **Ch 9: closed for Ray, but only by moving the rule.** *"I asked you once, on the hood of my truck. I don't ask twice"* (l.1947). In Ch 2 the vow was about the store (l.378: *"to put the store up once in his life and never twice"*), and in Ch 6 he says *"I asked the store once"* (l.1328). Applying it to Ellen, with Arlene's bed at stake, is a choice a reader could condemn, and nobody on the page condemns it.
- **Ch 9: not closed for Ellen.** She has just asked him to share the position (*"I want you on it with me,"* l.1857). She watches his hand go to the zipper over the check (l.1945). Then she says *"I can cover it myself"* (l.1951) with no interior reason for not asking. The book's question is whether she can share the position, so why she pays alone is the question a reader asks here.
- **Ch 10.** *"That goes back to the credit union"* (l.2200) answers where the money goes. Danny's question, *"buying in, or getting out of the way?"* (l.1330), is never answered.

**What a fix needs (two lines):**
- Ch 9, Ellen POV: one interior sentence giving her reason not to ask for the money now that she has asked for him. It has to be a reason that isn't Ch 7's, since the silence is confessed.
- Ch 8 or Ch 9: one beat where someone, Ellen or Denise or Arlene, names what Ray's "ask once" costs Arlene.

### Finding 5. Ch 9 storm day: the safer way to save the evening's sixteen hundred is never closed
**Where:** Ch 9 s3, l.1845 (*"The Open's evening at six: ten teams, and most of them drive home to Massachusetts tonight"*) and l.1901 (*"The Open's evening is cancelled. Evening entrants, please come to the counter for your entry, in cash."*)
**Question failed:** safer options (4).

- She moves the league's Memorial to Sunday at one, but refunds all ten evening teams as a block.
- *"Most"*, by the page's own word, aren't all of them. Sunday morning is open.
- The printed terms promise cash *if* weather stops play. Nothing on the page stops her offering a Sunday-morning slot or a credit first. A woman whose whole margin is this sixteen hundred, with Arlene's bed on it, would try.
- This refund is the reversal that drives the savings decision. If a reader thinks it was avoidable, the Act 3 crisis looks self-inflicted by accident rather than by choice.

**What a fix needs (line):** one sentence in which she offers Sunday morning and the evening teams decline (the Massachusetts plates, Monday work, Presidents' week ending), or a reason Sunday morning isn't hers to offer.

### Finding 6. Ch 4–5: the page no longer says what binds the owner to the league's vote
**Where:** Ch 4 s4, l.804–824 (*"Paragraph four says 'as the league's bylaws provide'"* ... *"There's a line in them says the September rate holds till the January meeting votes on it"* ... *"I did."*); Ch 5 s3, l.955 (*"shall hold at the rates of the September agreement until the January meeting has voted upon them"*) and l.991 (*"The September rate holds."*).
**Questions failed:** safer options (4), and the outline's own logic-gate answer F3.

- **What binds her.** A league's bylaws bind the league, not the building. The outline answered this with a season agreement Arlene signed every September, which Ellen inherited at closing. On the page that is reduced to the two words *"September agreement"* inside Verna's reading. Nothing says Ellen is a party to it.
- **What happens after the vote.** The page never says why the vote keeps the old rate for the whole season, or why she can't post the raise for the second half, when teams may leave without forfeit anyway (l.1399).
- A reader who runs a business will ask: *it's her building, why can't she raise her prices?*

**What a fix needs (line):** one clause in Ch 4, in Ellen's mouth: she inherited the September agreement at closing, it adopts the bylaws, and the meeting's vote holds the rate to season's end.

### Finding 7. Ch 10 doesn't let the reader judge the win: the numbers and the liquidity rule are gone
**Where:** Ch 10 s4, l.2228 (*"Sunday's snack bar closed part of the gap. This page closes the rest. Eighty-eight hundred, out of your savings and onto Arlene's note."*)
**Questions failed:** reader knowledge (9), safer options (4).

- **The figure the outline needed on the page is missing.** Outline item 5 has her say *"twenty-eight thousand nine hundred"* to Ray in Ch 10 so that *"the reader judges the win knowingly"*. The page never gives the winter's final figure or the gap.
- **The arithmetic is left to the reader.** Eighty-eight hundred is meaningful only to someone who remembers eight-to-one from Ch 7 (l.1363) and $1,715 from Ch 9 (l.1939).
- **The liquidity rule isn't restated.** It was planted 28,000 words earlier (*"with every dollar it makes still sitting in the account,"* l.232). The outline restates it through Judy in Ch 10 (F7). Without it a reader asks why she doesn't pay the gap from the operating account she just filled.

**What a fix needs (two lines):** Ellen says the winter's figure and the gap to Ray before the bank, and Judy gives the liquidity rule in one sentence at the desk.

### Finding 8. Pine Ridge: Ray puts Ellen's revenue on a bowling match without asking her
**Where:** Ch 8 s1, l.1528–1532: if Ray loses, *"Ray pays our lineage here to the end of the season, and his team bowls for you"* / *"—and Gagnon and Son bowls for Pine Ridge."*
**Questions failed:** safer options (4), moral weight (8).

- **The risk falls on Ellen.** If Ray loses, Ellen loses a third Tuesday team, Ray's own. That team prepaid its lineage "through Linda's night" (l.1288), and the page doesn't say what happens to that money. Ellen isn't told the terms until it's over. The page doesn't register this as a risk to her.
- **Gil's motive is thin.** Losing costs Gil two teams he has just gained, and the page gives him *"I like a match ... so I've had my night whoever bowls"* (l.1528).
- **Dale's acceptance holds.** *"Everything you put up, losing pays you"* / *"Then watch me bowl"* (l.1536–1538) answers why he agrees.

**What a fix needs (line):** one line where someone, Hollis or Denise, names what Ray put up of hers, and one line of real self-interest for Gil (bible: his reputation for fairness, or Denise).

### Finding 9. The Memorial's result lands without its setup
**Where:** Ch 10 s2, l.2102–2112: *"He bowled ordinary strings with it."* / *"It came down to lanes five and six, Lakeview Diner against Gagnon and Son"* / *"The secretary has Lakeview Diner by two pins."*
**Question failed:** reader knowledge (9); the page breaks bible plant "Denise anchors the Lakeview Diner (plant in Ch 1 or Ch 2)".

- The page never puts Denise on the Lakeview Diner team. Ch 3 mentions *"two women from the diner team"* (l.417) apart from Denise. The reader learns whose win it was only when Denise lifts the cup.
- The event the book was built toward is summarized in one sentence.

**What a fix needs (line plus scene):** plant Denise's team in Ch 1–3 in one line, and play the last box on the page.

### Finding 10. Ray gives Linda's name to the Open, which turns Ellen's condemned choice into a virtue, and nobody remarks on it
**Where:** Ch 10 s2, l.2094: *"From next year Linda's name goes on the Open, and anybody that pays the entry can win it, because Linda would've entered on day one."*
**Question failed:** moral weight (8).

- Ellen's primary wrong (Ch 5–6) was taking Linda's name for the Open for money (l.1041: *"She was taking Linda's name for the margin."*). In Ch 10 the book does the same thing on Ray's say-so and frames it as growth.
- Nobody says it: not Denise, who said *"Don't you touch Linda's night"* (l.1021), not Dale, not Ellen. A reader can take this as the book excusing her after the fact.

**What a fix needs (line):** one reaction, Denise's or Ellen's, that marks the difference: the name given freely by Linda's husband against the name taken for a margin.

### Finding 11. Why Ray waited two years, and what "by eight days" means
**Where:** Ch 1 l.145 (*"he lost this place to you by eight days"*); Ch 2 l.228 (*"Arlene had this place listed two years ... So she sold it to you over me."*); Ch 8 l.1634 (*"You'd have kept it a museum, Raymond. I wanted it to live."*).
**Question failed:** whether the choice follows from what the character knows (5).

- "By eight days" says timing. "Over me" and "a museum" say Arlene chose.
- The page never says why a man with $52,000 in savings and a store to borrow against let the alley sit on the market for two years. The bible has the answer: he wanted it for the quiet after supper. That answer is off the page.

**What a fix needs (line):** one line in Ch 2 or Ch 8 that settles what the eight days were and why he waited.

### Finding 12. Ch 3: she runs league against the road agent's warning, and the page gives no reason
**Where:** Ch 3 s1, l.399–403: *"I called you at ten past three and told you it wasn't stopping." / "You did," Ellen said.* ... *"She had run league on the three o'clock forecast."*
**Question failed:** whether the choice follows from what she knows (5).

- The cost is there (Dale's grudge, which pays off in Ch 7). The reason is not.

**What a fix needs (line):** one clause of reason in her interior at l.403.

### Finding 13. Dale, a witness the page plants and never uses
**Where:** Ch 7 s1, l.1371 (*"He could see everything at the hood and hear none of it."*); Ch 8 l.1536 (*"Everything you put up, losing pays you."*); Ch 9 l.1919 (*"took his wife's envelope out of Ellen's hand without a word"*).

**What a fix needs (line each):** in Ch 8, Dale names what he saw at the hood. In Ch 9, one look or line that makes the entry read as deliberate.

## Where the page and the intent part company

1. **The notary condition** isn't in the outline. The outline's HEA (Ellen tells Ray it funded, up the ladder; Arlene's car on Kettle Road; the wire confirmation counted with a licked thumb; Denise and the pool sheet) has been replaced by a bank scene with an unplanted obstacle. *(Finding 1)*
2. **The ledger.** Outline Feb 16: about $100 over. Page: *"a hundred and fifteen dollars under"* (l.1722). The storm is no longer what puts her under. *(Finding 3)*
3. **Ch 10 s3, the count**, where Ellen says *"twenty-eight thousand nine hundred"* and the $8,800 to Ray, is gone. So is Judy's restatement of the liquidity rule (outline F7). *(Finding 7)*
4. **The season agreement's paragraph four** (outline F3, a logic-gate answer) has become league bylaws Ray wrote. What binds the owner is lost. *(Finding 6)*
5. **Condemnable choice 4** (Ellen co-signs; *"every dollar after Feb 16 is Arlene's bed"*) is said and never paid. Arlene is never told and never at visible risk. *(Finding 2)*
6. **Denise's team** (bible: plant in Ch 1 or 2) is never planted. *(Finding 9)*
7. **Ray's fear in the bible** ("he wanted the alley so he'd have somewhere to go after supper") is the answer to why he waited two years, and it isn't on the page. *(Finding 11)*
8. **Kate's want in the bible** ("to be asked rather than told") never appears before *"call me first next time"*, so her yes in Ch 10 reads as easy rather than as a want satisfied.
9. **Gil's annual flyer** (bible: Denise takes it home every March) isn't on the page, so the Ch 10 hand-off reads as staged.
10. **The count of stranded people** is thirty-one in the outline and bible and thirty-three on the page (l.417, l.1891). The page is consistent with itself. Flagged for the continuity checker.

## The three items that most need answering before the book goes outside

1. **The notary condition (Finding 1).** Cut it, or plant it in Ch 8 and make it cost someone. As written, an outside reader sees the author's hand on the last afternoon. The defect covers three signatures and only one is fixed, and the lawyer, the bank and Ellen all miss it for no stated reason.
2. **Act 3's stakes (Findings 2 and 3).** Arlene's bed is the stake, and the page never puts it at visible risk. Arlene is never told, Ellen signs knowing she is already under (*"a hundred and fifteen dollars under ... with every lane of the Open full"*), and the shortfall is covered overnight. Act 3 costs less than Act 2, and Ch 10 ends on four soft scenes in a row.
3. **The safer options left open around the money (Findings 4, 5 and 7).** The page doesn't say why Ellen doesn't ask for Ray's check after asking him onto the position, why she doesn't offer the storm-day evening teams Sunday morning before refunding sixteen hundred dollars, or why she can't pay the gap from the operating account (the liquidity rule isn't restated). Ch 10 also never gives the winter's final figure, so the reader can't judge the win.
