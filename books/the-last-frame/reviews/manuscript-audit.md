## Findings (ordered by cost to the reader)

### Finding 1. Act 3's money risk is reported after the fact, and the climactic shot repeats the one from the chapter before
**Where:** Ch 09, scenes 3 and 5; Ch 08, Pine Ridge scene.
**Question failed:** 6 (stakes curve), with Part 7 "map the escalation".

The book's material low point is Ch 07–08 ("Eighteen hundred dollars short on Judy's first line... Twelve hundred and fifty short on her second." / "Short on both," Ellen said.). On storm day the reader never sees the margin she is putting at risk at the moment she puts it at risk:

> "At seven that morning the tape had cleared both of Judy's lines, with the evening and Sunday on it. Without them she would not know where she landed until she ran it tonight."

She refunds $1,140 ("That's eleven hundred and forty dollars walking down Kettle Road"). Two scenes later the danger is settled in one paragraph of tape: "Eight hundred and fifty over on one line and six hundred on the other. The evening she had handed back across the counter in envelopes would have covered either." By the book's own numbers the refund never put either line at risk. The reader learns there was no danger in the same sentence that tells them the danger is over.

The bowling climax has no stakes either. Before Ellen's tenth box: "Going into the third, Gagnon & Son trailed by more than one bowler could make up." Her shot is also the same shot Ray made one chapter earlier:
- Ch 08, Ray: "His first ball chopped... in front of them lay one fallen pin, crossways... The ball caught it square and the pin swung like a gate..."
- Ch 09, Ellen: "Her first ball chopped the left side off clean. Her second took two more and left one pin standing... with a fallen pin lying on the deck behind it... the pin went over backward into the fallen one."
- Ch 03 already has a version of it: "Her second ball went low and long, struck a fallen pin, and sent it skidding into the group on the right."

Act 3 does cost her more emotionally. She loses the thing she turned down $55,000 to protect ("Now she would never know how much of the twelve-fifty had come back on its own"), and her voice breaks on "floor." In money, though, the curve peaks at Ch 07–08 and then flattens into reported results.

**For the writer:** Before Ellen decides to refund, put the margin she is giving up on her tape in one line (for example "$1,140 against a cushion of $X"). Either make her tenth box count in the standings, or give it a different shape from Ray's Ch 08 tenth.

### Finding 2. Soft endings cluster in the last two chapters, with two pairs in a row
**Where:** Ch 09 scenes 5–6; Ch 10 scenes 4–5.
**Box failed:** Part 7, scene endings ("any two in a row → rewrite").

The count is under the cap: 12 soft beats in 57 scenes (21%). Five of them fall in the last seven scenes, though, and two pairs run back to back:
- Ch 09 s5: "She stayed on her feet. She took the 02/27 strip down... printed under REMARKS: WX. MOVED FROM 1800. LAKEVIEW DINER / D. LAVOIE." Then Ch 09 s6: "She sat down in the desk chair."
- Ch 10 s4: "Ray laughed... and he kept on, with her hand still in his." Then Ch 10 s5: "'Both words.'"

Two of the twelve soft endings are also the same image: Ray's truck waiting until her engine starts. Ch 01: "Across the lot, the pickup's lights came on... its taillights got smaller until the trees took them." Ch 07: "Behind her the truck's headlights came on and swung out onto the drive." Ch 02 has a third version: "He stood in the doorway until her engine turned over on the second try."

**For the writer:** End Ch 09 s5 on the tape or on an interruption rather than on the REMARKS closure. Keep "She sat down in the desk chair." Change the Ch 07 truck ending so it doesn't repeat Ch 01.

### Finding 4. The ledger discovery depends on a delay the page doesn't justify, and Arlene's line contradicts it
**Where:** Ch 03 last scene; Ch 05 ledger scene; Ch 08 Arlene scene.
**Questions failed:** 4 and 5.

On Dec 19 Ellen sees the problem: "The league nights were the thinnest columns on the tape, Tuesdays and Thursdays both, thinner than a slow Wednesday afternoon. She didn't know why." She then schedules the reconciliation four weeks out ("JAN / RECONCILE / ARLENE / LEAGUE"), while losing $700 a week with 13 weeks on the clock. This is a woman who "skipped nothing because it looked obvious." Ch 05 explains that she bought on summaries: "At closing Arlene's lawyer had sent three years of summary statements... Ellen had bought the building on those. The green books had stayed on their shelf." Then in Ch 08 Arlene says: "She asked me what the league paid before she asked me the price of the building." If she asked that before buying, the Ch 05 discovery is not a discovery.

There is also an unweighed moral choice here. Arlene sold a business with a known weekly loss, behind summary statements, against a 90-day balloon ("Arlene had known what the league cost her, every fall for twenty years."). The book presents this as wisdom. Some readers will count it against her.

**For the writer:** Either have Ellen pull the ledgers on Dec 19 and give the delay a reason (Judy's appraisal schedule, the green books being at Arlene's for packing), or cut Arlene's "asked me what the league paid" line, or change it to the prize fund.

### Finding 5. Safer options the page leaves open
**Question failed:** 4.
- **Ask Arlene for 30 days.** The text never says why Arlene can't extend. Her continuing-care entrance fee does not appear on the page (no hits for "entrance"). The nearest line is "Arlene moved into The Pines on the first of March" (Ch 08). After the boiler, any reader will ask this. **For the writer:** add one line from Arlene or Judy in Ch 03 or Ch 06 saying her unit fee is due at the door on March 1, so she can't extend.
- **The boiler out of cash.** "Twenty-four thousand five hundred... Paid by check Wednesday." A business owner with a $25,000 cash floor would ask about insurance, or about the installer's financing, before writing that check. Neither is mentioned. **For the writer:** one line closes both: insurance excludes wear and age, and new debt before the refinance would break Judy's underwriting.
- **Calling Ray's son for the rekey** (minor). The only cue is "Ellen looked at the stitching on his jacket and dialed." The reader can't tell whether she meant to call Gagnon & Son or had no choice. **For the writer:** add a half line saying it's the only locksmith in the book.

### Finding 6. The recorded release stays secret for 11 days in a town that hears everything
**Where:** Ch 08 lawyer scene; Ch 09 last scene.
**Questions failed:** 2 and 3.

The lawyer says: "I'll record it this afternoon. After that it's public. Anybody at the registry can pull it." The same lawyer handles Ellen's March 1 closing ("Closing's at one, at Leclerc's"). A same-day closing implies a title update that would show the release. Arlene told Ray, "Everybody on Main Street knew your bank was coming. I knew before you did." Yet on Feb 20 Ellen still believes "he still held the first refusal," and on Feb 27 she reads the stamp cold. The scene ends on "When will you tell her?" with no answer, so the channel is never closed.

**For the writer:** Add one sentence, either Ray asking Leclerc to hold her copy and keep it from the closing file until the 28th, or Judy's title rundown dated before Feb 16.

### Finding 7. Reader knowledge: the substitution is visible six chapters early
**Question failed:** 9.

The plant in Ch 03 points straight at the payoff: "'Fill it out. So you're legal if they're ever short.' / 'Short of what?' / 'Bowlers, in case anyone's counting.'" Ch 08 adds a second signal: "'Then Gagnon and Son's a man short in the final,' Denise said. 'And Ray—' / 'Ray'll find somebody,' Hollis said." An attentive reader knows by Ch 03, and is sure by Ch 08, that Ellen will bowl fourth for Ray's team in Ch 09. That is six chapters before the reveal. (The conditional promise in Ch 03 and the release in Ch 08 are dramatic irony by design, so they are not counted here.)

**For the writer:** Cut "Short of what?" and the answer to it. Let Denise slide the form across with no reason given.

### Finding 8. Questions the book asks and never answers
- **Arlene's choice.** Ch 10: "Ellen had carried the question since December. / 'Arlene. Why me?' / ... 'Raymond asked me that already. Ask him.'" Ellen never asks him, and the pit scene doesn't touch it. The outline has Arlene answer in one line.
- **Kate.** Ch 09: "'Mom. Scale of one to ten. How much of today is one person?' ... 'Ask me at six,' Ellen said." The final moves to 2:30, Kate leaves at 3:58, and the question is never answered.
- **Ch 04 party calls.** Ray proposes rebooking free and Ellen says "No." Then "Ellen picked up the receiver and put her finger in the wheel." The reader never learns what she offered eleven parties worth $2,640.
- **Ch 09 trophy request.** "From next year the Linda Gagnon Memorial goes to the Open." Ch 07 had already voted the Memorial in as the Open's final round, so it isn't clear what changes. The intended meaning (public entrants can win it) is not on the page.

### Finding 9. Running bits past their limit
- **The marble cake gag** lands four times: Ch 01 "The cake had been marble.", Ch 04 "'What kind of cake?' / 'Marble.'", Ch 05 "'What kind?' / 'Marble.'", and Ch 05 "the afternoon of the marble cake". Bible rule 17 retires a gag after three.
- **"Certified" as a callback**: Ch 06 "Is that wrench certified?" and Ch 10 "Is that transformer certified?"
- **"Which fella?"**: three times (Ch 04 twice, Ch 06 once).

### Finding 10. In three scenes the two leads sound alike with tags removed
See checklist item 6: the Ch 04 office argument, the Ch 06 basement and the Ch 10 declarations. Ray's "I love you" speech is the longest he has in the book, built as an Ellen-style itemized list ("Tuesdays I want out front... I'm sixty-one. I eat supper at a quarter past five... I love you."). ### 8. Timeline shape — linear with ellipses; ACCEPTABLE
