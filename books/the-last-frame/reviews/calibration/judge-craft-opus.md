# Judge: Craft persona (Opus) — anchor-A3-ch01 (A) vs anchor-A1-ch01 (B)

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A puts the two of them in a doorway arguing within the first page and lets Ellen want something she can't file, while B spends its first two pages showing me how neatly a woman can label a toggle switch, and only one of those is a reason to turn the page.

3. **Where A lost me:** It didn't, quite. The nearest thing to a drift was the contingency scene at the snack bar, around 78%: *"One contingency," she said, "for your planning. If I were to bring you half on March first, and the balance by the end of June, in writing, at the note rate."* — that's the only place Ellen talks like a memo instead of a person, and it's over in four lines. Everything else has a pulse: the ball held between two hands, "Nice arc. Wrong lane," the numbered list falling apart in her head when he looks at her mouth. The sentences run long and loose and the rhythm changes when Ray talks, which is the thing I'm listening for.

4. **Where B lost me:** At 2%: *"04 / 1830 / LG / 4 / —. By lane 7 she had the spacing right. By lane 10 she wanted to redo the first three."* That's where the thumb started moving, and the pegboard and the index cards kept it moving. It picked back up for the Danny phone call and the thrown ball (both genuinely good), then lost me again at about 70%, hard: *"Lane seven. Second string, eight-eleven. The table stopped twelve inches above the deck on its way down. The sweep is down. The motor ran four seconds after the table stopped, then quit."* — an incident report read aloud to a man who answers in three words. Ray and Ellen are in the same building for the whole chapter and have maybe six exchanges, all clipped, all business. The precision is impressive and I was bored by it.

5. **Confidence: high.**
