# Judge report: Skimmer (Sonnet)

A = anchor-A3-ch01.md
B = anchor-A1-ch01.md

1. **Winner:** A

2. **Why:** A puts the two of them in the same doorway arguing inside the first page and keeps them in the same room (the shared grip on the ball, "Nice arc. Wrong lane."), while B spends its first stretch on tape, a pegboard and index cards and keeps the man at arm's length for most of the chapter.

3. **Where A lost me:** The room-counting paragraph at about 30%: "First, the ones moving: the Highway Department crew straight off their trucks with their hoods still up; Denise Lavoie, in a red Kettle Lanes polo washed pink at the shoulders..." It is a long roll call and my thumb sped up. It sped up again briefly at the pinsetter on lane 7 (about 65%: "the machine at the end of lane seven ground, long and low, and then knocked"), which is a woman and a machine, but Ray comes in to listen within a few lines, so I stayed.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 2%: "She wrote new ones in block capitals, five columns, the way she had once written flight strips: lane, time, league or open, count, remarks. 04 / 1830 / LG / 4 / —." That reads as a ledger, and the pegboard and index cards right after it kept me skimming. I never really came back. The lane 7 breakdown at about 62% ("Second string, eight-eleven. The table stopped twelve inches above the deck... I touched nothing.") and the adding-machine close at about 80% are more of the same, with the couple doing business instead of looking at each other.

5. **Confidence:** High. A is clunky in a few sentences, but a tired reader gets the pull from the doorway scene and the ball on, and B never gets that.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same people, same running jokes (tower altitude, the refunded lineage, the pool sheet, Verna ruling on interference), and the same dry, wry voice, so these read as two passes at one chapter.
