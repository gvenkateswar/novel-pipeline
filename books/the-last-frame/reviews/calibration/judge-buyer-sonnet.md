# Judge report: Buyer persona (Sonnet)

A = anchor-A1-ch01.md
B = anchor-A3-ch01.md

1. **Winner:** B

2. **Why:** B puts the two of them in a room with a spark in the first few pages (the key, the "Ms.", his hand and hers on the same ball, "Nice arc. Wrong lane."), and I wanted to see what she'd do next, while A kept me watching a woman run a building.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 4%, "She wrote new ones in block capitals, five columns, the way she had once written flight strips: lane, time, league or open, count, remarks. 04 / 1830 / LG / 4 / —." That's a form, and my thumb went faster. It slowed down again for Denise, but I lost it a second time at about 72%, in the refund business: "Lineage was what each bowler paid the house for a night's three strings, seven-fifty a head," followed by the piles of money and the minutes of the meeting. That is a woman doing accounting. Ray gets almost no warmth in the whole chapter. He is a closed fist and a pickup truck, and nobody has felt anything yet.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 50%, "First, the ball, which she now had. Third, she would not look at his mouth, and she had already looked, and somewhere she had dropped the second item, the ruling, Verna's, final..." I had to reread it to see who was holding what, and the numbered-list joke stopped being cute. It wobbles again, less badly, in the long counting-the-room paragraph at about 20%, and in the March-first contract talk on the doorstep. I never wanted to quit, though.

5. **Confidence:** Medium. A is cleaner and funnier line to line, and Denise is the best voice in either chapter, so it's closer than I'd like. B has the romance I came for, so I pick it.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same Ellen, Ray, Denise, Hollis and Verna, same altitude gag and the same 1979 precedent line. B is just more florid and warmer.
