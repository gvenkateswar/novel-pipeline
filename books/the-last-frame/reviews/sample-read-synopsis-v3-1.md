# Sample read: synopsis v3, read 1

**Draft read:** `books/the-last-frame/reviews/sample-synopsis-v3.md` ("Built from outline v3.1 (Oct 30, 2026)", Chapters 1–5). Nothing else was read except `research/chosen-angle.md`, and only to build the persona.

**Persona:** Donna, 61, retired school nurse outside Akron, widowed six years, two grown sons. She reads on her phone in Kindle Unlimited, mostly in bed and in the car-line habit she never lost. Last month she read two later-in-life clean romances, one set at a beach cottage and one at a bakery, and DNF'd a third at the second grief flashback. She's tired of sweet, of everyone being nice, of heroines whose husbands left them in chapter one, and of books that make her do math. The last book she finished she paid $4.99 for, and she thought it was about a dollar too much. She has nine samples sitting on her phone.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

Chapter 5, about 75% through the page: **"Borrow it? A loan isn't a winter; the bank counts only what the building earns."**

They'd just kissed at New Year's and the very next thing was the counter woman and a ledger, then a banker who's out ice fishing, then her savings, then half the gap. I don't want to sit through a loan meeting in a romance, and I'd already done one in Chapter 2.

## 2. Buy

**No.** The line that decided it is the one above. By Chapter 5 the story keeps going back to the bank, and I still don't know what Ellen wants the alley *for*.

## 4. Which chapter I'd most want to read, and which I'd skim

- **Most want to read:** Chapter 3, the snowed-in night.
- **Would skim:** Chapter 5, from the counting with the counter woman down to the banker on the ice.

## 5. Do I care about these two yet?

**No.** I care a little about Ray because of his wife's name on the wall. Ellen is someone I've been told about: she sold her house for a bowling alley and I never find out why, so when she might lose it, all I feel is the money.

## 6. Did anyone make me laugh?

A smile, not a laugh. "Not a dollar under. I'm not buying your mistake cheap." And the locksmith already standing in her alley with a bowling shoe in his hand. "Nice arc. Wrong lane." was cute.

## 7. Did anyone make me want?

Once: "she holds the flashlight close enough that he can feel her breath on his wrist." Also, less so, "to her annoyance she'd like him to stay there and keep arguing."

## 8. The three most boring passages

1. Ch 5 (~75%): "Two days later Ellen counts December with the counter woman. If the rest of the winter goes the way winters go here, she'll come up short by about the whole of January. Borrow it? A loan isn't a winter; the bank counts only what the building earns. She phones her banker, who is out ice fishing. Ellen's own savings can lower the bar, but slowly; all of them wouldn't cover half the gap..." This is where I quit.
2. Ch 2 (~30%): "Her bank will lend her Arlene's money if this winter brings in thirty thousand dollars, and nobody has done that in this building for decades." Plus the buy-back offer "for what she paid, even if it means putting up the store his father nearly lost." I skimmed the money.
3. Ch 4 (~60%): "She tells him to send her a quote. From now on, everything he fixes in her building, he bills her for." It ends a chapter on an invoice.

## 9. Is their problem one that a single honest conversation would fix?

**No.** The deal with Arlene and the January shortfall are real money, and talking doesn't change them. But in Chapter 4 Ray says he wants to stop working and hand the store to his son. If that's true, I don't see why he wants her building at all, and one conversation about that might end most of the fight between them.

## 10. Does the stake feel big enough to carry a book?

**No.** In my words, the stake is that if she doesn't bring in thirty thousand this winter she loses the alley and her house money, he gets the building, and she's left in a rented camp. It's big on paper, but it's a number. The page never tells me what she came for, so "none of what she came for" doesn't hurt.

## 11. Does the hero want anything besides saying no?

**Yes, barely.** He wants to hand the hardware store to Danny properly and stop opening it at six every morning. I only find out because a radio sticks on at a party, and nothing he does on the page comes from that want. And he wants his wife's championship left alone, which is really just another no.

## 12. What I expect the ending to be

The open tournament happens in February on his wife's day, with her cup as the prize, and it's what gets Ellen over thirty thousand. Ray gives up the building or tears up his deal with Arlene, then retires from the store and ends up "behind hers" at the alley. The neon sign gets lit. They end up together, and someone in town wins the March first square, or nobody does.

## 13. What I can't predict

What "Then I'll make my no cost me what yours costs you" means. My guess is he puts his own money or the store on the line, or he finally buys the March first square, but I honestly don't know. I also can't tell how she covers January without a loan or her savings.

## 14. What I think is going on

Ray doesn't really want the alley. He wants it to survive and his wife's day kept, and the deal with Arlene is his way of keeping it from going to a stranger. The Chapter 1 betting sheet is set up to pay off: he'll end up holding March first, or deliberately not holding it. The "unless the numbers say otherwise" line is the fight the middle of the book is built around, and the open tournament is how the ending pays it off. The secret, if there is one, is that Ray decided to step back from the store well before Ellen showed up, and the alley is where he planned to go.
