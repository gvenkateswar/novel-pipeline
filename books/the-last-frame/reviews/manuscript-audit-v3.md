**File read:** `books/the-last-frame/manuscript.md`. This is v3, Oct 30: Chapters 1–10 plus "Epilogue: Ice-Out", 2,432 lines. The orchestrator's count is 32,495 words; I did not recount it. I read the manuscript first and in full. After that I read `outline.md` (v3.2 plus the v3.2a gate note), `reviews/outline-audit-v3-2.md`, `docs/ai-fiction-tells.md` Part 7 and `docs/reader-calibration.md`. I did not read the bible. I did not open `reviews/outline-audit-v3-1.md`, because v3-2 rules on every v3.1 item.

**Verdict: 4 must-fix, 15 should-fix.** Two must-fixes are unanswered safer-option or channel questions (Q3/Q4). One is a fact that the hero's POV hides from the reader and that contradicts an earlier page. One is a money line that misstates the Act 3 price. The central rupture passes the one-talk test.

Findings come first, ranked by what they cost a reader. The outside reviewers' questions follow, each answered from the page. The Part 7 checklist comes after that, and the three fixes come last.

## MUST-FIX

### M1. Storm day: the evening teams are in the building, the lanes are idle, and nobody bowls (Ch 09 s3–s4)
**The page:**
- s3: *"Every evening team but Dale's was already in the building. They had come up for the derby and come in off the cancelled ice with the families."*
- Ellen's briefing: *"Morning squads off the lanes by half past twelve, families on the settees, fifteen teams for the evening."*
- s4, at 1:40, Dale: *"I want everybody off the roads by four."*
- She refunds them team by team: *"At four o'clock the first evening squad would have thrown its first ball. Nothing threw. All ten lanes were lit for nobody."*
- The cost: *"Fourteen hundred dollars under, with the fries counted."*

**Question failed:** 4, why the safer option fails.
- From 12:30 to 3:45 there are ten free lanes and fifteen paid teams sitting on the settees. Moving one evening squad into that window keeps about half the refund money. Moving both may keep all of it.
- Ellen plans to the minute, and her whole arc is about not trusting a calm forecast. She would see this at 4:15 a.m., when Dale gives her the hour.
- The page never raises it. The 1,400 shortfall is what sends her to Kate's money, so all of Act 3 rests on a choice any bowler in the audience will question.

**A fresh reader asks:** "They were all there by noon. Why didn't they just bowl early?"

**What closes it (pick one, in one or two lines):**
- A rule from Verna's binder: an Open squad bowls at its posted hour or forfeits, so the scores count for the cup.
- The families' free kids' bowling holds the lanes from 12:30, by the derby's contract that Ellen signed.
- She does run one squad early and is still short. Then restate statement 3 with a new figure.

### M2. Ray's credit-union loan: withheld in his own POV, contradicted by Ch 06, then confessed and dropped in one line (Ch 08 s4)
**The page:**
- Ch 02 s4: *"Danny's signature was on the last line. Ray had never asked him for it. Danny had signed it anyway, put it in the drawer under the register."* The form needs *"one call before five"*, and Ray doesn't make the call.
- Ch 04 s3, Ray's POV: *"He could tear up the backup contract tonight. But the paper was what let Arlene hold her note."* The loan is not mentioned.
- Ch 05 s4, Ellen to Ray: *"And yours is what pays Arlene if I miss."* Ray nods.
- Ch 06 s2: *"Danny opened the drawer under the register, took out the credit-union form from December and laid it beside the envelope."* Danny: *"I signed that so you could buy it on her yes. You're standing in her paper waiting on her no, and I won't put my name to that."*
- Ch 06 s3, Ray to Ellen: *"So that when I tell you no, it's only a no, and nobody in this town can say I'm waiting on your building."*
- Ch 08 s4: *"The credit union's held me a loan since December, on the form Danny signed. I sent it through the Thursday after you put that key on my counter, to buy this place at appraisal if you missed. I never told you that. I'll phone them tomorrow and let it go."*

**Questions failed:** 2 (how did the form travel?), 9 (a fact hidden from the reader in the POV character's own head), and 10 (Ch 06 s3 is not "the whole truth").
- The form is in Danny's drawer on Jan 9, but by Ch 08 it went to the credit union on Dec 10. Both can't be true.
- Danny says he won't put his name behind "waiting on her no". Per Ch 08, his name has been doing exactly that for a month without his knowledge. Nobody on the page reacts, Danny included.
- Ray asks Ellen to free him of "waiting on your building" while he holds an undisclosed loan to buy it.
- The loan breaks the outline's own rule: *"No true sentence after Ch 04 s1 hides a fact."*
- It is the "secret revealed" device, which readers punish (`docs/reader-calibration.md` rule 1). It surfaces at the repair and is settled in a phone call we never see.
- It also reopens a safer option the page had closed. From Dec 10 a loan existed for Ray to buy the building, so "sell to Ray in January" was live and was never discussed.

**A fresh reader asks:** "Wait, the form was in Danny's drawer. And Danny never knew?"

**What closes it (pick one):**
- Cut the reveal. Ray's Ch 08 cost becomes handing Danny the December form, unsent, to tear up, plus the vow.
- Or put the loan on the page in Ch 06 s2 or s3, where Danny and Ellen can react, and give Danny a line in Ch 06 or Ch 10.

### M3. The ending is priced by 78%, and the vote's doubt contradicts Judy's own rule (Ch 08 end; Ch 09 end; Ch 10 s4)
**The page:**
- Ch 08, closing stair, Ray's POV: *"This morning he had ordered the K from Laconia at his own quote, billed to himself the first of March."* The key is held out: *"Not yet."* So at about 78% the reader holds both closing images (the K lit, the key back) and the date they happen.
- Ch 09 s3: *"Today and after, whatever Monday says."*
- Ch 09 s6, last line: *"And I'm going to want something for it."* Nobody names a price for a no, so Kate's yes is given away one chapter early.
- Ch 06 s4, Judy: *"For a winter that clears, it is"* (a formality).
- Ch 10 s4, Judy: *"That puts you at the line, with nothing to spare."* At the line is clearing. By Judy's Ch 06 rule the vote is now a formality, yet the page spends eighty minutes on doubt.
- Then the committee asks *"whether next winter's Open has a date"*, which tells the reader they are planning on her.
- *"Approved"* comes by phone. The reason is hearsay from a nephew planted the chapter before: *"I told them what my brother's boy told me at supper last night."*

**Questions failed:** 9 (the outcome can be guessed more than two chapters before the reveal), and Part 7's "ending priced early". This matches the cold reader's 85% note.

**A fresh reader asks:** "If she's at the line, why is there a vote? And didn't he already buy the K?"

**What closes it:**
- Move the K order to after "Approved", or keep it out of the reader's sight until the ladder.
- Give Judy one line in Ch 10 s1 or s4 saying that a line bought down by a paydown is not a winter that clears. That is exactly what the committee has to rule on.

### M4. "Fourteen hundred dollars": Ellen understates Kate's price in the scene built as her first honest ask (Ch 09 s6)
**The page:**
- Ch 05 s4: *"my own money lowers the line, slowly, and all of it wouldn't buy back half of January."*
- Ch 09 s6: *"If you say yes, it costs more than half of the rest."* Kate: *"How much is more than half?"* Ellen: *"Fourteen hundred dollars, and whatever Judy's committee puts on top of it Monday morning."*
- Ch 10 s1, Judy on speaker: *"It takes a good many of your dollars to buy down one of the winter's."*
- Ch 10 s1, Kate: *"I did the subtraction. Without the dollars."*

**Question failed:** 5. Read literally, Ellen tells her daughter the cost is $1,400. She has known the real cost since before Christmas, and it is a multiple of that. Two paragraphs earlier Kate said *"You're not doing the voice."* Either Ellen is lying in the one scene meant to show her not hiding, or the line answers a different question (the gap, not the savings) and the reader can't tell. Kate's "without the dollars" suggests the second. The page doesn't make it readable.

**A fresh reader asks:** "Is it fourteen hundred or more than half her savings? Which did Kate agree to?"

**What closes it:** Ellen answers Kate's actual question. Example: "Seven thousand, about, to cover fourteen hundred. Judy's arithmetic, not mine."

## SHOULD-FIX

### S1. The Open's size never closes, so statement 2 is never confirmed (Ch 07, 08, 09)
- Ch 07: *"ten lanes to fill twice over"* (20 slots).
- Ch 08 s1: *"Then you're the fourteenth team."*
- Ch 08 s4: *"With every lane of the Open full, I clear by six hundred dollars."*
- Ch 09 s2: *"Four squads, thirty teams."* Four squads on ten lanes is forty slots.

The reader can't tell whether the Open filled, so can't tell whether she stood at +600 on the morning of the storm, so can't check −1,400. **Fix:** one clause on Friday night saying every lane is full, and make the capacity agree between Ch 07 and Ch 09.

### S2. Revenue from Ray or the town is never closed (Ch 05 s4)
The seasoning rule closes deposits: *"Yours would be new money."* It doesn't close revenue. The winter's receipts count, and Ray books and pays for his staff party on her lanes every year (Ch 04 s3). A businessman, or a town that has turned toward her, can buy lane time. **Fix:** one line, from Ellen or Judy, saying the committee strips out bookings by an interested party, or that Ellen refuses to let the backup buyer buy her winter.

### S3. Ellen never asks Judy what the committee will take (Jan 16 to Feb 27)
After the ice (*"I read that as a formality"* / *"For a winter that clears, it is"*) Ellen knows the vote has discretion. Six weeks pass and she doesn't phone Judy to ask what would carry it: a paydown, a guarantor, next year's bookings. The page answers it only after the fact (*"They don't vote on a winter till it's over,"* Ch 10). **Fix:** one line in Ch 08 or Ch 09 where she asks and gets the Ch 10 answer early.

### S4. Scene endings: 31 of 44 end on a line of dialogue built to land, and two soft endings come in a row (Ch 10 s4 → s5)
Classified types vary on paper, but the delivery is one move: the button line. The longest runs:
- Ch 04 s4 through Ch 05 s4 (five in a row)
- Ch 09 s4 through Ch 10 s3 (six in a row)

Soft endings are 5 of 44, under the cap. But Ch 10 s4 (*"sat in it, and Ray watched her do it"*) and Ch 10 s5 (*"Both words."*) are consecutive, and the epilogue's last scene is a third soft ending in the final four. Full table in the checklist. **Fix:**
- End three of the six in the Ch 09–10 run on an action or a cut mid-line.
- End Ch 10 s4 on what Arlene does on the bench, not on Ellen sitting.

### S6. Side characters are told apart by their tics, not their wants

### S7. The six days after the break are Ellen-less (Ch 07 s3 → Ch 08 s2)
- The break is Tue Feb 2. The accountability scene is Mon Feb 8, the verification Tue Feb 9, the condition Mon Feb 15.

**Fix:** one Ellen beat seen by Ray in Ch 08 s1, for example something she does to the clipboard or the toggles that he sees from the pit. Also give one clause for why Monday (the building is shut).

**Fix:** let one Act 3 cost land on the page in a scene. Either Ray makes the credit-union call (if M2 keeps the loan), or he hands the December form to Danny.

### S10. Condemnable choice 6 isn't condemnable as written (Ch 10 s1)
Ellen calls Kate before wiring. Kate: *"You called me first. It's yours, Mom. Send it."* The risk now has the owner's consent. The vote is yes, so the stated cost (*"If they say no, none of it comes back"*) is never collected. A reasonable reader approves of paying Arlene first. This is not a gap in moral weight, because choices 2, 3 and 4 carry it. It is a gap between the outline's list and the page. **Fix:** drop it from the condemnable list, or keep it and cost it. For example, Kate's April line in the epilogue: "You sent it before they voted."

### S11. Ch 01 promises a rivalry that is gone by the end of Ch 02, and the book's spine is not in Ch 01
- **Ch 01 promises a fight over the building:** *"I'm not buying your mistake cheap"*, *"Not my alley"*, and Arlene's *"He'd have kept it nice."*

- **Paid but not promised:** the mother–daughter story carries the epilogue's main scene, and the bank's committee becomes the climax.
- **Promised and never paid:**

  - Ray moving her rack labels (*"He peeled her label off the wood, moved it two inches to the steel upright"*). Neither of them ever takes it up.

**Fix:**
- Plant Linda's Memorial, by name and date, in Ch 01. Hollis or Verna at the lane draw can do it.
- Say in one line in the epilogue where the December pool money went.

### S12. Ch 06 s2: three Danny paragraphs in a row read like a dropped Ray line
*"It's said Son on that sign since before I was born. I'd like it to mean me."* / *"Danny put the gun in his back pocket. 'You're allowed to want something that isn't on a shelf.'"* / *"Danny opened the drawer..."* With the tags stripped, the middle line could be either man's. **Fix:** give Ray a reply between the first two.

### S13. Ray's money is never re-closed after the release frees it (Ch 09 s4)
Ch 05 closes Ray's savings with two reasons: seasoning, and *"yours is what pays Arlene if I miss."* After Feb 15 the second reason is gone, and after M2 it was never true. In Ch 09 Ray offers his name, not his cash, and the page doesn't say why. **Fix:** one clause, for example: "Cash can't count; I know that much."

### S14. Scene openings recap what just happened (CLAUDE.md rule)
- Ch 09 s6: *"Ray had driven her up Kettle Road behind the plow's second pass..."*
- Ch 10 s1: *"He had fetched her from the camp before light... Danny had opened the store without him every morning since the morning after the release, when he signed for it."* Danny's signing of the store, the resolution of Ray's want, is told off the page here.
- Ch 03 s4: *"Hollis had said seven when he phoned at a quarter past six..."*

**Fix:** open on the action, and move Danny's signing to a line in which Danny does it.

### S15. Smaller items, each answerable in a clause
- **Ch 10 s2:** the Highway crew bowls *"lane ten"* in Linda's final on *"the refunded envelope"*. They were evening qualifiers who never bowled. Why are they in the final?
- **Ch 09:** *"for the first time since the winter of 'eighty-four Linda's final had not been bowled on it."* 1984 is the first open (Verna, Ch 07). The Memorial is named for a woman who died six winters ago. Pass this to the continuity check.
- **Ch 03:** "the captain" and Dale are not tied together until Ray's *"She's right, Dale"*. The reader may count two men.
- **Ch 08 s1:** how Gil learned that Linda's name is now on the Open is not stated. It can be inferred from Ellen phoning around.
