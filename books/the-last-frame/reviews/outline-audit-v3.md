## BLOCKING (logic gate)

### Finding B1. The paydown: who knows the mechanism, from when, and how it reached Ellen
**Where:** Ch 05 s3, *"Her own money? 'Not enough to matter. And I swore to Kate.'"*; Ch 05 s4, *"Money isn't a winter. Nobody can hand me one."*; Ch 09 s4, *"She tells him what she means to do"*; Ch 09 s6 (asks Kate for "most of what she has"); Ch 10 s1, *"Ellen tells her she'll bring new money to the closing. Judy restates the rule: ... new money means she lends that much less, so the committee needs that much less."*
**Questions failed:** 1 (who knew), 2 (how it travelled), 5, and the outline's own "nothing is withheld between the leads after Ch 04 s1."

- On the page, only half of the rule exists. Ch 02 has *"with every dollar it made still sitting in the account."* Nothing on Ch 01–03 or anywhere before Ch 10 s1 says that new money lowers the loan, and with it the bar. The outline says Judy "restates" it, but the second half has never been stated.
- Ellen still acts on that second half from Jan 2. "Not enough to matter" only makes sense if her money could matter in some amount, and on Feb 27 she asks Kate for a specific share of it.
- So on Jan 2 her answer to Ray, "Money isn't a winter," is a true sentence that hides a fact she holds: outside money can lower what the winter has to make. That is World rule 13's device ("mislead with a true sentence"), the same one v3 retires in Ch 04.
- A reader hears it as a contradiction. In Ch 05 money can't be a winter. In Ch 10 her money can.
- On the outline's timeline, a woman who wrote a weather page into her Open terms first raises a paydown with her banker on Sunday morning, about 26 hours before closing, and only after asking her daughter for the money.

**The architect must answer:** when Ellen learns the paydown works, from whom (Judy, on the page or by phone), and in which chapter Ray hears it from her. The fix that costs least: in Ch 05 s4, Ellen tells Ray both halves in two true sentences. Money can lower the bar, but only slowly, and more than she has. What he has put by is Arlene's floor. Ch 05's close then holds.

### Finding B2. Ray's freed savings, Feb 15 to Mar 1: the safer option is reopened and never closed in time
**Where:** "Ray's money" section: *"Ch 08: the release frees his savings, and the question reopens in the reader's head. Nobody raises it until a paydown exists."* Ch 09 s4: *"He doesn't offer."* Ch 10 s3: *"He doesn't offer."* Ch 10 s1: *"Ray: 'I've got—' Judy: 'It has to be hers, and it has to have sat in her name since before Christmas.'"*
**Questions failed:** 4 (safer option), 5, 10 ("adults ask").

- Ch 05 closes Ray's money with two facts. The second, *"what you have put by is what pays Arlene if I miss"*, dies on Feb 15 when he signs the release. The first is undercut by B1.
- From Feb 15 Ray's savings are free. In Ch 09 s3 he is on the position "today and after." In Ch 09 s4 he hears "Fourteen hundred under" and says nothing. He offered in Ch 05, and he starts to offer again on Sunday morning. The outline gives no reason for his silence on Saturday night.
- Ellen has just asked him onto the position and is about to break an oath to her daughter. She doesn't ask him either, not even to hear him refused.
- The only thing that closes it is Judy's seasoning rule, which first appears in Ch 10 s1. That is the shape of v2's notary snag (v2 F1): an unplanted bank rule that arrives on the last day and settles the question. Here it settles a safer option rather than making a crisis, and a reader will still see the author's hand.

**The architect must answer:** why Ray doesn't offer in Ch 09 s4. The recommended fix: plant the seasoning rule before Feb 27 (Ellen got it from Judy when she asked about the paydown, per B1). In Ch 09 s4 Ray offers, and in the empty building she closes it with Judy's sentence. Cut "I've got—" from Ch 10 s1.

### Finding B3. Storm day: the forecast, and why the evening squad wasn't moved before 4:10 a.m.
**Where:** Ch 06 s2, *"Plan if the ice goes?" "Page two"*; Ch 08 s4, *"One storm takes that."*; Ch 09 s2, *"Sat 4:10 a.m. Hollis: a storm"*; bible calendar Feb 24 *"snow coming"*, Feb 27 *"heavy storm"*.
**Questions failed:** 4, 5.

- Ellen has said the exact risk aloud, to Arlene, with Arlene's bed riding on it. She spent 31 years in weather and traffic. On Dec 17 she ran league on a forecast and stranded thirty-three people (Ch 03 page).
- The outline never says what the forecast showed Wednesday to Friday, or why she didn't move the evening squad to Saturday afternoon or Sunday ahead of time, or warn the evening teams on Friday.
- This refund is the reversal that drives Act 3, and a reader who thinks it was avoidable will blame the planner, not the weather.

**The architect must answer:** in one line, what the forecast said and what she did before 4:10. Recommended: Friday's forecast is a few inches, over by noon. At 4:10 the band has stalled over the lake again, Dec 17's mechanism. This time she recognizes it at once, which also pays off Dale's thread.

### Finding B4. Ch 08's condition: the reversal from Ch 06 has no stated reason, the cost to Ellen's own money is missing, and the safer middles aren't closed
**Where:** Ch 06 s3, *"That paper is Arlene's bed. You want me to sign it so your no feels clean."* Ch 08 s4, *"If you come back, you come back beside me. Not behind me. I'll sign Arlene's paper."* Ch 01 page, l.27–31: *"or I pay her off that afternoon, and the contract you signed sells it to me by suppertime"* / *"Appraised value. Not a dollar under."*
**Questions failed:** 4, 5.

- **The reversal.** On Jan 19 she refuses to sign for Arlene's sake. On Feb 15 signing is her condition. Two things changed: the margin went from under to six hundred over (off the page), and Ray said "Take it." The outline gives her neither as a reason. Without one, the condition reads as the plot needing the release.
- **Her own cost.** Under the backup contract, if she misses, Ray buys at appraised value. The proceeds pay Arlene, and the rest comes back to Ellen; that is what "not a dollar under" protects. After the release, a miss means foreclosure, the condo man at land value, and most of her down payment gone. That down payment is "the house," the money Kate agreed to on the Ch 02 page. A planner would know this. The outline never has her say it, and Act 3's escalation table doesn't list it. If she doesn't know, it's a stupid choice. If she knows and doesn't say it, it's withheld.
- **Kate.** Ch 09 s6 says she "asks before deciding, for the first time." The release is the larger decision about family money, and she makes it in Ch 08 without asking Kate.
- **Safer options left open:**
  - (a) Arlene holds all three signatures in her drawer until the count (the orchestrator's listed option).
  - (b) "Take it" has already cost Ray the building if she clears. Why isn't that proof enough?
  - (c) Sign on the morning of March 1, once the winter is counted.

**The architect must answer:**
- Ellen's reason for reversing, in her mouth, in Ch 08 s4. Recommended: "In January I'd have been signing her bed away. Now I'm six hundred over."
- The cost to her own equity, said aloud.
- Whether Kate is asked first, or why not.
- One line closing (a) and (b). For (b): "You gave it angry. I need you to choose it."

## SHOULD-FIX (in order of cost to the reader)

### S1. The paydown ratio can't be heard
Ch 10 s1, *"new money means she lends that much less, so the committee needs that much less from the winter,"* and Ch 10 s4, *"more than half of what she had."*
- A listener hears "that much less" as dollar for dollar. Nine hundred under would then cost nine hundred.
- Instead it costs more than half her savings, and the page gives no way to see why. She looks as if she overpaid, or the listener is lost.
- The ban on ratios has swapped v2's confusion for a new one.

Fix: one sentence from Judy that gives the size without a fraction ("Every dollar you bring takes about an eighth of a dollar off what I need from the winter"), or drop "that much less."

### S2. Ch 10's bank decision is unplanted, telegraphed, and budgeted as a summary
- **Unplanted.** Granite Lakes has no committee on the Ch 01–03 page. Ch 02 makes the loan sound automatic: *"writes me the loan the day this winter clears thirty thousand."* Committee discretion first appears in Ch 10 s1, which is the same unplanted-bank-rule risk as v2 F1. Plant it before Ch 08: Judy's bible line, *"I like you. The underwriter has never met you,"* in any earlier scene.
- **Telegraphed.** *"Judy hears it (one line)"* in s2 settles the committee in the reader's head two scenes before the call. That leaves "the bank in real doubt" (Open item 5) with one scene of doubt.
- **The wire before the vote.** *"At nine her wire goes"*, before the 10:20 approval. The outline doesn't say why a planner pays before the yes. Give it a reason that is also a cost, for example: the committee votes only on money that has arrived. If they say no, her savings are in a note that is already in default.
- **Why Monday.** The outline doesn't say why the committee can't sit Friday on projections (v2 F14). One clause: the bank can't count a winter until it ends.
- **The budget.** s4 is 450 words for the wire, the committee, the call and Arlene's key. The calibration (rule 4) names "in a few hundred words" as the punished pattern.
- **Agency.** The yes turns on Ray's PA line, which Ellen didn't ask for. Either let Judy cite something Ellen did (the 1:40 call, the refunds at the counter), or have Ellen ask Ray for the annual name, since asking is her arc.

### S3. Ch 04 s1 contradicts Ch 03's page on time, and the turn has no trigger
- The outline says *"two days after the sign."* The kiss is Friday dawn and Ch 04 opens Saturday at 7:02, one day later.
- Ellen's *"This one took thirty-six"* is wrong for a woman who gives exact numbers. The pit (after the 11:30 p.m. power cut, Ch 03 page l.71) is about 31 hours before Saturday 7:02; the kiss is about 24.
- The Ch 03 page ends her interior on *"This one would cost her him ... Nobody had ever asked her to pay that, and she would not."* About a day later she tells him. Ch 04 is Ray's POV, so the reader needs the trigger in her mouth: Denise's "Honey," Ray repeating "It stays" to someone on Friday, or her own rule. "I write up my own errors" explains what kind of woman she is. It doesn't say what changed since dawn on Friday.

### S4. The release paper doesn't hold together across Ch 06–08
- Arlene signs in Ch 06 s1 (*"She signs the release her lawyer drew"*), and Ray lays *"Arlene's signed release"* on the counter in s3. In Ch 08 s4, *"Arlene names the cost again and signs."* She signs twice.
- Ray, who wants out, brings Ellen a paper he hasn't signed himself.
- Ray holds Arlene's signature from Jan 9 to Jan 19 and spends Jan 16 in the bob-house with Ellen without mentioning it. That is ten days withheld between the leads, with no stated reason.

### S5. Storm-day arithmetic and calendar
- *"vacation week ends"* is false on the bible's calendar. Presidents' Day 2027 is Feb 15, so Massachusetts vacation is Feb 15–19, and derby weekend (Feb 27–28) is a week after it. Keep Monday work and the drive.
- The 150-person derby crowd, bowling and eating at the snack bar, adds nothing to statement 3 (+600 to −1,400 is exactly the refund). One clause is needed, or a listener will ask where that money went.
- The off-page refund (~$2,000) at the bible's $160 a team is 12.5 teams. The bible-builder needs a whole number.

### S6. The declaration scene is under the kiss floor
Ch 10 s3 is 700 words. The bible's HEAT RULES require at least 800 for any scene with a kiss, and s3 also holds the count, a move from the office to the pit, the declaration and Kiss 3. Budget it at 900 or more.

### S7. The epilogue is a summary
It opens with Ellen waking at ten past five, then *"Evening at the alley,"* which is a time jump inside 700 words. Then come nine beats: Kate and Ray's coaching, Denise's bag and nerves, the pool sheet, Normie's new sheet, and *"One line each: Danny opens the store at six now; Hollis won the cribbage,"* the K lit, and "Scale of one to ten." That is about 80 words a beat, three of them reports.
- Ray's want apart from Ellen (handing the store to Danny) is resolved only in a report line. Ch 08 s3 gives Danny one line and nothing changes hands in a scene.
- Ray is coaching Kate *"with his hand on her wrist"* while his hand *"finds Ellen's under the counter while Kate bowls."* Sequence those two.

Fix: one evening, one place, from the first line. Cut the waking and the report lines, and give the store hand-off a beat in Ch 08 or Ch 10.

### S8. Ch 10's soft run is relabelled, not fixed
s2 ends on Linda's ball on the house rack, which v2's audit classified as **soft**. s3 ends *"'Ten o'clock. You'll come.' 'I'll come.'"* s4 ends *"So am I."* Calling them consequence, consequence and cut doesn't change what they are, and s2–s4 can land as three soft endings in a row before the s5 image. Give s2 a real consequence: the two-pin loss, or Judy's face at the PA line cut before anyone knows what she'll do with it.

### S9. Ray's biggest Act 2 cost is refunded in a week, free
He quits thirty years of coaching on Feb 2 and *"he's coaching tonight"* on Feb 9, with no ask and no cost on the page. Have him ask to be taken back (Verna, or Dale's crew). Ray learning to ask is his arc (bible: *"To want to be asked more than he wants to keep things"*).

### S10. The midpoint executes an announced plan instead of reversing
- Ch 04 s1 has Ray's *"It stays,"* and Ch 04 ends on *"I'm going to ask you for it."*
- Ch 05 s4 is that ask and that no. By the outline's own "What changes" line, Ch 04 already ends *"opposed on the record."*
- The genre map wants a midpoint that "reverses direction, does not add a complication." Here the only new fact at 47–50% is "four thousand under."
- Fix: cut Ch 04's last exchange (end Ch 04 on the supper line, or on *"Your no costs you nothing" / "Doesn't make it wrong"*) so the ask lands as Ellen's first ask, unannounced.

### S11. "Linda's day" and "Linda's name" are used as if they were one thing
- O1 says *"that one day,"* Ch 04 s1 says *"Linda's day is the biggest day she has,"* and the Ch 05 ask is an Open with *"Linda's cup the prize."* Ch 06 then runs a nameless Open on the same derby weekend, so the day was never the obstacle; the name was.

### S12. Denise's reversal has no trigger
Ch 05 s3: *"Don't you touch Linda's night."* Ch 07 s2: she argues for giving it. The plants table reverses Ray's "Linda wouldn't have wanted," but Denise's own line is never answered. Give her one line that owns the change.

### S13. "The building in every outcome" isn't closed
After the release Ray could re-underwrite in 90 days (bible) and bid at the foreclosure sale. Close it with his Ch 01 vow, one line: *"I'm not buying your mistake cheap,"* which applies to a foreclosure too. Also, Ch 05's *"what you have put by is what pays Arlene"* is inaccurate: his savings are a down payment, not a quarter of a million. Write "is what lets me pay Arlene."

### S14. O5 is not an obstacle between the leads, and it fails the one-talk test
See the table below. One phone call to Kate dissolves it, and one call does. The outline promises *"her yes carries a condition and a cost"*. The condition ("call me first") is there; the cost to Kate is never named.
