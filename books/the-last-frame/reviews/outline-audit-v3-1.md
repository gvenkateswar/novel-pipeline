## BLOCKING

### N1. Storm day: she knows at 3:50 what she acts on at 1:40
**Where:**
- Ch 09 s2: *"reads the radar herself, and sees it at once: the band has stalled over the lake again, December's mechanism. At 4:10 Hollis calls: the derby's outdoor day is off, and its crowd is coming to her. She's up and dressed."* Then Dale: *"I'd like your call before the forecast's." "You'll have it."*
- Ch 09 s3: *"A hundred and fifty people in snow boots."*
- Ch 09 s4, 1:40: *"thirty-three adults in December, a hundred and fifty with children now, and a boiler that quits with the power. Send them home."*

**Questions failed:** 4 and 5.

- Every fact she uses at 1:40 she holds by 4:10: the stall, December, the boiler, and a crowd she now knows is coming.
- Two safer options exist at 4:15, and the outline closes neither:
  - (a) tell Hollis the building can't take the derby crowd today;
  - (b) move Saturday's squads onto the Sunday lanes she held spare for exactly this.
- "You'll have it" commits Dale to a call later. It says nothing about whether the morning roads are safe.
- As written, she recognizes December at 3:50 and then repeats it with four and a half times the people. The only difference from December is whom she phoned. That empties the payoff of Dale's thread, which is supposed to be that this time she acts on the warning.

**The architect must answer:** in one line, why the morning is safe and the crowd may come.
Recommended, at 4:15: Dale says, "Roads hold till early afternoon. It's the afternoon that goes. I'll call the hour." The 1:40 call is then his hour, and she empties the building inside it. Ch 09 s2 is 300 words and already carries five beats, so the line needs words found inside Ch 09 (see I).

### N2. The paydown is wired to Arlene's note before the committee votes, and the safer option isn't closed
**Where:**
- Ch 10 s1, Judy: *"They vote on money that's arrived. Yours goes to Arlene's note at nine. If they say no, it's gone into a note you still can't pay. I can't promise."*
- Ch 10 s4: *"The wire goes. Eighty minutes in the other chair."*
- Condemnable choice 4 lists the cost as *"more than half her savings, wired before a vote that can still say no."*

**Questions failed:** 4 and 5.

- "Arrived" and "paid into Arlene's note" are two different things, and the outline treats the second as following from the first. Money can arrive at Granite Lakes and wait there (in her account, with Judy, or in escrow) until twenty past ten.
- Anyone who has closed on a house knows you don't pay before the lender says yes. A woman who wrote a weather page into her Open terms would ask.
- As written, Act 3's last cost is one the bank invented and the planner never questioned.

**The architect must answer:** why the money can't wait in the bank's hands until the vote. Or: Ellen is offered that and refuses it, for a reason.
Recommended, one exchange that turns the bank rule into her choice (in character and condemnable):
- Judy: "I can hold it till they vote."
- Ellen: "Send it to Arlene's note at nine. If they say no, she's that much paid whatever happens to me."

She pays toward Arlene's bed before the committee answers. The `[CHECK]` on "a paydown applied before approval" stays with the domain checker either way.
