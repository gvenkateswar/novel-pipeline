# Sample read: synopsis v3.2

**Read:** `books/the-last-frame/reviews/sample-synopsis-v3.md` (header: "Built from outline v3.2 (Oct 30, 2026)"), Chapters 1–5. Fresh read. The only other file I opened was `research/chosen-angle.md`, and only to build the persona.

**Persona:** Carol, 61, a retired school nurse outside Columbus, Ohio. Widowed six years, two grown kids, Kindle app on her phone, Kindle Unlimited plus maybe one paid book a month. Last month she read three clean later-in-life romances: one with a bakery, one with an inn, and one where the husband ran off with the dental hygienist in chapter one. She finished one of them and paid $4.99 for it. She is tired of bakeries, inns, beach towns, sisters who bicker, and books where the misunderstanding could be fixed by one phone call. She has already given up on two "cozy" books this fall because nothing happened in them.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

At the end of Chapter 4: **"Then I'll miss by an honest bill."** (about 74% of the synopsis)

Chapter 3 ended with a kiss and the whole town at the window. Then Chapter 4 spent its morning sorting out a sentence she let him mishear on purpose, then a party where his son gets the store, then a talk about a quote for a sign. That's three conversations in a row about who owes whom, and I put the phone down.

## 2. Buy: no

**The line that decided it:** "If the winter goes the way winters go here, Ellen will fall short by about the whole of January." By Chapter 5 I had been walked through the money four times. I can already see the tournament that fixes January, so the math isn't scaring me. It's just more math.

## 3. Pull per chapter (1–5)

- **Ch 1: 3.** I wanted to see Ray's face when he finds out March first is the only open square. The sheet cake and "Nice arc. Wrong lane." kept me going. The quarter million, Arlene's retirement room and Ray's deal with Arlene all came at me in the first two paragraphs, before I liked anybody.
- **Ch 2: 3.** I wanted to know if he'd come in once she put the key on the counter. The lock scene works. The offer that expires at five, the bank's $30,000 condition and the promise to Kate about her savings felt like paperwork.
- **Ch 3: 4.** I wanted the town's reaction to that kiss. The storm, charging the plow crew and setting pins by hand in the dark is the part of this book I'd actually buy. But she watches him miss "unless the numbers say otherwise" and lets it go, and from then on I trusted her a little less.
- **Ch 4: 2.** Nothing. The misunderstanding is cleared up in one talk the next morning, which tells me it was never a problem. The store handoff to Danny is a business announcement, and his hand on her wrist again is the wrist from Chapter 2.
- **Ch 5: 3.** I wanted to know what "make my no cost me" means. That last line is the first real hook since the dawn kiss. But it's two minutes to five and she walks into his store again, the same move as Chapter 2, and she says why she bought the alley for the third time.

Average: 3.0.

## 4. Do I care about these two people yet?

Yes, a little, and mostly him. A widower who can't stand his own house after supper and planned to spend his evenings at the alley is a man I understand. Ellen I admire more than I like, and letting him mishear her on purpose, then kissing him, cost her some of that.

## 5. Did anyone make me laugh?

Yes.
- "until the government retired her with a sheet cake"
- "Nice arc. Wrong lane."
- Charging the plow crew to bowl, "to the plow crew's lasting fury."
- "Then he can go stand behind hers."
- "Clearing from the west, with a good chance of Ray."
- "Everybody she ever beat. Which is everybody." Then the news that Denise has come second four times.

## 6. Did anyone make me want?

Yes.
- "she holds the flashlight close enough that he can feel her breath on his wrist"
- "to her annoyance she'd like him to stay there and keep arguing"
- The key laid on his counter "without a word," and "They both watch the clock go past five." That one was the best.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. **Ch 1, about 8%:** "a quarter of a million dollars, due March first, the day her room opens at the retirement home." Then Ray's deal with Arlene right after it. I skimmed the loan.
2. **Ch 4, about 60%:** "on March first the store goes to his son Danny, and he'll come in Saturdays if Danny asks him. Danny toasts his father's last winter behind the counter." It's a retirement announcement at an office party.
3. **Ch 5, about 92%:** "If the winter goes the way winters go here, Ellen will fall short by about the whole of January. Then the alley goes to Ray, Arlene's room gets paid with his money, and the house Ellen sold is gone for nothing." I already knew all of this.

Runner-up: the Kate call in Ch 5. "A decision she has already made" and "the voice" are both too vague for me to care about, and I'd skim it.

## 8. What I think is going on

Ray will give in on the open tournament under Linda's name, or he'll do something with his own money or his Arlene deal so that he no longer wins if she fails. That's my guess at "make my no cost me." Everybody Linda ever beat shows up, the alley makes its thirty thousand on championship night, and Denise finally wins it, or loses it a fifth time and people cheer. Danny gets the store, and Ray ends up behind Ellen's counter, which is what the old man at the party already said. All ten lanes go at once in the last scene. Kate's "decision" turns out to be Kate making the same kind of leap her mother made, and Ellen blesses it. Nobody is hiding a secret. The only question is the money, and I'm fairly sure of the answer.

## Forced-choice answers the orchestrator asked for

- **Where I'd put the book down:** End of Ch 4, "Then I'll miss by an honest bill." (about 74%)
- **Chapter I'd most want to read:** Ch 3, the storm night.
- **Chapter I'd skim:** Ch 4.
- **What I expect the ending to be:** The open Linda tournament runs on her day with Ray's blessing. It brings in enough to make the bank's number, Ellen keeps the alley, Ray spends his evenings there with her, and the last scene has all ten lanes going.
- **What I can't predict:** What "Then I'll make my no cost me what yours costs you" means in practice, and how Arlene still gets paid on March first if Ray backs out of his deal. Also why Ellen kissed him "anyway" (the synopsis keeps it from me), what Kate decided, and what "You told my lock" means. I didn't follow that line.
- **Would one honest conversation fix the couple's problem?** No. They've already had it. She needs Linda's night to make money, he won't put his wife's name on a cup strangers win, and he gets the building if she fails. Talking doesn't make any of that go away. The mishearing in Ch 3–4 was a one-talk problem, though, and it gets fixed in one talk, so that whole stretch felt like nothing.
- **Is the stake large enough?** No. If she fails, the man she's kissing gets the alley and Arlene still gets her money. Losing the house she sold is the real loss, but I never saw that house or heard her miss it, so it's just a number to me.
- **Does the hero want anything besides saying no?** Yes. He wants somewhere to go after supper because he can't stand his own house since Linda died. For two winters he planned to hand Danny the store and spend his evenings at this alley. He also wants Linda's night kept the way it was.
- **Do I know why the heroine wanted the alley?** Yes, in her words: "I bought it to hear all ten going at once." And: "A pin is standing or it's down. The whole room can see which. Nobody can sound fine about a pin." She explains it in Ch 2, Ch 3 and Ch 5. By the third time I'd already been told.
