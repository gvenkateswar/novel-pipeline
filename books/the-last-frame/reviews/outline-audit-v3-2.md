## BLOCKING

### N3. She learns the seasoning rule a week before its cutoff
**Where:**
- Bank rules: *"Rules 1 and 2 were learned by Ellen before Christmas, off the page, when she phoned Judy the day she first saw January's book."*
- Rule 2: *"it has to have sat in her name since before New Year's. That closes Ray's money and any loan from anyone."*
- Ch 05 s4 (Jan 2), to Ray: *"I asked Judy before Christmas ... Yours couldn't count if you gave it, because it has to have sat in my name since before New Year's."*

**Questions failed:** 4 (and 3).
- In v3.1 the call was on Jan 2, so the cutoff had already passed when she heard it. In v3.2 it is at least a week ahead of her. As the rule is written, any loan or gift deposited between the call and Dec 31 counts.
- The sentence puts both dates side by side, said aloud to the man with savings, so the reader needs no arithmetic: she knew the door was closing and let it close. Ray, hearing it, would ask "Why didn't you tell me before New Year's?"
- So the outline's "That closes ... any loan from anyone" is false for the last week of December.

**The architect must answer, one of these:**
- **(a), recommended:** move the cutoff to a date before the call: *"it has to have sat in my name since before I signed for the building."* Change Ch 09 s4's restatement and the `[CHECK]` wording to match. Her sworn savings still count, and no window ever exists.
- **(b):** keep New Year's and close the week. Borrowed money never counts however long it sits (restore Judy's "a loan with a bow on it is still a loan"), and the only gift on offer was Ray's, which is Arlene's floor (already in the sentence).

**Either way, date the call Dec 21–24.** That is after Ch 04 (Ray's POV, Dec 19), so no Ellen-POV chapter (Ch 03 is Dec 17–18) holds January's book without her thinking about it.
