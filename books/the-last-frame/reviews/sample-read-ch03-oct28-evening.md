## 7. The three most boring passages

1. Ch 01, about 8%: "Ellen stood behind the counter and counted the room as it filled. First, the ones moving: the Highway Department crew..." I skimmed a list of people I hadn't met yet.
2. Ch 02, about 45%: "Arlene had this place listed two years," he said, "and the only other one who came up the drive was a fella from Concord..." through "I'm asking it once." This is a paragraph of loan history while she's kneeling an inch from him with a flashlight, and I skipped to the end of it.
3. Ch 03, about 70%: "Ellen counted the room. First, the ones already moving: the man with the keys, two women from the diner team..." It's the same device as chapter 1, and I recognized it and skimmed faster.

## 8. What I think is going on

Ellen sold or mortgaged her house to buy the alley. Kate is her daughter, and Ellen swore not to put "the rest", her retirement money, into it. She will. Ray signed behind her loan, so if she fails he gets the place anyway, and that's why he "won't" hold the pool money. Linda is dead, and locker fourteen holds her ball or her shoes. Around February the numbers won't work, and Ellen will have to change or charge for Linda's Memorial. Ray will feel lied to because he missed "unless the numbers say otherwise" under Denise's strike. Ellen will say she told him, they'll split, and she'll save the place on Memorial night with all ten lanes going. The third item she wouldn't brief is him, or being lonely. Nobody has bought the March first square on the pool sheet, and by the end Ray or Ellen will be standing on it.

## The one line I would most want fixed

"Granite Lakes writes me the loan the day this winter clears thirty thousand, with every dollar it makes still sitting in the account." (Ch 02, scene 2.) I couldn't tell who said it or what it meant on first read, and the whole book's clock hangs on it.
