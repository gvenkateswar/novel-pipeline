# External review synthesis: *The Last Frame*, first draft (Oct 27, 2026)

Three outside AI panels reviewed the full draft. Each used the same seven-role prompt (target reader, acquisitions editor, developmental editor, line editor, fact and continuity checker, skeptical reader, market strategist), and none saw our internal notes.

| Review | Score (out of 10, as is) | Reader stars | Verdict | Reliability notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 6.8 | 4 (3.5 rounded) | Revise first | **Word count wrong:** it claims ~14,700 words, but the file is 33,045. Ignore its length-based advice (expand the book, price at $0.99). Its line-level findings check out. |
| B | 6.0 | 4 | Revise first | Ran actual counts. It claimed our reader invented the letter-fold error, but that error was real and was fixed before the file it read was built. Otherwise rigorous. |
| C | 7.0 | 4 | Revise first | Sourced (S1–S21). It is the only review to catch the legal and banking problems. It also found the title collision. |

All three agree on these points:
- The premise, the texture, and the work-based intimacy are the book's identity.
- Ch 4, Ch 7, and the Ch 9 D-side readback are its peaks.
- It needs a focused revision, not a rework.
- Our internal full-book read gave 4 stars, which is consistent with all three.

## Findings, by how many reviews raised them

### All three
1. **Vacation week ($3,900) arrives unplanted** on the Ch 9 tape. Ch 5 even says February has no holiday weeks. Plant it, and make it Ellen's decision.
2. **Ray's final with Linda's ball happens in one sentence.** Put a few of his deliveries on the page.
3. **Denise's Lakeview Diner team is never planted,** so "Go bowl" in Ch 9 reads as if she's subbing for Gagnon & Son.
4. **Tuesday "nine lanes, one team against a blind score"** (Ch 7) is wrong. Ten teams minus two leaves eight.
5. **"$23,950 vs. $24,000 / fifty dollars"** (Ch 7) is unlabeled. Name what each figure is.
6. **Late names:** "Mr. Pike," "Gil Arsenault," and "Halsey Falls" first appear in Ch 9. Plant them early.
7. **Ellen's age:** the brief says 58, but the text only ever says she retired at 56. State 58 on the page.
8. **Missing warmth:** the leads never share an unhurried scene that isn't about the building. Add one, and give Ellen one fact about her romantic past.
9. **Ellen's Ch 10 declaration** recaps Ch 7. Ray's ("I eat supper at a quarter past five") is the better model.
10. **Tics book-wide.** Review B's counts: room-counting 19, crossword 16, the number 22 (13 times), sign buzz 8, Hollis's cap 8, "I'm aware" 7, "Mrs. Daly" 7, key ring 7, "upside down" 5, "without looking" 8 or more, "lavender parka zipped to the chin" 3–4.
11. **Scene endings** too often land on the same prop-and-silence rhythm.

### Two of three
12. **Ellen is benched at the Ch 10 bank decision** (B, C). Judy and an unseen underwriter decide while Ellen counts butterscotches. Ellen should make the HELD argument herself, in the room.
13. **Ch 8 compresses Ray's changes into a montage** (A, C). Give Danny a moment of resistance or real feeling at the handover.
14. **The final "Mrs. Daly"** (Ch 10) needs to be either a shared joke both clearly enjoy, or "Ellen."
15. **The title collides** with a dark thriller (Cedar James) and an older mystery (C), and reads as crime or mystery (A, B). B suggests retitling to *Cleared to Bowl*.

### One review, but important
16. **The right of first refusal is misdescribed** (C). Under ordinary New Hampshire law it gives Ray the right to match an accepted third-party offer. It is not an automatic transfer if Ellen misses the bank's numbers. Fix: if Ellen can't refinance Arlene's note, she has to sell, and Ray can match the first offer she accepts. With his bank letter, he is the likely buyer.
17. **The raffle split may not be lawful** (C). In New Hampshire, raffles belong to qualifying charitable organizations, so the alley can't book half the raffle as its own income. Fix: the derby owns its raffle and the alley earns an agreed venue fee for the indoor events, plus normal bowling and food sales. The Ch 9 numbers stay the same.
18. **The $25,000 minimum-balance covenant through next February leaves only $600 of headroom** against roughly $2,000 a month of debt service (C). Fix: $25,000 at closing, plus monthly reports against a seasonal budget.
19. **"In the bank" (Ch 3) vs. "cash on hand" (Ch 10)** (C). Set the rule in Ch 3 so Ellen proves compliance instead of being rescued.
20. **Prepaid league money** (C). Teams "paid in September," and the Legion "paid through April." What happens to that money when the rate changes or a team leaves? One line each.
21. **The unfinished apology** (C). Ellen's Ch 6 line "You have not heard one new thing in six years" used Ray's grief against him, and "Ray. That was—" never gets finished. Finish the apology in the Ch 9 office scene before the key.
22. **Candlepin details:**
    - "box" is the standard term for a turn; "frame" is informal (B, confirmed via Wikipedia).
    - The Ch 3 spread eagle is described wrongly; it's two separated side groups (C).
    - An official calls a lob, not the foul light (C).
    - "Respot" and "jaws" are tenpin terms; use "reset" or "setting cycle" (C).
    - Denise's tenth-box strike needs its fill balls (C).
    - At Pine Ridge, "Nine" should be "Nine to win" (C).
    - Three balls that clear all ten is a ten, not a spare (C).
23. **"End of a midnight shift" at 2 a.m.** should be "during" (C).
24. **Explanatory lines after images** (C):
    - Ch 1: "He had put all of it into a pencil..."
    - Ch 8: "jack post" explanation.
    - Ch 10: the "first machine that had died on her" reminder.
    - The third "wood stays on the lane" maxim (B and C both say to keep the physical payoff and drop one spoken repetition).
25. **KDP disclosure** (C). Under KDP's content guidelines this book counts as AI-generated and must be disclosed as such at upload. This is a publishing step, not a manuscript fix.

### Disputed or rejected
- **Expand to 25,000–30,000 words** (A): rejected. The book is already 33,000.
- **Kiss count** ("brief says four, book has three"): this was an error in our review prompt. The bible's cap is four, with the fourth optional in the coda. Three is by design.
- **Move the release out of Ch 8 so it's a surprise in Ch 9** (A): rejected. B and C both say Ch 8 is the right place, and the outline puts it there.
- **Ellen clears by a bigger margin so Ray's teams aren't what saved her** (A): partly accepted. Make the vacation week clearly Ellen's own decision. Keep the final figures.

## What our internal pipeline missed
- Ellen's stated age.
- The legal mechanics: the right of first refusal, the raffle, and the covenant.
- Several candlepin terms.

Lesson: a simulated reader that shares the story bible reads the book it expects. External, independent review is worth keeping as a pipeline step before publishing.

## Author decisions (not blocking the revision)
- **Title:** keep *The Last Frame* with a strong romance subtitle, or retitle (B suggests *Cleared to Bowl*, the Ch 1 title).
- **Price:** B and C both recommend $2.99 in KDP Select. A's $0.99 advice rested on its wrong word count.
