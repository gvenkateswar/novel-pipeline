## 1. The notary condition (Ch 08 and Ch 10) — the one new plot fact

Added below the outline by the Ch 10 tournament; the judges rewarded the shape (Ray in the chair he refused), the readers and the auditor called it paperwork invented for the occasion, and the page contradicts itself (three signatures, one re-signed). **Default for this round: keep it and make it hold** (the author may veto to the outline's plain ending; see §5).

- **Ch 08 s3 (Arlene's phone call to her lawyer), one clause:** the lawyer wants the buyer's signature acknowledged because the backup contract is on record at the registry; Arlene says Raymond will carry it to Ellen himself (so it never goes back through the lawyer's office; that is why nobody caught it).
- **Ch 08 s4, one clause in Ellen's reading of the release:** she reads it top to bottom and sees a line for a notary left blank under Ray's name, and signs anyway because thirteen days do not allow a trip to Concord and back through a lawyer (her choice, on the page, so the snag is hers as much as his).
- **Ch 10 s4, Judy's condition in one plain sentence that names why the bank cares:** the contract is on record at the registry in Laconia; Ray's release of it was never acknowledged; the registry will not take it and the bank will not wire over it; he signs in front of her before four or the wire does not go. Under NH practice only the party releasing the interest (Ray) acknowledges; Arlene and Ellen do not re-sign. Cut "I like you, Ray. The underwriter has never met you" if it no longer fits.
- **Cost on the page:** Ray's walk to the chair is the thing he swore not to do (half of Main Street will have it wrong); one line, his or the Bench's, that names that.
