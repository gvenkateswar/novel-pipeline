# Chosen Angle — Book 1

*Chosen by the author on October 26, 2026. This file is the brief for story-architect and bible-builder. It replaces the angles in `research/niche-proposals.md` (an earlier, biased round) and builds on `research/neutral/`.*

## Market position
- **Genre:** clean, later-in-life small-town romantic comedy.
- **Categories:** Romance › Clean & Wholesome (main), Romance › Later in Life, Romance › Romantic Comedy.
- **One-line pitch:** A clean, laugh-out-loud romantic comedy for grown-ups: two people over 55, one small town that isn't on a beach, and a complete love story in one sitting.
- **Length:** ~30,000 words, 8–10 chapters.
- **Series plan:** book 1 of a town series. Each book is a complete love story with a new couple and no cliffhanger. Plan seeds for books 2 and 3 using side characters, but resolve every thread that book 1 raises.

## Working premise: *The Last Frame*
A 58-year-old retired air-traffic controller impulsively buys the failing candlepin bowling alley in a small New Hampshire lake town. The widowed hardware-store owner, who has coached the alley's winter league for 30 years, wanted to buy it himself. She has until the league championship in February to make the alley pay. He has every reason to watch her fail, and an inconvenient habit of helping.

## Setting
- A fictional small town in New Hampshire's Lakes Region (or southern Maine), in winter: December through February.
- **Candlepin bowling:** skinny pins, small hand-held balls, three balls per frame, fallen pins ("wood") stay on the lane, and the maximum score is 300, which no one has ever bowled. (Corrected: an earlier version said 450. The record is reported as 245; bible-builder should verify it.) Alleys are closing across New England. Get the details right.
- Winter texture: ice fishing, snowed-in league nights, plowing, a town that empties out after the summer people leave.
- **Avoid:** fall foliage, beaches, Vermont, inns, bookshops, bakeries. The comparable books are full of these.

## Must-haves (from `research/neutral/standout-clean-romance.md`)
1. **Real romantic comedy.** Banter, funny set pieces, two leads who spar. Warm, never sappy.
2. **Heroine 58, hero roughly her age,** each with a full life: adult children, friends, history, opinions.
3. **She chose this change.** No cheating or vanished husband as the inciting event. She may be divorced or widowed in the past, but it doesn't drive the plot.
4. **Real stakes and a hard deadline** (money, the championship, the town's opinion of her), with no villain ex. The two leads want things that genuinely conflict.
5. **Real expertise, accurate practical details.** Her air-traffic-control skills (calm under pressure, sequencing, reading people, timing) should show up in how she runs the alley and handles crises. No implausible solo DIY. The money and business details should hold up.
6. **Complete ending,** no cliffhanger, no withheld secrets.
7. **Strictly clean:** closed door, no swearing, no affairs on the page. Kisses are fine.
8. **Small cast:** at most two or three supporting characters with real weight. No bickering-sisters ensemble.
9. **Prose:** controlled and specific, never repetitive, no filler meals or errands. It must not read like AI.

## Point of view
Dual close-third, alternating between the heroine and the hero. Story-architect may propose otherwise, with reasons.
