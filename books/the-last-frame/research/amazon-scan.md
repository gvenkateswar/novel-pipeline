# Amazon Kindle Category Scan

*Collected October 26, 2026, through the author's own Chrome browser (personal profile), signed in to Amazon.com. Top 50 Kindle bestsellers in six categories, plus targeted searches for South Asian titles. 292 unique books checked on their product pages.*

## How to read this

- **BSR** = Amazon Best Sellers Rank across the whole Kindle Store. Lower is better.
- **Daily sales** come from Kindlepreneur's free calculator (kindlepreneur.com/amazon-kdp-sales-rank-calculator), which counts sales and Kindle Unlimited reads together. These are rough estimates, not Amazon data.

| BSR | Est. sales per day |
|---|---|
| 100 | ~1,000 |
| 500 | ~175 |
| 1,000 | ~113 |
| 2,000 | ~90 |
| 3,000 | ~70 |
| 5,000 | ~34 |
| 9,000 | ~17 |
| 12,500 | ~14 |
| 25,000 | ~11 |
| 70,000 | ~3 |
| 140,000+ | under 1 |

- **Indie** = no publisher listed on the product page (self-published). Amazon's own imprints (47North, Montlake, Lake Union, Little A, Thomas & Mercer) count as traditional publishers here.
- **Recent** = released between April 26 and October 26, 2026.

**Data limits:**
- Amazon renamed Kindle Unlimited to "Kindle+". The product pages showed membership badges on traditionally published books too, so I could not reliably tell which books are in Kindle+. That column was dropped.
- One snapshot on one day. Ranks move hourly, and October skews toward horror and holiday titles.

## What each category looks like

### Horror › Occult & Supernatural (angle 1)
- Mostly traditional publishers: Tor Nightfire, Harper, Flatiron, plus Stephen King backlist.
- **Short indie horror can chart here.** *Fervor* (Meg Smitherman, 120 pages, indie) at BSR 3,976. *The Haar* (David Sodergren, 194 pages, indie) at BSR 3,406. *The Naming* (30 pages, indie) at BSR 4,053.
- **Asian diaspora horror sells when a traditional publisher backs it.** Kylie Lee Baker's *Japanese Gothic* (Hanover Square, $2.99 sale) is at BSR 1,760, and *Bat Eater and Other Names for Cora Zeng* (MIRA) is at 5,139 with 6,250 ratings.
- No South Asian horror title is in the top 50.

### Literature › Immigration (angle 1 or 3 as a secondary category)
- Very little traffic. Below the top few, books sell under 1 copy a day. Min Jin Lee's new book is #1 at BSR 193; #10 is already at BSR 71,588.
- Useful only as an easy secondary category where a book can show a "#1 in Immigration" badge. It won't bring readers by itself.

### Fantasy › Historical (angle 2)
- Hardest category for an indie. Only 5 of 50 are self-published, and only 1 recent indie release made it.
- Dominated by big names (Schwab, Kuang, Bardugo, Abercrombie) and Amazon's 47North imprint.
- One novella-length book charts: *Ring Shout* (P. Djèlí Clark, 194 pages, Tor) at BSR 5,322. Short historical fantasy can work, but this example has a major publisher behind it.

### Fantasy › Myths & Legends › Asian (angle 2)
- 43 of 50 are indie, but almost all are long East Asian cultivation and LitRPG series (Cradle, Path of Ascension, Beware of Chicken). Median length is 494 pages, and **nothing is under 200 pages.**
- Readers here want long progression series. A literary historical novella would be in front of the wrong readers.
- No South Asian title is in the top 50.

### Romance › Later in Life (angle 3)
- The most open category: 44 of 50 are recent releases, 38 of them indie, and #25 sells about 110 a day.
- **But it is flooded.** About 20 of the top 50 are near-identical "My Husband Neglected Me…" billionaire titles from a few pen names (Lira Rain, CM Maya, Nola Vero, Liv Zora), released days apart. The rest are midlife women's fiction (Boldwood Books) and paranormal midlife series (K.F. Breene).
- A quiet midlife South Asian romance would sit next to those titles and would need strong cover and blurb signals to stand apart.

### Romance › Multicultural & Interracial (angle 3)
- 46 of 50 indie. Dominated by Ana Huang, mafia romance, omegaverse, and BWWM.
- One South Asian-authored indie book is doing well: *In Another Lifetime* (Swati MH, 577 pages, released Oct 13, 2026) at BSR 1,306 with 158 ratings in two weeks. This is the strongest evidence for South Asian romance demand in the whole scan.

## Bottom line

1. **No South Asian title in any of the three angles is selling well as an indie book, except one romance.** The "gap" the first research round found is real on the supply side, but there is little proof of reader demand at the indie level.
2. **Angle 1 (horror) has the best mix.** Short indie horror can chart, Asian diaspora horror sells when well packaged, and the South Asian horror shelf is full of very short, unrated books a well-made novella could beat. Main risk: readers find Asian diaspora horror mostly through traditional publishers' marketing.
3. **Angle 2 (Chola fantasy) is the most original and the weakest commercially.** The categories it would sit in are either dominated by big publishers or filled with readers who want long series. Novella length works against it. Best treated as a passion project or a tie-in to the Chola game, not as the commercial first book.
4. **Angle 3 (romance) has the most readers and the most noise.** The Later in Life category is open but flooded with mass-produced titles. *In Another Lifetime* shows South Asian romance can break out, but it is a 577-page book, not a novella.
