## Problems (fix before the external round)

### 1. House balls labeled by weight and racked "lightest to heaviest" (Ch 1)
- **Page (L131):** Ellen labels every shelf of the house-ball rack "with the weight written on it"; Ray racks the balls "lightest to heaviest." The labels come back as a motif (Ch 10 L2122, "by Ellen's tape on the steel upright").
- **Verdict: wrong (terminology).** Candlepin rules set one ball specification: at most 2 lb 7 oz and 4½ in, with no finger holes. None of the sources describe weight classes. Sorting house balls by weight is a tenpin habit. A candlepin bowler would read it the way the first book's readers read tenpin terms.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia, Candlepin bowling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candlepin_bowling); [Special Olympics NH, Candlepin Coaching Guide](https://www.sonh.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Candlepin-Bowling-Coaching-Guide.pdf)
- **Smallest fix:** Keep the tape but drop the weights. Ellen labels the shelves with something a newcomer would write (lane pairs, or "HOUSE"), and Ray squares the balls "a quarter-turn each" without "lightest to heaviest." The other option turns it into a joke: Ellen wrote weights, and Ray moves the label because "they all weigh the same, Mrs. Daly."

### 2. "A four-horse" (Ch 8)
- **Page (L1548):** Gil says, "I watched her pick up a four-horse with her third ball."
- **Verdict: wrong (terminology).** The attested candlepin name is the **Four Horsemen**: 1-2-4-7 (left) or 1-3-6-10 (right). I found no use of "four-horse."
- **Sources:** [SONH Coaching Guide](https://www.sonh.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Candlepin-Bowling-Coaching-Guide.pdf); [Wikipedia, Candlepin bowling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candlepin_bowling); [Sun Journal on candlepin terms](https://www.sunjournal.com/?p=10716884)
- **Smallest fix:** "pick up the four horsemen with her third ball."

### 3. Linda's 187 called "the house record" (Ch 10)
- **Page:** The Ch 1 card reads "Women's House High Single, Linda Gagnon, 187" (L51). Ch 10 says he watched "while she set the house record" (L2102).
- **Verdict: wrong as worded** (an internal domain slip). A women's high single is not the house record unless no man in fifty years ever beat 187. That is unlikely in a league house, since the men's world record is 245. A 187 is still a strong women's mark: the women's world record is 210 (Jenny Rowe), and the previous record was 207.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia, Candlepin bowling (245)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candlepin_bowling); [Worcester Business Journal, women's record 210](https://wbjournal.com/article/candlepin-bowling-center-in-millis-becomes-site-of-new-world-record-game-thrown-by-franklin/)
- **Smallest fix:** "while she set the women's record" (or "her house record").

### 4. Concord "lends whatever the appraiser puts on the alley," yet Ray holds a down payment (Ch 4 against Ch 6)
- **Page:** "the bank in Concord'll lend you whatever the appraiser puts on the alley" (L692). Danny calls the $48,000 "your down payment on the alley" (L1314).
- **Verdict: wrong.** Banks lend a percentage of appraised value on commercial real estate, usually up to 75–80% for owner-occupied property. They do not lend 100%. The page contradicts itself as well: with a 100% loan, Ray would need no down payment. The 80% figure actually fits the book: $48,000 is about 20% of a $240,000–$250,000 building.
- **Sources:** [Navy Federal CU, commercial RE loans: 80% max LTV owner-occupied](https://www.navyfederal.org/services/business/loans/real-estate.html)
- **Smallest fix:** "Says here the bank in Concord'll lend you against whatever the appraiser puts on the alley" (or "four-fifths of whatever…").

### 5. The relief briefing's headings and order (Ch 9)
- **Page (L1837–1851):** Weather / Equipment / Traffic / "Special conditions," with Special conditions "fourth, where it had stood in every briefing she had given in her life."
- **Verdict: wrong in two details.** FAA JO 7210.3 ¶2-2-4 lets each facility tailor its position-relief checklist, but it requires the Status Information Area **first** and **traffic last**. Its listed items include "Special activities" and "Special instructions or restrictions." "Special conditions" is not on the list. A controller would notice traffic coming before the last item, and would notice the claim that it was always so.
- **Sources:** [FAA JO 7210.3, ¶2-2-4](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/foa_html/chap2_section_2.html); [FAA JO 7110.65 App. A, transfer of position SOP](https://www.faa.gov/AIR_TRAFFIC/PUBLICATIONS/atpubs/atc_html/appendix_a.html)
- **Smallest fix (pick one):** (a) Rename the heading "Special instructions" and cut "fourth, where it had stood in every briefing she had given in her life." Then the payoff still lands last and the order reads as her own short form. (b) Keep the scene's order and make the break deliberate: "She had given traffic out of turn, which no checklist allowed, to keep this last."

### 6. Ray hangs the neon K himself (Ch 10 against Ch 4)
- **Page:** In Ch 4 Ellen refuses Ray's offer: "I hire out what I am not qualified for… That is a neon tube… it is a sign shop's job" (L632–636). In Ch 10 Ray climbs the pole and fits the new K himself, with the disconnect open (L2244).
- **Verdict: unclear on the law, wrong on the book's own terms.** NH requires a licence for "electrical installations," and the homeowner exception covers only a single-family residence (RSA 319-C:15). The statute does not settle whether swapping one neon unit on a commercial sign counts as an installation. The book's own rule, though, is on the page, and Ch 10 breaks it without comment. Sign-service sources treat tube replacement as work for a sign serviceman. The disconnect detail is right (NEC 600.6: a disconnect within sight of the sign, or lockable open).
- **Sources:** [RSA 319-C:15](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXX/319-C/319-C-15.htm); [EC&M, NEC Article 600](https://www.ecmweb.com/code-basics/article-600-electric-signs-and-outline-lighting); [Signs of the Times, troubleshooting neon](https://signsofthetimes.com/troubleshooting-neon-signs)
- **Smallest fix:** The Laconia shop's man is up the ladder and Ray holds its foot (the image survives with Ellen at the disconnect). Or have Ellen hand Ray the job in one line that admits the exception ("You are on my clock, and the shop says it plugs in").

### 7. The K dark for years while the rest of the sign stayed lit (Ch 1, 2, 10)
- **Page:** The first letter has been dark "since the winter Danny got his license" and the rest of the script lights every night (L11, L286, L2244). The new K goes in, and "the transformer took hold."
- **Verdict: unclear, and a sign-trade reader may object.** Neon units on one transformer are wired in series. A dead or gas-lost tube passes no current, so the other letters in that section go dim or dark until someone jumpers out the bad unit. If the rest stayed lit, either the K had its own transformer or someone jumpered it out.
- **Source:** [Signs of the Times, troubleshooting neon](https://signsofthetimes.com/troubleshooting-neon-signs)
- **Smallest fix:** One clause in Ch 10: "…the old K, which Arlene's sign man had jumpered out the winter it died" (and the jumper comes off when the new one goes in). Or give the K "its own transformer in the can."

### 8. Re-signing the February release before a notary (Ch 10; new plot fact)
- **Page (L2198):** "The paper you signed in February to let go of your contract with Ellen was never notarized, so you sign it again at this desk in front of me before four, or the wire doesn't go. I like you, Ray. The underwriter has never met you." Only Ray re-signs. Judy's seal "crimped the page" (L2226).
- **Findings:**
  - **Acknowledgment.** In NH a conveyance must be "signed by the party granting the same and acknowledged by the grantor" (RSA 477:3). An instrument affecting title is not valid against anyone but the grantor unless it is acknowledged and recorded (RSA 477:7). So the signature that needs acknowledgment is the one giving up the interest in the land: **Ray's**, as the backup buyer. Arlene's and Ellen's do not need it for the release to clear title. *Confirmed.*
  - **Would a title insurer require it?** Yes, *if the contract or a memorandum of it is on record*. Title insurers list a recorded memorandum as a Schedule B requirement until a **recorded** release and termination is filed (the Fund's guidance, below). A recordable release must be acknowledged, so the insurer would refuse an unacknowledged one. Re-signing before a notary cures it. Strictly, Ray only needs to *acknowledge* his existing signature, but signing again in front of her is common practice and harmless. *Confirmed, with the condition below.*
  - **The gap on the page:** the page never says the backup contract was recorded. If it was not, nothing on the record needs releasing, and Judy's condition reads as "paperwork invented for the occasion." The cold reader said exactly that.
  - **Loan officer as notary at her desk, with an embossed seal:** fine. An NH notarial act must be under an embossed seal or carry a stamp (RSA 455:3). Judy knows Ray personally, which serves as identification. *Confirmed.*
- **Sources:** [RSA 477 (477:3, 477:3-a, 477:7)](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/xlviii/477/477-mrg.htm); [RSA 455:3](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XLII/455/455-3.htm); [The Fund, recorded memoranda must be released and terminated before insuring](https://www.thefund.com/member/general-counsel-blog/june-2022/important-update-on-memoranda-of-homeowner-benefit.aspx)
- **Smallest fix:** Put the registry in Judy's mouth: "Your contract with Ellen is on record at the registry in Laconia, and the release you signed in February was never notarized, so the registry won't take it. Sign it again at this desk in front of me before four, or the wire doesn't go." (Belknap County's registry of deeds is in Laconia; use whichever county the bible gives Ambrose.) That one clause makes the condition real, and it explains why only Ray signs.

### 9. The cashier's check "goes back to the credit union" by Danny's hand (Ch 10)
- **Page:** The check is a cashier's check bought Feb 1, payable to Ellen, "her name and the sum set out in a teller's careful printing" (L1355). Ray carries it a month, then hands it to Danny: "That goes back to the credit union" (L2200).
- **Verdict: plausible only if Ray takes it back in person.** An uncashed original can be returned to the issuer for a refund without the 90-day lost-instrument wait. The bank verifies the customer in person, has the back signed, and confirms the check hasn't been paid. The remitter is Ray, so Danny cannot do this for him, and Danny cannot deposit a check payable to Ellen. Carrying it a month is fine; cashier's checks don't lapse at 30 days.
- **Sources:** [Bank procedure, cashier's check refund (in person, ID, signature on back, no 90-day wait for a returned original)](https://sandbox-ccbg.helpjuice.com/90401-official-checks/cashiers-check-replacement-reissue-refund); [SFFCU, 90-day rule for lost third-party teller's checks](https://www.sanfranciscofcu.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Stop-Payment-Form-Teller-Check-payable-to-3rd-Party.pdf)
- **Smallest fix:** "That goes in the safe till I take it back to the credit union," or Danny: "I'll hold it till you walk it over."
- **Minor, unclear:** today a teller would print a cashier's check by machine. "In a teller's careful printing" suggests handwriting. Change it to "printed by the teller's machine," or leave it.

## Settled (confirmed or plausible; no change)

- **Retired "by statute" at 56, now 58, after 31 years.** Controllers are separated on the last day of the month they turn 56. Starting at 25 fits the under-31 hiring limit. [5 U.S.C. 8335](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/8335); [5 U.S.C. 8425 (FERS)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title5/html/USCODE-2023-title5-partIII-subpartG-chap84-subchapII-sec8425.htm). *Confirmed.*
- **"At the Center they call it the D-side… One works the frequency."** Centers pair an R-side and a D-side (radar associate) on each sector. [Boldmethod, ARTCCs](https://boldmethod.com/blog/2014/01/air-route-traffic-control-centers). *Confirmed.*
- **Wilco, Roger, Unable, Readback, "Over and out asks me to answer you and tells me not to."** [NATCA, Communications](https://www.natca.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Communications-1.pdf); [UK CAA SERA.14045 (OVER/OUT)](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/923-2012/Content/Regs/03440_SERA14045_Transmitting_technique.htm). *Confirmed.* "Traffic, twelve o'clock, one mile" matches the advisory format ([Plane & Pilot](https://planeandpilotmag.com/secret-code-controllers/)). "Separation" and "loss of separation" are the current terms.
- **Candlepin basics on the page:** box, string, wood, strike, three balls a box, small ball with no holes, 60 ft to the head pin, the lob line in the bylaws, wood left on the deck and played (Ch 4, Ch 8), sweep, table, pit, curtain, elevator, the machine set by a cycle. [Wikipedia, Candlepin bowling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candlepin_bowling); [Wikipedia, Pinsetter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinsetter); [SONH Coaching Guide](https://www.sonh.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Candlepin-Bowling-Coaching-Guide.pdf). *Confirmed.* Ch 9's table "hung at the top with ten pins held in the air" is a real Bowl-Mor fault ([Bowl-Tech](https://www.bowltech.com/forum/pinsetting-pinspotting-equipment/candlepin-bowl-mor/1077706-weird-bowl-mor-s-and-a-few-questions-for-other-mech-s)).
- **Verna's ruling (Ch 1): re-rack and re-bowl after interference from the adjoining lane.** Under the rules, outside interference means the pins are reset and the box is bowled over. [SONH Candlepin Rules](https://www.sonh.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Bowling-Candlepin-Rules.pdf). *Confirmed.*
- **A 187 women's high single, a 104 average, a "ninety average."** All sit within the record range (women's record 210, men's 245). *Plausible.*
- **Normie's "Saturday show out of Boston."** *Candlepin Bowling* ran 1958–1996 on WHDH/WCVB. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candlepin_bowling). *Confirmed.*
- **League "Memorial," tournament "Open," cups, "lineage."** Ordinary league usage; nothing contradicts it.
- **Bob-house stenciled "HOLLIS PIKE over his address."** NH requires the owner's name and address on the outside of the door, and removal by April 1, so bob-houses still out on Mar 1 is legal. [RSA 211:17-a](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XVIII/211/211-17-a.htm). *Confirmed.*
- **Eleven inches of black ice; "black or white."** Comfortably safe for foot travel; clear ice is the strong ice. *Plausible.*
- **Derby called for "four inches of slush on top… water up through every hole."** Slush after a heavy snow and a thaw is ordinary, and Lakes Region derbies have been cut short by ice conditions ([NHPR, Meredith derby 2023](https://www.nhpr.org/nh-news/2023-02-13/warm-winter-puts-meredith-ice-fishing-derby-on-thin-ice)). *Plausible.*
- **"Ice-out."** The page never declares ice-out. It shows slush, booming ice, and water standing around the bob-houses on Mar 1, which is right for a late-February thaw. Keep it that way. For scale: Winnipesaukee's earliest ice-out on record is **March 17, 2024** (earlier records March 18, 2016 and March 23, 2012), so a declared ice-out on Mar 1 would beat the record by about two and a half weeks. A small kettle lake would go out earlier than Winnipesaukee, but not that early. Use the chapter title only as a figure of speech. [Wikipedia, Lake Winnipesaukee Ice-Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Winnipesaukee_Ice-Out)
- **The derby raffle drawn early at the alley (Ch 9).** NH tickets must carry the organization's name, the **date and place** of the drawing, the prizes and the price. The statute sets no time, so drawing at three instead of six on the same date and at the same place is within RSA 287-A. Only charitable, civic and similar organizations may hold raffles, and "for-profit company raffles are prohibited." Ellen's "The raffle is the derby's" keeps the alley clear of the first book's raffle error. [RSA 287-A:3](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXIV/287-A/287-A-3.htm); [Swanzey, RSA 287-A synopsis](https://swanzeynh.gov/how_do_i/apply_for_obtain/raffle_permit.php). *Confirmed.* (If the selectmen's permit named a time, the treasurer's "The committee's going to hear about this" is the right consequence.)
- **Dale Hutchins as "road agent."** NH towns elect or appoint highway agents, who have charge of town roads; "road agent" is the everyday term. [RSA 231:62](https://www.gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XX/231/231-62.htm). *Confirmed.*
- **Four o'clock wire cutoff.** Fedwire takes customer transfers until 7:00 p.m. ET, so a 4:00 cutoff is a bank's own policy, and a common one. [Federal Reserve, Fedwire hours](https://frbservices.org/resources/financial-services/wires/operating-hours.html). *Plausible.*
- **The disconnect "open on the pole," and "the transformer took hold, and the buzz came down the pole."** NEC 600.6 requires a disconnect within sight (or lockable open), and magnetic neon transformers hum. [EC&M, Article 600](https://www.ecmweb.com/code-basics/article-600-electric-signs-and-outline-lighting). *Confirmed.* (See Problems 6 and 7 for who hangs the tube and the series wiring.)
- **Concord's commitment held to March 1 for a fee "whether you buy or not"; "ninety days to write it again"; the credit union committee meeting at five; the loan officer's call; Granite Lakes' committee with Judy on it; Judy asking "Any of it his?"** All of this is ordinary commercial lending practice. *Plausible.*
- **Boiler stops when the power goes ("Burner runs on the power, and so does the pump"); the rotary landline works in an outage.** *Plausible.* The landline holds only on copper service, not VoIP.
- **Ellen bidding holidays by seniority; writing up her own operational error.** *Plausible* (from memory; not sourced).
