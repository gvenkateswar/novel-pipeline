# April 2026 playbook vs. October 2026 scan

*Written October 26, 2026. Records why two rounds of research reached different conclusions about heat level, so the decision trail stays with the book.*

## The two rounds

| | April 2026 playbook | October 2026 neutral scan |
|---|---|---|
| Where it lives | A claude.ai chat in a different project (shared link kept by the author) | `research/neutral/` in this repo |
| Question asked | How viable is a high-volume romance operation on KDP, and which sub-niches could an author with South Asian and thriller interests corner? | Where can one ~30,000-word novella from a first-time author with no backlist break into a bestseller list right now? |
| Evidence | Web sources: Circana, Written Word Media and ALLi surveys, BookTok commentary, Goodreads shelf counts | Live Amazon scan of 55 Kindle categories and 1,864 books on October 26, 2026, plus web research |
| Book assumed | 65,000–80,000 words, book 1 of a series, release every 4–6 weeks, multiple pen names over 12–18 months | One novella, standalone or series opener |
| Author background used? | Yes; three of three recommended niches were South Asian | No, by rule (`CLAUDE.md`, Research rule) |

## What April concluded

- The commercial centre of self-published romance is open-door, spicy (heat 3–4 of 5). Clean and closed-door is a smaller, loyal market. Dark romance is the fastest-growing segment.
- Series beat standalones roughly 3 to 1. Income tracks catalog depth: most authors earn little until 5–10 books.
- The Amazon ADULT filter is triggered by packaging (cover, title, metadata, the 18+ checkbox), rarely by interior content. Keep the outside PG-13.
- Separate pen names for different heat levels.
- Recommended niches: South Asian historical romantasy, South Asian romantic suspense, South Asian dark contemporary.
- Warnings: KDP requires disclosure of AI-generated prose; US copyright does not protect prose a model wrote; romance readers actively look for and boycott AI authors. Recommended that the human write the prose and use AI for outlining, editing and marketing copy.

## What October concluded

- Nearly every short self-published book that charts is either explicit romance or one quick entry in a long series. Standalone short books barely appear outside horror.
- Ranked on three tests at once (new indie authors break in; short books chart; #25 still sells), the most open category with real sales was clean small-town romance with an older heroine. Historical cozy mystery was second. Older-heroine sci-fi monster romance was third and carried the explicit-content and series expectations.
- Gothic, monster and Halloween romance were likely inflated by the late-October snapshot.

## Why they differ

The two rounds agree that explicit romance is where the most short indie titles chart. They differ because April measured where sales concentrate across the whole romance market for a multi-book operation, while October measured where a single short book from an unknown name can get onto a list today. Explicit categories scored well on charting and poorly on openness: the titles there are deep in long series by established names. Clean categories had recent unknown authors on the charts and still-meaningful sales at #25.

## What this means for the two books

- **The Last Frame (this repo).** Follows the October finding. Clean, short, older heroine, series potential. Lower ceiling, lower barrier.
- **The April-style book (separate repo).** Follows the April finding. Open-door heat, series opener, full-length or near it. Higher ceiling, needs a backlist to pay off, and the April warnings about AI-generated prose and copyright apply more sharply because the goal is income under a pen name.

## Open decisions carried forward

1. Length for the April-style book: 30k novella to match the test, or 65k+ to match April's economics.
2. Whether to keep the pipeline's neutrality rule for the April-style book, or run it as a labelled author-fit angle (April's niches all leaned on the author's background).
3. Disclosure and copyright stance for any book sold under a pen name: disclose AI-generated prose on KDP upload, add a human rewrite pass, or accept unenforceable copyright on the prose.
