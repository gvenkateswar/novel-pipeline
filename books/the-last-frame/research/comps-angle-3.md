*A Kiss in Kashmir* showed no 2- or 3-star reviews, and *In Another Lifetime* showed only one, so neither tells us much about complaints. **There is almost no review evidence about midlife South Asian romance specifically.** That is a real gap in the research.

## What readers loved
- **A window into the culture.** Many readers, including unhappy ones, say what they enjoyed most was the family life, food, and customs.
- **Sonali Dev's writing.** Even critical reviewers praise how her prose sounds.
- **Warm family casts** and the friendship between older women in *Lies and Other Love Languages*.

## What disappointed them (ranked by how often it came up)

1. **Predictable "Bollywood movie" plots.** Readers called the stories formulaic, over the top, and easy to guess. This came up for *A Bollywood Affair* (at least 5 reviewers), *Recipe for Persuasion*, *Lies and Other Love Languages*, and *In Another Lifetime*.
2. **Too long, too much backstory.** Readers said the books were wordy, kept going after the story ended, and buried the couple under side characters' histories. One said *Recipe for Persuasion* could have been half as long. This came up for *Recipe for Persuasion*, *Lies and Other Love Languages*, and *Marriage & Masti*.
3. **The culture was either too thin or never explained.** Some readers felt *A Bollywood Affair* could have been set anywhere in America. Others had to look things up to follow *Recipe for Persuasion* and *Ayesha at Last*. Readers want the culture to matter and to be clear without a lecture.
4. **Characters who feel young or not believable.** Readers called the heroine too perfect, the writing juvenile, and the dialogue stiff. This came up for *A Bollywood Affair* and *Ayesha at Last*.
5. **No chemistry, and conflict built on characters not talking.** Readers were frustrated when the couple's problems would vanish with one honest conversation. This came up for *Recipe for Persuasion* and *Ayesha at Last*.
6. **The cover promised light, and the book went dark.** *Recipe for Persuasion* was marketed as a fun cooking-show romance but dealt with heavy themes. Readers felt misled.
7. **Typos and editing errors.** Two *Marriage & Masti* reviewers complained. For an indie book, this is a reputation risk.

## The opening: what a new book could do

1. **Short and focused on the couple.** The second biggest complaint is length and backstory. A 30,000-word novella that stays on the two leads and the wedding fits what these readers asked for.
2. **Adult leads with adult voices.** A widowed caterer and a divorced accountant in their fifties answer the "juvenile" complaint by design.
3. **Conflict from outside, not from a misunderstanding.** Running a 600-guest wedding together gives the couple real pressure (families, money, logistics, past marriages) without leaning on "they just won't talk."
4. **Culture that does work in the story.** Tamil and Punjabi wedding customs can create the comedy and the stakes. The context should be clear from the scene, without footnotes.
5. **Honest marketing.** If grief runs underneath the comedy, the blurb and cover should say so.
6. **Surprise inside the genre.** Readers expect the happy ending. The fresh choices can be in how the couple gets there and in what the wedding reveals.
7. **Pay for a professional copyedit.**

## Commercial caution
South Asian romance has many indie titles, and most sell under 1 copy a day. The Later in Life category is open but flooded with mass-produced "neglected wife" titles. The one current breakout (*In Another Lifetime*) is a 577-page book that spread through BookTok. See `amazon-scan.md`.
