# Neutral Niche Proposals: ~30,000-word KDP novella

Date: 2026-10-26. Sources: `research/neutral/market-analysis.md`, `research/neutral/amazon-scan.md`, `research/neutral/raw/short-indie-charting.md`. Ranked on market evidence only. No author-fit angle was requested, so none is included.

## Read this first: what the scan says about length and series

- Short indie = 200 pages or fewer. A 30k-word book is about 110-130 Kindle pages. Most charting short indie books are 130-200 pages (roughly 35-55k words). A 30k novella sits at the low end of that range. Charting books in the 100-125 page band exist (The Orc Who Turned the Page 118 pp; Fervor 120 pp; What Caroline Heard 124 pp; Hot Air, Hot Water 119 pp; Haunted Horror on Halloween 100 pp), but they are the minority.
- The scan's own conclusion: nearly every charting short indie book is either explicit romance or one entry in a long, fast-released series. Standalone shorts almost never appear outside short horror.
- Many of the October 2026 releases chart with under 50 ratings (for example Threatened With Murder, 6 ratings, rank 1,815). That points to launch-week visibility and an existing reader base. It does not show a cold-start author can repeat it. The snapshot is one day, and late October inflates horror and Halloween titles.
- Single-book authors are weak earners in the market analysis (1-3 books: about 80% earn under $100/month; 25+ books: median about $3,000/month). A KU full read of a 30k book pays roughly $0.40-$0.50 (my arithmetic from the analysis). Every angle below is therefore best treated as book 1 of a series, except Angle 5.

### Angle 1: The inn she can't afford to sell (clean later-in-life romance)

**Categories and scan numbers:**
- Romance/Later in Life: 41 of 50 indie, 38 recent indie, 20 indie under 300 reviews, 7 short indie, best short rank 322, median 316 pp, about 108 sales/day at #25.
- Romance/Clean & Wholesome: 41 of 50 indie, 36 recent indie, 17 indie under 300 reviews, 4 short indie, best short rank 987, median 281 pp, about 101 sales/day at #25.
- Both are among the most open categories in the scan. Later in Life has the highest recent-indie count of all 55 categories (38). Its 7 short indie books are fewer than SF Romance (8) and Cosmic Horror (11), so short-book proof is good but not the strongest.

**Gap it fills:** Short, warm, low-drama romance for women over 40. Many Later in Life shorts are fast "betrayal and grovel" titles under pen names. The clean small-town shelf is dominated by long series.

**Premise in one sentence:** A 51-year-old divorced hospice nurse inherits her late aunt's failing lakeside inn and has 90 days to raise the $40,000 in back taxes, while the town's widowed contractor, the only person who can restore the roof in time, has his own reasons to want the inn to survive. If she fails, she sells to a developer and loses the one place she has belonged.

**Short indie books on the charting list:**
- Absaroka Ambush (Millie Vaughn): 193 pp, rank 987, Clean #10, only 13 ratings.
- Coastal Reflections, Coral Sands 3 (Fiona Baker): 189 pp, rank 2,071, Clean #39.
- The Quiet Rebuild, Women of Harbor House 2 (Katie Winters): 185 pp, rank 2,289, Clean #43.
- The Maplewood House, Meredith Bay 5 (Grace Meyers): 69 pp, rank 2,382. Listed in both Clean (#46) and Later in Life (#47).
- Tied by Treachery (Wren Shaw): 155 pp, rank 322, Later in Life #6.
- Promises He Broke (M.L. Hall): 159 pp, rank 863.
- The Wife He Lost at the Masquerade (M.L. Hall): 62 pp, rank 1,141.

**Heat level:** None to sweet for the Clean category (the category's definition). The Later in Life shorts are a mix. The scan does not record heat per book, so treat their heat as unverified.

**Series:** Yes. Three of the four Clean shorts are books 2, 3 and 5 of series (Coral Sands, Women of Harbor House, Meredith Bay). Readers expect 3-5 more books in the same town. Absaroka Ambush's series status is not recorded in the scan.

**Comparable titles:**
- Women of Harbor House and Coral Sands (borrow: ensemble small-town setting, short length; change: give book 1 a hard financial deadline).
- Tied by Treachery (borrow: a clear emotional hook in the title; change: no betrayal-grovel formula).

**Commercial signal:** The category numbers above. Market analysis also records Writer's Digest's 2025 prediction of readers wanting "accessible starting points" and lighter, cozier reads (author prediction, not survey data).

**Risk:** The charting shorts are mostly later entries in established series, so a cold-start book 1 lacks that backlist. Clean small-town is also long-series territory. The 101-108 sales/day at #25 means the rank-25 books sell well, but a debut will not start there.

### Angle 2: The Duchess of Bow Street (historical cozy mystery opener)

**Categories and scan numbers:**
- Mystery/Historical: 28 indie, 24 recent indie, 11 under 300 reviews, 3 short indie, best short rank 391, median 293 pp, about 40 sales/day at #25.
- Mystery/Cozy: 32 indie, 24 recent, 13 under 300 reviews, 3 short, best short rank 1,155, about 98 sales/day at #25.
- Mystery/Amateur Sleuth: 24 indie, 16 recent, 12 under 300 reviews, 3 short, best short rank 1,517, about 80 sales/day at #25.

**Gap it fills:** Short, finishable puzzle mysteries with a period setting. The market analysis shows cozy mystery over-represented among authors earning over $500/month (6-9% against 4% overall, self-reported) and an anecdotal note that cozy novellas sell at $3.99, but "series do better than standalone."

**Premise in one sentence:** In 1923, a disgraced ship's stewardess turned lady's companion must clear her employer's name when the woman is accused of poisoning a rival at a country-house weekend, and a wrong answer means the companion loses both her post and her reference.

**Short indie books on the charting list:**
- House of Peril, Bow Street Duchess Mystery (Cara Devlin): 162 pp, rank 391, #1 Historical Mystery, 68 ratings.
- Threatened With Murder, Violet Carlyle (Beth Byers): 152 pp, rank 1,815, 6 ratings.
- Murder in the Mediterranean (Ella Strike): 173 pp, rank 6,668, 42 ratings.
- Familiar Spirits (Sara Christene): 200 pp, rank 1,399, 3,783 ratings.
- Skeleton Shivers, Nightsmith Novelties Cozy (Patti Benning): 81 pp, rank 4,529.
- Pumpkin Spice Scandal, Killer Coffee 21 (Tonya Kappes): 186 pp, rank 1,155.
- Scared Waters, Enchanted Waters 16 (Jenna St. James): 164 pp, rank 3,128.

**Heat level:** None.

**Series:** Yes. The scan's pattern is a new short book every month or two. Expect a reader to want 4-6 more books with the same sleuth. Several of the charting entries are late books (21 and 16), which means readers are used to short entries, but also that the incumbents have large backlists.

**Comparable titles:**
- House of Peril (borrow: period setting, sub-200-page length, #1 in its category; change: ground the clues in a trade the heroine knows).
- Violet Carlyle series (borrow: historical-cozy tone; change: tighter plot, one case per book).
- Killer Coffee Mysteries (borrow: short, steady cadence; change: avoid the modern-cozy template).

**Commercial signal:** Category numbers above. The scan lists Mystery/Cozy, Amateur Sleuth and Historical as the top "open" group.

**Risk:** Historical Mystery has only about 40 sales/day at #25, so it is the thinnest of the three categories. A new author competes with series of 16-21 books. Readers of this subgenre are series buyers, and a debut with no follow-up on the shelf converts poorly.

### Angle 3: Stranded with the enemy (older-heroine sci-fi monster romance)

**Categories and scan numbers:**
- Romance/Science Fiction: 39 indie, 16 recent, 14 under 300 reviews, 8 short indie, best short rank 461, median 311 pp, about 73 sales/day at #25.
- SciFi/First Contact: 45 indie, 30 recent, 16 under 300 reviews, 6 short indie, best short rank 461, median 328 pp, about 61 sales/day at #25.
- Optional third: Space Opera, 34 recent, 3 short, about 62 sales/day.

**Gap it fills:** The short alien/monster romance is the biggest single cluster of charting short books, but the Sept 2025 complaint compilation says sameness is the main fatigue: interchangeable male leads, thin worldbuilding, tropes standing in for character. Writer's Digest (June 2025) says readers want new creature types and older protagonists.

**Premise in one sentence:** A 44-year-old salvage pilot crash-lands on a hostile moon and is rescued by a seven-foot, four-armed alien engineer who is the last of his crew, and to repair her ship before the oxygen runs out she must trade the one thing his clan values, her knowledge of their dying machines.

**Short indie books on the charting list:**
- Ice Planet Barbarians (Ruby Dixon): 188 pp, rank 461, 54,142 ratings.
- Barbarian Lover (Ruby Dixon): 191 pp, rank 2,307.
- Barbarian Mine (Ruby Dixon): 192 pp, rank 2,931.
- Wed to the Ice Giant (Layla Fae): 137 pp, rank 2,294.
- The Alien's Mate (Skyla Gray): 153 pp, rank 3,511, 190 ratings.
- Captured Mate (Morganna Williams): 139 pp, rank 2,352, Space Opera #10, 46 ratings.
- Crash Landed with My Weredragon (Celeste Raye): 152 pp, rank 5,540, 46 ratings.
- The Orc Who Turned the Page (Nellie Voss): 118 pp, rank 2,578, 4 ratings.

**Heat level:** Explicit. The scan describes this group as "nearly all explicit and part of long series."

**Series:** Yes. Ice Planet Barbarians has multiple books, and the other charting titles are series entries or one-off "mate" books likely to be followed. Readers expect 3-6 more books, each with a new couple in the same world.

**Comparable titles:**
- Ice Planet Barbarians (borrow: a stranded woman in a harsh world among a species with its own customs; change: the heroine is older and competent, the plot hinges on repair work, not capture).
- The Alien's Mate (borrow: short and direct; change: more specific worldbuilding).
- The Orc Who Turned the Page (borrow: a 118-page length that charts; change: no small-town frame).

**Commercial signal:** The scan's "Romance/Science Fiction + SciFi/First Contact" row calls out 8 short indie books and Ice Planet Barbarians as proof the length works. Market analysis lists sci-fi romance as a declining subgenre by K-lytics and romantasy as saturated, so there is a counter-signal here.

**Risk:** Highest-heat angle and the most formulaic. Market analysis flags sci-fi romance as declining over 12 months. The best-selling titles are from an 11-year-old series or rely on pen-name volume.

### Angle 4: The Consultant Files (workplace cozy/humorous paranormal fantasy)

**Categories and scan numbers:**
- Fantasy/Humorous: 34 indie, 23 recent, 8 under 300 reviews, 4 short indie, best short rank 331, median 356 pp, about 67 sales/day at #25.
- Fantasy/Cozy: 37 indie, 33 recent, 12 under 300 reviews, 2 short indie, best short rank 3,128, median 316 pp, about 75 sales/day at #25.
- Market analysis: Cozy Fantasy has the highest sales-to-competition ratio in the K-lytics data (23.1) and contemporary fiction-fantasy is up 53%.

**Gap it fills:** Short, funny, low-stakes fantasy with an ordinary-job premise. Cozy fantasy is the hottest niche in the web research, but the scan shows few short books in it (2), so a 30k book would be unusual there. Humorous Fantasy is where short books actually chart.

**Premise in one sentence:** A burned-out insurance claims adjuster is hired by the city's supernatural-damage office to assess a claim after a troll flattens a bridge, and she must prove the troll was framed before the city's budget cuts close the office and her new job with it.

**Short indie books on the charting list:**
- Hot Air, Hot Water, Supernatural Consultant Files (Paula Lester): 119 pp, rank 5,293, Humorous Fantasy #49.
- No Bones About It (Cassandra Elizzabeth): 132 pp, rank 1,177, 937 ratings (Halloween seasonal).
- The Strongest Archmage Just Wants To Die (Scribe Apprentice): 175 pp, rank 4,057.
- Rank Zero Waystation Mage 1 (John Croix): 134 pp, rank 5,649, Cozy Fantasy #43, 210 ratings.
- Thanks for the Memories (N.J. Weeks): 160 pp, rank 6,414, Humorous Fantasy #13.
- The Worst Ship in the Fleet (Skyler Ramirez): 146 pp, rank 10,286, 7,442 ratings.
- Human for Hire 17 (T.R. Harris): 179 pp, rank 10,964.
- Vampire Pack, Soulmate University 1 (Lola Glass): 198 pp, rank 331, Humorous Fantasy #3 (paranormal romance flavor, heat not recorded).

**Heat level:** None to sweet. Not recorded per book in the scan.

**Series:** Yes. Hot Air, Hot Water, Waystation Mage 1 and Human for Hire 17 are series books. Readers expect 3-5 more cases or episodes.

**Comparable titles:**
- Supernatural Consultant Files (borrow: office-job-meets-monsters, 119-page length; change: a self-contained case with a real mystery).
- Rank Zero Waystation Mage (borrow: cozy-adjacent, short; change: less LitRPG stat tracking).
- Legends & Lattes (named in the market analysis as a novella-length cozy fantasy; borrow: the low-stakes feel).

**Commercial signal:** Category numbers above plus the K-lytics 23.1 ratio. Evidence for short books specifically is weaker than in Angles 1-3: only 2 short indie in Cozy Fantasy and several of these titles have low ratings.

**Risk:** Short charting entries are mostly deep-series or seasonal. Humorous Fantasy is a catch-all, and the best short rank (331) is a paranormal-romance hybrid, not pure comedy.

### Angle 5: The thing in the stairwell (standalone short horror) - weaker evidence

**Categories and scan numbers:**
- Horror/Cosmic & Eldritch: 37 indie, 30 recent indie, 29 under 300 reviews, 11 short indie, best short rank 3,976, median 271 pp, about 4 sales/day at #25 (rank 56,799).
- Horror/Monsters & Creatures: 31 indie, 24 recent, 17 under 300 reviews, 7 short indie, best short rank 3,571, about 13 sales/day at #25.
- Horror/Occult & Supernatural: 6 recent indie, 4 short, best short rank 2,205, about 92 sales/day at #25 (the only horror category with real volume).

**Gap it fills:** Horror is the one place the scan finds short, standalone indie books charting. Readers of short horror accept a single self-contained story.

**Premise in one sentence:** A night-shift security guard at a half-empty office tower discovers that the stairwell between floors 13 and 14 repeats a different night each time he climbs it, and he has until the morning shift to find the way out before the building keeps him.

**Short indie books on the charting list:**
- Fervor (Meg Smitherman): 120 pp, rank 3,976, Cosmic #3, 937 ratings.
- The Haar (David Sodergren): 194 pp, rank 3,471, 9,998 ratings (2022).
- A Thing for Storms (Adrian Blue): 45 pp, rank 3,571, Monsters #7.
- The Naming (Adrian Blue): 30 pp, rank 4,053.
- Raw Blood and Nerve (Alex Grass): 149 pp, rank 13,871, Cosmic #11, 16 ratings.
- An Unwanted Western Halloween (Elizabeth N. Harris): 109 pp, rank 2,205 (October).
- Haunted Horror on Halloween (Heather Graham): 100 pp, rank 13,791.

**Heat level:** None. Violence and dread, not sex.

**Series:** Not required. Fervor and The Haar are not shown as series entries in the scan, and Adrian Blue's titles are a linked set of very short pieces. A reader would not demand follow-ups, though the market analysis's catalog-size finding applies to income.

**Comparable titles:**
- Fervor (borrow: 120 pp, single-concept dread; change: a human-scale setting).
- The Haar (borrow: contained location; change: shorter).
- Creatures of the Night (borrow: pairing short pieces; change: one longer story at 30k words).

**Commercial signal:** The only standalone-friendly evidence in the scan: 11 short indie in Cosmic & Eldritch and 7 in Monsters. Fervor ranks #3 in its category with a release in July 2026.

**Risk:** Weakest sales evidence. About 4 sales/day at #25 in Cosmic and 13 in Monsters means even a top-25 finish yields modest income. The snapshot is taken in Halloween week. Several short horror hits are October releases or older breakouts (The Haar, 2022). Honest assessment: this angle fits the length best and needs no series, but the market is small and seasonal.

## Data caveats

- Rank-to-sales-per-day figures are Kindlepreneur calculator estimates, not Amazon data.
- Thriller/Crime (#25) and Police Procedurals (#50) rows have odd ranks (likely preorders); nothing here relies on them.
- Kindle+ membership status per book was not captured, so KU share is unknown for these categories.
- Heat levels per book are not in the scan; heat statements come from category definitions and the scan's group descriptions.
- Series status was inferred from titles. Where the scan did not show it, this document says so.
- The five premises are invented illustrations to make each angle concrete. They are not market data.
