# Judge report: Chapter 8, v0 (A) vs v2 (B)

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B the people go after each other. Arlene has watched his truck from her window, Ellen grabs his wrist at the door, she gets a laugh out of him with "In fries," and Dale says out loud that losing pays Ray. In A the same scenes are lovelier sentence by sentence, but Ray mostly stands there being handed papers.

3. **Where A lost me:** about 43% in, at Arlene's table: "He put his hand on it. Three pages stapled at the corner, and on the last one Ellen's signature sat on its line in her printing, every letter standing separate, the same as the numbers she had printed on the lane tape. She had signed it the morning of the first of December, at the closing, six hours before he knocked on her door..." A good kitchen stops so he can remember a contract and its dates. I picked up again at "Voyons donc," but I skimmed a second time at about 80%: the rotary-phone call to Hollis about the committee vote, and then "That was my room," which I had to read twice. A's best moment is the key going into Linda's locker and not turning. B doesn't have it, and I missed it.

4. **Where B lost me:** about 83% in: "That puts me a hundred and fifteen dollars under thirty thousand, with every lane of the Open full." The next paragraph does the sum over again ("A hundred and fifteen dollars was a kids' birthday party on a Saturday with the fryer going, or most of one..."), so a ledger lands in the middle of the love scene. "In fries" pulled me straight back. The signing paragraph near the end also runs a beat too long ("one letter and then the next... She lifted the pen between the two names and set it down again").

5. **Confidence: medium.** A writes the better sentences: the bowling climax ("sixty feet of somebody else's maple with no winter of his in it"), the planets carpet, Ray resenting that he likes the place, and the locker. B gives its people more to say to each other: Gil talks like a host who has already had his night, Arlene is funny and sharp ("the two of you have been sitting in cars"), Ellen's lines are clipped and stubborn and sound like no one else's, and Ray apologizes out loud. A reads closer to the A1 pattern of the couple doing business at a counter. B has the same business, but the two of them touch and laugh in the middle of it.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Whole lines are shared word for word ("Holy mackerel," the four-horse, "Coffee's on," "Not yet"), and the rhythm is the same, with long "and... and" sentences set against flat one-line answers.
