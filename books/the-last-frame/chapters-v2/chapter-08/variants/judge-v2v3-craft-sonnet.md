A = variants/v2.md; B = variants/v3.md. Persona: Craft. Model: Sonnet.

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A lets the scenes carry the feeling and has a real laugh in the last room ("In fries."), where B keeps stopping to tell me what Ray just felt and mangles the one conversation that needed to be quick.

3. **Where A lost me:** Mostly it didn't. The nearest thing is the opening terms speech, about 5%. "'Ray pays our lineage here to the end of the season,' Dale said, 'and his team bowls for you.'" Dale cutting across Gil's terms is a nice beat, but "our lineage" is a stumble and I had to reread to see who was paying whom. The 92% signing paragraph ("It went slow, slower than she wrote anything at her counter...") is one long run-on, but it earns its length and ends on the three names.

4. **Where B lost me:** Two places.
   - About 80%, the Memorial negotiation. The lines are split so that Ellen seems to speak Ray's own offer: "'The league bowls its Memorial at two on the last Saturday, all ten lanes.' / 'Your Open gets the morning and the evening, and a cup of its own, with whatever name you like on it.'" I couldn't tell who was giving what to whom. The device might be "she runs ahead of him," but the text has to say so. "He watched her run it. It took her less time than it took him to see she was doing it" explains the trick after I'd already been confused by it.
   - About 93%, the signature. "...and Arlene's bed going down onto the paper with every stroke, and his own name above hers coming loose from her building as she went." That is the book narrating its own stakes over a quiet moment, and it's the line where I felt the author's thumb on the scale.
   - Smaller tells of the same habit: "He had done that sum in the truck and come up anyway." is murky. "She would say yes. He had known it coming up..." gives away the Arlene scene before it plays. "He had heard the price of it at Arlene's table, in her words, and carried it out Kettle Road in his jacket" explains what we just watched.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two share nearly all their best material (the plank pin, the dollar, the yellow page, "Not yet"). I'm choosing on the margins: B's spoken exchange is murkier and its narration over-explains, and A's laugh and cleaner dialogue read better. B does have a few good touches that A lacks: the locker fourteen glance, Hollis holding the other side of the dollar, and the gooseneck mic switched off. None of them outweighs the muddled middle of its last scene.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The scene structure, the lines, the images and the voice are nearly the same text, and B is A with more interior glossing.
