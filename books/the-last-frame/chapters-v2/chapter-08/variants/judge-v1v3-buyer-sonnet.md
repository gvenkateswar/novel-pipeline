## 1. Winner
B

## 2. Why
B lets the couple be in a room together longer and warmer (the wrist grab at her door, the "fries in paper boats" laugh, Arlene sending him back down the stair "to ask"), while A tightens the same scenes until the stakes talk crowds out the two of them.

## 3. Where A lost me
Early, in the settlement of terms, where my thumb started moving: "You take it, the Highway Department and the Legion go home to Kettle Lanes Tuesday at the season rate, and they bowl Linda's Memorial. My man takes it, you pay their lineage here to the end of the season, and Gagnon and Son bowls for me." (about 4%). Then "Dale read the stake twice off the back of Gil's scorecard" and the lineman paragraph explaining why a man who throws hard has nothing in his arm for a tenth box (about 6-8%). That is the business-between-men register the author called boring in A1, only more of it. A also has one line I did not follow: "At the end of the settee Hollis wrote something in his notebook. Ray had not asked Ellen for one thing to her face all winter." (about 12%). The wrist moment at her door is clipped to almost nothing (about 66%). The signing paragraph (about 88%) stacks "Arlene's bed going down onto the paper with every stroke, and his own name above hers coming loose from her building" on top of the pen sounds, and I felt the author pushing.

## 4. Where B lost me
The opening catalog: "Pop music came down out of the ceiling, the screens overhead ran cartoons for nobody, and the carpet had planets on it." (about 3%). It is décor, but short. Then the one-sentence tenth-box paragraph, "...and he stood at the line with it and looked down sixty feet of somebody else's maple...", which runs about 200 words (about 25%). I liked the picture and still held my breath through it, and A has the same trap, only broken into shorter sentences. The three-line terms at Ellen's counter ("Linda's name comes off the Open." / "The league bowls its Memorial at two...") (about 78%) read a little like a list, but the "fries in paper boats" exchange saved it.

## 5. Confidence
Medium-low. These are the same chapter beat for beat, with about 90% of the lines shared. A's Arlene scene, with "She would say yes. He had known it coming up," is slightly sharper than B's, and A's stakes are clearer. I'd call it close to a coin flip leaning B, because B has more of the couple touching and one real laugh, and A reads more like procedure.
