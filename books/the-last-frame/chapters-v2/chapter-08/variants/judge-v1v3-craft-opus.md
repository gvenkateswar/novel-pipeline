# Judge report: Chapter 8, v1 vs v3 (Craft, Opus)

1. **Winner: A.**

2. **Why:** A lets the big moments run long in the body (the ball rolling "at a speed a child could have walked beside," her fingers on his wrist "one at a time," the mustard going up his nose, Ellen's crack about fries in paper boats), while B keeps pausing to tell me what Ray already knew and why, so B reads cleaner and A reads like someone feeling it.

3. **Where A lost me:** around 80%, at the Memorial negotiation, where the narration announces the device before it plays: "He gave her the terms in three lines, one at a time, and let each of them go before the next." Then come four lines of terms and a dollar sum. That is the couple doing business, the A1 problem, though it's short. The fries line pulled me back. A smaller wobble at ~5%: "He gave the terms short, for the settee," which is clumsy and summarizes the stake I wanted to hear Gil say.

4. **Where B lost me:** around 85%, in the same negotiation, where B adds an explanation in place of a laugh: "Ray offered her nothing. Anything he put on that counter the bank would count as his, and she would know it before he had it out of his pocket." Earlier at ~10% I felt the same thing: "It did. Lose, and her two teams stayed gone, and every dollar they took out of her winter moved her building a dollar nearer to him." And at ~48%: "She would say yes. He had known it coming up." B keeps explaining what the scene is about to show me. Its climactic tenth frame also goes flat at "and it rolled a long time," just where A slows down and earns it.

5. **Confidence: medium.** B has the sharper single ironies ("Then the door opened on the dark, because he had unbolted it"; "his own name above hers coming loose from her building"; the end stool wondering whether Ray "had meant the first two strings"), and its signing passage carries more at stake than A's showier "steady as a good sweep setting down on its stop." But A's rhythm is warmer from start to finish, Ellen gets a line no one else could say, and the wrist and key moments carry more longing. Both share the same procedural stretch. A is less boring for its sake.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same skeleton, same dialogue nearly line for line, same "and... and..." cadence. One version trusts the page and the other annotates it.
