1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** In A the two of them get a real laugh in the dark alley ("Snack bar might find you some of it, Linda's night." / "In fries."), and I can always tell who is talking. In B that whole stretch is a run of bare one-line paragraphs, and I had to work out who was speaking.

3. **Where A lost me:** The terms speech at about 6%, "Terms. Ray takes it, the Highway Department and the Legion go home to Kettle Lanes on Tuesday at the season rate and bowl Linda's Memorial. My man takes it—". I was doing arithmetic on who gets what instead of enjoying Gil. It's short and the interruption from Dale rescues it, so I kept going. The second slow spot is Arlene's ledger speech at about 58%, "The Pines gives my room to the next name, and I go on the bottom of that list at eighty-four... that condo man gets the lot for what the dirt is worth." It reads a bit like a contract, but it's her bed and her age, and Ray's cupboard-gap look afterward puts a person back in the room. I did not stop.

4. **Where B lost me:** Two places. At about 10%: "Dale read the stake twice off the back of Gil's scorecard. 'Everything you put up, losing pays you.' It did. Lose, and her two teams stayed gone, and every dollar they took out of her winter moved her building a dollar nearer to him." The book is telling me what I already worked out, and I skimmed it. The bigger one is the signing paragraph at about 88%: "...and Arlene's bed going down onto the paper with every stroke, and his own name above hers coming loose from her building as she went." That's the author leaning on me to feel it, in a long run-on sentence in a scene that was quiet and working until then. I'd have read the middle of that scene on my phone with my thumb moving. Also at about 75% the run of separate one-line paragraphs, "Linda's name comes off the Open." / "The league bowls its Memorial at two on the last Saturday, all ten lanes." / "Your Open gets the morning and the evening...". They look like alternating speakers, and it's actually Ray talking in chopped pieces. That's the closest either chapter gets to the "couple doing business" feel of A1. The neater beats are also gone: there's no "Go ahead," and no hundred-and-fifteen-dollars-is-a-birthday-party line.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two chapters are the same chapter with the same bowling, the same Arlene scene and the same beats, so it's close. A has the laugh, the clearer dialogue, and the signing paragraph is cleaner. B has some good touches (the padlock on locker fourteen, the gooseneck switched off), but the over-explaining and the confusing split dialogue cost it. I'd bet a dinner, not the rent.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes, same voice and the same recurring details (the pencil, the dollar, the gloves squared, the cupboard gap), so they read as two passes over one draft.
