A = v2.md, B = v0.md. Persona: Craft. Model: Sonnet.

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A trusts me more: people say less and mean more ("In fries." and "the two of you have been sitting in cars" do the work that B does with a rotary phone and a locker), and the sentences move at the speed of someone talking, where B keeps stopping to arrange a prop.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 93%: "Whatever happened to her number now, he could not buy her building if she put the keys in his hand." I read it twice and still wasn't sure whose number or which keys. The other soft spot is the signing sentence at about 88%, "...every letter in her printing standing separate from the one before it, and the pen going over the paper was the one sound in the whole dark building, a small dry scratch he could have heard from the lot." It is one long breath of pretty, and the lot detail is decoration. Earlier, at about 38%, "She had taken nothing from him since the hood that wasn't on an invoice, nothing from the man the March paper said would buy her building" is also murky. I could see what it was reaching for, but it read like a note to self.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 55%, the locker paragraph: "His thumb found the gap on the coil where Ellen's front key had been before he meant it to. The locker key was the smallest one he carried, a stubby brass thing, and he picked it out by feel with his eyes on the lock." This is a man alone with a key ring and a padlock, and the thumb started moving faster here. The scene is about Linda's ball, but the writing is about the key. It never pays off, because he draws the key out and leaves. A close second is about 80%: "'After that I have no room at all.' ... 'That was my room,' she said." That is a line explaining its own line, and the second half deflates the first.

5. **Confidence: medium.** B has the better closing image (she is still holding out the key behind the glass), the plow engine on Hollis's phone is a real pleasure, and the deck-bare moment in the strike is good. A's laugh, the Bench coffee payoff for the dollar bet, and the shorter, harder Arlene scene tilt me. It is close enough to a coin flip with a thumb on the scale.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same cupboard-coming-down and plank-across-a-ditch images, the same Arlene speech nearly word for word, and the same "Coffee's on" beat. It reads like one writer revising twice.
