# Judge report: Chapter 8, v1 vs v2 (Skimmer)

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** It's the same night in both, but in B I got through the bowling and the signing without slowing down, and it gave me a laugh ("In fries.") right when the money talk was about to lose me. A makes me wade through two very long sentences at exactly the moments I most wanted to know what happened.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 54%, at Arlene's table, when she spells out the contract: "I'm paid months on, when my lawyer's done selling it out from under her, and that condo man gets the lot for what the dirt is worth." My thumb sped up there and didn't slow down until the phone call. Then at about 95%, during the signing, I gave up reading word by word and started skimming: "the point coming clear of the paper between each one and coming down again exactly where she meant it to, steady as a good sweep setting down on its stop, the red gone out of her fingers now and the nails cut short and square". It's one sentence that runs the whole paragraph, and I skimmed to "She capped it." The bowling climax at about 21% is also one long sentence ("stood at the line with it and looked down sixty feet of somebody else's maple...and set his eyes there and kept them there"). I got through it, but I was tired by the end.

4. **Where B lost me:** At the same spot, about 57%, with the same contract line: "I'm paid months on, when my lawyer's done selling it out from under her, and that condo man gets the lot for what the dirt is worth." I skimmed it just as I did in A. I slowed again for a moment at about 83%: "A hundred and fifteen dollars was a kids' birthday party on a Saturday with the fryer going, or most of one". That's money math, but "In fries" and "He laughed out loud, once" two lines later brought me back. Every other part of B held me.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two versions share most of their lines. B wins on the two set pieces (the third ball and the signature), which are shorter and easier to follow, and on the one laugh. A has nicer texture in places: the planet carpet, and her hand taking his wrist "four fingers and a thumb he could feel one at a time." Neither one reads like A1.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** The dialogue is nearly word for word the same. Only the long sentences around it differ.
