A = variants/v3.md, B = variants/v1.md (persona: Craft, model: Sonnet)

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A's sentences change speed with what is happening (short flat beats after the long roll, "Then there was nothing on the deck," "Nobody on the rail clapped"), and it trusts objects to carry feeling ("he left every one of those things where it was") where B explains a little more and ornaments a little more.

3. **Where A lost me:** In the first scene, at about 8%: "At the end of the settee Hollis wrote something in his notebook. Ray had not asked Ellen for one thing to her face all winter." I stopped and went back, because the second sentence doesn't follow from the first. A similar stumble at about 25%: "Hollis had taken the other side of that dollar in the lot." That comes before the lot scene, so I didn't know what it meant. I also felt the tension go out when it says "Ray could take him. Gil had known it when he said yes." and then, a few pages later, Ray is nine pins behind. Near the end, "and Arlene's bed going down onto the paper with every stroke, and his own name above hers coming loose from her building as she went" tells me what the signing means after the pen scene had already shown it.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 22%, the long mid-bowling run-on: "...and stood at the line with it and looked down sixty feet of somebody else's maple to the near end of the lying pin, where its head touched the deck, and set his eyes there and kept them there and let the ball go easy, so easy somebody on the rail groaned, and it rolled the length of Gil's lane at a speed a child could have walked beside..." The rhythm imitates the slow roll, but it goes on past the point where I felt it and into showing off. The signing paragraph at about 88% does it again: "steady as a good sweep setting down on its stop, the red gone out of her fingers now and the nails cut short and square, and he watched every letter of it...". The opening list ("the screens overhead ran cartoons for nobody, and the carpet had planets on it") reads as set dressing.

5. **Confidence: medium.** It is close. B has the better laugh ("The snack bar sells fries in paper boats") and a cleaner closing line for the Arlene scene ("and she was sending him back down it to ask"). A is tighter and varies its rhythm more, but it has three small muddles that B doesn't.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same dialogue, same plain "squared the gloves" gestures, and the scenes and lines carry over almost unchanged between the two.
