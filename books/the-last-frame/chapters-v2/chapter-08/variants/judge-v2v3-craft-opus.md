# Judge report: Chapter 8, v3 (A) vs v2 (B)

**1. Winner:** B

**2. Why:** B gives the two of them one real laugh in the dark building and lets Ray want things off the page, like the creases going soft from reading her letter in the truck, while A walks the same road with more "and... and..." sentences and more of the narrator explaining the money.

**3. Where A lost me:** At about 81%, in the Memorial deal. Four lines in a row with no tags: "Linda's name comes off the Open." / "The league bowls its Memorial at two on the last Saturday, all ten lanes." / "Your Open gets the morning and the evening..." / "Hollis says the committee voted on an Open. Nobody voted on a name." I read the first one as Ellen talking, then had to go back and work out that all four are Ray. It's the two of them doing business line by line, and right after it comes "Anything he put on that counter the bank would count as his, and she would know it before he had it out of his pocket." That's the narrator doing the sum for me. A won me back at the signing ("Arlene's bed going down onto the paper with every stroke"), which is the best sentence in either version.

**4. Where B lost me:** I drifted a little at about 57%, in Arlene's payout mechanics: "I'm paid months on, when my lawyer's done selling it out from under her, and that condo man gets the lot for what the dirt is worth." That's contract plumbing read out at a kitchen table. A has the same line word for word, so it doesn't decide anything. B never lost me for longer than a line, because the Ellen scene keeps breaking out of the deal: "Snack bar might find you some of it, Linda's night." / "In fries." / "He laughed out loud, once, and it went down the dark lanes."

**5. Confidence:** medium. A's prose is often richer line by line. The lying-pin paragraph, "Ray enjoyed it and held that against himself" and "for the first few forkfuls he forgot what he had come to say" are all better than B's versions. A also sets up a better turn at Arlene's: "She would say yes. He had known it coming up." But the last scene is where the chapter lives, and B's last scene is two people while A's is a negotiation. B also gives Gil and Dale different voices: Dale cuts in on the terms and Gil talks straight over him "as though nobody had spoken."

**6. Same-author check:** Yes. They share the same skeleton, the same lines of dialogue and the same images (the plank over a ditch, the cupboard coming down). These are two drafts of one hand.
