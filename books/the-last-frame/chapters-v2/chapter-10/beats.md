### 4. The closing condition (new mechanism): logic and domain

**What it is.** Arlene recorded a memorandum of the backup contract at the county registry. She would have, to protect her bed. On the morning of closing, the title company runs its last search and finds it. The Feb 16 release (Ch 08) is a page on Arlene's lawyer's letterhead, signed by Arlene at her kitchen table and by Ellen at her counter after league. Nobody had it notarized. Title won't insure the refinance over a recorded contract to sell to Ray unless Ray acknowledges the release before a notary. Judy is one.

**Logic-gate answers (the writer needs none of this on the page beyond Judy's one sentence):**
- **Why wasn't it notarized in February?** It was drawn fast, signed at a kitchen table and a counter at night, and treated by three people as a private release among themselves. The need appears only at the closing-day search.
- **Why can't Ellen or Arlene fix it?** Only Ray can acknowledge Ray's signature.
- **Why can't it wait a day?** The Pines holds Arlene's place only through the first of March (Ch 01, Ch 08).
- **How does the information travel?** Title tells Judy, who takes it into committee. The committee approves subject to it. Judy says it aloud across her desk, and Ellen's phone, face up on the other chair, carries it to Ray's counter.
- **Why is Ray reachable?** Ellen put him on the chair by phone. That is Kate's rule, "call me first … like this", on its first working day.
- **Why doesn't Ray's money come into it?** He empties his jacket before he goes: the envelope goes into Danny's hand. At the desk Ellen says "None of it is his," which is true, and she never states a false fact.
- **World rule 15** (nothing by luck): the snag comes from what three people did in Ch 08. The cure comes from what Ellen did in s4's first line.

### 9. Listener rules

- **The "Ray" drumbeat.** No more than two paragraphs in a row open with "Ray", and no more than eight in the chapter. Open paragraphs on the object, the sound, the other person, or "He".
- **Speakers.** Every line at an emotional peak is cued by an action or a sound. On any phone line:
  - Judy's lines are cued by a sound of hers (a door, heels, her chair, cellophane, a drawer);
  - Ellen's by her nearness to the phone;
  - Kate's by her car or her hospital (a door, a turn signal, a page in the distance; never "That's me").
  - **Four voices at most in s4's phone passage:** Ellen, Judy, Ray and Danny. The Bench have no lines.
- **The request** is cued as "Kettle Lanes," he said, "this is Gagnon. Request." Ellen's answer comes after an action of hers.
- **The title beat** is "He waited to be corrected." The token "Ms." does not appear on the page.
- **Kiss sentences** run to 30 words at most. Every "she" and "he" points to the nearest named person. The laugh is its own sentence.
- **The cooling motors** get one long sentence with at most two *and*s, then a short one.
- **Clock times.** Three in the chapter: half past six (s1), two (s4, the committee) and four (s4, the wire room and the van; the same hour, said twice).
- **Lane numbers** always take "lane" ("lane five"; "the rack between lanes four and five").
- **Numbers** are in words, and none are stacked.
