1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** I'd keep reading B because Kate answers out loud on a speakerphone, in a voice that is nobody else's ("it's medieval", "Is he the one who followed you out as far as the plow had been?"), where A has Ellen report the answer secondhand and spends its energy on paydown talk and callbacks.

3. **Where A lost me:** around 30%, in Judy's lines at the counter: "The winter's cash stays where it is, every dollar it made still sitting in the account. Anything you put on the note is new money, and I'll want to see where it came from." That is a banker explaining the plot to the room. It comes back at about 55% with "Twenty-eight thousand nine hundred" and the stacks of bills, then "Tomorrow I sign eighty-eight hundred of mine onto Arlene's note", which is the couple doing business. The Danny line, "Not buying in, not getting out of the way. Neither invoice, then.", is a callback I couldn't parse without having the bible. The biggest loss is the opening: "Kate said, 'Then do it. And call me first next time. Like this.'" The chapter's biggest answer arrives as a quotation, and the scene I was waiting for never happens on the page.

4. **Where B lost me:** at about 65%. "It's approved subject to one thing. Your contract with Arlene is on record at the county, and the release from February was never notarized, so title won't insure over it." A last-minute notary snag is manufactured, and it sends Ray sprinting up Main Street at the very point where the chapter should be quiet. I also tripped slightly on the old K "lit by the letters it used to sit with and dark itself" at the end, which is a little mannered. Neither made me want to put the phone down.

5. **Confidence: medium-high.** The ending (key on the palm, "Throw her", "Both words") is nearly identical in both, so the choice rests on the first half and the love scene. B's version is warmer and funnier: "Third item," Ray says in her voice, then "And one you left off. I've loved you since about the first night." It also has the watch ticking faster than any motor, and Linda's last league sheet with the poor box written down as carefully as the good ones. A's love scene is competent but more declarative. A reads closer to A1 (ledger, binder, committee procedure), though not close enough to be a stop line.
