A = variants/v2.md, B = variants/v1.md (persona: Craft; model: Sonnet)

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A's phone call, the kiss and the bank all do their work through small things people do with their hands and mouths (Ellen's hands flat on either side of the phone and staying there, "Third item" coming out in his voice, "Is that Ray Gagnon on my chair?" / "In it."), where B states the same beats more evenly and smooths them out.

3. **Where A lost me:** the catwalk paragraph, "Down the catwalk the motors were giving up the day's heat, ticking as they cooled…" (about 48%). Three motor ticks and a list of sounds before anyone speaks, and I was waiting for her to talk. It is shorter than B's and it ends on the watch, which earns the wait. One smaller spot, early on (about 12%): "Nobody in the alley moved." Odd phrase, and I had to stop and place it.

4. **Where B lost me:** "His feet ached from three strings in shoes he'd had resoled the winter nine's belt let go the first time." (about 47%), and then the paragraph it leads into, with the heel "worn down on the inside," the "smudge of pin grease across the back of one hand that she hadn't found yet," and the boots on the grating. That is a row of nice details laid out one after another while the scene stands still, and I skimmed it. The kiss paragraph ("She smelled of fryer oil and the cold off the lot… he could hear her breathing, and his own, and nothing else in the whole building") also goes soft and generic at the end.

5. **Confidence: medium.** They are the same chapter beat for beat, with much of the same text. The edge is in the phone call, the dialogue and the kiss, not in the structure.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same sentence music and the same recurring objects (the key, the ring, the ticking, the gooseneck), with the second version cut and reordered rather than rewritten.

Notes for the record (as a reader, not fixes): A's call lets Kate say what she did ("I did the subtraction anyway. Without the dollars. It's harder than it sounds.") and then stalls on Ellen's hands, so the "Then do it" lands as a verdict on the withheld "Ten." B rushes it ("I lay down on it a while. Then do it."). A's callbacks pay off: the watch ticking, the left headlight "aimed at the ditch since Christmas," the slush depth on Judy's blotter. B has fewer of them. Neither reads like A1; neither has codes or procedure running the scene. The bank scene in both is procedural in the middle (release, notary, wire), but A keeps it moving with talk.
