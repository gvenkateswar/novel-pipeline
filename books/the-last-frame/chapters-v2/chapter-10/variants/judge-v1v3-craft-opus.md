# Judge report: Ch 10, v3 (A) vs v1 (B)

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B the people sound more like themselves and the hands are doing the feeling for them: Kate's "I mean I lay down on it a while," Ray's "I'll bowl it bad and ordinary, Denise," the pencil smudge on Ellen's forefinger that he rubs once and that stays, and Ellen kissing him straight through his laugh. A has the same bones and a few sharper cuts, but more of it is a room being counted.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Hollis's golf pencil had him back in at anchor on the Gagnon and Son sheet." (~33%). That's the scorebook talking, right after Ray's announcement, and it was the one sentence I had to read twice. A softer drift at the room inventory just before it, "Gil Arsenault stood at the rail in his good overcoat... Judy from the bank had a place in the standing room by the shoe cubbies" (~15%): a roll call before the key comes free. And the bank both versions share: "Approved subject to one thing. Your contract with Arlene is on record at the county." (~72%). A gets out of that faster than B does, because Judy is cut off mid-sentence ("or Arlene's money sits till—") and the next line is The Pines.

4. **Where B lost me:** the same bank paragraph, "It's approved subject to one thing. Your contract with Arlene is on record at the county, and the release her lawyer drew in February was never signed in front of anybody. Title won't insure over it." (~68%). B lets Judy finish all her sentences, and then adds "Your own signature, of your own free will." (~76%), which is a notary reading me the form. That's the A1 texture for a few lines: the couple doing business. It's short, and the walk up Main Street past Arlene pulls me back out. Elsewhere B didn't lose me. "He could hear her breathing, and his own, and nothing else in the whole building" (~55%) is stock, but by then I was inside the scene.

5. **Confidence: low.** Nearly a coin flip, since these are line-level siblings with the same scenes, beats and last line. B wins on voice contrast (Ray against Denise, Kate's sleep line) and on the catwalk, where her closing the inch herself and kissing him through the laugh is better than A's "He kissed her with the laugh still in him." A has better single images: Linda's cross little tick "at the alley or over the checkbook," "There was no ring on the glass where it had stood," Arlene watching the door from the sidewalk. If the panel goes the other way I wouldn't argue hard.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. They have the same sentences in the same order, and one is a revision of the other.
