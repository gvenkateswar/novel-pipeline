## Flags (log against outline, resolved here)

1. **The gap's wording.** The outline says "about four thousand short." Ellen's profile gives exact numbers and no hedges, and the ledger's Jan 5 projection is exactly $26,000. She says "twenty-six thousand," "thirty," "four thousand short," and does not say "about." The figure doesn't change.
2. **Two money statements, one with figures.** The outline allows one money fact per chapter. The orchestrator adds the loan line (which loan is which). That line carries no figure. The gap (s4) is the chapter's only figure. The meeting doesn't state lineage, the rate, the $700 or any amount.
3. **Ray's vouching names the place, so Denise's question gets sharper.** Ray tells the room Ellen said "It stays" in the pit behind five on the storm night. Denise was bowling on five that night and has never heard the condition. So when Ellen says "Unless the numbers say otherwise" to her in s4, "Who'd you say that to?" really means *did Ray hear that half?*, and Ellen's silence answers it. **From Jan 5, Denise suspects.** Denise isn't a lead, so rule 20 holds. The Ch 06–08 beats must account for her keeping it to herself.
4. **New invented bylaw (the outline's "Get right").** Verna reads it in s3: *"The house shall set the lanes and the hours of championship weekend, and the league shall bowl its championship within them."* This gives Ellen the legal power behind the Ch 06 plan. Once Ray's speech is in the minutes, her word is the only thing holding the Memorial where it is. Ch 08's condition restores the league's two o'clock Memorial by agreement, not by bylaw. Domain checker: invented and plausible.
5. **Ellen's contractions.** In her own POV (Ch 01, Ch 03) she uses contractions freely. In Ch 04, seen from Ray's side, she has none with him until "I'm very good with a clock." Read that as her guarded register after the kiss. This chapter lets her notice the change once, in s2 (see the s2 notes).
6. **Kate calls on a Thursday.** The bible has her calling Sundays at seven. She calls on New Year's Eve from an ER break. That's off schedule, which is the point. One clause.
7. **Dec 19–31.** One line in s1 and nothing more: Ray has sat in the second chair after close on six of the twelve nights since, and neither of them has said a word about the sign or about January. **Continuity fact set here: no kiss between Dec 18 and Dec 31.** Christmas stays off the page, so the line doesn't name it.
8. **Names.** Normie and "Arlene's Normand" are both off the page in this chapter. Neither is needed.
9. **Verna's lavender parka** was spent in Ch 01. Don't describe her clothes.

### Scene 1

**Setting:** The office, Thursday, December 31, twenty to twelve at night. Glow night, half full: five lanes going under the black lights, muffled through the shut door. The heater's orange bar is ticking. **Sensory anchor:** Kate's voice on speaker from the phone on the blotter, with an ER's overhead pages faint behind it.

**Characters:** Ellen; Kate (by phone).

**Want:** Ellen wants Kate on the line at midnight. It would be the first turn of a year in thirty-odd she spent on the phone with her daughter that wasn't from a break room. Kate is the one on shift now, and her break is ten minutes. The reader learns the want when Ellen asks how long the break is, hears "Ten minutes," and looks at the watch whose face Ray's palm covered on the nineteenth. Midnight is twenty minutes off.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. Open inside the call, with no set-up. Kate: "Mom. Scale of one to ten." Ellen: "Of what." Kate: "You know of what." Ellen: "Six." Kate: "So nine." Kate translates her mother's calm without being asked. That's the laugh, and it lands on Ellen.
2. Kate is calling on a Thursday, off the Sunday schedule, from the break room. She called early because at midnight she'll be on the floor. Sideways, never directly, she makes it a family tradition: she learned you call when you get your break, not when it's midnight. There's a sheet cake in the break room ("somebody always brings one to a holiday shift").
3. Ellen has the answer to that in order: the bids, the seniority, the schedule she didn't make. She doesn't give it. She asks how long the break is.
4. **The one line on Dec 19–31**, in Ellen's interior, while Kate talks about the cake. The second chair is where she hooked it out on the nineteenth, facing hers. Ray has sat in it after close on six of the twelve nights since, with his jacket zipped, while she ran the tape, and neither of them has said a word about the sign or about January. Her boot finds its rung (the body).
5. Kate, quicker now that her break is running out: "Is anybody there with you tonight?" Ellen draws breath. An overhead page comes through the speaker, Kate says "That's me," and the line goes dead.

**Subtext:** Kate wants to be asked something, and so does Ellen, and each briefs the other instead. The answer to Kate's question is in the chair, and Ellen was going to say it, or wasn't. The reader can't be sure, and neither can she.

**Texture budget:** One. The ER page through the speaker shows Kate living her mother's old life on purpose. The black lights are setting, not texture.

**Point of view:** Ellen. Her contractions run normally with Kate (family register). Her interior runs longer than her dialogue, which here is mostly two- and three-word answers.

**Ends on:** **Question nobody answers.** "Is anybody there with you tonight?" Then the page, "That's me," and the dead line. At most one sentence after it, and no reaction beat, no feeling named, no look at the chair after the call.

**Word target:** 600

### Scene 2

**Setting:** The pit behind lane seven, 11:58 p.m. to 12:04 a.m. One caged bulb over seven-and-eight. Through the gap under the masking the lanes show bright and backward, pins glowing under black light. **Sensory anchor:** Denise's countdown on the PA comes through the curtain half a beat behind itself, then the house roars and the noisemakers go.

**Characters:** Ellen, Ray. Denise's voice on the PA only.

**Want:** Ellen wants to be out of the town's sight at midnight. Denise has the mic, and every face in a glow necklace will be watching the owner to see where Ray stands. Under that, she wants him to come and find her without her asking, because she doesn't ask. The reader learns it in one interior sentence as she sits down: she hasn't told him where she was going, and she has come to the one place in the building he would look. Ray wants to say something that isn't what you say to anyone. In the way: Tuesday, five days off, and what she knows.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. At 11:58 she goes through the steel door by lane ten and along the catwalk to behind seven, carrying the paper cup of sparkling cider Denise put in her hand at ten. She sits down on the catwalk step.
2. Seven sets in front of her, and the sweep comes down on time. She has learned to hear that. One sentence, no jargon.
3. The countdown comes through the curtain, then midnight and the roar.
4. The steel door; his boots on the catwalk. He's on call tonight (R. GAGNON on her clipboard, ten to close). He comes along and lowers himself onto the step beside her, and his knees say what they think of it.
5. **For a whole minute he doesn't say Happy New Year.** She counts it without looking at her watch; she always knows the time. Instead he talks about what she's holding (his tender register: her hands, never her). "You've carried that since ten and not drunk any of it."
6. She answers with contractions, something like: "I don't like it. It's cider pretending." **She hears the contractions go out to him and lets them. The first one got out at the desk on the nineteenth, and he heard that one too.** One interior clause, once in the book. Don't explain it beyond that.
7. He takes the cup out of her hand and stands it on the catwalk. **The kiss, chosen by both: he moves first, and she meets him.**
   - Her bare hands go inside his open canvas jacket. Under her palm, through the flannel of the shirt pocket, is folded paper she knows is the Worcester page.
   - His hand goes to the back of her head, in her short hair.
   - Mouths named once. One embrace.
   - **Her desire register:** the list goes. One long sentence joined by *and*, out of order: the cup, Tuesday, his knees, the five words, the house still roaring through the curtain, how far she has to look up even sitting down. She notices the order has gone and doesn't go after it.
   - **Fresh wording.** Do not reuse the Ch 03 constructions: no "she let it run," no hand on the back of his neck, no hand to his face, no breathing out against her mouth, no warmth off the kickback, no "had the sentence ready."
8. The first line after the kiss, Ray, low: "Happy New Year, Ellen." Ellen, exact: "It's four minutes past." Ray: "I know what time it is."

**Subtext:** He waited a minute because "Happy New Year" is what you say to anyone. She hears that he waited on purpose, and she kisses him five days before he stands against her, knowing what he didn't hear. The reader holds that; the scene doesn't state it.

**Texture budget:** One. Seven's sweep on time, heard by her ear. It shows she's learning his.

**Point of view:** Ellen. Desire register required (bible, THE CORE, high point). Play it in real time: no summary, no cutaway, no aftermath cut.

**Ends on:** **Dialogue, the chapter's one button.** "I know what time it is." Last line. Nothing after it.

**Word target:** 850   **Word floor:** 800 (intimate). The kiss beat (approach, kiss, first line after) gets at least 150 words.

### Scene 3

**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, Tuesday, January 5, six o'clock, before the Tuesday league bowls. The lanes are dark. Folding chairs have come up from the cellar in rows facing the counter, the settees are pulled round behind them, and twenty captains sit in front of their teams. Verna is at the scorer's table under the gooseneck lamp, with the binder and the Grange gavel. **Sensory anchor:** the folding chairs, cold from the cellar and smelling of it; the room's coats steaming dry.

**Characters:** Ellen, Ray, Verna, the captain (Dale Hutchins; call him "the captain"), a Legion man with mustard on his sleeve, Hollis (silent), the room.

**Want:** Ellen wants her rate, on the merits, in three minutes, without asking anyone for it. Once the Memorial comes up, she wants the floor back and won't take it when it's offered. Ray wants to beat her rate without the room thinking he's angling to buy cheap, so he puts her word on the record to show the Memorial is safe. That's the move that beats her. The reader learns Ellen's want from where she puts her hands: on her knees, when Verna asks her the question.

**Rule for this scene:** they share a room and **never speak a word to each other.** The longing lives in that.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **The hand-off line, then closed.** From the end of the counter, Ellen sees the old couple from the one-and-two settee in the front row. They were first down the drive on the eighteenth, oldest first, with the lot empty at seven forty-three. They will raise their team's hand with the rest. One sentence. Don't count the room; that motif belongs to Ch 1, 3 and 9.
2. Ray is in the Tuesday rows with Hollis beside him. She knows where he is without looking, and she looks.
3. **Verna reads the bylaws aloud, in full.** The page gives one summary clause and quotes two:
   - Ray's: the September rate holds until the January meeting votes, "adopted January, two thousand five, on the motion of R. Gagnon."
   - The championship-weekend line: *"The house shall set the lanes and the hours of championship weekend, and the league shall bowl its championship within them."*
   - Ellen files the second one: it gives the house the weekend. Hollis writes something in his waterproof notebook with the golf pencil, and nobody else does.
4. Verna: "The owner may speak for three minutes. I have a watch. So do you, I see." (Not "Mrs. Daly"; Ray has this chapter's one use.)
5. **Ellen's three minutes are a briefing, not a request:** three items, no contractions, no figures. Something like: first, the rate hasn't moved since two thousand four; second, the house has paid the difference every week since, and the house is now her; third, the new rate takes effect on the twelfth if this meeting approves it. She yields with forty seconds left. The room hears an outcome where it wanted to be asked, and its faces show it. **The narration never says so** (rule 5).
6. **The laugh.** Verna: "The chair recognizes the gentleman with mustard on his sleeve." The Legion man asks: if they vote her down, what happens to the Memorial? The derby's that weekend; will she rent their Saturday to the fish people?
7. **Ray stands.**
   - He has nothing to fix, so his thumbnail works a loose screw on the back of the folding chair in front of him. Ellen notices he does that hardest when the talk is about himself.
   - **The loan line**, said once and plainly. It costs him: he tells the league out loud that he's paid to be the man at the bottom of her drive. Model (the dialogue doctor may reshape it, but both lenders and both purposes stay in one beat): *"Most of you heard about my mail before I did, so here it is. The store's offer went to the credit union, to buy this place off her clean, and that one died at five o'clock on the third of December. Concord's the bank that'll lend me what the appraiser says to buy it March first if she can't pay Arlene, and I paid them to hold it there. Weigh what I say next by that."*
   - Then, for the league and against her rate, short and figure-free: the Highway crew and the Legion can't pay it, and this is the league Linda bowled.
   - Then **the turn**, to the room and to Verna's book: *"And Mrs. Daly gave me her word, the night of the storm, in the pit behind five, that Linda's Memorial stays the way it is. 'It stays,' she said. That book of yours lets the house set that weekend any way it likes, so her word is what the Memorial's got, and I'll hold her to it. Put it in the book, Verna."*
   - He quotes her two words exactly, because they're hers. On "It stays" he looks at her for the first time.
8. **Ellen's interior, the longest passage in the chapter:**
   - His voice is low enough that the back rows lean in. He calls her Mrs. Daly in public, and she hears the formality and what it costs him.
   - She wants him to keep talking, even now, against her.
   - Her own words are in his mouth, and the other five aren't.
   - **Bible rule 1, one clause only, fresh wording:** a woman who has written up every error of her working life before anyone asked is sitting on this one, because this one is priced in him.
9. Verna writes, then says the sentence aloud again for the minutes, word for word. It's the confirmation Ellen never got, now in Verna's voice, with the half still missing. **Don't use "readback" or "read back" in this scene;** the reader makes the connection.
10. Verna, over the binder: "Does the owner wish to respond?" Ellen's hands are on her knees. "No." Verna: "The owner declines." The page holds on it for one beat, no more.
11. **The vote is by team.** Verna calls it, and Ellen stands to take it (bible habit). "Opposed, eighteen. In favor, two." The September rate holds. "Mostly," and it isn't close.
12. The room breaks up toward the shoe cubbies. The captain stops at the end of the counter on his way to pay his crew's lineage: "Ray says you gave your word. That'll do for my crew." She rings it.
13. She goes to the panel and throws the ten toggles herself, under her own tape, for the league that has just voted her down, at the rate it voted to keep. On six, Ray takes his jacket off and doesn't look at the counter.

**Subtext:** She has the five words, Verna has offered her the floor, and she says "No." He means it as a gift: binding her word in public because he trusts it more than the bylaw. Every hand that goes up against her went up easier because of him.

**Texture budget:** Two. Verna reading the bylaws (two quoted; shows Ray wrote the rule that beats her, and the house's power over the weekend). The loan line (shows what Ray stands to gain, said against himself).

**Point of view:** Ellen. **No contractions in her three minutes** (fear: polite, procedural, imperative, calm on top). Show the gap between her calm and her interior once.

**Ends on:** **Consequence.** The last sentence is an action: the lanes she lit for the league at its rate, and the first ball going down six. Not a line of dialogue. Don't use "a pair at a time."

**Word target:** 850   **Word floor:** 700 (orchestrator request: this is the midpoint, and the scene the writer would most likely compress. Play Ray's speech, Verna's repetition, the question and the "No" in real time.)

### Scene 4

**Setting:** The office, about a quarter to seven the same night, the door shut on the Tuesday league. All ten lanes are going behind it, muffled: the office's own pleasure, and tonight it's costing her. Then the lot, her car, engine running, the sign red on the snow. **Sensory anchor:** malt vinegar and fryer oil off the paper boat Denise brings in.

**Characters:** Ellen, Denise; Hollis (by phone, last three lines).

**Want:** Ellen wants Denise to hear a decision before anyone can say no to it. She briefs, she doesn't ask, and the reader learns this when she starts with the number instead of a question. Denise wants Linda's night left alone. It's her shot at the cup she has wanted longer than she'll say, and the friend she lost it to twice. Then Ellen wants the derby's crowd before her nerve or the town can stop her, and she makes the call from the car so nobody hears it.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. Ellen is standing at the adding machine. The lineage notice is still face down under the tape where she laid it on the nineteenth. Denise comes in with a paper boat of fries, sets it down on the notice, and sits in the second chair, Ray's. Ellen notices whose chair it is (the body). Denise: "You're standing. What happened?"
2. **The ordinary pleasure, three or four sentences:** Ellen eats standing, the fries too hot, malt vinegar. She enjoys them without comment, on the worst night of the winter.
3. **The money line, the chapter's one figure,** in her ledger terms, no contractions: *"At the September rate the winter comes in at twenty-six thousand. The bank's number is thirty. I am four thousand short."*
4. Ellen, no figures: an Open on championship weekend with its own cup, with the town and the derby crowd entering, would close it with nothing over. Put Linda's name on it and the town comes out for Linda, and she has room for one bad thing.
5. Denise: "Don't you touch Linda's night." Ellen: "Unless the numbers say otherwise." (Quoted exactly.) Denise: "Who'd you say that to?" No answer. Ellen takes a fry. (See Flag 3: Denise heard Ray's speech an hour ago, and the silence answers her.)
6. Denise: "What about your own money?" Ellen: "Not enough to matter. And I swore to Kate." Denise: "So it's a cushion." Ellen: "It's separation." **Ellen sits down,** which means it's decided. Denise sees her sit.
7. **In her head, plainly, once, no figures:** an Open without Linda's name would clear the bank's number, and she is taking the name for the margin. One or two sentences. No contrast construction, and no softening clause after it.
8. **The car,** in the lot under the sign, engine running, the league's shapes moving in the glass block. She calls Hollis. "How thick is the ice off the public access?" Hollis: "Why?" Ellen: "I'm walking out to your bob-house Saturday." Her contraction is back now that she has decided.

**Subtext:** Denise is asking whether Ray heard the second half, and Ellen's silence answers yes. "Cushion" is Denise naming the wrong. "Separation" is Ellen dressing it in the word for the one thing she lost once. The reader holds both.

**Texture budget:** Two. The adding-machine tape and the projection (her ledger, the gap). The Open's shape: championship weekend, its own cup, the derby crowd. No figures in it, and no entry fee.

**Point of view:** Ellen. Her lines to Denise lose their contractions as she argues; the car line has one. Denise talks in paragraphs and Ellen answers in single lines (their double act), but Denise gets one short speech at most (rule 18).

**Ends on:** **Cut.** "I'm walking out to your bob-house Saturday." Last line. Nothing after it: no dial tone, no reaction.

**Word target:** 700
