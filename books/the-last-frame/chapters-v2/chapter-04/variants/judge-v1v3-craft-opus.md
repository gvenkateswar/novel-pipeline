# Judge report: Ch 04, v1 (A) vs v3 (B), Craft

**1. Winner: A**

**2. Why:** A has more warmth in it. Ray tells a broken machine "She was only sulking," and later keeps the radio on his knee and grins his way down the catwalk. Ellen goes the whole chapter without a contraction until "I'm very good with a clock," so when that line comes you hear her let go. B spends her first contraction on "Then I'll call Worcester" at about 10%, and the ending has less to give.

**3. Where A lost me:** "First. December of two thousand four, posted on the board and charged from the next league night, in Arlene's hand." (~84%). The First/Second/Third reads like a clerk reading out the file, the same counting trick that sagged in the anchors. I came back at "Who put it there?" / "I did." Smaller drag at "Bank in Concord'll lend you what the appraiser says the alley's worth…" (~41%), which is a letter read aloud. The motor simile also shows up twice (her pulse "the way a motor runs fast before it's warmed through," then "a motor the second before it catches"), and I noticed it the second time.

**4. Where B lost me:** "December of two thousand four," she said. "First, she posted it on that board and charged it from the next league night, in her own hand." (~83%). It's the same procedure, and here it runs on into a longer bit with the ledger (the book falling open at December, a finger running down Arlene's column). That's clever, but it's competence on display while the scene's real business waits. Earlier, "Then I'll call Worcester." (~10%) doesn't lose me, but it flattens her voice. And "The click cut her off." (~58%) comes after Denise has already finished her sentence, so nothing got cut.

**5. Confidence: medium.** B has the single best moment in either chapter: "Her watch was under his palm, where she couldn't have read it, and she didn't move." It sets up "I'm very good with a clock," and so do the mic hiss before "Unable" and "the corner of her mouth didn't hold at all." Taken scene by scene, though, A gives me more I want to reread. The pool sheet has "a hand Ray had taught to hold a pencil." The wrist lesson has Danny's orange-soda wrist. The radio scene has him learning her call sign by ear and the cutoff that actually cuts Denise off mid-sentence. And A keeps Ellen's voice whole until the last page.

**6. Same-author check:** Yes. These are two passes over the same draft, with the same beats, the same lines of dialogue and the same last image.
