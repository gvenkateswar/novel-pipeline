# Judge report: Ch 04, v0 (A) vs v1 (B), Buyer

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** In B he drives past her kitchen light before dawn, she has already written his name on her schedule before asking him, the whole town is betting on Valentine's, and it ends with the two of them shut in a tiny office and her hooking a chair out for him with her boot. A sends him off alone to talk to a bank on the phone and then sit in the pit by himself, and it ends outdoors over paperwork, with me no surer what she feels than he is.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 38%, the phone call to the bank: "Appraised value, Mr. Gagnon, and not a dollar over. The commitment's good through the thirty-first." From there it's a loan officer explaining extension fees, and she isn't in the room. I came back for the radio banter, then drifted again at about 70% with him alone in the pit thinking about clocks: "He had always kept time by what broke: the winter the K went, the winter nine's belt went the first time, the two pinsetters after Linda." The drive scene at the end turns into a contract lecture at about 85%: "Every September Arlene had signed a season agreement with the league, and this one had come to her at closing with the ledgers and the keys, and paragraph four held the rate where it stood until the January meeting voted." That's the couple doing business, the thing the author called boring in A1.

4. **Where B lost me:** Only for a moment, at about 85%, when she numbers off her case: "First. December of two thousand four, posted on the board and charged from the next league night, in Arlene's hand." The First/Second/Third thing reads like a memo, and it's the same trick the judges flagged in A3. It didn't lose me for long. Two lines later she asks "Who put it there?" and he says "I did," with his hand on the ledger, and I was back in. The bank business in B is one paragraph read out by Danny, and it gets me the betting sheet and Normie reading the envelope, so I never felt I was sitting through procedure.

5. **Confidence: high.** Several lines are word for word the same in both ("I'm very good with a clock," the neon tube, "say intentions"), so the difference is all in the scenes around them. B puts the couple in a room every time it can and makes the room smaller each time. A keeps sending him off by himself.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. It's the same voice and the same dialogue, with the scenes arranged differently.
