1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A is the one where the two of them sound most like themselves. Ray plays with the radio ("Wilco, then") and Ellen corrects him with a dictionary ("Wilco means you will comply. I have not asked you for anything yet"). The Bench gets a real laugh ("somebody was pouring coffee with great care"). B keeps telling me what Ray feels ("grinning at the pinsetters like a fool," "Ray thought of her printing on the bottom line of the clipboard") where A just lets him whistle on the catwalk where nobody can hear him.

3. **Where A lost me:** at about 83%, in the office: "'Three things,' Ellen said. 'First. December of two thousand four. Posted on the vestibule board and charged from the next league night, in Arlene's hand.'" She announces a numbered list, and for a paragraph the scene turns into a hearing. It comes back with "Who put it there?" / "I did." A smaller wobble at about 96%: the second motor simile ("the way a good motor sounds under load, every part doing its share and not one of them complaining") goes on long enough that I noticed the writer reaching for it.

4. **Where B lost me:** at about 84%, in the same place: "'First. December of two thousand four, posted on the board and charged from the next league night, in Arlene's hand. That is where I took the form.'" It's the same recital, slightly softened because she tips her head at the ledger. Before that, at about 55%, the radio section started to repeat itself: "a lane and a trouble and nothing extra," and then a page later "with a lane and a trouble and nothing else." That's the same phrase twice, and it read like a tic. At about 38%, "Ray thought of her printing on the bottom line of the clipboard, his name in it before she'd asked" explains a link I had already made for myself.

   In fairness to B: its office scene is physically closer and warmer than A's. I liked the purple ink on her fingers, the check that "gave a small crackle when he breathed in," and "the small change you hear in a motor the second before it catches," which is the better motor line of the two. If the chapter were only that scene, B might win it. Across the whole chapter, A's dialogue does more, and its humour comes from the people rather than from narration about them.

5. **Confidence: medium.** These are near-twins with the same beats and many of the same lines, and the difference is texture. A has more voice and B has more heat in the last scene. I'd bet on A, but not a lot.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** The scenes run in the same order, and many lines and images are identical (the K, "Nobody you can take it up with," "Unable," the chair hooked out with her boot). These are two drafts of one chapter.
