# Judge report: Ch 04, v1 (A) vs v0 (B)

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** In A the two of them keep finding ways to be in each other's air (the ten lanes lit for nobody, her pulse under his fingers, "Which item was that, Counter?", the door shut and a foot between them, the chair hooked out with her boot), and B keeps leaving the room to make a phone call to a bank or explain a foreclosure in a parking lot.

3. **Where A lost me:** ~83%, the office scene, when she starts numbering: "First. December of two thousand four, posted on the board and charged from the next league night, in Arlene's hand. That is where I took the form." ... "Second. Paragraph four says lineage stands as set in September..." ... "Third. I asked Verna for the bylaws the day after I closed." That is the same "First... Third" device the calibration file names as a weak spot in A3, and the bylaws-binder procedure that follows ("the binder does not leave the secretary") is the couple doing business. It wins me back fast with "Who put it there?" / "I did." and keeps me through the chair. A smaller dip at ~4%, the empty-building lane check ("throwing one down every lane so each machine swept and set ten fresh... Three came round half a second late"), which is a man alone with machines, but it pays off in the image of her stopping in the door to listen.

4. **Where B lost me:** ~37%, the bank call: "Appraised value, Mr. Gagnon, and not a dollar over. The commitment's good through the thirty-first." / "And after the thirty-first?" / "It lapses..." / "There's an extension fee. You pay it whether you close or not." A loan officer I will never see again, walking me through terms, with Ellen nowhere in it. It came back with Danny's line, but it went soft again at ~85% on the foreclosure explainer at the truck ("that contract couldn't close, because there'd be nobody on the other end of it with money to close it. So Arlene would have to take the place back the long way—") and "Read paragraph four, Mrs. Daly," which is Ray reciting a contract term at her, the exact move the calibration log says lost every judge in the old Ch 02. The opening paragraph also lost a little before it started: the key, Linda's card, the Memorial, Hollis's plow, "same as Friday," all catching me up on things before anything happens.

5. **Confidence: medium.** B has the better single passages (the four minutes she sits in the car, "her coffee smelled better than anything the Bench had ever poured him," the kept-time-by-what-broke paragraph, her voice twice from the radio and the PA), and its last scene under the red sign is prettier. But A's dialogue is sharper line for line, the radio exchange in A actually flirts where B's is a numbers gag, and A ends on an invitation where B ends on Ray unable to read her face. Not a coin flip; not a bet I'd make large.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Same Ellen syntax down to the shared lines ("I hire out what I am not qualified for," "I'm very good with a clock"), same motor-and-machine similes for her; one writer, two drafts.
