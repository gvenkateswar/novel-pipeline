# Judge report: Chapter 4, A vs B (Buyer, Opus)

**1. Winner:** A

**2. Why:** After that kiss I wanted to feel them touching and not touching, and A gave me the moment I'd read the chapter for: his palm over her watch, so she couldn't have seen the time she told him. She'd been counting. B is funnier on the radio and ends with him reaching for the door, which I liked, but it never gave me anything as good as that.

**3. Where A lost me:** "December of two thousand four," she said. "First, she posted it on that board and charged it from the next league night, in her own hand." (~80%). Bylaws, paragraph four, the binder with Verna. My eyes slid until "Who put it there?" / "I did." pulled me back, and then her waiting with her hand on the ledger until he moved his palm had me again. A small slow spot at the very start too: the long first sentence of road directions before I knew whose kitchen light it was (~1%). I kept going.

**4. Where B lost me:** "Three things," Ellen said. "First. December of two thousand four. Posted on the vestibule board and charged from the next league night, in Arlene's hand." (~82%). It's the same spot as A's, but chopped into pieces like a list, and I skimmed it harder. B also flattened the wrist scene for me: "He held it a beat longer than the lesson needed, and then another" tells me what A showed me (~20%). The "Wilco means you will comply. I have not asked you for anything yet" exchange and his whistling down the catwalk were the best things in B, and they almost won it.

**5. Confidence:** medium. They are close. B has the better first line, the better banter and the better last beat (the doorknob, then the chair). A has the better touching, and as this reader the touching is the reason I bought the book.

**6. Same-author check:** Yes. It's the same scenes and many of the same lines word for word, as if one writer drafted it twice.
