A = variants/v1.md, B = variants/v2.md. Persona: Buyer. Model: Sonnet. Read with docs/calibration.md.

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A gives me more of the two of them in the room feeling something (his hand on her wrist and the memory of Danny's nine-year-old wrist, the shut office door with "no farther to go," the purple ink on her fingers, the "small change... a motor the second before it catches"), where B is a hair funnier but keeps a little more air between them.

3. **Where A lost me:** the bylaws recital in the office, about 83%: "First. December of two thousand four, posted on the board and charged from the next league night, in Arlene's hand. That is where I took the form... Third. I asked Verna for the bylaws the day after I closed." That is two people doing business, the A1 problem, though it is short and ends on the line that rescues it ("Who put it there?" / "I did."). I also drifted briefly in the Concord loan-letter beat in the store, about 38%: "Bank in Concord'll lend you what the appraiser says the alley's worth... They'll hold it to March first for a fee." Ray is alone with his son, not with her, and it is mostly terms.

4. **Where B lost me:** the same bylaws speech, about 84%: "Three things," Ellen said. "First. December of two thousand four. Posted on the vestibule board..." The numbered "Three things" makes it read even more like a memo than A's version does. I also slowed at the loan-letter beat, about 37%: "Keeping it to March first costs you a fee, and you pay it whether you buy or not." Ray is alone with his son there too. B also lets the two of them separate a little early in the office: "He took his hand off the ledger. Nothing on the shelves was out of line now" is lovely, but then the glasses-on-a-cord paragraph is the narrator looking at her rather than the two of them in it.

5. **Confidence: low.** These are the same chapter with the same beats and mostly the same lines. I'd call it close to a coin flip, leaning A on heat and the Danny memory. B has the better laughs ("Wilco means you will comply. I have not asked you for anything yet." and "You did not bill lane one." / "Lane one was before ten."), and a reader who prizes banter over ache could flip this.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same Ray and Ellen cadence, same motor-sound similes, same radio bit and chair ending, so they read as two takes by one hand.
