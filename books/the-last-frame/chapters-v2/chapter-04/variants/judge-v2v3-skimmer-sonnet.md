1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** Both chapters spend their first and last minutes on a man alone with machines and a woman reading him a contract, but A keeps turning the paperwork into hands and bodies (her watch under his palm where she can't read it, her finger going down Arlene's column to the night the rate changed), so my thumb slowed down where B's kept moving.

3. **Where A lost me:** Twice, and neither stop was long. First, the opening lane check, around 8–10%: "Five and six came round together like two old sisters on the church steps. Eight was a half beat late setting, and had been since the winter the cellar pump quit." The sisters line got me back, but I was skimming the machine inventory before it. Second, the lineage notice, around 78%: "First, she posted it on that board and charged it from the next league night… Second, paragraph four says lineage stands as set in September… Third, I asked Verna for the bylaws the day after I closed." That's a contract read aloud. I went fast until "Who put it there?" / "I did." Nothing else in A is a ledger I skipped, and the Highway crew and Legion lines are short enough to read.

4. **Where B lost me:** Also twice, and the second one was worse. First, around 8%: "Two and three set clean. Six took her time at the top, a half-beat slow… and he wrote a six on the back of his hand with his pencil." This is the same machine inventory, but here it reads like a checklist. Second, around 78%: "Three things," Ellen said. "First. December of two thousand four. Posted on the vestibule board and charged from the next league night, in Arlene's hand… Second. Paragraph four says… Third." The announced "Three things" with "First." "Second." as separate fragments looks like a list on the page, and my thumb went straight through it. B also has more business back to back in the office (invoice, check, "You did not bill lane one," tape, ledger, bank, notice) before anything physical happens between them. That's the A1 shape: the couple doing business. The doorknob line at the end ("He reached behind him for the doorknob") is a small letdown before the chair, and A's ending is cleaner.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two chapters are the same skeleton with nearly the same lines. A is slightly better at the two things a tired reader notices: it keeps the contract beat from looking like a list, and it puts one real touch in the office scene. B has the funnier radio exchange ("Wilco means you will comply. I have not asked you for anything yet"), and I'd give it that.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes in the same order, same Normie/Danny/Denise bits, same motor-under-load closer, and Ellen's "That is a neon tube" voice.
