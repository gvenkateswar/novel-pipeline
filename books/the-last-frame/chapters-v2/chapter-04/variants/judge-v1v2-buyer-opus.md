# Judge report: Chapter 4, v2 (A) vs v1 (B)

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** Right after that first kiss I want to feel how close he is to her, and B keeps putting him a foot away with nowhere to go ("he was a foot from her, and there was no farther to go"; "the front of his jacket brushed her vest") while A keeps a stride of floor between them and spends its best warmth on the radio instead.

3. **Where A lost me:** the bylaws recital, about 84%: *"Three things," Ellen said. "First. December of two thousand four. Posted on the vestibule board and charged from the next league night, in Arlene's hand."* Once she says "Three things" and starts numbering, it turns into a meeting and my thumb moved. There was also a smaller dip at about 5% in the machine run-through (*"He threw all ten toggles under her tape at the counter, and went down the line rolling one house ball down every lane…"*). Before that, A had me: the radio banter is the funniest thing in either version ("Wilco means you will comply. I have not asked you for anything yet."), and the doorknob just before she hooks out the chair is a better button than B's. But A's last look at her is a simile I had to stop and work out (*"the way a good motor sounds under load, every part doing its share and not one of them complaining"*), and it cooled the moment instead of heating it.

4. **Where B lost me:** the same bylaws numbering, about 83% (*"First. December of two thousand four, posted on the board and charged from the next league night, in Arlene's hand."*), though without the "Three things" announcement it reads more like her talking. Two smaller bumps. At about 22%, in the middle of holding her wrist, he thinks of *"Danny's, nine years old and sticky with orange soda"*. It is sweet, but I didn't want his son in that moment. At about 68%, the zipper business (*"He pulled his jacket zipper down a hand's width, put the check in his shirt pocket… and zipped the jacket back to his chin"*) is fussy. The crackle of the check when he breathes, a little later, pays it off, so I forgave it.

5. **Confidence: low.** It is close to a coin flip. The scenes, the lines and the ending are nearly identical. A is tighter and funnier in the store and on the radio. B is warmer in the two scenes where they share a room, and for a reader like me those are the scenes that count. If the office scene in B had A's doorknob beat, it would not be close.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. They are the same chapter with most of the dialogue word for word; only the staging and the bits of physical business differ.
