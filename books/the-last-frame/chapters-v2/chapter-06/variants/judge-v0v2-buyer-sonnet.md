1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** B keeps putting the two of them in a room with something unsaid between them (her trying to pinch the page in his gloves, the money counted out loud so she can't ask her question), where A moves the hard part to men talking to each other.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 85% in, at the adding machine: "Danny punched it in and pulled the handle, and the machine went chunk and ratcheted and fed out tape, and he punched and pulled again." After that comes a paragraph of Ray working out the bank's number and what Linda's name brings in per night, and it read like the paperwork I'd skim. She isn't in that scene at all, and the last scene before the chapter ends is two men and a tape. The committee scene (about 35%) is fun, with the perch and the license check, but it's the town voting and she's just briefing them. Ray has no vote and it doesn't touch the two of them. The pad scene with Dale at about 55% is good, and Denise's "finish your shoes" is the best laugh in either version.

4. **Where B lost me:** Only a little, at about 83%, in the credit union scene: "That's your down payment on the alley," Danny said. "It was." / "If she takes it, you can't buy it March first." That is the son reading the plot out loud to the reader, and "buying in, or getting out of the way? Because those are different invoices" is a bit neat. I kept reading because of the check lying face down and the pen on its chain, which I could see. The money counted onto the counter in the alley scene stalls a hair at "Halsey Falls is forty minutes on the winter road," but Ellen is standing right there and I kept going.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The bob-house scene is nearly the same in both, and both have a lovely glove moment. A's Denise beat and its fish-on-two-tip-ups beat are real points for it. B gets the nod for the store counter scene ("One. Two. Three..."), where he counts so she can't say her piece, and for a closing scene that doesn't read like A1's business procedure.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The same Gagnon & Son envelope, the same hands and gloves, the same small town talk, the same "I know what I put in it" line from Ray.
