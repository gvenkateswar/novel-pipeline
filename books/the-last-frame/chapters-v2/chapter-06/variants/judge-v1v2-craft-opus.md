1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** B acts out what A narrates. Ray counts the bills out loud one by one at her, Normie gets a real sportscaster's riff, and Ellen answers back once ("I know what is in the minutes"). A keeps telling me how long the silences lasted, and with every one of them the scene goes slack.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Ray let the quiet go on past where a man would say something to be polite, and then past that." (~23%). It's a sentence about a pause that does nothing but describe the pause, and A does it again four paragraphs later with "he let the quiet run as long as it ran, and she let it." By the store, at "Neither of them said anything for a while. Ray's thumb found the seam of a glove in his pocket." (~50%), I was reading for the next line of dialogue and skipping the silences. The counting is only summarized ("counted them into his palm out loud, one, two, three, steady"), so the cruelest beat in the chapter happens offstage, and "It was a good notice." is a shrug where a feeling should be.

4. **Where B lost me:** it mostly didn't. The one flat spot is "Ray always saw when she sat, and he knew she had settled something on the walk out." (~18%). A handles that same moment better ("Ray, who had watched her stand through a storm and a vote, knew it was settled"). The credit-union exchange with Danny (~88%) is a back-and-forth about money in both versions, but B puts a clock on it with the teller bolting the door at five, and I stayed with it.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two versions share the same bones and many of the same lines, and A has a few things B lacks: "it walked the way it stood at a counter," "He had never held the alley in one hand before," the receipt "in both their hands." But B wins on rhythm and on voices that sound different from each other: "the way he'd seat a part he meant to keep," "She had spelled it right," "Danny said it to the deposit slips," Normie's "first time in the history of the franchise," and "along the stools nobody ate."

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scene order, same dialogue, often the same sentences; one is a reworked draft of the other.
