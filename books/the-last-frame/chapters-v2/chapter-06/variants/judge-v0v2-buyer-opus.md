A = v2.md, B = v0.md. Judge: Buyer persona, Opus. Read in order A then B; also read docs/calibration.md.

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B he holds her frozen hand between both of his before the glove goes on, "which he hadn't meant to do," and then she catches a perch and I'm smiling. In A he puts his gloves on her so she can't turn her own page, then counts bills out loud to shut her up, and I liked him less every scene.

3. **Where A lost me:** "He carried it to the stand-up counter with the deposit slips and the ballpoint on its bead chain, and laid it face down." (~85%). She's gone, it's a bank lobby, and I'm reading about the pen chain and the teller's printing coming through backwards. Earlier I had a wobble at "'Without Linda's name, you mean.' / 'The Open needs a final.'" (~22%), where I had to read it twice to understand what they were fighting about. And at "'One,' Ray said, and laid a bill across his palm. 'Two.'" (~56%) I thought he was being mean to her. I kept reading, but I no longer wanted him to get her.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Ray didn't know how far under she was. She'd given him the bank's number and the seven hundred a week, and told him the old rate wouldn't make hers..." (~88%). It's a man and his son with an adding machine and a bank statement, and I skimmed the arithmetic to get to Danny's line at the door. Before that, "She briefed from page one, standing" (~44%) started to sound like a meeting, but the flags went up a paragraph later and saved it.

5. **Confidence: medium.** A keeps the two of them in a room together more often, and the receipt handed across the counter is a good beat. But B's glove scene is the one I'd go back to, and B is funny where A isn't (the widow's roof, Denise's run-on, "something that isn't on a shelf"). Both end on the money, and both go slack there.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. They have the same bones, the same lines and the same unwritten envelope. A just reads like the same writer in a worse mood.
