A = variants/v1.md, B = variants/v3.md. Persona: Craft. Model: Sonnet.

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A sounds like people and a place (the K of Kettle with the tack through it, "somebody else's letters" on his own wall, the glove "still warm from his hand"), and B keeps reaching for the tidy line and tagging every speech, so the two leads start to sound like they're taking turns.

3. **Where A lost me:** The bylaws trade at about 40%: "The house sets the lanes and the hours of championship weekend," ... "I am inside the bylaws." It's procedure, and the couple is doing business across a cooler. It's short, and the glove business on either side keeps it alive, so I only slowed down. Second, about 68%, when Dale's logistics pile up: "Halsey Falls is forty minutes on the winter road." I skimmed that one, though the pen and the cash pull me back. One smaller thing at about 27%: "Ray let the quiet go on past where a man would say something to be polite, and then past that." The text is explaining its own silence there.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 30%, the echo run, where Ellen repeats his points back as short replies: "I was in the room." / "I rang it." / "You did." / "He said it across my counter." Each answer is clever, but they come in the same shape four times and she starts to sound like a clever machine. Right before it, at about 22%, the stray paragraph break: "Without the Open, I do not make my number," Ellen said. That sentence is orphaned from the speech it belongs to. At about 68% the alley scene drums "Dale said / Ray said" on nearly every line, and I felt the thumb speed up. The line "She had never done any of it before. She did it right." at about 10% is the book telling me she's competent, where A has her easing her left knee and I believe it without being told.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The chapters share nearly all their beats and nearly all their dialogue, so this is a close pair. B has two things A lacks that I liked: "took longer over the back than there was on it to read," and the ending "It fit in the envelope with room to spare." A's ending, "He had never held the alley in one hand before," is a bit editorial but lands. A edges it on texture and on fewer dialogue tags.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The scenes, the exchanges and the final images are almost the same, and only the sentence-level handling differs.
