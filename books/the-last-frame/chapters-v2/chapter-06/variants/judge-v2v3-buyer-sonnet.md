1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** The bob-house glove scene is the reason I'd keep reading, and A lets it run slower and closer (he works the glove down "the first one to the web and pressed it home with his thumb. Then the second," and so on), so I feel the two of them in the room while he says "I put my name next to yours." B has the same beats but trims them, and in the last scene A tells me what the money cost him ("thirty years... one deposit at a time") where B only hints at it.

3. **Where A lost me:** The Tuesday scene at the alley counter, about 62% in: "'Go before the half and you forfeit,' Ray said. / 'Half's Saturday,' Dale said. 'We go the Tuesday after.' / 'Halsey Falls is forty minutes on the winter road,' Ray said." That's men trading schedules and penalties, and my thumb sped up. It recovers when Ray lays the pen down and pays in cash. I also tripped on "He had asked her once," just after "I said it." I couldn't tell whether it meant he'd asked her in the pit or earlier, and I had to stop and puzzle over it.

4. **Where B lost me:** The same Tuesday exchange, about 60%: "'Half's Saturday. We go the Tuesday after.' / 'Halsey Falls is forty minutes on the winter road.'" B runs it as bare back-and-forth with no speaker tags, so it reads like A1-style procedure, closer to a transcript. A second place is Normie's entrance, about 52%: "the owner's in the store ahead of the Bench, boys, first time in the history of the franchise, and the veteran's counting money the boy counted Saturday." I had to work out who "the veteran" and "the boy" were, and the joke died on me. Smaller drag at about 90%: the teller bolting the door "at five" is one more beat sitting on the ending.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two chapters are nearly the same chapter. I'd lean A about 60/40, because the glove scene is warmer in A and the ending is clearer.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes, same recurring objects (the gloves, the chair, the count aloud, the bead-chain pen), and the same dry, clipped way of talking.
