1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** In the bob-house, A lets her sit there and take it while he works the glove down each finger, and the couple stays in the room and in the quiet. B has her answering every accusation back ("I rang it." "You did." "He said it across my counter."), so it reads more like two people trading lines.

3. **Where A lost me:** It didn't really lose me. I slowed once at the legal talk in the bob-house, "The house sets the lanes and the hours of championship weekend... I am inside the bylaws" (about 40%), which reads like a meeting, but the glove on her hands keeps it alive. I also leaned on the credit union ending a little: "Kettle Lanes was a quarter mile out Kettle Road from where he stood" (about 96%) is a bit of map-reading. The last line, "He had never held the alley in one hand before," pays it back.

4. **Where B lost me:** At the register scene, "One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine." / "Ten, eleven, twelve." (about 58%). He counts the money out loud on the page and I skimmed it. A does the same beat in a line. B also tags nearly every line with "Ellen said" or "Ray said," in the bob-house and again at the credit union ("Danny said," "Ray said"). By the second scene that stiffness is what I'm hearing. The pairs "Without the Open, I do not make my number" / "Without Linda's name, you mean" are split into single-line paragraphs, and that reads a bit like a script.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two chapters share almost every beat and many exact lines, so this is close. I'm picking A for its physical detail (the wet gloves, the smelt smell, "a size and a half too long," "He swallowed") and for the tighter pacing. Neither text reads like calibration anchor A1. Neither is full of codes or clipped procedure, and both keep the couple in a room.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The beats, the bead-chain pen, the cotter-pin coffee can and the Worcester page receipt are the same. Only the texture and the dialogue tagging differ.
