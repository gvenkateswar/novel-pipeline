# Judge report: Chapter 6, v2 vs v3 (Craft)

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** It's the same chapter, but A trusts the reader. It lets the gloves do the talking ("the thumb of his glove ran an inch past hers and folded over at the end"), and Normie sounds like a man doing play-by-play. B tags every line "Ellen said" / "Ray said" until the bob-house sounds like a deposition, and it explains things A only shows ("She had never done any of it before. She did it right.").

3. **Where A lost me:** ~95%, at the credit union: "Beside it lay the envelope with the store's name in the corner in the red ink his father had picked, and beside that the pen on its chain, chewed at the end by somebody waiting." It's a list of things on a counter, and my eye sped up until "It fit in an envelope." pulled me back. There's a smaller stumble at ~25%, "Ray stopped talking.", when he hadn't really been talking. Neither one made me want to stop.

4. **Where B lost me:** ~32%, in the bob-house, after the plan has already been laid out once: "'Linda's Memorial is the final,' Ellen said. 'It is bowled on the last Saturday in February, on all ten lanes, for her trophy. All of that stays.'" / "'With anybody off the ice who's got the entry,' Ray said." / "'With anyone who pays,' said Ellen." That's the second time I heard the Open explained, and it comes back again to Hollis, on the flyer and in Normie's mouth. With a tag on every line, the glove scene turns into a meeting. In the store, "That's a first in the books" is a flat line where Normie should have a voice.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two share most of their scenes and many lines word for word. A wins on rhythm, fewer tags and a sharper Normie, not on anything structural. I'd bet on it, but not much.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** It's the same draft and the same lines. B reads like A after an editor added a tag to every line and a gloss to every gesture.
