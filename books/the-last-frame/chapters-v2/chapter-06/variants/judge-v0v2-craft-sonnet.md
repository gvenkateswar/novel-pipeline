1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** B keeps finding a physical thing for the people to do instead of explaining them. Ellen can't pinch the page in Ray's too-big gloves, Ray counts the drawer out loud so he doesn't have to hear her, and Ray answers Dale by paying cash, so the feelings sit in objects and the scenes stay between two people in a room.

3. **Where A lost me:** The adding-machine scene in the back room, about 80%: "Danny punched it in and pulled the handle, and the machine went chunk and ratcheted and fed out tape, and he punched and pulled again." This is the couple doing business, and it drifts toward the A1 smell: a ledger scene, a man and a machine, the answer delivered as a bottom line. The paragraph after it explains what the tape means ("She could give the league its Saturday back and still come out ahead"), so I never had to infer anything. The same explaining shows up at about 65% in the Dale scene: "To set the minutes straight he'd have had to stand up in front of Dale's crew and tell them he'd vouched for half a sentence because he hadn't heard the other half." That is the narrator doing the reader's work. Danny's closer, "You're allowed to want something that isn't on a shelf," is a line a screenwriter would be proud of, and I felt that. The opening was strong. The glove scene is the best thing in A and as good as anything in B. The committee scene in the middle (license check, perch, show of hands) is lively but mostly Hollis business, and I eased off a little there.

4. **Where B lost me:** Only briefly, around 82%, in the credit union logistics: "He carried it to the stand-up counter with the deposit slips and the ballpoint on its bead chain, and laid it face down. He had brought a store envelope in his jacket that morning. Danny made the deposit and came and stood beside him, folding the empty bag, and his elbow caught the pen and set the chain rattling along the edge." That is a lot of stage furniture before Danny speaks. It pays off, because the chained pen swings back at the end. "Hissed" is also used five or six times in the first scene, and I noticed it. Neither slowed me down enough to stop.

5. **Confidence: medium-high.** The two chapters are close. The bob-house scene is about equal in both, and A's glove business is slightly more tactile. B wins on scene variety, on the money-counting and receipt beats (the "Your receipt, Mr. Gagnon" moment is the best exchange in either draft), and on Danny's line "Is this you buying in, or getting out of the way? Because those are different invoices." That line states the theme as an invoice joke, and it does that better than A's "isn't on a shelf." A also has a small slip: it says Ellen "had said five words next to him" when the sentence she quotes is seven.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Both have the same dry New England rhythm, the same repeated-question gag, the glove ritual, "I asked the store once," and the Dale pad with the empty top line. They are two passes at one outline.
