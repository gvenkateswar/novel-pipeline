1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** The glove scene is the one place the two of them are really in a room together, and A lets it do more: his accusations are tied to what she actually did ("Dale laid his lineage on your counter. You rang it."), he fits the gloves on while he says them, and the slipping leather, the creased page and the "I never heard that" land harder because there is less explaining around them.

3. **Where A lost me:** "She sat down on the upturned pail by the cooler before she had said a word to him. Ray always saw when she sat, and he knew she had settled something on the walk out." (~22%.) It's a narrator telling me what to feel, and it's a little clunky. I didn't skim. The Dale and Legion scene at the counter (~60%) got slow, with the pad, the forfeit and the Pine Ridge business. The cash and receipt beat saved it.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Ray let the quiet go on past where a man would say something to be polite, and then past that." (~25%.) It explains the silence instead of letting me sit in it. Then, at the notice (~48%): "Linda's name was there in somebody else's letters... It was a good notice." The last line is flat and sounds like a reviewer. In the same stretch B explains the gloves ("a good pair kept two winters by the look of the palms and ruined in three hundred yards") where A just says "They had been good gloves an hour ago."

5. **Confidence: medium.** They are nearly the same chapter beat for beat, and a reader of either would stay. A has the better small details ("Somebody had told her how, once." "She had spelled it right." "It fit in an envelope."). B's "He had never held the alley in one hand before" is good, but B spends more words on stage business. I'd put money on A, but not much.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes and same recurring props (the gloves, the chair, the bead chain, the Worcester page, the counting aloud), and the same dry flat voice, like one chapter drafted twice.
