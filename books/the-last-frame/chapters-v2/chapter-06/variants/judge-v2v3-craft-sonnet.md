1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** B sounds more like a person looking at a particular room, with details only this scene could have ("the way he'd seat a part he meant to keep," the glove thumb running an inch past hers and folding over, the pen "chewed at the end by somebody waiting"), while A has nearly the same scene but spends more of its sentences on "Ellen said / Ray said" and on explaining what the reader just saw.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 12%, "The flag got folded down and set. She had never done any of it before. She did it right." The paragraph had already shown me she knew how to do it, and this told me again, flatly, like a caption. A second, smaller loss at about 42%: "He had asked her once. Ray looked at his own hands on his knees." I couldn't tell whether that was a memory, a stage direction, or the narrator commenting, and it sat right after her big line, "I said it," where I wanted to stay in the room. I also felt the tag pile-up through the middle ("Ellen said," "Ray said," "she said," "Ellen said") flatten the rhythm of the glove-and-contract section. That is where the thumb started moving faster.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 58%, Normie's entrance: "the veteran's counting money the boy counted Saturday." It tries to carry too much (franchise, veteran, the boy, Saturday) and sounds written, not shouted by a man coming in out of the snow. A's version of the same beat ("The owner beats the Bench to the coffee. That's a first in the books") is cleaner. That's the only place B made me aware of an author.

5. **Confidence: medium.** Very close. A has the best single exchange in either text ("I put my name next to yours." / "You did." / "Dale said that'll do for my crew." / "He said it across my counter."), where Ellen's clipped formality and Ray's insistence sound like two different people, and I'd keep that line-for-line if I could. A also echoes her "It stays" in "All of that stays," which is a smart plant. But B has more texture and less narrator, and the gloves, the bank counter and the pen read as lived-in. Against the calibration anchors, neither one reads like A1 (procedure and codes with the couple doing business). Both have some "doing business" in the Monday-morning counting and the lineage cash, but both put a body, a chair or a glove between the two of them.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. A is clearly a revision of B: same beats, same dialogue skeleton, same images, with A adding attribution and explanation.
