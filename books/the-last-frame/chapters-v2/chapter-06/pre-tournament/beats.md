## Flags (where the log and the outline disagree, and how each is resolved here)

1. **Ice thickness: the bible's sample line is unsafe.** "Inch and a half of black ice under four of white" falls below NH Fish and Game's walking guidance, and Hollis would never call thin ice walkable. Here he gives one measurement on the safe side: **eleven inches of black ice at the public access, drilled Thursday. Walking ice, not truck ice.** It is new to the ledger. Domain checker: confirm it sits above NH F&G's minimum for foot travel and below what they'd want for a pickup.
2. **"A tip-up flag is down."** Read as every flag down: nothing biting at dawn. That gives Hollis his pretext to step outside "to check his flags" when there's nothing to check.
3. **How Ray ends up in the bob-house.** Rule 15 says somebody arranged it. Ellen's Ch 05 call told Hollis she was walking out on Saturday. Here she also told him she'd like Mr. Gagnon there, and Hollis asked Ray out for first light without saying why. She is telling Ray before the committee and before the town, somewhere he can't walk away from fast. She brief, she doesn't ask, and she makes sure he hears it first. That is decent of her, and it's also where she lies to him.
4. **Ray has the Ch 03 rule too: he asks once.** He asked her on Dec 17 that the Memorial stay. In the bob-house he argues and asks one straight question, and he doesn't ask a second (Lucien's 1974 rule, Ch 02). This is why he takes "I said it" and doesn't press.
5. **Ray lets Verna's minutes stand (new shading, author awareness).** After Jan 9 Ray believes she said the condition and he missed it, so he knows the minutes hold half a sentence. He doesn't correct them. Correcting them means telling Dale's crew that a man who can hear a clutch slip from the parking lot didn't hear a woman a foot away, and the minutes are the last thing holding Linda's Saturday. This mirrors Ellen's silence without matching it. It sets up Ch 07, where the captain quotes the minutes and Ray finally says "That's mine," and the outline's "he'll tell the whole league tomorrow." It isn't added to the condemnable-choices list. One or two sentences of interior in s3, no more.
6. **What Hollis's money line answers.** "Our people come in off the ice to bowl. Not to watch your league." Hollis says it to Ray's suggestion that the derby crowd could watch the league's Memorial free, as always. It does not get ahead of Ch 08's condition: that version gives the Open its own evening cup the derby's people can enter, so it meets Hollis's terms. Ray proposes no split here.
7. **The check's timing.** A cashier's check is drawn at a teller window during business hours. Ray drew it at noon on Feb 1 at the credit union, from his own savings. In s4 he has Danny run the tape on what he has already done: a question he already knows the answer to, which is his habit (bible). The outline's order (the tape, then the sealing late) holds.
8. **The ratio is Ch 07's money fact.** Danny's tape gives a number. The page says what it's for and nothing else: no ratio, no eight-to-one, no thirty thousand, no figure on the check.
9. **Denise (orchestrator requirement 4).** She is on the page in s3 and nearly says it. Her reason, in her terms: it's Ellen's to say, and Denise is giving her the chance, the way she'd want it for herself. And she isn't certain. If she's wrong she has made Ray a fool twice in front of the league. "Honey" is her word before something hard. She says it, and then doesn't say the hard thing. **"Honey" goes to 3 of 4, and Ch 07's line takes the last.**
10. **The Open notice, Jan 18.** One clause in s3: Ray saw it Monday on the board by the Bench's stools in his own store. Ellen brought it in herself, so he saw it first. No scene for it.
11. **Gloves (new plant).** s1: his gloves go on her hands. s2: she hands them back first thing, warm from inside her vest. **Pays off in Ch 08 s3:** "she isn't wearing gloves; he wants to give her his again and doesn't." Don't write the phrase "gloves off" anywhere in this chapter (motif 2 of 4 used). Write "pulled his gloves" or "took his gloves."
12. **Ellen's contractions.** They came back to Ray in Ch 05. In s1 they're gone again, which is her fear register, and Ray hears it once and blames the cold. Not elsewhere.

### Ellen's true sentence (for the writer; never stated on the page in Ray's POV)

**The true sentence:** *"Yes. I heard the strike take the second half, I saw you hadn't heard it, and I let it stand."*

**Why she doesn't use it, in her own terms:**
- **She briefs; she doesn't ask.** "I said it" is a report: a fact on the record, closed, with nothing in it for him to decide. The true sentence is an error report, and an error report hands the other person the decision. Here the decision is whether he keeps her. In thirty-one years she has never handed anyone that.
- **In her accounting the condition was never late.** As she told herself all December: "a condition was not his to hear until the vote had decided whether it came due" (Ch 05). It came due on Jan 5, and she is delivering it now, on schedule. His question is about something else: whether she *knew*. That isn't a condition. It's an error, the one error of her life she hasn't reported, because this one is priced in him.
- **His gloves are on her hands when he asks.** She answers with the exact true sentence that does the lying (bible rule 13).

**On the page (Ray's POV), only what he can see and hear.** She pauses. Her gloved hands close on the pages. She looks at his gloves and not at him. Her voice is level, with the short words still gone out of it. Ray hears machines, not lies. He takes the plain meaning: she said it, and he's the one who missed it. He doesn't catch it. The reader, who has known since Ch 03, does.

### Scene 1

**Setting:** Hollis's bob-house on Great Kettle Lake, about three hundred yards off the public access. Saturday, January 9, dawn, about ten to seven, in clear hard cold, with the sun not yet up. Six by eight, plywood on skids, a blaze-orange door with H. PIKE and his address stenciled on it, two holes in the floor skinning over, and a small window onto the tip-ups. **Sensory anchor:** the propane heater's hiss. The shack is warm at head height and freezing at the ankles, and Ray, at six foot one, has his head in the warm and his boots in the cold.

**Characters:** Ray, Hollis, Ellen.

**Want:**
- At the open, Ray wants a quiet Saturday and not to be asked the question Hollis asks him.
- When Ellen arrives, he wants her word to hold: the Memorial as Linda bowled it, last Saturday in February, all ten lanes. In the way is the plan she has printed on three pages.
- Then he wants one plain answer. The reader learns this want at the line "Did you know I didn't hear it?"
- Ellen wants to tell him first, before the committee and the town, and to get through it without making the report she has never made.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Open on the laugh, with no setup.** Every tip-up flag through the window is down. Hollis, pouring thermos coffee into the cap: "You courting her, or just fixing her machines a lot?" Ray answers with a story about some other fella (2 of 3): local, short, ending on a turn. Hollis asks the question again, the same words, the same tone. Ray doesn't answer the second time, because he's looking out the window.
2. A figure is walking out across the ice from the access: a navy vest over a parka. Ray reads her by wear, as he reads everyone: even weight on both feet like someone braced for a long shift, and bare hands, with the gloves in the vest pocket.
3. **The ice thickness, once.** Ray: who told her it'd hold? Hollis: "Eleven inches of black ice at the access. I drilled it Thursday. She asked was it black or white." (Writer may add Hollis's "That's walking ice. That's not truck ice.") Ray: "You knew she was coming." Hollis: she said seven, and said she'd like Ray here. Ray hears "like" in her mouth and knows it for an order.
4. She knocks on the orange door. Hollis gets up "to check his flags." Ray points out they're all down. Hollis goes anyway and shuts the door. (Plain and short: the laugh is spent.)
5. **She sits on the upturned bait bucket before she says a word.** Ray is the one who notices when she sits, and he knows it means something has been decided. (One sentence.)
6. **Her brief.** She takes her gloves and lays three printed pages on the cooler lid. No contractions; Ray hears that the short words have gone out of her again, and blames the cold. The plan, in items if the writer wants one list:
   - a public Open on derby weekend that takes the whole Saturday;
   - Linda's Memorial becomes its final, on all ten lanes, open to anyone, with Linda's trophy as the prize;
   - the league teams bowl in it with everyone else.
   - Optional, and it's her true-sentence method again: "Without the Open I do not make the bank's number." True. Ray hears "without Linda's name." The reader knows the unnamed Open would clear.
7. **Ray's anger register.** He stops talking. When he starts again it's short and formal: she gave him her word; he told the whole league so; it's in Verna's book; Dale's crew went home on it. "Mrs. Daly," once. He doesn't ask her to keep the Memorial. He asked once, on Dec 17, and he doesn't ask twice. He argues the word, not the want.
8. **The longing, inside the argument.** Her fingers have gone white on the pages, past the knuckles. He takes his gloves and puts them on her hands, one hand and then the other, working each finger home the way he'd seat a part, **without stopping his argument**. His sentences stay short and hard and his hands stay careful. She lets him, and she stops turning pages. **He never gets to page three.**
9. She, exact: "I said, 'It stays. Unless the numbers say otherwise.'" (Quoted exactly.)
10. **Ray's interior, the first long passage.** One clause of memory, not a paragraph: the pit behind five, Denise's strike, the whole house going up. He can hear a clutch slip from the parking lot, and he didn't hear a woman a foot from him. "I never heard that."
11. **The question, straight:** "Did you know I didn't hear it?"
12. The pause. The ice booms somewhere off across the lake (one sound, no more). Her gloved hands close on the pages. She looks at his gloves on her hands and not at him. In a level voice: **"I said it."**
13. **Ray takes it.** He hears a plain answer and takes its plain meaning: she said it, and the missing half is his. He asked once, and he doesn't ask a second question. He looks at his own bare hands (body only). No interior gloss on what he believes.
14. He gets up, stooped under the roof, and opens the orange door, and the cold comes in at the ankles. He goes out bare-handed, leaving his gloves on her, to Hollis, who is standing over a flag that's down.

**Rule for steps 7–13:** no joke, no fella, no motor comparison, no "she" for a machine. Play it in real time. From "You gave me your word" to "I said it" gets **at least 400 words** on the page. No summary, no cutaway.

**Subtext:** She came to tell him first, which is the decent part. She answers his one question with the condition, which was hers to deliver on schedule, and doesn't answer it with the error, which is hers alone. He puts his gloves on her because he can't stop himself looking after her hands even while she takes Linda's night. That is the book's whole problem in one gesture. He believes her because she's wearing them.

**Texture budget:** Two.
- The ice thickness, through Hollis. It shows her precision ("black or white?") and that Hollis arranged the morning.
- The tip-ups, flags all down. They give Hollis his pretext and give the scene its stillness.

**Point of view:** Ray. Anger register in the argument (short, formal, "Mrs. Daly," no *and*). Plain physical interior. Her lie is visible only from outside.

**Ends on:** **Consequence.** Ray, to Hollis, across the ice: "She's got business for your committee." Last line. Nothing after it, and no look back at the shack.

**Word target:** 1,050   **Word floor:** 900 (the turn; orchestrator requirement 2)

### Scene 2

**Setting:** The same bob-house, Saturday, January 16, about ten in the morning, clear. The derby committee meets where the chair is. Five people in six by eight. **Sensory anchor:** five people's breath fogging the one window, and Ray on the bait-bucket lid with his knees against the treasurer's.

**Characters:** Ray, Ellen, Hollis, the derby treasurer (unnamed walk-on; the bible's Ch 09 objector), one other committee member (unnamed, silent, votes).

**Want:**
- Ray wants the committee to say no, so that Linda's Saturday stays as it was without his having to ask her a second time. In the way is a plan with contingencies, which is the one thing Hollis respects. The reader learns the want at Ray's line about the derby crowd watching the Memorial.
- Ellen wants the derby's yes and its crowd.
- Hollis wants a derby weekend where nobody gets hurt and nobody blames him, so he wants the plan for when the ice goes.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. Ellen comes in. **The first thing she does**, before Hollis opens anything, is hand Ray his gloves, folded together, warm from inside her vest. One line from her, a handover in her paying-not-owing register ("Thank you for the loan" or similar). He puts them in his pocket and doesn't put them on. She's wearing her own.
2. Hollis: the committee will hear the owner. Ellen briefs from page one: the Open, the final, Linda's trophy. No figures, no entry fee.
3. Mid-pitch, two flags spring up through the window at once. **Ellen: "Stand by."** (the chapter's one ATC phrase). She is out the door before anyone moves, and Ray goes for the other flag.
4. On the ice, side by side at two holes, the line runs off the spool (motor comparison 1 of 2 available here). She brings hers up hand over hand, bare-handed. Ray: "Don't horse it." Ellen: "I am not horsing it." She lands it: a yellow perch or a pickerel, writer's choice, and a derby species. Ray's line goes slack and his fish is gone. (Ray loses, quietly. Not a joke beat.)
5. **The laugh, back inside.** Hollis, retired conservation officer, about anyone who touches a line on his ice: "Got a license?" Ellen: "Yes." Hollis waits. Ellen waits. Hollis: "Let's see it." She has it inside the vest, behind the terms. He reads both sides. Optional tail: she bought it online at ten past four in the morning. "Nobody buys a license at four in the morning." "I was up."
6. Hollis: "Plan if the ice goes?" Ellen: "Page two." He reads page two and writes in his waterproof notebook, the same one that has her bylaw in it from Jan 5.
7. **Ray's move, and the money fact.** Ray, to Hollis, speaking for the league as he always does: "Your people can come watch Linda's Memorial for nothing, same as every year." Hollis: **"Our people come in off the ice to bowl. Not to watch your league."** The writer may add one plain clause so a listener can repeat the fact: they'll pay to enter a final, not to watch one. That clause can come from the treasurer, in entries, without a figure.
8. The treasurer reads page three aloud: cash refunds if weather stops play. "Cash?" Ellen: "Cash." (Plant for Ch 09.)
9. Hollis calls the vote, and three hands go up. Ray has no vote. He watches his oldest friend's hand go up, and Hollis doesn't look at him.
10. **Cut into motion.** Before anyone has put a hand down, Ellen is out the door to Ray's empty tip-up, the one that lost his fish, re-baiting it bare-handed from the smelt bucket. Ray is up off the bucket with his gloves in his pocket and going out across the ice after her.

**Subtext:** He came hoping his best friend would do his arguing for him, and Hollis took her side because her plan has a page two. She returned his gloves so she wouldn't owe him, and then she goes out bare-handed to fix the line he lost.

**Texture budget:** Two.
- Tip-ups in action (the flag, the spool, hand over hand). Her competence at something new, and his loss.
- The printed terms, pages two and three. They show why Hollis votes yes, and they plant Ch 09.

The license check is the laugh, not texture.

**Point of view:** Ray. Ellen has no contractions with the committee. The committee's talk overlaps in short lines (rule 18), and nobody makes a speech.

**Ends on:** **Mid-action.** Ray crossing the ice toward her, gloves in his pocket, her hands bare in the cold. Cut while he's moving: no arrival, no line, no image.

### Scene 3

**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, Tuesday, January 19, about ten past six, before the Tuesday league bowls at half past. The lanes are lit, the settees are filling, and the Highway crew's burgers are on the flattop. **Sensory anchor:** the burgers' smoke drifting down the lanes from the snack bar. This is the crew's Tuesday supper at her counter, and its snack-bar margin is what walks with them in Ch 07.

**Characters:** Ray, Dale Hutchins (the captain), Denise, Ellen (at the counter, seen, not speaking to him).

**Want:**
- Ray wants to keep the league whole without telling anyone in public that he didn't hear her. In the way is Dale, who keeps his word and expects the same, and who has Verna's minutes on his side. The reader learns the want when Ray has the captain's pen in his hand, starts "She said—", and stops.
- Dale wants Ray's name at the top of the pad, because the rest follow Ray.
- Denise wants to say it, and wants more for Ellen to say it.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **One orienting clause, no recap.** The Open's notice went up Monday on the board by the Bench's stools in Ray's own store, and Ellen brought it in herself so he'd see it first. By tonight the league has it.
2. Ray is on six's settee lacing his shoes. Down at the counter Ellen rings the crew's burgers. He knows where she is without looking, and then he looks.
3. Dale comes down the settees with a legal pad. At the half, the Highway Department and the Legion go to **Pine Ridge Lanes in Halsey Falls, Gil Arsenault's house**, which has lanes open Tuesday nights. **Teams can leave without forfeit only at the half, and the half ends Saturday the thirtieth.** Either man may say the rule; Ray knows it cold. Dale wants Ray's name first.
4. "What broke," once, from stock: Ray dates the Legion by the winter they broke the ball return on four.
5. Dale holds out the pen. Ray takes it and starts: "She said—". He stops. Instead: "She's inside the bylaws, Dale." Dale: "You put her word in Verna's book." Ray: "I know what I put in it." He hands the pen back with the pad unsigned.
6. **Ray's interior, the second long passage (Flag 5), one or two sentences.** To correct the minutes he'd have to stand up in front of Dale's crew and say a man who can hear a clutch slip from the lot didn't hear a woman a foot from him. And the minutes are the last thing still holding Linda's Saturday. Let the reader see that he's doing what she did. The narration doesn't say so.
7. Dale goes back up the settees, and the pad goes from hand to hand along the Tuesday teams. Ray watches it go. At the counter Ellen watches it too, and doesn't come down. (She doesn't ask.)
8. **Denise comes down** with six's score sheets and the cigar box of pencils, which is her job on a Tuesday. She sets them on the scorer's table. "Ray. Honey." She looks past him at the counter, at Ellen. Optional: she gets as far as "That night in the pit—". Then she swerves to something else that is entirely Denise: an average, the fryer, his shoes. One model: "You were some help to her that night. That's all. Change your shoes, you're holding up the pair."
9. Ray hears "honey" with nothing hard after it, and doesn't ask. He doesn't ask questions he doesn't know the answer to.

### Scene 4

**Setting:** Gagnon & Son, the back room, Monday, February 1, about half past four, with dark coming down Main Street. Then the same room late, with only the desk lamp on and the store shut. **Sensory anchor:** the printing adding machine's chunk and ratchet, and the tape curling off it.

**Characters:** Ray, Danny.

**Want:**
- Ray wants to hear out loud what he has already done. He asks a question he knows the answer to, as he always does, and it isn't really a question. Tomorrow is the first night of the second half, and Dale's pad is full.
- Danny wants his father to want something for himself, and to stop pretending a check is arithmetic. The reader learns Ray's want when the check comes out of his jacket after the tape has run.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **The three weeks, in one line at most:** he has sat in her second chair after close most nights since the ninth, and neither of them has once said Linda's name. (Or nothing. Not both a line and a summary.)
2. Ray lays his own credit-union savings statement on the desk and asks Danny to run it against her bank's number, the way a bank figures it. Ray rounds and Danny doesn't. The adding machine runs (motor comparison 2 of 2 available here).
3. **The tape gives a number. The page doesn't say what it is.** It says what it's for, in Ray's interior, the third long passage. Ray doesn't know her figures. He does know from thirty years of Memorials what Linda's name can bring this town in a night. The number on the tape is what his money would take off her bank's number, and it's more than the name could bring her: enough that she could give the league its Saturday back and still have room. No ratio, no figure, no "thirty thousand."
4. Danny slides the store's long checkbook across, the one with the stubs: the store has a line at the credit union. Ray puts it back in the drawer and shuts it without hesitating, unlike Dec 19. "I asked the store once." (Lucien and the truck in the dooryard in 'seventy-four, one clause at most, or nothing; the reader has it.)
5. Ray takes the cashier's check out of the inside of his jacket, where his own checkbook lives. He drew it at noon at the credit union window, from his own savings, nearly all of them, not the store's. Danny reads the face of it, and the page doesn't give the figure. Danny: "You'd already done it." Ray doesn't answer, or gives one short true thing.
6. Danny, getting his coat: **"You're allowed to want something that isn't on a shelf."** He goes. The bell over the front door, once.
7. **Late, alone, the lamp.** Ray seals the check in a Gagnon & Son envelope and runs his thumb along the flap once. (Ch 04 did twice; don't copy it.) He uncaps a pen to write her name and caps it again, and writes nothing. He puts the envelope inside his coat where his own checkbook lives and zips the coat to his chin.

**Subtext:** He can't ask her a second time to keep Linda's night, so he means to buy it back from her instead, and he hasn't asked himself what else he'd be buying. Danny has asked it for him twice now.

**Texture budget:** Two.
- The adding machine and its tape. Ray needs it said out loud by a machine.
- The cashier's check from the credit union window against the store's long checkbook. This is the line Ray drew in 1974 and holds to, and it shows the money is his own.

**Point of view:** Ray. His interior is patient and physical. The tender register goes to what his hands are doing (the flap, the pen, the zipper), never to her. Danny talks in inventory.

**Ends on:** **Question** (the outline's ending). The envelope goes inside his coat with nothing written on it, and the zipper goes up. Last beat. No interior about tomorrow, no plan stated, no clock, no light off, no snow in the window. The reader should be asking what he'll do with it.

**Word target:** 550

## Writer's checklist (the orchestrator's nine requirements)

1. **The couple in the room.** s1 and s2 have both of them in a six-by-eight shack. s3: she's at the counter in his sightline, and the scene turns on what he won't say about her. s4: every action is his, because of her. The committee scene and the check are about her.
2. **The turn.** "Did you know I didn't hear it?" / "I said it." Floor 900 for s1, at least 400 words from "You gave me your word" to "I said it," and no joke inside it. The gloves are the longing, put on her during his argument and still on her hands when she lies. The true sentence and her reasons are above. She doesn't use it.
3. **Money.** The chapter's fact is Hollis's, with no figure. The check is Ray's own savings from the credit union, nearly all of them, never the store's (the two checkbooks again). Danny's tape is a number described by what it's for, not by what it is. No ratio.
4. **Denise** is on the page in s3. She says "honey," nearly says it, and swerves. Her reason: it's Ellen's to say, and she isn't certain.
5. **Ice.** Bob-house, tip-ups, the license check (Hollis at Ellen, the laugh), and the ice thickness once (eleven inches of black ice; domain check). Facts plain.
6. **Endings.** s1 consequence; s2 mid-action; s3 dialogue (the one button); s4 question. No neat images.
7. **Ray's tics.** Motor comparisons, two at most and none in the s1 exchange. "What broke" once, from stock (the Legion and the ball return on four). Sulking zero.
8. **Time.** The jump from Jan 19 to Feb 1 gets one line or nothing (s4). The half ends Saturday the thirtieth, said in s3.
9. **Full apparatus:** a turn, a longing, a laugh, texture budgets, the plant list with payoffs, the s1 floor, a total of 2,900, and assigned ending types.
