# Reader Notes: Chapter 6, "Walking Ice"

**Draft read:** final.md, tournament winner, Oct 29. Chapters 01 to 06 `final.md` read in order. No other files opened.

---

## 1. Pull score: 5 / 10

**I'd keep reading, but.** The bob-house scene is the payoff I've been waiting for since the pit in Chapter 3, and it lands. Then the chapter spends three more scenes on people pointedly not saying things: Ray counts a drawer over her, she won't ask for the back room, he won't write her name on the envelope. By the end I'm watching a standoff instead of feeling one, and the last image (a blank envelope zipped into a pocket) is a quiet question I can mostly answer myself. A reader who has other books on her phone gets through the bob-house on momentum and puts the phone down somewhere in the credit union.

---

## 2. Where I got bored

- **The page-turning in his gloves, bob-house, about 30% in.** "She put a gloved finger to the corner of the page. The leather slid over the paper. Pinched, two sheets came up together and dropped back together. On the next try, slower, with the thumb, the thumb of his glove ran an inch past hers and folded over at the end." The fight finally starts, then stops so I can watch someone fail to turn a page. I get what it means (she can't present her plan with his gloves on). Four sentences is too long to make that point while I'm waiting for "I said it."
- **The flyer, store scene, about 55%.** "From the register he could read it. The Kettle Lanes Open, across the top in heavy black type... The top tack on the left had gone in crooked, and the sheet hung a hair off true from it." This is the third time the book has read me a posted document line by line: the pool sheet, the lineage notice, now this. I skimmed it. "She had spelled it right" is the only part that mattered.
- **The credit union opening, about 88%.** "On Monday, the first of February, the day before the second half started, Ray stood at the credit union's left-hand window at ten minutes to five. Danny was behind him in line with the store's deposit bag, as he was every weekday before five, and behind Danny a woman in a Sportsmen's Club jacket held a coin roll in each fist..." By now every scene opens on a clock: "at ten to seven", "at a minute past six on the Monday after next, nine days after the bob-house", "at ten past six", "at ten minutes to five". I've stopped hearing the times, and here I skipped ahead to find out why he was at a teller window.
- **The overall shape after the bob-house.** It's withholding three times running. The interruption is the clearest case: "Mr. Gagnon. I would like—" and the bell goes. I've had five chapters of people not quite saying the thing, and here it reads as the book's only gear rather than as tension.

## 3. Where I got confused

- **"his had only Linda's chair, which she kept her eyes off."** (store scene). Which chair? We've had the Bench's three stools and Ellen's second chair at the alley. A chair in the hardware store that was Linda's has never been on the page. Did Linda work the counter? I stopped to look back, at the most emotional moment of the scene.
- **The cash on the counter in the Dale scene.** "He took out his wallet and counted cash onto the counter, on top of the envelope." The envelope already holds his team's lineage. So what is the cash? It might be the Open entry fee, prepaid lineage, or the team paying the house's difference. "Every Tuesday after tonight, up to the Memorial" suggests prepaid lineage, but I'm guessing, and this is the payoff of the scene. I read it twice.
- **What the check buys.** "So she can give the league its Saturday back, is all." In Chapter 2 the bank's condition was the winter clearing thirty thousand "with every dollar it makes still sitting in the account." Does a gift from Ray count as something the winter made? Chapter 5 hints that her own savings could count ("Not enough to matter"), so maybe. The ending hangs on this and the page never quite says.
- **Ice-fishing basics.** "Every flag out the bob-house window was down" and "tip-ups". A reader who has never ice-fished won't know that a flag down means no bite. Context carries it, but the chapter's first line asks me to decode it.
- **"Ray stopped talking."** He'd said one short line. Stopped talking from what?
- **The timeline.** Saturday, then "the Monday after next, nine days after", then "the last Tuesday before the half", then "Monday, the first of February". It works out, but I was doing date arithmetic in a love story. "The half" (the league's half-season) is never explained.

## 4. Where I stopped believing

- **The convenient bell.** "Mr. Gagnon. I would like—" and then "The coffee maker quit, and the bell went." The Bench walks in at exactly the half-sentence. Ray has already cut her off once with the count, and that's earned because it's his choice. A second cutoff by the plot itself is the author's hand.
- **Ellen not asking for the back room.** "She didn't ask for it." It matches "I don't ask" from Chapter 1. But this is the second time that trait has been used as a plot brake rather than shown costing her something. Here it just ends the scene.
- **Ellen's choices under pressure.** Walking out on the ice is fully closed. She checked black versus white ice, Hollis drilled it Thursday and every hundred yards, and she bought a license at 4:10 a.m. That's very her, and I believed it. Bringing the plan to Hollis's shack with Hollis present also follows: she needs the derby man. Hiding the clause is consistent with Chapters 3 and 5. "I said it" (true, and not an answer) is exactly what this woman would say.
- **Ray paying cash to keep his team.** I believe it, if I've read it right (see Confusion).
- **The check.** A widower who swore never to put the store up twice withdraws "nearly all of it" from his personal account instead. That follows from "I asked the store once", and Danny closes the safer option ("Take it out of that") on the page. Good. What isn't closed is why Ellen would take it at all. She returned his invoice and pays by the call. The chapter ends before that comes up, so it's open rather than broken.
- **Dale.** In Chapter 5 he paid "on Ray's say-so." Now he defects because Linda's cup is open to outsiders. "My crew paid for that cup in September" is a little thin as stated: what exactly did they pay for? But it follows.

## 5. Where it sounded like a machine

- **Trade similes for every touch.** "working each finger down into its place to the web, the way he'd seat a part he meant to keep." "the way he creased a parts slip." Across the book: her pulse "the way a motor runs fast before it's warmed through", her voice "the small change you hear in a motor the second before it catches", her breathing like "a pinsetter... when a clutch was about to go." It was charming once. Now every feeling Ray has gets turned into a machine part.
- **Explaining after the gesture.** "She sat down on the upturned pail by the cooler before she had said a word to him. Ray always saw when she sat, and he knew she had settled something on the walk out." The sitting already told me. (It's also a callback to Chapter 3's "Ray saw her sit", and Denise's "Now you're sitting" in Chapter 5. That's three uses. It's a tic now.)
- **One-line paragraphs used as a stamp.** "Somebody had told her how, once." "She had spelled it right." "She gave it to him exact." "Ray stopped talking." Each one sits alone so I'll feel it. Four in one chapter, and I felt the hand.
- **Scenes ending on a clipped line or a small gesture, every time.** "Page two." / Normie's cut-off read-out / "signed it without reading it, and handed back the pen." / "pulled the zipper up over it." All four scenes close the same way.
- **Recycled props.** The Worcester page in the shirt pocket: "behind the Worcester page" again, its fourth or fifth appearance. The second chair "with nobody in it": its fifth or sixth. Hands warmed between his hands, second time (Chapter 3 trench, now the gloves). The bell over the door is a scene-starter or scene-ender every time we're in the store. "The heater hissed" three times in the bob-house, and the coffee maker hisses twice in the store.
- **Normie's sportscaster bit.** "And the owner's in the store ahead of the Bench, boys, first time in the history of the franchise." It's the same joke shape for the fourth time in the book. The cut-off at the end of the scene is good. The opening of the bit is wallpaper now.
- **Danny sounds like Ray.** "Because those are different invoices." It's a good line, but it's Ray's metaphor kit in his son's mouth. Dale and Ray also share the clipped back-and-forth ("Half's Saturday. We go the Tuesday after."). Hollis is the only side voice who's distinct, and he's distinct by saying less.

## 6. What I think is going on

Ray will not write Ellen's name on that envelope. He'll offer the money another way, probably as a team or sponsor entry in the Open, or by dropping it on her counter at the worst moment, and she'll refuse it. She swore to Kate over her own savings, and she will not owe him. The real reckoning is Ellen writing herself up: before the Memorial, in public, probably in Verna's minutes or over the PA, she will report the one error she wouldn't, that she watched him miss "unless the numbers say otherwise" and let it stand. That is her third item, or tied to it. I suspect the third item is that she had already seen Ray, or Linda's Memorial, the night she stood in the vestibule last winter. Page two gets used: the ice goes or a storm hits on derby Saturday, the derby crowd comes indoors, and the Open fills. Locker fourteen gets opened, and Linda's ball or shoes are bowled in the final, probably by Denise, who wins the cup she's wanted. The Highway crew comes back for the Memorial. Ellen clears thirty thousand, or close enough that Ray's held loan quietly expires unused. Danny's Valentine's square (D.G., on the sheet with no title) is a bet on when they get together, and he wins or nearly does. The March first square on the original pool stays blank. Final image: the K on the sign gets lit, by a sign shop she hires, not by Ray.

## 7. The listener pass

- **Clock times and dates read in full.** "a minute past six on the Monday after next, nine days after the bob-house", "On Monday, the first of February, the day before the second half started... at ten minutes to five", "at ten to seven", "a minute to seven", "at seven", "ten past four this morning", "ten past six... twenty minutes to league", "twenty past", "five". Aloud, it's a train timetable.
- **Untagged runs in the Dale scene.** "Go before the half and you forfeit." / "Half's Saturday. We go the Tuesday after." / "Halsey Falls is forty minutes on the winter road, Dale..." A listener can sort out Ray from Dale, but only by working at it. Same for the new proper nouns dropped together: Gil Arsenault, Pine Ridge Lanes, Halsey Falls. I heard "Pine Ridge" and "Halsey Falls" as two separate places.
- **Same speaker across two paragraphs.** In the bob-house, Ellen's "Page two is what happens if the ice goes." is followed by a new paragraph, "'Without the Open,' she said..." Aloud it sounds like a new speaker until the tag.
- **"with the thumb, the thumb of his glove"** stumbles aloud. The whole glove page-turning passage is mush by ear.
- **The drawer count works.** "One... Two... Three. Four. Five. Six... Seven. Eight." It's the best thing in the chapter by ear, and the voice slowing through it does the work.
- **The flyer read-out** ("The Kettle Lanes Open... the last Saturday in February, with the Great Kettle Derby, any team of four... cash refunds if weather stops play") is a document read aloud, then Normie reads part of it aloud again. That's a double hearing.
- **The stencil HOLLIS PIKE in capitals** is fine aloud. "Weather, and a numbered list" is fine.
- **Sentence shapes.** Bob-house paragraphs keep falling into "He... and ..., and ..." chains, broken only by one-line stamps. The rhythm is narration, beat, narration, beat, all the way through.

## 8. The romance

Yes, once, and it's the gloves. "He was already pulling off his gloves, the right and then the left. He took her hand off the page by the wrist... Then he started his right glove over them, working each finger down into its place to the web," and then "'Other hand,' he said. She let him." He's furious and he's still taking care of her hands while he says the hard thing. Then "He went out bare-handed with his gloves still on her." I wanted them together most at the moment he walked out.

It's undercut slightly by the simile, and by having done hands-warmed-in-his-hands in Chapter 3.

Laughs: Hollis asking the same question twice in the same voice. "which is more courting than I ever got out of you." "Going to check my flags." / "They're all down." / "Then it won't take long." Those got a real smile. Nothing in the second half of the chapter made me laugh, and nothing in it made me want them together. "The chair is where you left it" comes close.

## 9. What I want to know next

- Does Ray give her the check, and does she take it? (Medium pull. I think I know: no.)
- Will Ellen ever say the words "I watched you miss it"? This is the real question of the book now, and it's the one keeping me in.
- Do the Highway crew and the Legion actually leave for Pine Ridge, and what does that do to her twenty-six thousand?
- Page two: does the ice go on derby Saturday?
- What's in locker fourteen? (It was planted in Chapter 1 and opened a crack in Chapter 3. I'd almost forgotten it, which says something.)
- What's Ellen's third item?

Honestly, these are mild. Nothing in this chapter made me need the next one tonight. The strongest pull is a character question (will she confess), not an event, and the chapter gives me no sign that anything new is about to happen to force it.
