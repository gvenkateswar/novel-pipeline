### Scene 1 — The ordinary scene

**Setting:** Ray's house on School Street: the back step, then the kitchen. Wednesday, the end of the afternoon, full dark coming. **Sensory anchor:** the kitchen's heat after the cold step, with hamburg, green pepper and canned tomatoes cooked down. Use one or two bible details at most (the yellow kitchen, the oilcloth, the dented pot, the radio on the refrigerator).

**Characters:** Ellen, Ray. Nobody else, and no phone.

**Want:**
- **Ellen** wants to sit at his table with nothing to give him and nothing to brief, and to be asked back. In the way: she has never once arrived anywhere in this book without business in her hands, and she has to keep the rule she brought with her (no alley, no money, no league, no Linda) without saying it. The reader learns the want at **"What do you make the second night?"**, which is her asking to come back, sideways.
- **Ray** wants her at his table on his clock, and he wants her to be the one who asks for the next time. The reader learns it when he opens the door before she can knock, and again at "That one."

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Open on her hands, empty, on his back step.** No envelope, no list, no pie. They are in her vest pockets and then out of them. The step is iced at one corner and she has taken it on the right leg, favoring the left. She is a minute early and is deciding whether a knock a minute early breaks thirty years of his supper. One line of bridge, no more: his invitation came pencilled across the bottom of an invoice Denise handed her, the only way he could get her to take anything (writer's choice of wording; one clause).
2. **He opens the door before she knocks.** He heard her boots on the ice. Model: **"Heard you come up. You're early."** / **"By one minute."** / **"I'll allow it."** Then the time arrives as he stands aside: a quarter past five, said once, by him or by the radio, not both.
3. **The kitchen.** He ducks the light over the table out of thirty years' habit. She watches his height in a low room, since she likes looking up at someone and seldom gets to (one clause, hers). Two bowls, the pot. The ordinary pleasure gets three or four sentences: the hot bowl, the heat on her face, eating, and nobody commenting.
4. **Ray, mock-solemn about the supper (his comic engine), in his own words.** He has made one supper for thirty years and it has never failed him. Do not use the bible sample's sentence.
5. **Interior passage 1.** Her attention moves through the room and keeps landing on him: his hands on the ladle, the nicks across his knuckles, the radio talking to nobody. It is one long sentence that picks things up and lands on a short one. **No list, no ordinals, no and-collapse.** Desire shows as what she keeps looking at.
6. **The knee.** Ray reads people by wear, and he saw her take his step. Model: **"What'd you do to the left one?"** She has never told anyone, and she tells him, short and in order: two thousand nine, a trail Kate picked at fourteen, and how Kate went down it and how she went down it. Give her one literal line that is the joke (writer's choice). **Ray dates it by what broke:** "That's the winter the town dock heaved", or another break from the store or the town that has not been on the page. Never seven's clutch, nine's belt, the ball return on four, or the K.
7. **The laugh.**
   - **Ellen:** "What do you make the second night?"
   - **Ray:** "That one."
   - Let a beat sit, then one more exchange at most, from Ray, mock-solemn, with the weight on the last word. Nobody laughs at their own line.
8. **"Supper," in her mouth, once.** She says it, hears which word she used, and leaves it. One clause, no gloss.
9. **The radio goes on to the weather.** Heavy snow Saturday, beginning before noon, accumulations… Neither of them says the alley. Ray's hand goes up to the dial. **Cut there, mid-action.**

**Subtext:** This is the repair's fourth beat: two people who have hurt each other eating at the same table with no terms on it. Her question about the second night is the first thing she has asked him for that isn't business. His "That one." is yes, with the plain word last. He switches off the weather so the scene stays theirs, and the reader knows what Saturday holds.

**Texture budget:** One. The one supper and the one pot reveal the man who keeps things the same, and he offers it to her.

**Point of view:** Ellen. Quiet register: sentences of fifteen to eighteen words with real spread, one ordinary pleasure given room, and the comedy in the gap between a question and its answer.

**Ends on:** **Mid-action.** His hand on the dial, mid-forecast.

**Word target:** 850   **Word floor:** 800 (the ordinary scene, repair beat 4). Play it in real time from the step to the dial. No summary of the meal.

### Scene 2 — The relief briefing (the turn)

**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, the pit behind lane three, storm Saturday, after midday. **Sensory anchor:** the warmth off the motors and pinsetter grease. Through the masking come nine lanes going, then ten, which is a full house: her pleasure, heard from behind. One caged bulb.

**Characters:** Ellen, Ray.

**Want:**
- **Ellen** wants Ray on the position with her: today, and after Monday whichever way Monday goes. She wants to ask without it meaning she has been relieved. In the way is her wound (asking means someone noticed she needed help), along with Monday itself, and the fact that she has never asked him for anything to his face. The reader learns the want at **"I want you on it with me."**
- **Ray** wants to be asked. He puts the wrench down and gets up off his knee to hear it.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Open on Ray's hands in three's table.** Under the day's load it hung at the top, and he's bringing it down. **Ray: "Told you I'd put it away for later."** That is the Ch 04 payoff, and nothing more about it. She came along the catwalk from the steel door by ten, and she waits until he has finished. One clause, no gloss.
2. Three sets. Ten lanes going at once through the masking.
3. **The briefing (set piece; cap of five ATC phrases in this scene). Ellen stands; Ray is still on one knee by the machine. Model lines:**
   - **"I would like to give you a relief briefing."**
   - Ray: **"Go on, then."** (Never "Go ahead", which is hers.)
   - **"Weather. Hollis called at ten past four. The ice is closed for the day, and he says it gets worse after two."**
   - **"Equipment. Three was late at the top of its table. You have just put it right."**
   - **"Traffic. A hundred and fifty in the building, forty-one of them children. Both morning squads are done. The Memorial at two, on all ten lanes. The Open's evening at six: ten teams, and most of them drive home to Massachusetts tonight."**
   - **"Special conditions. Coolers on lane ten. The derby's raffle is in a ball return, and its treasurer counts it twice."**
4. **The ask:**
   - **"At the Center they call it the D-side. Two people on one sector. One works the frequency. The other one sees what she can't while she's talking."**
   - **"I keep the position. I want you on it with me. Today, and after today, whatever Monday says."**
5. Ray puts the wrench down and stands. He is taller than the pit ceiling allows and has to duck the beam. **Ellen: "Read it back to me. I want to hear it in your voice."** (Bible, Ellen tender 9.)
6. **The readback. Ray gives it back in her order, exactly, with no rounding:**
   - **"Hollis at ten past four. Ice is closed, and worse after two. Three was late at the top of her table, and she's right now. A hundred and fifty in the building, forty-one of them kids. Mornings done. Memorial at two, all ten. Evening at six, ten teams, Massachusetts tonight. Coolers on ten. Raffle's in a ball return, and the treasurer counts it twice."**
   - He has made three a *she*, and Ellen lets it stand. Show this by her not correcting it, and never in narration.
7. **Interior passage 2:** her own words coming back to her in his voice, low, the voice of a good pilot at three in the morning. "Pilot" once at most. It is one long sentence that lands short. **No mention of December or of what she didn't get then.** The reader supplies the missing readback.
8. **The last two items, his:**
   - **"And I'm on the D-side with you. Today, and after today, whatever Monday says."**
   - Then the item she left out, which is her: **"And you haven't sat down since Hollis called."**
9. **Ellen: "Readback correct."** End. In Ch 04 she answered his added item with "Negative on the last item." This time she accepts it. Nobody remarks on that.

**Subtext:** The woman who briefs instead of asking asks, in the only language she trusts. The man who keeps everything the way it was says yes to a Monday he can't buy his way out of, since he has signed that away, and adds the one thing about her that she would never put in a briefing. She lets him have it on the record.

**Texture budget:** Two.
- Three's table: the Ch 04 "later", paid. It shows Ray keeps track of her machines the way she keeps track of time.
- The relief briefing, the chapter's ATC set piece. Her competence becomes the vehicle for her first real ask.

**Point of view:** Ellen. Procedure register in her dialogue, and the interior long and slow under his voice. The couple close in a cramped place: his height under the beam, grease on his hands, a foot of catwalk between them.

**Ends on:** **Dialogue (the chapter's one button).** "Readback correct."

**Word target:** 650

### Scene 3 — The weather call (the loss)

**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, the counter and the room, storm Saturday, from a little before two to the empty building after a quarter to four. **Sensory anchor:** wet wool and road salt, snow melting off a hundred and fifty pairs of boots, the fryer going. Later, the same room silent.

**Characters:**
- Ellen, Ray, Hollis, Denise.
- The derby treasurer (unnamed, Sportsmen's Club jacket).
- A young Legion bowler, Judy's nephew (unnamed, one line).
- The raffle winner (unnamed).
- The crowd: coolers on lane ten, children at the snack bar, Massachusetts plates in the lot.
- **Lead for this scene: pressure.** The clock to four, the crowd, and the envelopes. Enter late and cut every transition.

**Want:**
- **Ellen** wants to keep the evening and keep everyone safe, and she can't have both. The reader learns that she wants to keep them, and won't, at **"Thirty-three in December. A hundred and fifty today, forty-one of them children, and a boiler that quits when the power does."**
- **Denise** wants the room kept, which means the evening's money and Linda's day bowled on Linda's day.
- **Ray** wants her to make the call he'd make, and he has to say it himself, knowing what it costs her and what it costs Linda's day.
- **The treasurer** wants his evening raffle draw.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Open on Hollis's phone in his glove** at the counter, snow on his cap. **"Dale says the town wants everybody off the roads by four. His crew won't be off theirs before morning."**
2. **Hollis, measurements:** the Memorial is three strings on ten lanes, two hours and some, with a team out plowing. It doesn't go and get everybody home by four. Keep it short; Hollis talks in measurements.
3. **Denise, her one speech (run-on, answering herself; no "honey"):** keep them, like December. Thirty-three of them slept on the settees and paid for strings and they're still talking about it. Keep this lot the same, bowl the Memorial tonight, bowl the evening, and she'll run the fryer till morning.
4. **Ellen, the count (the third and last room count in the book): "Thirty-three in December. A hundred and fifty today, forty-one of them children, and a boiler that quits when the power does."**
5. **Ray, beside her at the counter, on the D-side, using her words once and at the worst moment: "D-side says send them home."** It costs him Linda's day on Linda's Saturday. It costs her the evening. Neither says either.
6. **She makes the call on the PA (fear register: polite, imperative, present tense; no ATC phrase needed).** The Memorial moves to Sunday at one, on all ten lanes, for the league's teams. The Open's evening is cancelled. Evening entrants come to the counter for their entry in cash.
7. **The young Legion bowler, Judy's nephew, at the counter for the Sunday time (one line):** **"Aunt Judy was taking the afternoon off the bank to watch me. I'll tell her one tomorrow."** Ellen's response is one beat of body or nothing. The woman whose committee reads her winter will be in the building Sunday; the reader can do the arithmetic.
8. **The treasurer objects.** His evening raffle draw dies with the crowd: half the tickets were sold for six o'clock. Ellen: the raffle is the derby's, so draw it now. He draws from the ball return with half his ticket-holders already in their cars, and tells her the committee will hear about it. **She doesn't win this one (World rule 14).** She has no answer and doesn't try for one. Denise reads the winning number on the PA twice to a room that's leaving. Nobody comes up. The prize, an ice auger, leans against the end of the counter.
9. **Interior passage 3: the envelopes.** Page two's cash refunds, at the counter, envelope by envelope out of the cash box, with the names in Denise's hand and the Massachusetts plates going down the drive behind Hollis's plow. **Her voice to each entrant stays exact and courteous, while underneath the interior runs fast and the reader is shown the gap.** Ten envelopes. No dollar figure. Don't make it a list; one long passage landing short.
10. **Out by a quarter to four.** The lot empties and the last taillights go down to Kettle Road.
11. **The laugh.** The door opens on the empty building. A man with snow to his knees and his hat white has come back up the drive on foot for the auger he won. Give him one line and give Ellen one exact, literal answer (writer's choice; no ticket number on the page). He carries an ice auger out into the storm, on a day the ice is closed.
12. **The empty building. Ellen and Ray at the counter, the cash box open and light.** She says it to him, standing:
    - **"Seventeen hundred and fifteen dollars under thirty thousand. Sunday can't bring that much."**
    - Then what rides on it: **"Arlene's room is on it, and the last name on that page is mine."**
    - Ray offers nothing; he heard her oath in December. Something goes into his hands that isn't a screw (the cash box lid, the auger's empty place on the counter). That is all.
13. **Ray: "What'll you do?"** **Ellen: "Ask."** **Ray: "Ask who?"** Cut. He hopes it's him. It isn't, yet.

**Subtext:** The thing Ch 08 said could happen, happens, and nobody cheats it. She was right to send them home, and being right costs her the margin, a raffle, and the town's goodwill for an afternoon. The D-side does its job: he says the thing she can't say while she's talking. Then the woman who has always reported outcomes says she is going to ask, and the man who wants to be asked has to hear that the first person isn't him.

**Texture budget:** Two.

### Scene 4 — Kate

**Setting:** Ellen's camp, Saturday night. She is standing at the dark window over the lake. **Sensory anchor:** the ice booming under the new snow (the bible's camp sound). Nothing that ticks.

**Characters:** Ellen; Kate, by phone.

**Want:**
- **Ellen** wants Kate's yes *before* she decides, which is the first time she has ever asked her daughter anything that wasn't already settled. In the way: she swore, and she has always briefed Kate on outcomes. The reader learns the want at **"I'm calling to ask you something before I decide it."**
- **Kate** wants to be asked. The reader learns it in her last line.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Open on the phone in her hand**, Kate's name on the screen, at the window. She is standing.
2. Kate answers, with something ordinary behind her (water running, her night off). **Kate: "It's Saturday."** Then, clinical: **"Are you hurt?"** **"No."**
3. **She doesn't brief her:** **"On the second of December I swore to you. The house, not the rest."** Then: **"I'm calling to ask you something before I decide it. I want to break it."**
4. **Kate, fast, interrupting herself:** how much of the rest? **Ellen, exact without a figure:** **"Nearly all of it, if tomorrow brings nothing. I'll know tomorrow night."** Optional, one line: Kate, sideways, about the birthdays, or the Ch 05 question (**"Is anybody there with you?"** / **"No. I wanted to ask you first."**). Pick one or neither. Never both.
5. **Kate: "Scale of one to ten."** **Ellen: "Ten."**
6. **Kate: "Then do it. And call me first next time. Like this."** Cut. No reaction beat after it, and no sitting down.

**Subtext:** Kate briefs her mother the way her mother briefs everyone, on purpose, and this is the first call in the book where the order is reversed. "Ten" is all the reason Ellen gives, which is a first for her. The yes is permission, and it is also the daughter asking to be asked again.

**Texture budget:** None. If anything, Kate's clinical scale.

**Point of view:** Ellen. Plain, short, asking. Contractions are free here.

**Ends on:** **Cut** (outline). Kate's line, last.

**Word target:** 350
