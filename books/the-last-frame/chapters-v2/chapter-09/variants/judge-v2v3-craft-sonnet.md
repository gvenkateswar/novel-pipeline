1. **Winner:** A

2. **Why:** A's people sound like themselves (Ray's "I varied the bowl," Kate's fourth-grade birthday story about her mother arriving at twenty past twelve), so the final call is two people with a history, not a plot function. B has the same bones in a flatter register.

3. **Where A lost me:** Mostly it didn't. The nearest thing is Hollis's logistics speech at about 49%: "Three strings a bowler on all ten lanes is two hours and some, with Dale's team out on the roads. Start at two, done past four. Who drives home after?" That is a man doing arithmetic aloud, and my thumb sped up. One sentence in the Kate call also puzzled me: "She had been on the schedule for that birthday, and for every one after it until this October." I could tell it was meant to hurt, but I had to read it twice.

4. **Where B lost me:** The first loss is at about 7%: "The clock on his stove said fourteen past. That left sixteen minutes to the weather." It reads like a procedure, and the chapter then watches the clock again ("The stove clock said twenty past, and there were ten minutes yet"). The bigger loss is the envelope litany at about 60%: "Two, three, four. Through the door glass the lot was showing gaps, dark squares where cars had stood since morning..." followed by "Six, to a team of sisters from Nashua... Seven, eight, nine." The rhythm is deliberate, but it is still the couple doing business, closer to A1 than anything in A. B's Kate call is also thin, nearly all logistics ("How much of the rest? ... Don't give me dollars"), and Kate sounds like a generic daughter. The nearest A1-style hit is the same Hollis arithmetic speech at about 50%.

5. **Confidence:** Medium. They are close in structure, and B's envelope sequence has more music than A's compressed version. A's supper scene and phone call are clearly better.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes, same beats, and the same habits (the radio, the "readback correct," "snow to his knees").
