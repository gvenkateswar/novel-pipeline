2. **Why:** For most of the chapter I liked A's sentences better. Then B's phone call gave Kate a voice of her own ("Nobody else's mother knew to the minute"), and that earned the "Ten" at the end. A's call just stops on the number.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Two, three, four. Through the door glass the lot was showing gaps..." (~67%). By the fifth envelope I was counting along with her. Then "Six, to a team of sisters from Nashua... Seven, eight, nine." turned it into a tally sheet. Before that, I was already watching the clock with her at supper ("That left sixteen minutes to the weather." ~6%, "The stove clock said twenty past, and there were ten minutes yet." ~13%), which made a meal feel like a countdown. The phone call at the end goes flat. Kate asks two questions and says "Scale of one to ten," and she could be anyone.

4. **Where B lost me:** "He had walked his crew out over Linda's cup in February, and he had entered her evening." (~63%). That's the writer explaining the scene to me. "He had heard her half of that call through her front door in December." (~78%) does the same thing. B also uses more words than A in the places where A was better. Compare "She stopped. The heading was there in its place, fourth, where it had stood in every briefing she had given in her life..." with A's "nothing she had a form for was under it," and compare "He was right. In her whole working life she had sent back every figure that came to her rounded" with A's line about the rounded number. The long paragraph at the window before she presses the name slowed me down too, but the call itself paid me back.
