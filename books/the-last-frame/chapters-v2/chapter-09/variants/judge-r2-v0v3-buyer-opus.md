1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B he turns the forecast off to give her one easy evening, sees her stand up, and turns it back on louder, and that's the moment I knew he understood her. After that I wanted to see her sit down behind three, see the lanes go dark one at a time, and hear Kate ask "And if I say no?" A has the same evening and is kinder to read, but it never squeezes me like that.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 45% in, at the readback: "Hollis at ten past four. Ice is closed, and worse after two. Three was late at the top of her table, and she's right now. A hundred and fifty in the building, forty-one of them kids..." I'd just read the whole list in her voice, and now I was reading it again in his. My thumb went down the screen until "And you haven't sat down since Hollis called." I came back for the envelopes ("she heard herself wish the fifth captain a safe drive in exactly the voice she had used on the first"), but the Kate call ended too easily. "Then do it" closed the question before I'd had time to be afraid for her.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 78% in, at the money: "In January I was four thousand short, and four thousand at eight to one was more than I have. Seventeen hundred and fifteen at eight to one is nearly all of it." Right after "That was sixteen hundred dollars. I'm seventeen hundred and fifteen under thirty thousand," it was arithmetic in bed, and I lost hold of what he was offering and what she was refusing. The lane lights going dark got me back. "He was right. In her whole working life she had sent back every figure that came to her rounded" also made me stop and read it twice.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The supper and the catwalk go clearly to B. A's counter scene is a little better. B's numbers by the cash box cost it the high confidence.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. They share the same scenes, the same jokes ("By one minute." / "I'll allow it."), and the same rhythm, so this is the same writer on two different days.
