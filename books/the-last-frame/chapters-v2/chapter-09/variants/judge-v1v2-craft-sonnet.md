1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A lets its jokes and gestures do the work (the second bowl, Ray turning the radio back up "with his arm up and her on her feet") and cuts the stage business, while B keeps parking the two of them side by side at the counter and stops trusting the scene to carry the feeling.

3. **Where A lost me:** "The bar sat where the bank had set it, and Monday was the bank." (about 70%, scene three). The narrator stops to restate the stakes in ledger voice in the middle of the envelope run. It is a neat line, but it is a note to the reader. My thumb sped up through "Six, to a team of sisters from Nashua… Seven, eight, nine." right after it. It never lost me completely, because the HUTCHINS envelope and the auger man pull the run back.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Ray put his hand flat on the counter beside hers." (about 75%). This is the third time in the scene the narration puts his arm or hand against hers ("Ray's forearm lay on the counter by the box," "his arm still against hers," then this, then "He leaned on the counter beside her"). After two, it reads as the draft reminding me they are close, and the closeness stops landing. The kitchen scene has the same habit at about 15%: "He was watching her mouth." and "She held her face exactly as it was." Those are romance-template beats, and the scene's own jokes had already done that job. The summary line "Thirty thousand stood where Judy had set it, every envelope went the wrong way from it, and Monday was two days off" (about 69%) is also muddier than A's version of the same beat.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two share nearly every plot beat and a lot of identical lines, so it comes down to line work. A is leaner and funnier. B has a few things I would not want to lose (the shoulder shake at the auger line, "Ten lane lights burned over nobody," and the "Forty cars, one plowed lane" sentence). I would still bet on A, but not heavily.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The scene skeleton, the briefing, the Kate call and the ending exchange are the same, and the deep sentence rhythm matches. These read as two passes at the same draft, not two writers.
