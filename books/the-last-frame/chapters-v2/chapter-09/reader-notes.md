# Reader Notes: Chapter 9, "The D-side"

**Draft read:** `chapters/chapter-09/final.md`, Oct 29, 3,001 words (the tournament winner, as the orchestrator named it; I did not recount). Context read cold, in order: `chapter-01/final.md` through `chapter-08/final.md`. I opened no other file.

---

## 1. Pull score: 4 / 10

**I'd keep reading, but only because I'm nine chapters in and the end is close, not because this chapter made me need the next one.** The romance is settled: she asks him onto the position, he reads it back, and they're a couple. The only open question left is a bank number, now $1,715. It's the fourth time that number has moved, and the genre already tells me she'll make it. The middle of the chapter is also a rerun of Chapter 3: snow, Hollis, the plow, the boiler that dies with the power, a room full of bowlers who can't leave, with Denise saying "like December" out loud. The last line, "Ten.", is warm and it closes something. It doesn't open anything.

---

## 2. Where I got bored

- **About 75% through, the money paragraph.** *"In January I was four thousand short, and at eight to one that came to more than I have. Today it comes to nearly all my savings. I can cover it myself."* I glazed over here. I had to go back to Chapter 7 to work out what "eight to one" meant, and no reader will. Just before it: *"That was sixteen hundred dollars. I'm seventeen hundred and fifteen under thirty thousand, and Sunday can't bring that much."* The book keeps asking me to care about a ledger line. I care about Arlene's bed, but she isn't in the scene, so what I get is arithmetic.
- **About 65%, the derby treasurer and the raffle.** *"Half of these were sold on a six o'clock draw. You send them home at three, you've killed it."* through *"The ice auger with the red bow on it leaned against the shoe cubbies."* My thumb sped up. It's a new minor character complaining about a raffle while the main decision has already been made. The auger only earns its place with the man who comes back for it, and that payoff is good, but I skimmed the setup to reach it.
- **About 70%, the refund line.** *"The first went to a Massachusetts captain… She said it to the second captain in the same words. The third said… The fourth had a boy asleep…"* It's compressed and some of it is lovely (the envelope in his teeth), but it's still a list of ten envelopes, and I knew from the PA announcement exactly how the scene would end.
- **The opening paragraph.** *"Her hands were empty on Ray's back step. They went into the pockets of her vest, and the right one found his key in the bottom corner of its pocket and let it lie. They came out again bare into the cold. The left corner of the step had iced where the eave dripped, so she took it on her right leg and kept the left one back…"* That's four sentences of hand and foot choreography before anyone speaks. By Chapter 9 I've read Ellen's bare red hands a dozen times.
- **Structural drift across the middle third.** Another storm, another plow, another boiler, another set of lane toggles going off one by one. The images work, but I've already seen this chapter.

---

## 3. Where I got confused

- **Who says "Read it back to me. I want to hear it in your voice."?** It comes right after a paragraph about Ray standing up under the beam, so I read it as Ray's line. Then Ray starts reading back, so it must be Ellen's. "I want to hear it in your voice" also sounds like Ray, not Ellen. I lost the speaker at the emotional peak of the chapter.
- **"He was right. In her whole working life she had never let a rounded number stand."** What rounded number? He said she hadn't sat since Hollis called. I read it three times and couldn't connect it to anything.
- **"Thirty-three in December. Today, this room, and a boiler that quits when the power does."** Nothing tags who says this. I think it's Ellen answering Denise, but "Today, this room" doesn't name the hundred and fifty, so it lands as a fragment.
- **Why Ellen stands up at supper.** Ray turns off the forecast and she stands with her napkin and says nothing. I worked it out (she won't be shielded from bad news, the readback idea again), and his turning it back on louder is the best beat in the chapter. But for a beat she might have been leaving, and nothing on the page confirms either reading.
- **"Then I'll be a minute early for supper." / "Supper," he said.** I needed a second pass to see she was promising to be his repeat customer. It's very oblique.
- **Monday.** "After Monday, whatever the bank decides" and "would sit there on Monday": I take Monday to be March first, but this chapter never says so, and the date has been told in so many forms (thirteen days, March first, the last Saturday) that I'm not certain.
- **"That was the winter the ice got under the town dock."** Ray says it after the ski story. I took it as how he dates things, but it reads as a non sequitur.
- **Aunt Judy "from the bank."** Is that a plant or just color? It got my attention and then went nowhere.

## 4. Where I stopped believing

- **The storm call holds up.** Keeping everyone the way she did in December is the obvious alternative, and the page rules it out: a hundred and fifty people, forty-one of them children, a boiler that fails with the power, and the town wanting everyone off the roads by four. Rescheduling the evening for the Massachusetts teams is ruled out because they're driving home. That decision works.
- **Ray's check is the safer option, and only pride keeps it off the table.** It's in his jacket, he zips it up, and he says "I don't ask twice." She once said "I don't ask." So two people's private rules are being allowed to risk an 84-year-old woman's room at The Pines. The page explains it in character, but I was irritated, not moved. By now Ellen asking him would be the growth move, and she asks her daughter instead. That's in character, but it felt like the book steering around the obvious answer so it could reach the Kate callback.
- **"D-side says send them home."** Ray picks up her jargon the same afternoon and uses it in public, which is a little too neat. It's a line built to pay off the scene before it.
- **Dale's wife's envelope.** I liked it, but "He had walked his crew out of her building over Linda's cup, and he had entered her evening" explains a gesture that had already made its point.

## 5. Where it sounded like a machine

- *"She could not remember the last door she had stood at with nothing to hand anyone."* This states the chapter's theme in the second paragraph.
- *"which he had plainly done so long he no longer knew he did it, and she liked looking up at someone, which she seldom got to do."* Two explanations of a feeling, back to back.
- *"The heading was there in its place, fourth, where it had stood in every briefing she had given in her life, and she had never once had to put a thing like this under it."* This tells me what the moment means while it's still happening.
- *"The bar sat where the bank had set it and would sit there on Monday."* An authorial aside dropped into the middle of the refund line.
- *"The evening went down the drive in license plates."* A writerly flourish. It's good once, but it's built to be quoted.
- *"At work a silence that long meant somebody was writing something down, and she let Kate write."* The air-traffic filter now runs on everything. Here it's earned, but the book has used it so often that it's turned into a tic.
- **Recycled details** (they read as tics by Chapter 9):
  - The "white seam of an old scar along the side of one finger" (Chapter 1).
  - The ice going off "a long boom" (Chapter 6).
  - "the hum came first" at the gooseneck (Chapter 7, "The hum came first, as it always did").
  - The toggles taking the building dark one lane at a time (Chapter 7).
  - "Ray saw her sit" (Chapter 3, Chapter 6, and twice here).
  - Hollis calling at "ten past four this morning" (the same time as the fishing license in Chapter 6).
  - "the first thing of his she had accepted off an invoice since the gloves" (nearly word for word Ray's thought in Chapter 8).
  - The zipper pulled up to the chin over the check, again.
- **Every scene ends on a button.** The radio "started in on the inches." Ray following her up the catwalk with the radio and the wrench. The building going dark lane by lane. "Ten." Each one is placed. Together they feel assembled.

## 6. What I think is going on

Ellen makes the number. Kate gives her the yes the "Ten" all but promises, Ellen puts nearly all her savings on Arlene's note at eight to one, and Monday clears. The Sunday Memorial crowd and Denise's fries probably make up some of the gap, so it isn't only her money. Arlene keeps her room at The Pines. The K comes back from Laconia and the last image is the sign reading Kettle Lanes. Ray takes back the front key she's been carrying in her vest ("Not yet" pays off as "now"). Locker 14, ignored since Chapter 3, gets opened for Linda's Memorial and holds Linda's ball or shoes. Ray either bowls with it or hands it on, and I'd bet Denise wins Linda's cup. The Bench's pool square for March first, left blank in Chapter 1, comes back: either nobody wins or the money goes into the alley. Kate probably meets Ray. There's no secret left in this book. Everything still open is a ceremony waiting to happen.

**Prediction for Chapter 10:** Sunday at one. Kate's answer comes early, either confirmed in person or by her turning up. The league bowls Linda's Memorial on all ten lanes. Ray opens locker 14. The final frame of the Memorial is the climax ("The Last Frame"), with Denise or Ray on the last ball. Then Monday's bank number, then the K and the key. **How sure:** 85% on the number being made and the key going back on his ring, 90% on the K being lit by the end of the book, 60% that locker 14 is opened in Chapter 10, 55% that Denise wins the cup.

## 7. The listener pass

- **Unclear speakers aloud.**
  - "Read it back to me. I want to hear it in your voice." A listener will hear Ray, then hear Ray reading back, and get lost.
  - "Thirty-three in December. Today, this room, and a boiler that quits when the power does." Untagged.
  - "D-side says send them home." Untagged, but the line before it makes it recoverable.
  - "He pocketed the phone. 'Three strings a bowler…'" A voice won't make clear this is still Hollis.
- **Numbers read in full.**
  - "seventeen hundred and fifteen under thirty thousand"
  - "sixteen hundred dollars"
  - "four thousand short, and at eight to one"
  - "A hundred and fifty in the building, forty-one of them children, and I can hear all forty-one" (this one works aloud)
  - "Two thousand nine"
  - The money lines in particular are dead air in the ear.
- **Clock times stacked.** "a quarter past five," "ten past four," "after two," "by four," "at two," "at six," "Sunday at one," "a quarter to four," "four o'clock," "Sundays at seven." Aloud, the chapter sounds like a timetable.
- **Lane numbers as nouns.** "three's table," "A ball came through on four," "a strike went down on five," "the belt on six." A listener hears bare numbers with nothing attached. "Ray's hands were inside three's table to the wrist" is a scrambled opening sentence for a scene when you hear it.
- **HUTCHINS in capitals.** A TTS voice may spell it out or shout it.
- **Run of identical shapes.** The refund sequence: "The first went to… She said it to the second… The third said… The fourth had… The sixth said… The tenth went to…" Aloud it's a list. "Keep to the plowed side of Kettle Road. Drive slowly." three times is deliberate and mostly works, but by the third time I wanted it skipped.
- **Pronoun drift in the opening.** "Her hands were empty… They went into the pockets… the right one found… They came out again." A voice reading "They" right after "his key" briefly points at the wrong thing.
- **"Read it back" / "Readback correct."** These work aloud. They're the cleanest exchange in the chapter.
- **Kate's call** reads well aloud: short lines, clear speakers, real sounds on the line (the pan, the burner knob, the refrigerator).

## 8. The romance

Yes. This is the chapter where I believed them as a couple, and it made me laugh twice.

- *"Doc Moreau told me back about ninety-eight I ought to vary it, and I went home and gave it some thought, and I varied the bowl."*
- *"You finish the run?" / "All of it. Some of it standing." / "How much of it standing?" / "To the third turn."* Ray laughing until he wipes his eyes was the best laugh in the book since "Traffic, twelve o'clock, one mile."
- *"And you haven't sat down since Hollis called." / "Readback correct."* This is the payoff of the readback idea from the pit in Chapter 3, and it landed.
- *"Ray's shoulder shook once against hers."* (the auger man)
- *"Is anybody there with you?" / "Not in the camp. Ray Gagnon followed me out from the alley as far as the plow had been, and then he turned around."* This answers the question Kate never got answered in Chapter 5. It's good.

The catch is that once he says "I'm on it with you. Today, and Monday, and after," the relationship stops pulling. They're together. Nothing between them is still at risk.

---

## 9. What I want to know next

Plainly: **nothing urgent.** The questions left are formalities whose answers I can already see.
- Does Kate say yes? The "Ten" almost says it for her.
- Does Ellen make the number Monday? Genre says yes, and her savings covers it.
- Who wins Linda's cup on Sunday? Mild curiosity.
- What's in locker 14? This is the only real unknown left, and the book hasn't mentioned it since Chapter 3, so it isn't pulling at me. I had to remember it myself.
- Does the K get lit, and does Ray take back the key? I expect yes to both and want to see them, the way you want to see the last scene of a film you've already guessed.

I want the ending as a ceremony. Nothing in this chapter left me with a question I couldn't answer.

## 10. Best moment

> Ray looked at her standing there. Then he reached up again and turned it back on, louder than before, and kept his hand on the dial, and she stood in his hot kitchen with the napkin in her fist while the man on the radio started in on the inches.

It's a man who once missed half a sentence choosing to hear all of the bad news with her. Nobody explains it. It's the one place in the chapter where a gesture is left to do the work alone.
