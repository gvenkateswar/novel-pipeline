### Scene 1 — The ordinary scene, with the weather coming

**Setting:** Ray's house on School Street: his back step, then the kitchen. Wednesday, a quarter past five, dusk going to dark. **Sensory anchor:** the kitchen's heat on her face after the cold step, with hamburg and green pepper and tomatoes cooked down. One or two bible details at most: the yellow kitchen, the oilcloth, the dented pot, **the radio on top of the refrigerator** (required).

**Characters:** Ellen, Ray. A voice on the radio. No phone.

**Want:**
- **Ellen** wants to sit at his table with nothing to give him and nothing to brief, and to be asked back. **In the way:**
  - his front key, in her vest pocket since Ch 07, which she has decided not to offer again (he said "Not yet"), and which her hands keep finding;
  - the forecast, which she has heard twice today and has brought none of into his house;
  - her own habit of arriving with business in her hands.

  The reader learns the want at **"What do you make the second night?"**: she is asking to come back, sideways.
- **Ray** wants her at his table on his clock, and wants her to be the one who asks for the next time. The reader learns it when he opens the door before she can knock, and at "That one." Then he wants to keep the evening, and he gives that up when he sees her stand.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Open on her hands, empty, on his back step.** They go into her vest pockets, the right one finds his key, and they come out again. The left corner of the step is iced. She takes the step on her right leg and keeps the left back. She is one minute early.
   - **Bridge, one sentence, which carries the day on the page:** the invitation came pencilled under the total on his invoice for the belt on six: *Supper. Wednesday. A quarter past five. No charge.* She paid the invoice and kept the bottom of it. Writer's wording. "No charge" is the first thing of his she has accepted off an invoice since the gloves, and the reader should hear the joke in it.
2. **The radio through the storm door,** talking. She knows what it will get to at the half hour, because she heard the noon and three o'clock forecasts and the Saturday system has moved east both times. One clause of that, in her ordered way. It is the thing neither of them will say.
3. **He opens the door before she knocks.** Model: **"Heard you come up. You're early."** / **"By one minute."** / **"I'll allow it."** Do not restate the time; the invoice carried it.
4. **The kitchen.** He ducks the lamp over the table out of long habit. She likes looking up at someone, which she seldom gets to do (one clause, hers). Two bowls that don't match, and the pot.
5. **Ray, mock-solemn, in his own words (comic engine c):** one supper for thirty years, which has never failed him. A doctor once told him to vary it. Writer's choice. Do not use the bible sample's sentence. "Chop suey" once at most.
6. **The laugh, placed right after the boast so the reader cannot misread it:**
   - **Ellen:** "What do you make the second night?"
   - **Ray:** "That one."
   - One more exchange at most, from Ray, mock-solemn, with the weight on the last word: the pot has cooked for one for six years and has never had a repeat customer (writer's wording).
   - **Ellen:** "Then I'll be a minute early for supper."
   - **"Supper" gets no narration.** Ray hears it: he repeats the one word, "Supper," and goes back to his bowl. That is the whole beat.
7. **Ordinary pleasure (3–4 sentences):** the bowl too hot, eaten too soon, eaten anyway, salted right, hot through to the oilcloth under her wrists. Nobody comments on it.
8. **Interior passage 1 (the longing).** Her attention moves through the room and keeps landing on his hands: too big for the spoon, held at the end of the handle, the white seam of the scar, the grease no soap has got out. It is one long sentence that lands on a short one. **Do not explain the look**, and no "She had sat at a great many tables." Then he catches her, and it turns out he has been looking too:
9. **The knee.**
   - **Ray:** "What'd you do to the left one?" He saw her take his step, and the inside of her left boot heel is worn further down.
   - She tells it short and in order: **two thousand nine (said once, by her),** Kate at fourteen picked the trail for its black diamond and its name, Kate went down first and waited, and Ellen met a tree on the third turn.
   - "You finish the run?" / "All of it. Some of it standing." / "How much of it standing?" / "To the third turn." Ray puts his spoon down to laugh.
   - **Ray dates it by what broke,** in one clause, without saying the year: the winter the ice got under the town dock. No town history after it.
10. **The half hour.** The radio finishes the oil prices and starts the weather: *a storm coming up the coast, heavy snow Saturday, beginning before noon, accumulations of—*
    - Ray reaches up and turns it off. He is keeping the evening.
    - **Ellen stands up from his table**, with her napkin still in her hand. She doesn't say anything.
    - Ray looks at her standing, reaches up again, and turns it back on, louder than before.
    - **Cut mid-action:** the forecast going on in the hot kitchen, his hand still on the dial, her on her feet, the man reading the inches. Cut before the figure.

**Subtext:** This is repair beat four: two people who hurt each other, eating at the same table with no terms on it. She asks for the second night, which is the first thing she has asked him for that isn't business. "That one." is his yes, with the plain word last. Turning the radio off is tenderness. Turning it back up is the bigger tenderness, because he lets her have the bad news, and it is the first time the reader sees him be her D-side before she has a word for it. The key stays in her pocket, and neither of them says so.

**Texture budget:** One. The one supper and the one pot show a man who keeps things the same, offering that to her.

**Point of view:** Ellen. Quiet register with a clock under it: sentences of fifteen to eighteen words with real spread, one ordinary pleasure given room, and the comedy in the gap between a question and its answer. Her contractions are free.

**Ends on:** **Mid-action.** The forecast back on, his hand on the dial, her standing.

### Scene 2 — The relief briefing (the turn)

**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, the pit behind lane three, Saturday, after midday. **Sensory anchor:** the warmth coming off the motors along the catwalk, with grease and hot belt in it. Through the masking comes the crash of all ten lanes going at once, a full house, her pleasure, heard from behind. **Do not write the caged bulb or "backward and bright."** Pick one new visual: the ten pin-deck spots under three's table, or the masking curtain moving each time a ball comes through.

**Characters:** Ellen, Ray. Denise's voice on the radio at the end.

**Want:**
- **Ellen** wants Ray on the position with her, today and after Monday, however the bank goes. She wants to ask without it meaning she has been relieved. **In the way:** asking means someone has noticed she needs help, she has never asked him for anything to his face, and she has only one language to ask in. The reader learns the want at **"I want you on it with me."**
- **Ray** wants to be asked. He puts the wrench down and gets up off his knee to hear it.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Open on Ray's hands inside three's table,** bringing it down off the top of its stroke a notch at a time. Under the day's load it hung at the top with ten pins held over the deck, and a family stood at the line waiting for them. She came along the catwalk from the steel door, and she waits behind him until it sets.
   - **Ray, one line:** three has been half a second late at the top since December, and he let it ride. (Ch 04 payoff, and nothing more.)
2. A ball comes down three, and the machine sweeps and sets.
3. **The briefing. Ray stays on one knee and Ellen stands.** For the listener: tag the first item with her name, and put one physical beat from Ray or the machine between items, so no two quoted paragraphs run untagged.
   - **"I would like to give you a relief briefing."**
   - Ray: **"Go on, then."**
   - **"Weather. Hollis called at ten past four this morning. The ice is closed for the day, and he says the snow gets worse after two."**
   - Beat. Ray's hands still on the wrench.
   - **"Equipment. Three was late at the top of its table. You have just put it right."**
   - Beat. Three sets again.

### Scene 4 — The ask

**Setting:** Ellen's camp, Saturday night. She stands at the dark window over the lake. **Sensory anchor:** the ice booming under the new snow, starting near the camp and running away out of hearing. Nothing that ticks.

**Characters:** Ellen; Kate, by phone.

**Want:**
- **Ellen** wants Kate's answer *before* she decides, and she means to keep her word if the answer is no. It is the first time she has ever asked her daughter about something that wasn't already settled. **In the way:** she swore, she has always briefed Kate on outcomes, and Arlene's bed rides on Kate's answer. The reader learns the want at **"I'm calling to ask you something before I decide it."**
- **Kate** wants to be asked, and wants to know whether her careful mother has done something reckless. The reader learns it in her questions.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Open on the phone in her hand,** with Kate's name lit on it, at the window.
   - **One sentence, which answers "where did Ray go":** out on Kettle Road, the headlights that followed her car from the alley turn around at the end of the plowing and go back toward town.
   - On the table behind her lies the legal pad with this call written out in three items. She has left it there.
2. **Kate picks up,** with water running and a pan going onto a burner. Fast, clinical.
   - Kate: **"It's Saturday."**
   - Ellen: "It is. I checked."
   - Kate: "You call Sundays at seven—" The water goes off. **"Are you hurt?"**
3. **Ellen's answer and the ask in ONE paragraph (listener fix):** **"No. I'm calling to ask you something before I decide it."**
4. **Kate, one line.** Writer's choice: "Okay." or she turns the burner off. **Do not write "I'm sitting down" or "You're standing."**
5. **Ellen, one paragraph:** **"On the second of December I swore to you. The house, and not the rest. I want to break it."**
6. **Kate, fast, interrupting herself:** how much of the rest? Wait, it's the alley. Don't give her dollars or she'll be doing subtraction at the stove all night.
   - **Ellen:** **"Nearly all of it, if Sunday brings nothing."**
7. **Optional, one exchange, before the crux and never after "Ten":** Kate's Ch 05 question, answered at last. Kate: "Is anybody there with you?" Ellen answers truthfully and plainly: not in the camp; and there is someone, who followed her home as far as the plowing goes. Writer's wording, with Ray named or not. Ellen's line must not suggest she called Kate instead of him, or before something with him (the reader misread "No. I wanted to ask you first.").
8. **The crux. Kate: "And if I say no?"**
   - **Ellen, plainly, in one paragraph:** then she keeps it. Arlene Fortier loses her place at The Pines, and it will be Ellen's name that put her there. She says this because Kate asked, as a fact, without pleading.
9. Kate is quiet long enough that the refrigerator in Manchester comes down the line. Out past the point the ice goes again, farther off.
10. **Kate: "Scale of one to ten."**
11. **Ellen: "Ten."**
12. **Cut.** No reaction beat, no sound after it, no sitting down. Kate's answer is withheld.

**Speaker clarity (listener fix 8):**
- No two consecutive paragraphs by the same speaker.
- When Ellen answers a question and adds a line, both go in one paragraph.
- Kate gets audible business (water, burner, pan) or a tag at least every third line.
- No untagged run longer than four short paragraphs.

**Subtext:** Kate briefs her mother the way her mother briefs everyone, on purpose, and this is the first call in the book where the order is reversed. Ellen has asked, and has said what a no would cost and that she would obey it. "Ten" is all the reason she gives, which is a first for her. The reader now knows Kate can say no and that Ellen would take it, and the chapter stops there.

**Texture budget:** None, except Kate's clinical scale.

**Point of view:** Ellen. Plain, short, asking. Contractions are free.

**Ends on:** **Cut, answer withheld.** "Ten."

**Word target:** 400
