### Scene 1
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, lane one, Saturday Dec 19, about seven in the morning, before the first party at ten. Only lane one's lights are on; the other nine lanes run dark to their pits.

**Characters:** Ray, Ellen

**Want:**
- **Ray** came in early to give her the lesson he's owed her since the owner's ball, and under that he wants the morning after the sign without talking about it. The reader learns it from the two house balls in his hands, one of them the lightest on the rack, picked for her.
- **Ellen** wants to report her error before he can say good morning. The reader learns it from her first line.
- **In the way:** what it will cost her (him), and what it costs him to hear it after he has vouched on it.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. Ray at lane one with two house balls. He let himself in on the front key she laid on his counter, the first time he has used it (one clause). She comes out of the office with a clipboard against her chest and speaks before he can say good morning: **"Yesterday at your counter you told the Bench Linda's Memorial stays. You told them on my word. My word had two parts, and you heard one."**
2. Ray sets the heavier ball back on the rack in its place by weight and keeps the light one.
3. She gives the whole sentence once: "In the pit I said, It stays. Unless the numbers say otherwise. Denise's strike took the second half." Then what it means, once: **"If this winter comes up short, Linda's Memorial is the biggest day I have, and I will want it."**
4. "Read it back." He does, flat: "It stays unless the numbers say otherwise. If the winter comes up short, you'll want Linda's day." "Correct."
5. **"You gave me no readback, and I let it go."**
6. Ray: **"You knew. Under the sign?"** "Yes."
7. Why now, in her mouth, the trigger chain:
   - "The town had us by noon yesterday." Ray: "Normie had us by seven." (the small laugh on the tense page)
   - The Bench asked him whether she'd take Linda's day, and he told them "She told me it stays." Normie told Denise. At close, Denise asked Ellen whether that was what she'd said, and Ellen told her "It's half."
   - **The one line for what changed since "she would not":** "While it was the two of us, I could carry it. Hollis's light gave it to the whole town, and then you vouched for me at your counter on half my sentence."
   - The count: "Every error I ever made at work, I wrote up inside the hour. This one took me thirty hours and nineteen minutes. Denise asked me at eleven last night. I could have phoned you then. I am counting that too."
8. Ray's interior, one beat: Friday at his own counter with his hand flat on the wood, saying it to the Bench. They heard him, and so did Danny.
9. "Did you kiss me so I wouldn't ask?" "I kissed you because I wanted to. I kept quiet because the price of telling you was you."
10. Ray, in short sentences with no *and* (his anger register): **"Then hear mine. It stays."** Neither of them says a position again in this chapter.
11. A gap. He could go. He turns the light ball in his hands and doesn't go.

**Subtext:**
- She wants the kiss to survive the truth and won't ask whether it does.
- He wants to know whether it was real, asks, and gets an answer he believes. His staying is his answer, shown and never said.
- His "It stays" turns his vouching to the Bench into his own word: what he told them is still true, because he'll hold it.

**Texture budget:**
1. The readback, Ellen's one procedure, used to confess. It shows that her trade's form is how she pays.
2. The house balls racked by weight. It shows that when he's hurt he puts something in its place.

Count "Read it back" and "no readback" as one device against the chapter's ATC cap of six (Ch 04 is a set-piece chapter).

**Point of view:** Ray. Long patient sentences ending on a short one. Under anger, five-word sentences and no *and*. No name for her in this scene: "Mrs. Daly" is spent in S5. Show her calm from outside only (a thumb on the clipboard clip, a voice he has heard perfect over the PA). Narrate nothing from inside her.

**Ends on:** **question.** He holds the light ball out across the ball return: **"Lane's open. You turn it loose a foot off the floor and it lands like a dropped plate. Want that fixed?"** Cut before she answers. Her answer is the first image of S2.

**Word target:** 850 · **Word floor:** 800. This is her accountability beat, the self-report, and the sample reader called the compressed version "never a problem". The floor keeps it played, every exchange in real time.

### Scene 2
**Setting:** Lane one, about twenty minutes later, the approach still cold. Ray's knee on the cold boards at the foul line.

**Characters:** Ray, Ellen

**Want:**
- **Ray** wants to fix her throw, because fixing is how he says what he can't, and he wants to stay near her after what she told him. The reader learns it from his line: "Under my hand."
- **Ellen** wants to get one box right on her own lane, and she has let him show her, which she never allows. The reader learns it from the opening image: the small ball already in her bare hands.
- **In the way:** her high release, and the morning between them.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. Enter late, on her third box. Ray kneels beside the foul line and holds his flat hand out, palm down, six inches off the boards: "Under my hand."
2. To get under his palm she has to bend, and for once she's looking down at him. He looks up at her from the boards and loses what he was going to say next. "Is there a next instruction?" "There was. Give me a minute." (longing and laugh in one)
3. "Let the lane have it." Her ball goes in low and quiet, takes more wood than she has taken all winter, and leaves two pins lying across the deck.
4. She starts to want a reset, as Verna gave one on her first night. Ray: **"The wood stays on the lane. You play what's left."** (its one use in the book)
5. He gets up off his knee and steps back. He's the one who looks away first.

**Subtext:** "You play what's left" is the morning: what's down stays down, and you play it as it lies. Neither of them hears it said, and the writer doesn't point at it.

**Texture budget:**
1. The candlepin delivery: a low release and no hook. It shows that he teaches by putting his hand where hers should be.
2. The wood rule. It shows a man who plays what's left, which is why he's still here.

**Point of view:** Ray. What he finds attractive in her: her mouth when she's trying not to laugh, her bare hands, and that she bowls badly in front of him and doesn't pretend otherwise. There's no touch in this scene.

**Ends on:** **open image.** Her second ball is on the lane, low, rolling at the wood lying across the deck. Cut before it arrives.

**Word target:** 350

### Scene 3
**Setting:** The pit, the catwalk behind the machines, mid-afternoon on party Saturday. Holiday parties on all ten lanes; the alley's best day; no figure. The anchor is the warmth coming off ten motors at once, and a radio on Ray's belt.

**Characters:** Ray; Ellen (radio); Denise (radio)

**Want:**
- **Ellen** wants every lane running and paid for on her biggest day: her move toward the bank's number, which the Ch 02 cold-read flag asked for. The reader learns it from her first transmission.
- **Ray** wants to keep her ten machines going, and he wants her voice. The reader learns it when he turns the radio up while she's talking to somebody else.
- **In the way:** the machines, and her procedure, which turns everything he says into a correction.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **First exchange.** Ellen: "Kettle Maintenance, lane nine has a birthday at the half hour, and nine's ball return is running slow." It's information only; she hasn't asked for anything. Ray, doing her phraseology badly on purpose: "Roger that, copy, ten-four, wilco, over and out." Ellen: **"Wilco means you will comply. I have not asked you for anything yet."**
2. He clears nine's return (one line). A machine may sulk once if the writer wants it (2 of 3).
3. Denise comes on the snack-bar radio without a call sign. In one run-on, answering her own questions, she reports a twelve-year-old at the snack bar in house shoes he could sleep in, who says they're fine. Ellen answers in one line. Ray, on the catwalk, turns the radio up.
4. **Second exchange.** Four has gone quiet on her end. Ellen: "Kettle Maintenance, say intentions." Ray: "Intentions are to quit and go home." Ellen: **"Denied. Lane four."** He goes along the catwalk to four.
5. **Third exchange (the turn).** At four, with his hands in the machine: "Kettle Maintenance, the last Saturday in February. How many lanes does Linda's Memorial take?" Ray: **"All ten. I drew the lanes at Thanksgiving."** She doesn't answer him. Ray hears where his answer has gone: onto the sheet where she keeps the winter.

**Subtext:**
- Under the jokes is the morning.
- "I have not asked you for anything yet": she will (Ch 05).
- "Say intentions" is his intentions.
- The Memorial question shows each position by what they do: she's counting Linda's day, openly; he drew it weeks ago. Nobody argues it.

**Texture budget:**
1. The radio phrasing, the chapter's one ATC set piece: three exchanges plus Denise. Each has to be legible to a listener who has never heard a radio, because the meaning is in the next line.
2. One machine fix, one line.

Ellen's ATC count here is say intentions, wilco and denied. Ray's pile is mockery and doesn't count against her.

**Point of view:** Ray. In the noise of the pit he hears her voice whole, the voice that carries across ten lanes without being raised. One clause of that, not a paragraph; he heard it come down a lane in Ch 02. He doesn't think about why she bought the alley; Ch 05 owns that.

**Ends on:** **cut.** Her next transmission isn't to him. "Snack Bar, two baskets for lane six." She's already on the next item. Cut there.

**Word target:** 400

### Scene 4
**Setting:** Lanes nine and ten, about four, Gagnon & Son's staff party, which Ray books and pays for every year. A sheet cake on the scorer's table, paper cups of birch beer, the burnt-orange settee.

**Characters:** Ray, Danny, Normie (two clerks and the rest of the Bench unnamed). Ellen arrives for the last beat only, without a line.

**Want:**
- **Ray** wants to hand Danny the store whole and then be asked back, instead of turning up at six to count a drawer Danny already counted. The reader learns it from the last words of his speech: "If he asks."
- **In the way:** two winters of not saying it; his own habit of recounting; and the date, because March first is also hers.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. The Bench is at the staff party, which it isn't, for the free cake, the way it comes for the free coffee (one line, a laugh in narration).
2. At the cake, Ray stands up, unasked.
3. **Interior, paragraph 1.**
   - A thing said in front of the Bench is said.
   - He has put it off two winters.
   - Lucien was behind that counter the week he died, counting Ray's drawer after him. Ray wants to hand it over whole, which his father never managed.
   - Under that is the thing he won't say even to himself in this paragraph: somewhere to go after supper.
4. **The speech,** in his *and*-chains with the important word last: **"Thing is, I've been retiring after the season since about the winter the roof rakes came in short, and Danny counts a drawer better than I do, and I count it again behind him anyway. March first, the store's his. I'll come in Saturdays. If he asks."**
5. Danny has waited two winters for this. He holds his paper cup in both hands and for a second doesn't raise it. Then: **"To Dad's last winter behind the counter. He swears it's real this time."**
6. Normie, from the end of the settee with his cup on his knee: **"Then he can go stand behind hers."** Lanes nine and ten laugh.
7. Ellen is ten feet off on the walkway behind the settees with the cake knife, which Danny asked the snack bar for and she brought down herself. She heard it. Ray watches her face go calm, the calm she told him about in the pit, and he knows now what it costs her.
8. **Interior, paragraph 2.**
   - The two things he kept in separate drawers come out as one sentence in Normie's mouth: the store to Danny on March first, and the building on March first.
   - The sentence isn't wrong. For two winters he meant to give Danny the store and go up Kettle Road evenings to Arlene's alley, and he came up the drive eight days after Ellen did.
   - If she misses, the building comes to him the same day the store leaves him.
9. Ellen holds the knife out, handle first. He takes it. She goes back up the walkway.

**Subtext:**
- Danny's joke is love and two winters of doubt.
- Ray's recount is his shame, said as a joke on himself.
- "Hers" is Ellen.
- O2 is seen: every no he will ever say her is to his profit, and he did want the building. He says none of it.

This scene is where the sample reader called the hand-off "a business announcement". It isn't one if every line is father and son and the date does the damage.

**Texture budget:** One piece: the drawer (counting twenties first). It shows the want and the habit he wants to stop.

**Point of view:** Ray. One memory clause each, no paragraph of backstory. Keep the staging new: no Bench going quiet, no clock watched, no coffee pot ticking (all Ch 02's). Don't name Normand on this page; Normie speaks here.

**Ends on:** **consequence.** He doesn't say any of it. He cuts the cake. This is the chapter's one withheld-action/prop ending.

**Word target:** 700

### Scene 5
**Setting:** The bottom of the drive under the sign, 5:20 p.m., full dark, between the afternoon and evening parties. Ray's truck idles at the mouth of the drive with the door open and the heater running for nobody. Over them is the dead K; at their feet is Friday's trench, drifted half full.

**Characters:** Ray, Ellen

**Want:**
- **Ellen** wants him paid, so that nobody, Normie included, can call his help the backup buyer minding his own building. The reader learns it from her line: "From Monday, everything you fix in this building, you bill me for."
- **Ray** wants to give her something and not go home to the quiet house. The reader learns it from "I could have her lit by Christmas", and from his not getting into the truck.
- **In the way:** twenty years of giving for nothing, and what a bill costs her winter.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. Ray stands out of the truck looking up at the dark letter. Ellen comes down the drive in her vest without a coat, bare hands; she saw the truck stop from the glass block.
2. Ray: **"I eat supper at a quarter past five. Have for thirty years. It's five-twenty."** "Then go." He doesn't go.
3. **The quote.** He looks up: "I could have her lit by Christmas." (The Laconia shop bends tubes, one clause.) Ellen: **"Send me a quote."** He looks at her until she says what's under it. "I heard Normie." "Whole two lanes heard Normie."
4. **Her rule, said as a rule, once:** "From Monday, everything you fix in this building, you bill me for. Your hours and your parts, in my format. Then nobody in this town can say you are minding your own building."
5. "Haven't got a rate." "You have the Worcester man's. It's in your shirt pocket." His hand goes to the pocket. It's there: he has moved it shirt to shirt since the third. She knew. (longing and laugh)
6. "Twenty years, Arlene never got a bill off me." "I'd like to know what I owe you."
7. **Ray's interior, one line:** he takes it for his own reason. A bill is the only answer to Normie's sentence he has.
8. **The cost, said once:** "Every dollar you pay me comes off the winter your bank is counting." Ellen: **"If I miss, I'll miss by an honest bill."**
9. He stands closer than a quote needs. Neither of them says this is where they stood yesterday at dawn.

**Subtext:**
- She's protecting him from Normie's sentence as much as she's protecting herself.
- The bill is how he gets to stay.
- He's late for supper because the house is quiet from a quarter to six on; this is unsaid, and Ch 05 s3 and Ch 09 s1 own it.

**Texture budget:**
1. The neon K, one clause (the Laconia shop). It shows a man who offers before he's asked.
2. The invoice terms. They show a woman who answers a gift with a form, and that the form is his own trade's.

**Point of view:** Ray. Tender lines are about her hands or what she's holding, with the plain word last. "Mrs. Daly" is used here once: the chapter's one use.

**Ends on:** **reversal.** "Then you'll be paying me, Mrs. Daly." / **"Ms. Daly. Net thirty."** Her line is the chapter's last; no reaction beat after it. The man who fixed the building for nothing for twenty years is her contractor, at her request, because of a sentence Normie said and neither of them could answer. This is the chapter's one placed button. It isn't an ask.

**Word target:** 700
