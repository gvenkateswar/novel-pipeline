# Ch 04 cold-read triage (orchestrator, autopilot)

Verdict: the reader would keep reading. The pit scene and the bank email are the best moments so far. The remaining problem is sameness: everyone is calm and precise, and Ellen comes out ahead every time. One revision round: a prose-writer targeted pass, then a voice-editor pass.

Don't change plot facts, money figures, ATC phrases, the pit scene's "Traffic, twelve o'clock, one mile" and the 187 story, the marble cake answer, or Ray's lender email. Keep the length at 3,300–3,500.

## Ellen must lose ground on the page

1. **Office argument (S3).** Ray wins this one.
   - Cut Ellen finding the breaker panel in the dark, and cut Ray's thought about needing to feel along the wall.
   - Instead, Denise flips the breaker from the counter ("Sorry!" can stay), or the lamp just comes back. Ellen does not get a competence beat here.
   - Let "On whose money?" land, then give Ray an answer that scores. Ellen has no comeback before the interruption. One or two lines.
   - Keep the interruption, but have Ray finish his point first. The reader felt Denise's entrance saved Ellen.
2. **The room pushes back (S2).** At least one stranded bowler makes a real scene about being charged. For example, a Highway Department man whose kid is home with a sitter, or someone loud about Arlene's free three days in '78.
   - Ellen holds her position, and it costs her. Somebody leaves the line, or sits out unpaid and doesn't bowl, and she lets them.
   - Keep it short, about 100–150 words, and take the space from the logistics list.
3. **Cut the clap** after "Everyone in this building is a guest," if there is one. The line stands with no applause.

## Clarity

4. **$11,800.** Make clear it's the season's running total, up from $5,600 on December 19. Something like "the season's running total."
5. **Date.** Anchor it once early: Thursday, January 7, the Thursday league night. Use the day and date once.
6. **"Mr. Pike."** Hollis's surname hasn't been used before. Use "Ray and Hollis" or "Mr. Gagnon and Hollis."
7. **"He had spent six years planning to change nothing."** Cut it. It's a summing line, and the six years are unexplained.
8. **Hollis's background.** Spread the info lump about him (plowing her lot since December, the derby, the scanner) over two beats, or cut what this chapter doesn't need.
9. **The derby and the championship on the same weekend.** If Ellen says "I know," give one clause on how she knows, for example that it's on Denise's calendar or the derby poster in the vestibule. Otherwise cut the exchange.
10. **"Denise," Ellen said. "The Friday party list."** Add a beat between Ellen's two consecutive lines.

## Voice and sameness

11. **Hollis checks the sky.** Keep the first instance, cut the second, and change the third to a different action.
12. **The fella stories.** No more than two in the chapter. Cut the one that most feels like a lounge-act request.
13. **Denise's PR line.** Cut "Coffee's free all night, and that was her idea, so don't thank me." Give her one line of skepticism instead, about the free coffee costing money or the hot dogs.
14. **Summing lines.** Cut or rewrite:
   - "That was the whole meeting."
   - "They will remember that I called." (unless it's dialogue that matters)
   - "Ray noticed the *your* and couldn't tell which of them it was for."
   - "He had been reading invoices upside down across a hardware counter for forty years."
   - any other "for thirty/forty years" skill line
15. **Clipped register.** Loosen at least two exchanges so a character talks in a longer, messier way. Good candidates are Hollis at the plow (let him ramble about the plow's hydraulics) or Verna's ruling (formal and long-winded). Not everyone speaks in four-word sentences.
16. **Endings.** Keep S4's mid-motion ending. Let S1 and S3 end less neatly. The chapter can still end on "You going home now?" with no answer, but cut anything after it that reads as a button.
