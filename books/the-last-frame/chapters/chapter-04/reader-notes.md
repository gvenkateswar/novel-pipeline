# Reader Notes: Chapter 4, "Say Intentions"

**Draft read:** final.md, tournament winner, Oct 29. (Read chapters 01 through 04 final.md in order. No other files.)

**Warning above the score:** I'd keep reading, but this is the slowest chapter so far, and it comes straight after the kiss. Chapter 3 ended on the kiss and Denise calling "Honey." from the door. Chapter 4 never gets back to either. The chapter is about a clipboard, a bank fee, a lineage notice and a 2005 bylaw. The radio scene is what kept me reading.

---

## 1. Pull score: 5 / 10

The radio flirting is the best stretch in the book. Everything around it is money and procedure, the morning after the kiss gets skipped, and the only conflict set up for later is a league meeting in January, which feels weeks away. I'd turn the page because the last image (the chair pushed out with her boot) is nice, not because I need to know anything.

## 2. Where I got bored

- **Opening, paragraph 4:** "You ran every machine once before a party day, empty, and you listened. He took a ball off the rack and walked the line, throwing a full box down every lane, three balls apiece..." The last chapter ended on a kiss in front of thirty-three people, and the next one opens with a maintenance routine. I wanted Ray thinking about her. What I got was Ray testing ten machines. "Three came round half a second late at the top of her table, and he put that away for later" is filed for later, and I had nothing to file it against.
- **Middle of the hardware-store scene, the bank letter:** "Says here the bank in Concord'll lend you whatever the appraiser puts on the alley, if it comes to you buying it. Says that's good through New Year's Eve, and then it's gone. Says they'll hold it to March first for a fee..." Three "Says" sentences of loan terms. My eyes slid off them. Danny's "If she makes it, Dad, what've you bought?" brought me back, but I'd already drifted.
- **Office scene, the numbered bylaw exchange (about four-fifths through):** "December of two thousand four. Posted on the board in Arlene's hand, and charged from the next league night." / "Second. Paragraph four says lineage stands as set in September, 'as the league's bylaws provide.'" This is where I drifted worst. It's two people walking a document trail one clause at a time. The payoff, "Who put it there?" / "I did.", is good, but I had to reread the run-up to understand what "the form" was and which notice was which.
- More generally, there's no clock in this chapter. "Best Saturday in Arlene's ledgers" is the only progress number. I don't know how far she is from thirty thousand, so the money talk doesn't feel urgent.

## 3. Where I got confused

- **The first line.** "Ray drove past the alley in the dark and kept going out Kettle Road." For a paragraph I didn't know where he was going or why. He looks at her lit kitchen and turns around. I can read it as lost nerve after the kiss, but it never comes up again, so I can't tell whether it was nerves or just how he got to the alley. I also didn't know what day it was until Normie, halfway through the chapter, says the kiss was Friday. Only then did I know this was the next morning.
- **"Three came round half a second late at the top of her table."** Three what? (Lane three, I worked out.) And whose table, Ellen's or the machine's?
- **The loans.** Chapter 2 had the credit union committee at five, with Danny's signature putting up the store. Now there's a bank in Concord. Did Ray apply there? When? Is it the same backstop he signed for Arlene? In chapter 1 he says the contract "sells it to me by suppertime," which sounded like an obligation. Here it sounds optional: if the loan lapses, "there'd be nobody to buy it." Which is it?
- **"I checked the name on it twice."** Checked whose name? Is Danny surprised that Ray applied at all, or that it's in Ray's name and not the store's? I couldn't tell what Danny meant.
- **The new pool sheet.** "Nothing on it said what it was for." I assume it's on when they get together, or get engaged, and that Danny holding Valentine's is the joke. I liked it, but I'm guessing.
- **Seven hundred dollars a week.** Why does the league cost her $700 a week? Heat, staff, cheap lineage? Stated once, never explained.

## 4. Where I stopped believing

- **Nobody mentions the kiss.** Not Ray, not Ellen, not Denise, who was standing in the door saying "Honey" at the end of the last chapter and is at the register all day here. They're alone in the office at the end of a fourteen-hour day, a foot apart, with the door shut, and they talk about bylaws. I believe these two are reticent. I don't believe Denise is. Her silence is the author holding back, not the character.
- **Ray in her building before her.** He lets himself in at dawn and runs all ten lanes on her power, and all she asks is "Why are all ten running?" She asked for a key in chapter 1 and called a locksmith over it. Chapter 3 half-explains this (she wanted him able to walk in without asking), so I mostly bought it, but I noticed it.
- **The bylaw being Ray's.** The one clause that blocks her number turns out to have been written by him. It's set up well enough (he protects the league), but it's convenient that the obstacle is personally his.
- **Ray being obliged to buy.** See confusion above. If he's contractually bound to buy on March 1, then paying the fee to keep his financing isn't a gesture. It's what he has to do. The scene plays it as a gesture ("I'd sooner it was me"). The page never says which.
- No danger choices in this chapter, so there's no safer-option check to run.

## 5. Where it sounded like a machine

- **Ray hears everything as a motor.** "Under the band her pulse was going quick, the way a motor runs fast before it's warmed through." Then: "under it he heard the small change you hear in a motor the second before it catches." Chapter 2 already had her breathing as "a clutch was about to go." That's three motor-equals-feelings comparisons in two Ray chapters, two of them in this one.
- **The "a beat, then another" move again.** "He held on a beat longer than the lesson needed, and then another." Chapter 3: "She did not look away for a beat after she had any reason to, and then for one more." Chapter 1: "For a second, and then for longer than a second." Every chapter uses the same rhythm for the same feeling.
- **"Sulking."** "She was only sulking," he told the radio. Chapter 2 already had "Sulking, is all" and "she'll go slower out of spite." The machines-have-moods bit is wearing thin.
- **First, Second, Third.** Ellen's numbered items show up in every chapter, and here they're split across dialogue: "First." / "Second." / "Third." It was charming in chapter 1. By chapter 4 I can hear the template.
- **The narrator explains the gestures.** "His hands had come out of his jacket pockets without his say-so." "He took longer over it than two words take." "He had nothing left in reach to fix." After the screw-tightening at the shelf, that last one tells me what the gesture already showed.
- **Every scene ends on a button.** "...it went off down lane one toward the wood." / "...read the address, moving his lips." / "'Unable.'" / "...and left it there, facing her." Four scenes, four tidy final images. All of them are good, and that's the problem: none of them ever lets a scene stop in the middle.
- **Normie's sportscaster line.** "And here comes the veteran... boys" is the third use of the same bit. Funny once, routine now.
- "Ray had to look down at the notice." The "had to" tells me what he felt instead of showing it.

## 6. What I think is going on

Ellen will clear most of the thirty thousand on parties and on the snowstorm kind of luck, but she can't make it without the lineage increase. So the January meeting becomes the midpoint fight: Ray stands up against her in front of the league, and she loses the vote or wins it in a way that costs her with him. Her "third item" from the pit is something about him, or about being afraid she's coming apart again with no tape to tell her. The clause Ray missed under Denise's strike ("unless the numbers say otherwise") will come back late: to make her number she'll need all ten lanes for paying business on the last Saturday in February, which is Linda's Memorial, and Ray will find out she said it and he didn't hear it. That's the black moment. Kate is Ellen's daughter. Ellen sold her house to buy this and swore on the phone not to touch "the rest" (her retirement money, I think), and she'll be tempted to break that oath before March 1. Locker 14 holds Linda's ball and shoes. Ray will open it near the end, probably to give the ball to Denise for the Memorial or to give Ellen the key. Ending: March 1, Ellen comes up a little short and Ray does not exercise his right to buy; or he buys and they run it together. Ray lights the K on the sign in the last chapter, despite "it is not on my list this winter." Danny wins the Valentine's square, or close to it. Denise wins the Memorial cup.

## 7. The listener pass

- **The office bylaw exchange has no speaker tags for about a dozen lines** ("Arlene put this same notice up once" through "And Verna said."). Read by one voice, "First." / "Had a feeling there'd be a first." / "December of two thousand four..." / "Took it down to the commas, I'd guess." / "Second..." keeps swapping speakers, and by ear I lost who said "Took it down to the commas." The one-word "First." "Second." "Third." lines sound like a list being read, not a person talking.
- **The bank letter** is three sentences starting "Says" with loan terms. Aloud it sounds like reading a document, which it is.
- **"Three came round half a second late at the top of her table."** By ear, "Three" sounds like a count, not a lane.
- **"Normie had him before it stopped."** Heard, "had him" is unclear (spotted him? got the line in?).
- **Denise's radio rant ends on an em-dash cutoff** ("bowl in the ones you came in—"). A text-to-speech voice won't cut off. It'll just stop, and "The click cut her off" lands late.
- **"R. GAGNON" and "GAGNON & SON"** in caps. Fine aloud, but the caps do nothing for the ear. "D.G." reads as "D G", fine.
- **The radio exchanges work very well aloud.** "Pit, Counter" is clear once set up, and Ray's one-breath readback ("Counter, I've got it that nine's ball return is taking her sweet time...") is better heard than read.
- **Sentence shape:** a long run of "He [did X], and [did Y], and [did Z]" chains in the hardware-store back room (checkbook, tear, envelope, thumb along flap, twice). Aloud they have the same rhythm.
- **Opening paragraph:** "A mile on, the plowing stopped at a summer cottage somebody had winterized, on the water, and that was where she rented." Aloud, "where she rented" arrives late, after three clauses, so the listener doesn't know whose cottage it is until the end.

## 8. The romance

Yes, more than in any chapter except the kiss.

- The radio. "Roger that, copy, ten-four, wilco, over and out." / "One of those will do. Ten-four is the state police. Over and out asks me to answer you and tells me not to." / "Wilco means you will comply. I have not asked you for anything yet." I laughed.
- "Negative on the last item." / "Which item was that, Counter?" Then the open channel with her voice missing for a full second, and "Nine." That's the scene where I wanted them together.
- The wrist. "It is nine minutes past seven. I have been counting." / "The face of it was still under his palm. He took her word for it." She can't see the watch, so she's counting how long he's been holding her wrist. Lovely.
- "If somebody's going to be standing at the bottom of your drive on March first, I'd sooner it was me."
- "I'm very good with a clock." / "Good. I'd hate to beat somebody who wasn't ready." Good. Then the chair hooked out with her boot.
- "No. I hire out what I am not qualified for, and so should you. You are a pinsetter man. That is a neon tube." That made me laugh too.

What's missing is the kiss. All of this romance is sideways, which suits these two, but a chapter after a first kiss with no direct word about it makes me feel the book is avoiding its own big moment.

## 9. What I want to know next

- When are they going to say anything about the kiss? Does he sit in the chair?
- Does Ray ever find out about "unless the numbers say otherwise," and is the Memorial in danger?
- What did Ellen swear to Kate, and what is "the rest"?
- What's in locker 14?
- How close is she to thirty thousand? I don't know, so I can't worry about it.

Honest finding: none of these is pressing at the end of this chapter. The January vote is the only new question, and it's set far away. The chair gives me a mild "and then?" The rest are questions held over from chapters 1 to 3. This chapter adds no question of its own that I need answered tonight.
