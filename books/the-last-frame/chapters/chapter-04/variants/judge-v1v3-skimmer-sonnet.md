2. Why: A has people doing things to each other in a room (the knuckles stopping an inch under his palm, "You'll want that hand in your vest. Pen'll keep.", Denise and the house shoes), where B keeps handing me a man with a clipboard and a ledger.

3. Where A lost me: ~58%, "A thing said in front of the Bench was said. You didn't get it back; they kept everything they heard on a shelf in their heads, dated, the way he kept a part on a nail..." That is the store-handover paragraph (the drawer counted twice, twenties first, Lucien, the keys). My thumb sped up through to "Under that was something else... and he left it down there." The opening block of dialogue (~8-15%, "Thirty hours and nineteen minutes", "I am counting that too") is the closest thing to a ledger. It is short and the "Read it back" beat is a game, so I stayed. The A1 danger (procedure, codes, couple doing business) is present but she gets a laugh and a flinch in it.

4. Where B lost me: ~45%, "Behind the open line the adding machine ran one short line and stopped." That comes right after "The draw was on the league clipboard on the seat of his truck, in his own hand, as it had been every November there'd been a Memorial to draw." Clipboard, tape, draw, adding machine, sheet: that is a man alone with paperwork, and the couple isn't in the room (she's in the office behind a shut door). Earlier, "Ten machines going at once on a party Saturday put out a warmth Ray could feel in his face from the end of the catwalk..." (~33%) is a slow machine paragraph. The ending also runs long on Ray's "Twenty years I had my head in those machines, and the boiler twice, and Arlene fed me a hot dog..." (~88%). I slowed there.

5. Confidence: medium. The chapters are nearly the same skeleton and many lines are identical. B has a couple of lines I liked better ("Then you bill me for the stop," "Near enough is not a quote"), so it is not a blowout.
