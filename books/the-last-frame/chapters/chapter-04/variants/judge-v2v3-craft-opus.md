# Judge report: Chapter 4, v3 (A) vs v2 (B)

1. **Winner: A.**

2. **Why:** Both chapters follow the same steps, but A writes the moments that count with more nerve. The best example is her watch under his palm, where she couldn't have read it. Another is the ledger falling open at December because she'd already been there. And she watches him with her head tilted, the same way he listens to a building. B is chattier and gets more laughs ("Wilco means you will comply"), but at the turns it explains itself ("He held it a beat longer than the lesson needed, and then another"). A just lets the hand stay.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 4%, the walk down the lanes: "Five and six came round together like two old sisters on the church steps. Eight was a half beat late setting, and had been since the winter the cellar pump quit." That's a man alone with ten machines, and I skimmed until her car came up the drive. A smaller snag at about 30%: "It was a dollar." has no speaker. B had cut it from "That's your writing," and without that line I had to go back to work out that Danny said it.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 82%: "'Three things,' Ellen said. 'First. December of two thousand four. Posted on the vestibule board and charged from the next league night, in Arlene's hand.'" It's the numbered list again, in clipped fragments, and the scene turns into a hearing. A has the same three points but runs them as speech, and it reads better. A second, softer spot at about 8%: "he wrote a six on the back of his hand with his pencil," which shows he's competent and does nothing else.

5. **Confidence: low.** Close to a coin flip. B has the better comedy in the radio section (the wilco exchange, Normie's "No comment from the veteran at this time," the whistling nobody heard, "You did not bill lane one"). A Buyer-persona judge could fairly pick it. I chose A because its sentences carry the longing and B's tell me about it.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. They share the scenes, much of the dialogue word for word, and the same motor similes. These are two drafts of one chapter.
