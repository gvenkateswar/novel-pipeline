A = variants/v2.md. B = variants/v1.md. Persona: Buyer. Model: Sonnet.

1. Winner: A

2. Why: In A the Bench party scene gives Ray a speech that sounds like a stubborn old man, and Danny's toast and Ellen standing there with the knife make it cost something. B says the same thing in half the room, so it reads like summary.

3. Where A lost me: the paragraph that sets up the party, at about 55%: "Danny had the sheet cake out on the scorer's table, white, with GAGNON & SON across it in green, and a row of paper cups of birch beer going flat beside it..." It's a prop list, and I moved my thumb down it. A smaller slip came at about 88%, when the bill talk starts ("From Monday, everything you fix in this building, you bill me for"). After the kiss, that's another money conversation. "Is there a next instruction?" and "Net thirty" kept it from losing me outright.

4. Where B lost me: at about 88%, the invoice haggling: "Say I'm driving by and I happen to stop." / "Then you bill me for the stop." It's a couple doing business, and the "bearing with grit" line doesn't make it more than that. I also drifted at about 45%, when Ray's own call-and-answer details turn to housekeeping: "The draw was on the league clipboard on the seat of his truck, in his own hand..." Ray's retirement speech at about 60% is also too short to land. "March first, the store's his. I'll come in Saturdays" arrives before I've felt what it costs him.

5. Confidence: medium. The two are the same chapter with the same beats and the same ending, and it comes down to texture. B has two good touches that A lacks: the matching radio click from the walkway at his back, and "which pocket it went in." A is warmer and funnier, and the people sound more like themselves.

6. Same-author check: Yes. Same beats, same running jokes, and many lines are word for word.
