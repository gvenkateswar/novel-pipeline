1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** The two chapters share a skeleton, but in B the two of them get more room to spar. Ellen settles the shoe kid with "If he arrives in them, they're fine," and the end scene has "Near enough is not a quote" / "Then you bill me for the stop." Ray gets one long, rambling Arlene-and-the-hot-dog speech that sounds like nobody but him. A gives too many of those beats to the narrator, and the narrator gives a feeling to every object in the building: the boiler is sore, the heater takes it personally, the pot "would know."

3. **Where A lost me:** About 72%, the cake scene, where the chapter stops to lay out the stakes: "If she missed, the contract she'd signed would sell him the building the same afternoon the store went out of his hands. Any no he said to her between here and March would be a no that paid him." I already had this from Normie's one line, and here it's spelled out again. Earlier, about 3%, I also skimmed "On the drive out he had decided there were two ways to go at this morning," which was setup before she had even come through the door.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 70%: "Up at the snack bar somebody keyed a radio and let go without a word. The click came off Ray's belt, and with it the same click again, smaller, from the walkway at his back." I could feel the radio being moved into place to tell me she was behind him. Right after it comes the same stakes paragraph A has ("He'd kept two things in separate drawers two winters…"), and I skimmed that too.

5. **Confidence: low.** It's nearly a coin flip. A has the better single lines ("except that this morning he'd wiped his feet," "There was nothing on the bottom of it he hadn't already seen on top," "You'll want that hand in your vest. Pen'll keep."). A also has the better cake gesture, Ray handing the first piece to Normie, where B just has "He cut the cake." But A's ball-turning habit happens three times in one scene, and its talking machinery reads like the narrator showing off. B is plainer, and I like the people in it more when they're talking to each other.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Same scenes, same dialogue lines word for word in places, same narrator; only the amount of decoration differs.
