2. **Why:** A puts her in the room with him fast and keeps them together, and the bank business goes by in one paragraph Danny reads off a page. B made me sit through a phone call with a loan officer and a foreclosure explanation in the dark by the truck, so my thumb moved twice.

She numbers off First, Second, Third about bylaws and a binder, and I skimmed to "Who put it there?" That line and "I did." pulled me back right away. (~3%), a man alone with the machines, but she's through the door four lines later.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Appraised value, Mr. Gagnon, and not a dollar over. The commitment's good through the thirty-first." (~40%). It's a whole phone call about commitments and extension fees, and I skimmed it to Danny's question. (~78%), followed by "Read paragraph four, Mrs. Daly," which is a contract clause. Smaller drifts: the boiler/fifty-four degrees/nine-or-ten small talk at ~10%, which the book itself admits is stalling, and "He had always kept time by what broke..." at ~65%, a man alone in the pit listening to machines.

5. **Confidence: high.** Both chapters share the wrist lesson, "Unable," and the clock exchange. A gets to each of them with less paperwork in between, and it ends with her hooking out the chair, which I want to see what happens after. B ends with him unable to read her face, which made me want to stop.
