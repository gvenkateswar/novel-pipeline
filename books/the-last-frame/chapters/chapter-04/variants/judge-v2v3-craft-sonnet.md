1. Winner: A

2. Why: A sounds like someone is telling it. The boiler that "thought better of it," Ray holding the small ball three separate times while he decides to stay, and Denise's "He may keep his opinion" all give the building and the people their own voices. B is clean and correct but reads flatter, with more "he believed her, it didn't make the morning easier" summary.

3. Where A lost me: the opening sentence, ~2%: "Ray had let himself in at the front on the key that was laid on his counter on the third, the first time it had been in that door, and he had stood on the mat after it turned and listened to the place..." It is tangled, and "the way" is overused through the first page ("the way she did," "the way he'd pick a washer," "the way he'd listened"). Second, ~92%: "He had been paid for a good many things across a counter in forty years, and never once by somebody who meant it to cost her" is a bit much, and it explains what the scene already showed.

4. Where B lost me: ~88%: "He did the arithmetic the way he did all arithmetic, roughly, and didn't care for where it came out." It says nothing and sits in the middle of the best exchange in the chapter. The opening, ~3%, is also thinner ("The heat wasn't up yet. He stood at the house-ball rack in his canvas jacket with a ball in each hand"). It is competent, but nothing there is alive yet.

5. Confidence: medium. Both share the same skeleton and many of the same lines, so this comes down to texture. A's mannerisms could tire a reader. B's plainness could read as restraint to someone else.

6. Same-author check: Yes. The scene order, the readback and "Net thirty" beats, and many lines are shared. A is a rewrite of B with more voice.
