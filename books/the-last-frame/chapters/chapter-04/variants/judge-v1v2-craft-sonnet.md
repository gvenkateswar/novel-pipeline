A = chapter-04/variants/v2.md; B = chapter-04/variants/v1.md. Persona: Craft. Model: Sonnet.

1. Winner: A

2. Why: A is the leaner chapter. It spends its words on things only these people would do (the tape on the rack, Danny holding the cup "the way he held a carton he didn't trust the bottom of", "Nineteen degrees. I checked on the way out."). B often says the same beat twice, once in action and once in a gloss.

3. Where A lost me: right at the top, 1%: "Ray had let himself in on the front key she'd laid on his counter, the first time he had put it in her door, and he had lit lane one and left the other nine running dark to their pits." I had to reread it. The second spot was around 12%: "The front door was forty feet back up the approach with the key to it in his pocket, and down on Main Street there was a drawer he had walked out on this morning without counting." That is a lot of furniture for a man deciding whether to leave. The two-drawers paragraph at about 78% ("He had kept two things in two drawers all month...") also explains what the scene had already shown.

4. Where B lost me: about 77%, "and every no he said to her between now and then, the whole town could work out which pocket it went in." It is a clever line that explains the stakes after the kiss and the cake knife had already shown them. About 90%, Ray's "Twenty years I had my head in those machines... and I don't know as I'd know how." is the stock taciturn-Yankee speech, and it deflates the sharper "Send me a quote / Near enough is not a quote" run before it. B's Denise rowboats line and "bill me for the stop" are better than A's trimmed versions. B's radio-click echo is a good device. B's gestures and glosses double up, though: "squared it with the flat of his hand, and squared it again", "Her thumb lay along the clip", "She had not moved the clipboard an inch", "Nothing on her moved but the thumb".

5. Confidence: low to medium. The two are the same chapter, line for line, in most places. A is a tightened and re-voiced pass of B, and I am picking A on density and Ray's interior, against B's sharper Denise/Ellen banter. It is close to a coin flip with a slight lean.

6. Same-author check: Yes. Same scenes, same beats, same recurring props (the dead K, the shirt-pocket page, "net thirty"), and the same habit of ending beats on a flat deflection.
