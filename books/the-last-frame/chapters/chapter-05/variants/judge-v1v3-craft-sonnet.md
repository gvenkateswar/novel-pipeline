A = variants/v1.md ("On Zero"). B = variants/v3.md ("Midnight"). Persona: Craft. Model: Sonnet.

1. Winner: A

2. Why: A sounds more like a particular woman noticing particular things (the shoe-size voice, "She kept minutes the way other people kept their hands in their pockets," the ten balls arriving as one sound that then breaks into its parts), while B is smoother but reaches for stock phrases at the moments that should be most specific.

3. Where A lost me: "Ray's right hand came up off the wood for the first time since she started, reaching for the one kind of help he knew how to hand across a counter." (~85%). The narrator explains the gesture the page had already shown. The next beat, "She had that answer in order too, and she gave it to him in two sentences, in the same voice, so that he would have all of it," is the same habit. The "in her format" and "the way X" tics also run a little hot by the counter scene.

4. Where B lost me: "Standing in a pit with the bulb over his head he was a good deal of man to look up at, and she did not mind it at all." (~35%) is a romance default. "slowly, as if he expected to be given the procedure" (~38%) is a joke that doesn't land in a kiss. In the last scene the Judy/Arlene money recital runs as a paragraph with little air (~88%). B's best idea is that she had never said "I am asking" to anyone and the first time she said it was to him. B states that insight outright, so it goes soft.

Both versions keep the same weakness at the end: the counter scene is a money conversation after a kiss. Neither fixes that. A's counter scene is the more physical of the two (the thumb, the drawer, the 5:05 jacket), and B's is more tidy and explained.

5. Confidence: medium. They are close, because the structure and many lines are the same. The gap is in texture and specificity, and a reader who prefers B's cleaner rhythm could flip it.

6. Same-author check: Yes. Same skeleton, same recurring devices (the counted minute, the flat hands, "format," "went out to his supper").
