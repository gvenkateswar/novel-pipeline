A = variants/v2.md ("Not Since Midnight"); B = variants/v1.md ("On Zero"). Persona: Craft. Model: Sonnet.

1. Winner: A

2. Why: A's sentences carry more per line and the people sound different from each other. Kate is flat and fast, Denise runs on and jokes, and Ray talks in short, priced pieces. B says the same things at greater length and falls back on stock romance phrasing.

3. Where A lost me: "Forty people," Denise said, "and I'm leaving out the vestibule, watched the two of you come out of that pit..." (about 45%). The aside is garbled and I had to reread it. The kiss paragraph at about 35% is one very long block. It works because of "somewhere in this it went. She did not know when," but it taxes the eye. Mild only.

4. Where B lost me: "He said her name once, low, under all that noise, and she heard every letter of it." (about 33%). That is the romance default. Just before it, "both of them were choosing it" explains the beat, where A lets the two cups on the steel carry it. B also pads the counter scene. "She heard her own voice go out across the counter level and pleasant... Her voice did not hurry for any of it" says the same thing three times, and "Whatever he was going to say, he had said it to himself already somewhere, in the truck maybe" is a narrator explaining Ray.

5. Confidence: high. B has its good lines, such as Denise's "no, they did not" run-on and Kate taking "her mother's vitals", but A does what B does in fewer words and adds a few moves B lacks: "Tell your thumb", the thumb "like a part he had found in the wrong bin", and Denise's "You should've asked my knees." These pass the sound-different test better.

6. Same-author check: Yes. The scaffolding, the beats, the dialogue spine and the closing line are identical, and the second looks like a tightened pass on the first.
