1. Winner: A

2. Why: A sounds like a particular woman thinking (the Kate call, with the "I'm listening" voice, lands as a mother learning her own habit back), and Denise, Ray, and Ellen each talk like different people, where B has the same good bones but reads more like a clean pass over them.

3. Where A lost me: Denise's "Forty people," Denise said, "... watched the two of you come out of that pit a few minutes past twelve with your faces arranged, and did a single one of the forty go home and keep it to themselves? They did not." (about 50%). It runs one clause too long before the forecast joke arrives. A smaller stumble earlier, about 33%: "Standing in a pit with the bulb over his head he was a good deal of man to look up at, and she did not mind it at all." That is the one place the narration winks at the reader.

4. Where B lost me: "She held out his cup. At eleven he had told her that seven's sweep went on the bill to midnight, in her format." (about 30%). It is a backfilled explanation to set up a joke that already works. The second spot is "She had read up on the derby. The Sportsmen's Club put half the county out on the ice for it every winter, in bob-houses and pickups and lawn chairs..." (about 70%), where the scene stops to show research in the middle of the ask.

5. Confidence: medium. B has some lines I would steal, such as "like a part he had found in the wrong bin," the kitchen chair nobody sits in, and the hand starting for the back pocket. A's interior work (Ellen noticing it was the first time she had said "I am asking" to anyone; the counted minute that gets set down in the kiss; the collar callback at the door) is the stronger sustained craft.

6. Same-author check: Yes. It is the same scenes, beats, and lines in two registers, and the dialogue skeleton is identical.
