1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** The two chapters are built the same way, but in B the other people get to act back. Ray says "Go on and give me the rest of it, and I'll take it in one go," his "No" lands "no louder than a price," Danny's ears go red, and Denise gets a joke ("You should've asked my knees"). In A, Ellen's head does all the talking, and everyone else is a pair of flat hands on a counter.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 77%, in the store, when the ask turns into a display of how well she runs her own mind: "under it she was going as fast as she had ever gone on a bad night: what he would say, and in what order, and what she would answer to each, and which of her answers she could not give in front of Danny. She had the whole of it laid out before she came to the end of the sentence." From there I read the hands-flat beat three more times ("Ray's hands stayed flat on the wood" / "Ray's hands had stayed flat on the wood through all of it" / "put his right hand back flat on the counter"). Then "She had that answer in order too, and she gave it to him in two sentences, in the same voice, so that he would have all of it" (~92%) set up the Judy money lines, and I read those two like a memo.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 70%, when the scene stops to recap earlier chapters: "She had told him on her first night that she did not ask, and on the radio the Saturday of the parties that she had not asked him for anything yet." It happens again at ~80% with "He had heard her say *the house* into a telephone the morning he rekeyed her door, with her old pins in his shirt pocket." The Judy lines (~93%) are word for word the same as A's, and I skimmed them here too. B's version is easier to take because "His hand stopped short of the pocket" breaks them up.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two share most of their dialogue word for word. A has the better single lines ("she could have walked to him in the dark without touching a wall," "She kept minutes the way other people kept their hands in their pockets") and the bigger crash at zero. B's sentences are tighter ("Kate would hear whatever was in her voice, so there would be nothing in it"), and its people sound less like each other.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. They are the same skeleton with the same lines of dialogue, redressed sentence by sentence.
