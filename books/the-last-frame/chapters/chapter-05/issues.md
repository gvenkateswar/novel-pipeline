# Chapter 05 issues (draft.md, v3 tournament winner)

Read against: ch 01-04 final.md, beats.md, outline v3.2a, story-bible-draft.md (governs over story-bible.md where they differ; the outline over the draft), continuity-log.md (Ch 04 (v3) entry and flags, plant ledger, numbers ledger, master flags). Nothing from `chapters-v2/`.

**Issue 1** · MUST-FIX
- Problem: Ellen's interior says she kissed Ray in the pit, but the page has him kissing her there.
- Location (s4): "She would not ask him in the pit, where she had kissed him."
- Conflicts with: s2 of this draft, "He came the last step and took her face in both his hands ... and kissed her" (and beats s2: "He moves first this time"). In Ch 03 she kissed him, under the dead K, and the line may be remembering that one. The pit kiss is his.
- Suggested fix: "She would not ask him in the pit, where he had kissed her."

**Issue 2** · MUST-FIX
- Problem: the chapter's hook line has no antecedent for "yours". Ellen never says no, and the last "yours" she speaks is Ray's money.
- Location (s4): "Yours couldn't count if you gave it, because it has to have sat in my name ..." followed by "Then I'll make my no cost me what yours costs you."
- Conflicts with: beats s4 item 13 ("'Yours' is her no to his money, said a moment before, and the reader holds that by adjacency") and the outline's Ch 05 ending. On the page, adjacency points the other way. Read by ear, "what yours costs you" means "what your money costs you", and Ch 06 s1 ("he signs the release first") needs it to mean her refusal of his money.
- Suggested fix: make her refusal a spoken word. Open her second true sentence with "No." ("No. Yours couldn't count if you gave it ..."). Alternatively, after "His hand stopped short of the pocket", add one narrated beat in which she does not take what he offered. Do not explain it.

**Issue 3** · MUST-FIX
- Problem: two trade-part similes sit in Ellen's narration, and the first reuses Ch 04's "out of a bin" image.
- Location (s4): "He looked down at it on the wood like a part he had found in the wrong bin" and "Then he nodded, once, as if she had named the part he'd guessed."
- Conflicts with: World rule 19 ("Ray's comparisons to motors, clutches and machines ... none in Ellen's narration"; ear-for-a-part is Ray's habit of thought, not hers). Ch 04: "the way he'd pick a washer out of a bin" (the bin image is now a tic).
- Suggested fix: cut both similes. "Ray's thumb stopped. He looked down at it on the wood." And "He looked at her a while. Then he nodded, once."

**Issue 4** · SHOULD-FIX
- Problem: s3 never says what day it is, so "by breakfast" and "by Tuesday" read as news of the same morning.
- Location (s3): "the Bench had it by breakfast", "They had it by Tuesday."
- Conflicts with: beats s3 ("Saturday Jan 2, ten in the morning") and the bible calendar. The first day word after s2 is "the end of a Saturday" in s4, and s4's "two nights ago" is the only back-anchor. A reader can place s3 on Friday Jan 1 at ten, the morning after midnight. That gives "breakfast" no gap and makes s4's "Saturday" a jump nobody marked.
- Suggested fix: one clause in s3, for example "and the Bench had it by breakfast yesterday", or "Saturday" in the setting sentence.

**Issue 5** · SHOULD-FIX
- Problem: lane seven is called "seven" with no "lane" five times, against the write-for-the-ear rule.
- Location: s1 "seven's, and stayed out" and "on the catwalk behind seven"; s2 "Ray was behind seven with the sweep back in", "Seven was set behind its curtain", "On seven's deck beyond the curtain".
- Conflicts with: World rule 7 ("Lanes always take the word 'lane' ('lane four,' never 'on four')"). Seven is not the lane word's only candidate for "seven" in a text-to-speech read: there is also "seven's sweep" in the same paragraph.
- Suggested fix: "behind lane seven" (both scenes); "lane seven was set behind its curtain"; "on lane seven's deck". "Seven's sweep" may stay as the machine once "lane seven" has been said in the paragraph.

**Issue 6** · SHOULD-FIX
- Problem: two clock times stacked in one sentence.
- Location (s2): "At eleven he had told her that seven's sweep went on the bill to midnight, in her format."
- Conflicts with: World rule 7 ("Never stack two clock times or two dollar figures in a sentence"). The bible ledger has the job as 11 p.m. to midnight, but the page need only carry one of the two.
- Suggested fix: "Earlier tonight he had told her that seven's sweep went on the bill up to the midnight throw, in her format."

**Issue 7** · SHOULD-FIX
- Problem: the standing ten pin and "throw at it" sit in the same paragraph as his mouth, one paragraph before the kiss.
- Location (s2): "She watched his mouth come to the place where a man says it ... On seven's deck beyond the curtain the ten pin was still standing in its corner ... and nobody came down the lane to throw at it."
- Conflicts with: World rule 2 (no pin or ball double meanings; check every bowling sentence) and beats s2 ("Keep pins out of every kiss sentence"; "no pin or ball double meaning anywhere near the kiss"). The mouth sentence and the "standing ... throw at it" sentence are the same paragraph.
- Suggested fix: move the ten-pin sentence up into the paragraph that follows the throw ("Pins went up into the violet light ..."), and cut "to throw at it" ("and nobody came down the lane").

**Issue 8** · SHOULD-FIX
- Problem: "the two a.m. shift" misnames the shift. The incident was at two in the morning on a midnight shift.
- Location (s1): "the one after the two a.m. shift, when she had sat in the car in the driveway with the engine off until the school bus came".
- Conflicts with: bible draft, Ellen's background ("it happened *during* the midnight shift ... write 'on a midnight shift'"); the Ch 03 page ("I worked midnights a long time. One night, at two in the morning ..."). The phrase also puts "two" within 26 lines of Kate's "Two." (the listener flag in the beats).
- Suggested fix: "the one after the bad midnight shift".

**Issue 9** · SHOULD-FIX
- Problem: in s4 Ellen's clock is back whole, with no trace of the minute she lost in s2, and the line is stated absolutely.
- Location (s4): "It was five past five. Ellen knew it as she knew every minute she was awake."
- Conflicts with: s2 ("She had always known the time ... and somewhere in this it went. She did not know when.") and "What time is it?" Two nights later "every minute she was awake" erases the cost (beats: the lost clock pays Ch 09 s1, a minute early, and the epilogue).
- Suggested fix: keep her knowing the time, but without the absolute. "It was five past five. She had the time back, and checked it against nothing."

**Issue 10** · SHOULD-FIX
- Problem: the page says Ray "reached for" his pocket at her front door; in Ch 02 he made an offer and reached for nothing.
- Location (s4): "He had reached for the same thing at her front door, every dollar back: the one thing he knew how to hand across a counter."
- Conflicts with: Ch 02 s2 (he knelt, spoke to the lock, then to her face, with his hands on the cylinder). The hands-over-money gesture in Ch 02 is the envelope he handed back at the lane, not the door.
- Suggested fix: "He had put money on the table at her front door too, every dollar back: the one thing he knew how to hand across a counter."

**Issue 11** · SHOULD-FIX
- Problem: the pronouns in Ellen's second true sentence slide. "It" means "yours" in "if you gave it", then "money that counts" in "because it has to have sat", then Ray's savings again in "it's what pays Arlene".
- Location (s4): "Yours couldn't count if you gave it, because it has to have sat in my name since before I signed for the building, and it's what pays Arlene if I miss."
- Conflicts with: outline Ch 05 ("Ray's money: closed by facts") and beats ("what pays Arlene" must be Ray's savings as Arlene's floor). The sentence is the outline's verbatim, so the fix is to the outline's wording, not to the writer. A listener cannot tell which money pays Arlene.
- Suggested fix: "Yours couldn't count if you gave it. Anything that counts has to have sat in my name since before I signed for the building. And yours is what pays Arlene if I miss." (Update outline Ch 05 and "Ray's money" to match, or authorize the writer to differ.)

**Issue 12** · SHOULD-FIX
- Problem: Ellen's "Still the rest." to Kate on Dec 31 is a true answer that leaves out a fact the chapter then shows. Nine days earlier she asked Judy what her own money would buy, and she tells Ray "all of it" would not buy back half of January.
- Location (s1): "The rest is still the rest?" / "Still the rest."; (s4): "I asked Judy before Christmas: my own money lowers the line, slowly, and all of it wouldn't buy back half of January."
- Conflicts with: Ch 02 oath ("The house." / "Not the rest." / "I swear."), outline Ch 08 s4 ("There's money I swore to my daughter. It isn't in this.") and Ch 09 s6 ("First she tells her what she didn't ask in February"). Nothing on the page tells the reader what "the rest" is, and the page never notes that Ellen has already priced the sworn money. Ch 09 s6's "why wasn't I asked" must now also cover this call.
- Suggested fix: leave the lines. Add to the continuity log's Ch 09 s6 flags: Kate will have Dec 31 on her side of the ledger ("You told me Dec 31. You'd already asked Judy."), and Ellen's reply must own it. If the author wants the reader to see it now, add one clause in s1 of Ellen's interior after "Still the rest" naming what she has not told Kate.

**Issue 13** · SHOULD-FIX
