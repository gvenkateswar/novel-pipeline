# Chapter 05 — The Push (Midpoint): Beat Sheet

**POV:** Ellen (close third, past tense)
**Dates:** Saturday, January 16, 9:58 a.m., to Sunday, January 17, about 1:15 a.m. (holiday weekend; Monday the 18th is the holiday, never named)
**Target:** 3,200 words (scene targets sum to 3,200)
**Chapter promise to set up:** Will she tell him, and what will he do when she does?

---

## Pre-flight flags (read before drafting)

1. **Ch 04 state (checked against the continuity log's Ch 04 entry and Master Flags).** Facts this chapter relies on:
   - The season's NOI was $11,800 as of Jan 8. Friday Jan 8 and Saturday Jan 9 were closed by the outage, which cost eleven parties and $2,640. Ellen refused to rebook them free.
   - Ray's lender extended his commitment letter through March 31. He asked for it on Dec 14, the morning of the first lesson. He told Hollis, "She knows I've got one." **Ellen doesn't know about the extension.** The Master Flags allow the reveal in Ch 05–07.
   - The Ch 04 office argument ended with Ray's case standing ("Pine Ridge is forty minutes up the road") and Ellen without an answer. Ray's line, "Arlene never charged the league for a lot of things," points at the ledgers.
   - Ray told Ellen he never saw Linda's 187; he set her tenth by hand. She's the only one who knows. **Don't reuse it here.** It belongs to Ch 08/09.
   - A Highway Department man (22 years of Thursdays) and two others sat out unpaid, and nobody bowled lane 8. The Highway Department also half dismantled lane 8's ball return. That pays off in S2.
   - Hollis's derby falls on the same weekend as the championship, with twelve hundred people in a good year. Verna's motion of thanks is still pending (it belongs to Ch 07; don't use it here).
2. **Ray's bank extension: surface it here, in S3, before the kiss.** The Master Flags allow Ch 05–07. World rule 7 (no secret carried more than a chapter) and Ray's "would never lie" both point to this chapter. The Ch 04 cold reader named it the strongest hook. **This isn't a new plot turn.** It's a disclosure of an existing fact, said by Ray, plainly, before he asks to kiss her. Ellen already knew he had financing and the right of first refusal, so nothing in the outline changes. It also lands rule 19 at the chapter's most dangerous point. *Orchestrator: to hold it for Ray to tell in Ch 06 instead, cut the two lines marked [EXT] in S3. The kiss still stands, but rule 19 then rests on S2 and S4 alone.*
3. **Rule 20 is the chapter's main job.** Ellen loses three times, each in a different way:
   - **S2, to the town.** The lunch staff refuse to be sequenced off their lane. They're right, and it costs her a slot.
   - **S2, to Ray, in public.** He tells the Rotary "She gave her word," and she lets it stand mid-rush. That's the price of the December readback she skipped.
   - **S4, to the ledger.** There's no card for it. Every fix she runs on the adding machine fails except the one that breaks the promise.
   No competence beat recovers any of these. The day succeeds at the register. It doesn't win her the arguments.
4. **Money on the page: three figures only.** $7.50 (lineage, ledger scene only, as the outline requires), $700 (weekly league loss), $26,000 (projection). Show the false victory in S3 without a number: her pencil line on the graph-paper pace sheet touches the straight line for the first time since December 1. Keep $15,500, $38,800, $45,000 and any percentage off the page. The Ch 04 reader was confused by a bare running total, so S3 makes clear the line is the *season*.
5. **"Fella I knew…": one, and it's cut off** (the log allows at most one). In S3 Ray starts one to dodge a direct question, and Ellen says "No." He has to say the thing himself. "Which fella?" is retired (Ch 04 used its three) and must not appear.
6. **Don't attribute the 2004 freeze to Ray or Linda.** Arlene's ledger says "per league." The cold reader may suspect Ray, and the text must leave that suspicion unconfirmed. Denise doesn't know about the freeze (revision log 9).
7. **No symbols on cue (rule 17).** The sign doesn't flicker, light up, or die during the kiss. It buzzes the way it always does in the cold. The dead K comes up once, in dialogue, as a repair. It isn't snowing (forbidden: snowfall on an emotional cue). The night is clear and cold, and not described romantically.
8. **Save for later chapters:** "the voice" (Ch 07, 09). The loss of separation (Ch 07). "Relief briefing" and "the D-side" (Ch 09). The wood line (Ch 08, 09). "Mrs. Daly" uncorrected (Ch 10). The "wedding date" square (Ch 06). The 187 (Ch 08/09). Use the "Ms." correction at most once, and not at the kiss.
9. **Ray works the day on a booked work order, not as a favor (rule 14).** Ellen booked him for the full Saturday at his per-call rate on Wednesday, the day she bought the radios. He logs in on the R.G. card anyway at 9:30. Neither of them comments on it.
10. **Ellen's sit/stand tic carries the structure.** She stands all day. In S4 she sits to reconcile, expecting good news, and **stands** when the number comes. In S5 she sits again once she has decided.

### Scene 1
**Setting:** The counter and vestibule, Saturday Jan 16, 9:58 a.m. A line of eleven-year-olds in socks and parkas runs out the vestibule door into 4°F. The boot tray overflows onto the 1974 cigarette-burn scar. Hollis's derby poster is on the corkboard. A new handheld radio is clipped to Ellen's collar, and its antenna knocks against her reading-glasses cord. For the first time the strip board is full end to end, every card a party, every time in her capitals.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Denise, Ray (on the radio only, never in the frame), the first birthday party (a parent and the eleven-year-olds, unnamed)
**Surface action:**
- **Open mid-count.** Ellen counts the line under her breath and gets a different number twice, because eleven-year-olds don't hold position. She settles on twenty-six and writes it on the first strip.
- **Denise at the shoe cubbies** sprays each pair with her two-count, toe and heel, and grades them out loud as she hands them over: "You'll live." "You'll live." "Those were a seven in 1998, they're a four now, you'll live." A kid asks what a seven was. Denise doesn't answer. Keep her to short bursts while she works; she rambles later.
- **The radios (rule 14, comic middle).** Ellen bought four radios on Wednesday. Ray, booked for the day, thinks a man can holler down a lane. First exchange of the day. Ellen keys up: "Kettle Maintenance, say intentions." (**ATC 1.**) Ray, from the pit, flat: "Intentions are to quit." The birthday parent hears it on Ellen's collar and laughs. Ray's second transmission, unasked: "Four's table is fine. I can hear it from here. You don't need the radio to know that." Ellen: "I need the radio to know you're there."
- **ATC 2.** Ellen tells the parent, whose party is due on lanes 1–2 while lane 2 still has the 9 a.m. walk-ins: "You're number two for lane two. Four minutes." Literal and exact. The parent repeats "number two" to a child as if it were bad news.
- **The push, laid out in one line (not a list).** Three birthday parties, the youth hockey team, the Rotary, and the Ambrose Elementary lunch staff's retirement party, every lane from 10 until close. Show it as a glimpse of the board.

**Interior passage 1 (Ellen, 2–4 sentences):** Why she bought it, in her idiom. Twenty-six children who won't hold position, ten lanes, six parties stacked to the minute. It's the first time since the sheet cake that a room has needed her to put it in order. Her hands are steady. She notices that, and treats it as a fact about the day, not about herself.

**Subtext:** The radios are a cost she chose and he grumbled about. They're also how she keeps him in her ear all day without asking him to come up front. "I need the radio to know you're there" is the most open thing she says before S3, and she says it as a logistics point.
**Tension source:** Situational (twenty-six children, one door, four minutes). Underneath, a low interpersonal hum: Ray mocks her equipment over her own equipment.
**Ends on:** Mid-motion, cut early. Denise holds a pair of size-five rentals up to the light and starts to grade them. Ellen is already keying the radio to tell lane 2 it's clear. Cut before the grade or the transmission lands.
**Word count target:** 550

### Scene 2
**Setting:** The snack bar and lanes 1–8, from 12:05 to about 1:30 p.m. Fourteen pizza boxes are stacked on the snack-bar counter, and grease is coming through the bottom box onto the menu board's ledge. The popcorn machine is off so the office heater can run. All ten lanes are going at once, a drawer of wooden spoons dumped over and over.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Denise, Ray (in person, briefly, at lane 8's ball return), the head of the lunch staff (unnamed, sixties), the retiree (unnamed, early seventies, 41 years of school lunches), the youth hockey coach (unnamed), a Rotary member (unnamed; not the Ch 04 Highway Department man)
**Surface action:**
- **The pizzas as a logistics problem (outline beat).** Ellen ordered three parties' pizzas staggered for 12:00, 12:20 and 12:40. The pizza place sent all fourteen at 12:05, because it has one driver. Ellen to Denise: "I asked for them twenty minutes in trail." (**ATC 3**: "miles-in-trail" bent to minutes, because that's how she ordered them.) Denise rambles for three or four sentences: the pizza place has one driver, his name, his car, the time he put it in the lake in 2016, so no, honey, nothing comes in trail. While she says it she gets the stacks onto the hot-dog roller's warming shelf in party order. It's Denise's idea, and Ellen says so out loud to the nearest parent. (Denise is being asked.)
- **ATC 4.** The hockey coach comes to the counter. His team is booked for lanes 1–2 at 1:15. Ellen: "Stand by." The coach repeats himself, longer each time. He's been standing by, the kids have been standing by, they've got a game in Gilford at four, and he isn't trying to be a pain, but standing by isn't a thing twelve-year-olds can do.
- **THE LOSS (rule 20, the town pushes back).** The lunch staff have lanes 1–2 until 1:15. At 1:12 the retiree is in her ninth frame, bowling slowly, with her whole staff watching every ball. Ellen walks over to move them along: lane 9 at 1:40, a free string. The head of the lunch staff answers at length. She hedges, repeats herself, and never raises her voice. Forty-one years. She fed this whole town, the hockey coach and his brother included, and probably Ellen's daughter if Ellen had lived here, which she didn't. The retiree is going to finish her string on the lane she started it on, and then they'll have cake, and the hockey boys can watch and learn something about finishing. The retiree throws her third ball in the middle of this and doesn't look around.
- **Ellen has no good answer.** The hockey coach is right, and so is the lunch lady. Ellen says "Finish your string" and gives the hockey team lanes 9–10 early. That bumps the Rotary by 25 minutes, which costs her the 4:30 walk-in slot she had penciled in. Denise, passing with pizza, delivers the line that lands it: "Honey, you don't move the lunch ladies." Nobody comes out ahead. The hockey coach pays and doesn't say thank you.
- **THE SECOND LOSS (rule 19 and the promise plant).** Ray comes up front because lane 8's ball return is jamming again. He put it back together after the Highway Department took it apart on storm night, and a day of children has found the one thing he didn't replace. (This pays off the Ch 04 detail. Don't explain it beyond one clause.) The Rotary man, waiting out his bump, asks him what everyone asks: is the championship still the last Saturday in February? Ray, kneeling at the return, without looking up: "Last Saturday in February. All ten lanes." Then, with the important words last: "She gave her word." Ellen is three feet away at the strip board with a pencil in her hand. The Rotary man looks at her for confirmation. She tells him his lane will be ready in twenty-five minutes. Going back to the pit, Ray touches the trophy-case glass with one knuckle (the chapter's one use).

**Interior passage 2 (Ellen, 2–4 sentences), right after "She gave her word":** She knows exactly what she said on December 11 and how much of it the sweep covered. In thirty-one years she let one thing go unconfirmed on purpose, and it was a handshake. Keep it to fact, with no jargon. ("Readback" is saved for S4.)

**Subtext:** The town is not a sequence. Ellen paid for being right about the clock and wrong about the people. Then Ray says her promise out loud to a customer, and she keeps quiet. The silence is a second, smaller skipped readback, and it costs her again.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and public (the lunch staff, the coach). Internal: her promise said back to her in its short form, in front of a witness.
**Ends on:** Mid-conversation, cut away. The Rotary man is still asking whether the banquet is the same night too, because his wife wants to know. Ellen has already turned to the board and is moving his card down twenty-five minutes. Cut before she answers him. Don't describe her face.
**Word count target:** 700

### Scene 3 — KISS 1
**Setting:** The counter at 10:52 p.m. (close), then the foot of the driveway at Kettle Road around 11:05 p.m. It's clear and windless, about 2°F. The neon sign on its pole by the road buzzes louder than it does in daylight. Ellen is bringing in the sandwich board she put out at noon ("FULL — NEXT LANE 7:40" in her capitals). Ray's truck is on the shoulder of Kettle Road, because the lot was full by eleven this morning (rule 14). Across the road the town beach is a flat white field.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Ray; Denise (beginning and end)
**Surface action:**
- **The false victory, at the counter, in one short beat.** Ellen pencils the day onto her graph-paper pace sheet. The season's line has run under the straight diagonal to March 1 since December 1. Tonight it touches it. She says nothing. Denise, coat on, reading it upside down, to Ellen: "Don't." Ellen: "I didn't say anything." Denise: "You were going to frame it." Denise goes out the front with her keys in her hand. Her car is still in the lot, and her exit time sets up the end of the scene.
- **Ray at the counter.** For the first time since 9:30 a.m., Ellen sees him rather than hears him. He signs out on the R.G. card: 01/16 / 0930–2255 / ALL / PUSH. He hands back the radio, and she takes it.
- **The walk down.** Ellen goes for the sandwich board and Ray goes to his truck: same direction, same pace, no conversation for the first half of the drive. Specific detail: she carries the flashlight even though the sign gives plenty of light.
- **The dead K (once).** At the pole, Ray stops and listens to the sign. "That's not the tube on the K. You can hear it isn't." Ellen: "It's on my list." Ray: "It was on Arlene's list." No resolution. (It pays off in the coda. Don't decide the fix.)
- **Her question.** She asks why he came up front at noon for a ball return he could have talked a teenager through on the radio. Ray starts: "Fella I knew in Meredith—" Ellen: "No." (The fella bit makes its one appearance and gets cut off.) He stands there. Then he says it plainly, in his order:
  - **[EXT]** First, the thing she should know before anything else. The bank extended his letter through March 31. He asked on December 14.
  - **[EXT]** She answers literally: "The morning of my first lesson." He says, "Yes." He doesn't explain or apologize, and she doesn't ask him to. Rule 19, on the page, in the cold, ninety seconds before the kiss.
  - Then he asks, with the important words last: he's keeping the letter, and he'd like to kiss her, if she's agreeable. "Agreeable" is his word. Let it be slightly old-fashioned.
- **Ellen's answer is literal and exact.** She doesn't say yes. She says what she's aware of: the letter, March 31, and that he has asked. Then she leans the sandwich board against the sign pole, with both mittens free, so it's clear she has decided.
- **The kiss (clean rules).** Show it only through context and consequence: her mitten on the GAGNON & SON stitching over his pocket, the sign buzzing above them, the flashlight still on in her other hand and pointed at the snow. One embrace at most. Nothing about mouths or warmth. **Ellen speaks first afterward.** She says something exact and unromantic, such as the time, or that the flashlight was on the whole time. The drafter chooses, but it must not be the tidiest line in the scene.
- **Denise's headlights** come down the drive. (Rule 14: she left the counter at 10:58 and sat warming the car.) She slows, rolls down the window, and says she isn't saying anything, at far more length than that takes. She drives off toward town. (The "wedding date" square on the pool sheet comes in Ch 06. Don't plant it here.)

**Interior passage 3 (Ellen, 2–4 sentences), on the walk down the drive:** Thirty-one years of voices she never put a face to: pilots she'd have known in two words on the frequency and walked past in an airport without a glance. All day she's had his voice on her collar and not his face, and she finds she wants to check one against the other. State it as an observation. Don't call it longing.

**Subtext:** He tells her the worst thing first because he won't kiss her under a false impression. That's his decency, and it's also his want, said out loud. She kisses him knowing it, which is her own choice. Neither has given up the building, and that's what makes the kiss theirs.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. The kiss happens on top of an open conflict, not in place of one. Internal: Ellen carries her S2 silence about the promise down the drive and doesn't raise it.
**Ends on:** Mid-conversation, cut away. Denise's taillights are heading toward town. Ray: "She's going to say something." Ellen picks up the sandwich board just as Ray reaches for it. Cut before either lets go. (This is the one ending in the chapter that's close to a quip. Keep the board unresolved so the quip isn't the last beat.)
**Word count target:** 750

### Scene 4 — THE LEDGER (the midpoint reversal)
**Setting:** The office, 11:40 p.m. to about 12:55 a.m. The steel desk, the printing adding machine, and the space heater, running because the popcorn machine is off. Arlene's green clothbound ledgers lie open three at a time across the desk: 1979, 2004, 2025. Pinned above the desk is the strip from Ch 03: JAN / RECONCILE / ARLENE / LEAGUE. The 2019 fuel-oil calendar hangs on the wall. The building ticks as the baseboard heat cools behind the closed lanes.
**Characters present:** Ellen alone
**Surface action:**
- **Why tonight (rule 14).** She scheduled it. The JAN / RECONCILE strip has been waiting for the first half of January to close, and January closes on today's push. She sits down to it in a good mood, expecting confirmation, not news.
- **The method, briefly.** She lines up December and January by night of the week against Arlene's last three winters. Tuesday and Thursday are the thinnest columns again, as they were in Ch 03. This time she goes looking for the reason, with Ray's "never charged the league for a lot of things" in her head. Don't quote it again; one clause of recall at most.
- **The discovery, in Arlene's green ink, in order.** One ledger at a time, so the reader assembles it:
  1. **2004:** "Lineage stays 7.50. Per league." (**$7.50**, the outline's figure, appears on the page here only.) Every winter after that, the same number, entered without comment.
  2. **1979:** "N. — house adds $1/bowler to prize fund. Good for the league." Then the dollar creeps up, a line every few years, to $5.
  3. Every spring, the banquet, the trophies, and free strings for the secretary and the captains, all paid by the house.
  4. **Comic middle (rule 15):** Arlene's margin notes for the mechanic: "R.G. — no charge — wouldn't take it," repeated for years. One reads, "R.G. — no charge — tried to give him a ham." Beside the 2004 lineage line, small: "Voyons donc." Ellen reads it twice. Arlene knew.
- **The arithmetic.** On the adding machine she builds a week of league nights the way Arlene never did, with Ray's invoices in it. The tape comes out negative: **$700 a week.** She runs it again with a different heat figure and gets nearly the same number.
- **She stands up** (the tic: she stands for bad news). She does the projection standing. February has no office parties and no holiday weekend. Six league weeks are left, and every one loses $700. Holiday Saturday doesn't come again. The tape says about **$26,000** against Judy's $30,000.
- **She tries to fix it and can't (rule 20).** In three or four lines she runs the alternatives, and a plain fact kills each one. Glow nights are already priced and already full. February has no parties to book; she has called. She can't cut the prize fund without the league, and the league is Ray's. Raising lineage *is* the league. She reaches for a blank index card to make a strip for it, then puts the card back in the box. There's no lane, time, or party for this. Don't comment on it.
- **The reversal, plainly, in narration.** It isn't the boiler, or the weather, or Arlene's old pinsetters. It's Tuesday and Thursday nights. It's his league.

**Interior passage 4 (Ellen, 2–4 sentences), the promise plant (priority 3):** "Unless the numbers say otherwise." The numbers say otherwise, and she said those words out loud in her own building. She also knows exactly which half he heard. The sweep came down over the end of her sentence while he was counting the cycle, and she watched him not hear it and chose not to ask for the readback. (**ATC 5**: "readback," in her interior, earned, because the whole promise turns on it.) Today he said the first half to a customer, and she let it stand.

**Interior passage 5 (Ellen, 2–3 sentences), her fear (rule 10):** Her calm hides a failure until it's too late. Four hours ago she was the calmest person in the building, her pencil line touching the diagonal, and the whole time this number sat two shelves away in green ink. Keep it plain. No "the voice." No 2 a.m. memory (that's Ch 07).

**Subtext:** She bought the alley to find out whether she could carry it. Now she knows what carrying it costs, and the bill goes to the man she kissed an hour ago, at the place he has kept unchanged for his wife.
**Tension source:** Internal and situational. The only fix big enough breaks a promise she half-made on purpose.
**Ends on:** An action, not a reflection. She tears the tape off the adding machine and folds it once. She uncaps her Sharpie over the blank REMARKS box on the 02/27 / 1800 / LGM / 10 strip, then caps it again without writing anything. Cut. (Still image 1 of 2.)
**Word count target:** 850

### Scene 5
**Setting:** The counter, about 1:10 a.m., Sunday Jan 17. The house lights are off and only the counter lamp is on. The lanes run back into the dark: ten pinsetters stopped, the settees empty. Through the front windows the sign is still on. She forgot to turn it off; it's an oversight, not a symbol, and nobody remarks on it. The two radios she took back sit in their chargers with green lights.
**Characters present:** Ellen; Ray by text only
**Surface action:**
- **She counts the room** (the tic, under her breath): one.
- **She sits on the counter stool.** That means she has decided. Don't say so.
- **The decision, shown as procedure.** On the back of a score sheet she writes the conversation as a sequence, the way she'd brief a hand-off: the ledger, the $700, the projection, the promise, both halves. She crosses out the order twice. Leading with the promise sounds like an excuse, and leading with the money sounds like a bill. **Comic beat (rule 15):** the third draft starts "Mr. Gagnon," and she crosses that out too.
- **The text.** She picks up her phone, types, and deletes twice. (Don't quote the drafts; show only the deletions.) What she sends is short and exact: tomorrow afternoon, a time, here, about the league. No "we need to talk." Nothing about the kiss.
- **The reply comes in under a minute,** which means he's awake too, and she notices. Keep the reply very short, lowercase, and noncommittal ("ok," or a time back). It must not be warm, and it must not be the tidiest line in the chapter.

**Interior passage (optional, 1–2 sentences, only if the scene needs air):** She runs tomorrow as a sequence and can't get the last step to hold still. Skip it if the scene runs long; four passages already satisfy rule 10.

**Subtext:** She's going to tell him the same day she looked, while she could still pretend she hadn't. That's the point of the scene, and the prose must not say it.
**Tension source:** Internal (how do you hand someone this?) and anticipatory (the chapter promise).
**Ends on:** The reply on the screen, and Ellen not putting the phone down. Her thumb hovers over the keyboard and she doesn't type. Cut. No reflection, no look at the sign. (Still image 2 of 2.)
**Word count target:** 350
