# Cold Reader Notes: Chapter 5, "The Vote"

**Draft read:** final.md, Oct 29 (chapters 01 to 05 read in order, nothing else opened)

---

## 1. Pull score: 5 / 10

I'd keep reading, but only because of the last two scenes. Ray reads her private "It stays" into the minutes in front of the league, and then she tells Denise she's going to put Linda's name on an Open. That is the first real collision in five chapters. Getting there means a phone call about sheet cake, a second kiss built from the same parts as the first, and a vote whose result I knew before Verna opened the binder. If I had other books on my phone I would have skimmed the first third. I wouldn't have quit, but I'd have been looking for a reason to.

---

## 2. Where I got bored

- **The Kate call, start of chapter to about 18%.** I drifted at: *"Kate went on about who had cut it and how badly, and about a resident who had eaten a corner piece off a tongue depressor because the forks were locked in a cabinet and nobody had the code."* The narrator has stopped listening to Kate and summarises her, so I stopped too. Before that comes a sheet-cake riff with yellow roses, blue roses and Happy spelled with one p. It's the cake from the retirement party in Ch 3, brought back again. The only live line in the call is the last one, "Is anybody there with you tonight?", and it gets cut off. I sat through two pages of throat-clearing to reach one good line.
- **Ellen's unspoken three-item answer to Kate, about 10%.** *"Ellen had the answer to that, and it was in order. First, holiday shifts went out on bids... Second... Third... She gave none of it."* By Ch 5 I can see a First/Second/Third list coming from a paragraph away. Here she thinks a whole list and then doesn't say it, so I read a list that does nothing.
- **The bylaws reading, about 55%.** *"'The secretary will read the bylaws in full,' Verna said, and did, dues and nights and lob line."* Then come two quoted clauses in legal register. I understood they were plants (whoever holds the building sets championship weekend), but it read like a contract, and I slowed down only so I wouldn't miss the clue.
- **Ray's speech, about 60%.** *"The credit union would've lent me against the store to buy this place off her clean, and I let their five o'clock go by on the third of December without making the call, and the other loan's with the bank in Concord..."* I watched both of those things happen in Ch 2 and Ch 4, so this is a recap aimed at me, the reader. I skimmed until "Mrs. Daly gave me her word."
- **The second-to-last paragraph, about 92%.** *"An Open without Linda's name would clear the bank's number with nothing over, if nothing went wrong..."* She had just told Denise exactly this. Same arithmetic, said twice.

The stakes are still a spreadsheet: $26,000 against $30,000, $4,000 short. I understand the number. I don't feel it. What I feel is Linda's name, and the book only gets to that in the last scene.

## 3. Where I got confused

- **"Scale of one to ten." "Of what." "You know of what."** I still don't know what. Loneliness? Ray? How the business is going? "Is anybody there with you tonight?" hints it's about loneliness or a man, but that comes a page later and the call dies before it pays off. I'm opening a chapter on a riddle and never getting the answer.
- **Dale versus "the captain."** In Ch 3 I took Dale Hutchins (road agent) and "the captain" (who says "follow my truck") to be possibly two people. In Ch 5 "the captain uncrossed his arms" and then Ray says "Dale, your crew pushes snow." I had to stop and work out that they're the same man.
- **"*Don't* and *it's* went out to him, the short words she had kept from him since the morning under the sign... as she had let the first one go at the desk on the nineteenth."** This is the first I learned that Ellen's dropped contractions were a deliberate signal. I had noticed she talked stiffly but didn't know it was meant. Now I'm being told to remember a contraction from Ch 4 ("I'm very good with a clock") and a date, "the nineteenth," that Ch 4 never gave me.
- **Date soup.** The nineteenth, the eighteenth, the third of December, the twelfth, "seven forty-three." I had to rebuild the calendar to follow "five days after he had said her first."
- **"The five words he had never heard" / "her own two words in his mouth, and the other five nowhere in it."** I had to count "Unless the numbers say otherwise" on my fingers. It's clever once solved, but it's a puzzle in the middle of an emotional beat.
- **The bob-house.** I'm not sure what one is (an ice-fishing shack, I think), and I don't know why she's going to Hollis's. It's meant as a tease, and it half works.
- **Verna "wrote a slip of her own and folded it into the binder without adding it to either column."** No idea. Noted.

## 4. Where I stopped believing

- **The Legion man asks "She going to rent our Saturday to the fish people?"** before Ellen has proposed anything. Her Open plan comes out a scene later, to Denise, in private. How does a random bowler land on the exact plan? It reads like the author planting the idea through the nearest mouth.
- **Ray: "I'll get to the fish people."** He never does. The line is set up and dropped.
- **Ellen declines to respond at the meeting.** The page gives a reason: correcting Ray would reveal he missed the clause, and that is "the one error of her life she would not report, because it was priced in him." I follow the logic. But there was an obviously safer option the page never closed. She had six evenings alone with him in the second chair and could have told him privately before the meeting, at no public cost. Her plan to take Linda's name now guarantees a much bigger public blow-up. For a woman who "wrote it up myself before anyone asked" every single time, keeping quiet for six nights needs at least one line of her nearly saying it in the chair. As written, those six nights were skipped.
- **"Ray had sat in it after close on six of the eleven nights since... and neither of them had said one word about the sign or about January."** Six nights alone with a vote coming that sets them against each other, and neither of them raises it once? I don't buy it. Worse, this sentence covers the part of the romance I wanted to watch. Ch 4 ended on her hooking out the chair for him, and Ch 5 tells me in one line that he sat in it six times.
- **The vote, 18 to 2.** No tension, because Ray said "mostly" in Ch 4. Fine as an outcome, but the scene plays as if it might be close.

## 5. Where it sounded like a machine

- **The order-breaks-down run-on, for the third time.** *"She meant to keep the order, and she thought of the cup standing level by his boot, and Tuesday, five days off, and his knees on the way down... and she heard that Tuesday had come round twice and nothing was in its place, and she did not go back for any of it."* The same device ran at the ball in Ch 1 and in the trench in Ch 3. I now know the formula: each romantic beat gets a long "and... and" sentence in which her list falls apart and she lets it.
- **Repeated sentence frame.** *"Her voice went out over the chairs level and courteous, and under it she was counting Verna's second hand."* Ch 3: *"Her voice was level and kind and a little bored, and under it she was still counting."* Nearly the same sentence.
- **Tics now visible:**
  - Ray's thumb on a backed-out screw (Ch 2 key cutter, Ch 4 bracket screw, now the chair): *"his thumbnail found a screw backed out on the chair in front of him and worked at it, and Ellen had seen him do that hardest when the talk was about himself."* The narrator is now explaining the tic too.
  - The quarter turn: *"gave it a quarter turn so it sat level."* It's in every chapter.
  - Ray's knees: *"both his knees reported on the way down."* The knee clicked in Ch 2, and here it comes up twice in one scene.
  - First / Second / Third, three times this chapter (the unspoken answer to Kate, "Three items" at the meeting, Denise extracting "the second half").
- **Feeling explained after the gesture.** *"a half-tone high on the title, a man putting the formality back on."* *"the counting was something to hold."* *"owing him"* type glosses carry over from earlier chapters.
- **Abstract phrasing.** *"kept back the one error of her life she would not report, because it was priced in him."* *"She had the five words, and the floor, and the time she had given back."* These are a narrator's summaries rather than a woman's thoughts.
- **Neat closers.** *"I know what time it is."* / *"Ray had said mostly. It had not been close."* / *"It is separation."* / *"Now you're sitting."* / *"I'm walking out to your bob-house Saturday."* Every scene, and some paragraphs inside scenes, end on a clipped button line.
- **Talk-alike.** Denise and Ray both talk in long comma-and chains ("I can subtract, I was in the third row, I watched eighteen hands go up and half of them..."). Kate does too ("Somebody brought a sheet cake, somebody always brings a sheet cake..."). Three characters share one breathless rhythm, and only Ellen and Verna sound different.

## 6. What I think is going on

Ellen will run a championship-weekend Open on derby weekend and put Linda's name on it to make her $4,000 margin. The bob-house trip is her pitching the Open to the derby crowd through Hollis, who is clearly connected to the ice (plow, waterproof notebook, bob-house). Ray will find out at the worst moment, probably when a flyer goes up on the vestibule board, and will take it as her breaking the word he put in the minutes. Then someone (Denise, now that she's heard "Unless the numbers say otherwise," or Verna's folded slip) will produce the clause he didn't hear under the strike. Ellen will have to "write it up" in public, which is the confession she has been dodging since Ch 3. Locker fourteen holds Linda's ball or shoes, and Ray will open it for the Memorial or Open weekend. I think Verna's slip is the secretary's own note that the house controls that weekend, or her own vote. The ending: the Open clears thirty thousand with Linda's name on it because Ray stands up for it in the end. He lets the Concord hold lapse, and the K on the sign finally gets lit, probably by Ray after she said no to it. Danny's Valentine's square on Normie's sheet means they're "official" by February 14.

## 7. The listener pass

- **Opening exchange with no speakers.** "Mom. Scale of one to ten." / "Of what." / "You know of what." / "Six," Ellen said. A text-to-speech voice gives no cue for four lines about who is talking, or that one voice is on a phone.
- **"*Don't* and *it's* went out to him."** Aloud, without italics, this is nonsense. The ear hears "Don't and it's went out to him" and parses nothing.
- **Numbers and dates read in full:** "eighteen minutes to twelve," "on the nineteenth" (twice), "the eighteenth with the lot empty at seven forty-three," "the third of December," "two thousand five," "two thousand four," "two minutes and thirty-four seconds," "the twelfth," "twenty-six thousand... thirty... four thousand," "Opposed, eighteen. In favor, two." By the meeting scene I'm hearing a ledger.
- **Denise's countdown.** "'Ten,' said Denise on the PA, and the curtain brought it to her half a second behind the room, so that every number came twice." This works on the page and is lovely as an idea, but aloud it is one more number in a chapter full of them.
- **Ellen's count.** "ten, eleven, and at eleven it was lost, and she went on counting to eighteen." Listeners have to know that eleven of twenty is a majority to catch the moment the vote is lost.
- **Ray's two-paragraph speech.** The second paragraph opens with a new quotation mark ("Dale, your crew...") and no attribution, so on audio it can sound like a new speaker.
- **The 90-word kiss run-on.** It chains "and... and" with "Tuesday" twice. Aloud it sounds like the reader lost their place, which is the intent, but it's the third time.
- **"The five words... the other five nowhere in it."** A listener can't scroll back to count them.
- **Run of identical shapes.** The Verna section is a string of "Verna said / Verna read / Verna wrote" lines in the same rhythm.
- **Scene-break asterisks** will be read aloud or skipped depending on the engine.

---

## 8. The romance

Yes, the New Year's scene worked, and it's the best romance writing since the trench in Ch 3. *"She had told no one where she was going, him least of all, and she had come to the one place in the building where he would look first."* That's her wanting him without being able to ask, done through action. *"For a whole minute he did not say Happy New Year... by the thirtieth second she knew he was keeping it on purpose."* It's good, and it uses her clock-keeping for something besides a joke. The cider exchange made me smile: *"It's cider pretending."* So did *"Half the town looks like an X-ray."* The close, *"It's four minutes past." / "I know what time it is,"* is a nice button, even if every scene has one.

The meeting is the stronger relationship beat: he uses her word against her in public five days after kissing her, and she lets him. I wanted to see her face, and the page gives me her want, *"She wanted him to keep talking... with every sentence of it against her."* That's the first time the romance and the plot have pulled against each other, and I felt it.

Laugh: *"The chair recognizes the gentleman with mustard on his sleeve." / "A Legion man looked down at his sleeve, and then stood up, since he had been recognized."* That's the funniest line in the chapter.

---

## 9. What I want to know next

- When does Ray learn she's putting Linda's name on an Open, and does he find out from her or from a flyer? This is the main pull, and it's real.
- Will anyone (Denise now has the phrase) tell Ray what she actually said in the pit?
- Why the bob-house? Mild curiosity.
- What's on Verna's folded slip?
- Who were the two teams that voted for her?

I don't care whether she finds $4,000. The money question pulls nothing. The Linda's-name question pulls everything. Locker fourteen has gone cold; I'd forgotten it until I wrote this list.

## 10. Best moment

> "And Mrs. Daly gave me her word, the night of the storm, in the pit behind five, that Linda's Memorial stays the way it is." He turned his head and looked at her, for the first time that night. "'It stays,' she said. That book of yours lets the house set that weekend any way it likes, so her word is what the Memorial's got, and I'll hold her to it. Put it in the book, Verna."

Runner-up: Denise, *"Who'd you say that to?"* That one line does more than the whole Kate call.
