# Ch 06 cold-read triage (orchestrator, autopilot)

**Verdict:** "I'd keep reading, but more out of goodwill than urgency." The wrench beat ("Is that wrench certified?") and the inside-out sweater are the best moments. The problems are a fight played too quietly, two scenes that rerun Ch 05, a staged trout win, and a kiss with no build that is then forgotten.

**Process:** one revision round (a prose-writer targeted pass, then a voice-editor pass).

**Keep unchanged:**
- plot facts and the money
- the promise wording from Ch 03
- the wrench beat and the inside-out sweater
- Hollis's license-check questions
- "she married a dentist"
- the "purpose of visit" ending hook
- the Kettle Open plan itself

**Length:** stay at 3,300–3,600.

## Cut the reruns

1. **S2, the ledger in Ellen's office.** The reader saw every entry in Ch 05. Ellen hands Ray the 2004 book open to the page and says the number ("Your league costs me seven hundred dollars a week"). Then stay on Ray reading. Quote no ledger lines except at most one new detail. "Linda took the ham" is new and good, so keep it. The scene is about his reaction.
2. **S6, the derby pitch.** Ray already heard the plan at the store, and so did the reader. Give the pitch one sentence of summary, then spend the scene on what is new: Hollis's interrogation, the committee's objections, and what they ask for in return.
3. **The trout.** Ellen doesn't land a fish. When the flag goes up she keeps pitching. Someone says "Your flag" and she answers "Stand by" or keeps her place, and someone else (the treasurer or Hollis) goes out to it. The committee says yes for a reason they state: her written contingency plan, which nobody in derby history has handed them, plus the Legion bean supper leaving them no indoor fallback. Cut the "the way Hollis must have told her on the phone" line.
4. **S3, the boiler checklist.** Readbacks are old news. Cut the call-and-response to one exchange at most. Show her working the checklist in summary.

## Raise the voice of the fight (S5, the store)

5. Ray finds out she knowingly let him repeat the promise in public, to the Rotary, and he is angry. He raises his voice once. He says something that lands and that he can't take back, about her letting him walk around town vouching for her, or about Linda's name on a plan with an entry fee. No swearing. Ellen doesn't get to be calm the whole time either: she snaps back once with something true and unkind. The fight costs both of them something real.
6. Drop the "Mrs. Daly" line if it softens the fight, or deliver it as a cut, not a habit.
7. **Acknowledge the cellar kiss in S5.** One visible beat of both of them not mentioning it (a look, a sentence started and abandoned). Keep it short.
8. **The WEDDING DATE row.** Move it to after the fight, when Ray is alone in the store. He sees Normie's handwriting on the pool sheet with names in it, after she's gone. Make it sting.

## The cellar kiss (S4)

9. Build to the kiss with one or two beats that come from the scene: the cold, her shaking hand he didn't offer to steady, the key. Don't let it arrive straight after "Ellen."
10. "That's the first time you've said that without 'for now' after it." Ray never said "for now" on the page. Cut the line or change it to something he actually said.
11. Don't reuse "She kissed him once" from Ch 05. Write this kiss in its own words.
12. Cut the drain banter after the kiss ("Somebody qualified." / "I'll put it on the card.").

## Clarity

13. Give a one-phrase day or date anchor at the top of S3 (Tuesday, Jan 26), S5 (Thursday, Jan 28), and S6 (Sunday, Jan 31).
14. "He went back out through the alley" should become "through the lanes" or "through the building."
15. Define "the Open" the first time it appears, as a short phrase in dialogue.
16. "went out the back to coach a league..." Make the move between buildings clear: he locks up the store and drives across the cove to the alley.
17. Hollis's notebook after "Told her last night": clarify what he writes or cut it.

## Voice

18. Machine similes ("the way he'd feel a belt for glazing," and so on): one in the chapter at most.
19. Cut stated feelings: "if anybody had asked which he wanted...", "He had kept the league up for Linda.", "Now she was selling it to the hardest room on the lake without him..."
20. At least two scenes end mid-motion or mid-line. No more than two end on a placed object or a small gesture.
21. Give the treasurer and the raffle woman different registers from Hollis and from each other: one rambles, one hedges.
