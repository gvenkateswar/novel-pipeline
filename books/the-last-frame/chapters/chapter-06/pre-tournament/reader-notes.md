# Reader Notes: Chapter 6, "Walking Ice"

**Draft read:** final.md, Oct 29. Chapters 01 to 06 read in order. No other file opened.

**Warning above the score:** I'd keep reading, but only because of the bob-house fight, and only grudgingly. After that scene the chapter loses steam three times in a row: a committee meeting, a petition, and a page of arithmetic. If I were reading on my phone, I'd have put it down for the night during the Danny scene, and I'm not sure I'd have come back.

---

## 1. Pull score: 4 / 10

The first scene is the best confrontation in the book so far. The chapter then undoes it. One sentence tells me Ray has been back in her second chair "most nights since the ninth," so the fight changed nothing between them. After that come a committee vote I could see coming, a petition he won't sign, and a check in an envelope with no name on it, which I can guess the end of.

---

## 2. Where I got bored

- **Line 145, about 50% in:** "She briefed from page one, standing, and she gave it to them as she had given it to Ray, no figures, Linda's name in the middle of it." I've already heard this pitch once, eighty lines ago. Now the book is telling me she gave it again. My eyes slid down the page looking for the flags to go up.
- **Line 227, about 78% in:** "She'd declined to answer him at the January meeting, and he'd turned that over and put it down to a woman who wouldn't haggle over a word with the league watching. To set the minutes straight he'd have had to stand up..." A paragraph of Ray explaining to himself why he won't sign. This is the scene's tension, and it gets handed to me as a summary of his reasoning. I didn't feel it.
- **Line 263, about 90% in. This is where I drifted for good:** "Ray didn't know how far under she was. She'd given him the bank's number and the seven hundred a week, and told him the old rate wouldn't make hers, and past that she'd never said a figure..." Bank numbers, a paydown, a tape, a statement, a check whose amount nobody says out loud. I stopped following the money and waited for the scene to end. The whole Danny scene is a man at a desk doing sums about a decision he has already made ("You'd already done it." "Wanted to hear the machine say it."). Nothing happens in it that his last paragraph doesn't also cover.
- **Across the book:** bylaws, lineage rates, committees, minutes, a derby committee vote. Six chapters in, the plot runs on procedure. Chapter 6 has two meetings (bob-house committee, Dale's petition) on top of the league meeting in Chapter 5. A reader who wanted a love story has been sitting through a lot of meetings.

---

## 3. Where I got confused

- **The time jump into scene 2.** Scene 1 ends with Ray telling Hollis, "She's got business for your committee," so I read scene 2 as the same morning. Then, in the middle of the paragraph: "She'd had them a week." The week is tucked into a sentence about gloves. I had to go back.
- **Why does a fishing-derby committee vote on a bowling tournament?** I gather it's about putting the Open on the derby's schedule, but the page never says what their yes gets her, or what their no would have cost her. So the vote has no stakes for me.
- **The money in scene 4.** "Run that against her bank's number, the way a bank'd figure a paydown." In Chapter 2 the bank's condition was that the winter clears thirty thousand with every dollar still in the account. How does Ray's personal money change that? Is it a gift into the account? A paydown on her loan? Does it count toward the thirty? I don't know what the check does, so I don't know what it means.
- **"The minutes were the last thing holding Linda's Saturday where it was."** The Open has already moved Linda's Memorial onto derby day and opened it to anyone. What are the minutes still holding? I couldn't follow Ray's logic for refusing Dale.
- **"Most nights since the ninth."** The ninth of what? I had to work out that the ninth was the bob-house Saturday. If so, he walked out on her and then sat in her office every night afterwards. How did that get patched up? It happens off the page.
- **Dale and "the captain."** Back in Chapters 3 and 5 I wasn't sure whether "the captain" and Dale Hutchins were the same man. Chapter 6 assumes I know Dale's crew is the Highway team. I mostly do, but I had to piece it together.
- **The treasurer and the woman in the snowmobile suit** are introduced, vote, and have no names. Fine as walk-ons, but in a shack this small I wanted to know who I was looking at.

---

## 4. Where I stopped believing

- **Ellen keeps the condition from Ray.** She's a woman who "wrote it up myself before anyone asked" after every mistake of her career. She let him vouch for half a sentence in front of the whole league, saw that he'd missed it, and said nothing for weeks. The page's reason is "it was priced in him," and I can see it. But the obvious safer choice was to tell him in the second chair on any of six nights, before the vote. The page never really rules that out. It reads as the misunderstanding the plot needs. When he finally asks, "Did you know I didn't hear it?", her answer, "I said it," lands, but it makes her hard to root for.
- **The fight has no aftermath.** He walks out bare-handed onto the ice, which is great. Then one sentence later in the chapter he's been sitting in her chair nightly "since the ninth." Either the rift is real or it isn't. The page tells me it wasn't.
- **Her hands go white from the cold, again.** Shoveling in Chapter 3, the bob-house in Chapter 6, then she strips her gloves twice more on the ice. A woman who asks whether the ice is black or white, and buys a fishing license at 4:10 a.m., doesn't forget gloves three times. It reads as the author arranging another chance for Ray to warm her hands.
- **Two flags go up at once in the middle of her pitch.** Convenient. Her fish comes up and his line comes up empty, which is too neat a picture of the plot.
- **Ray empties his savings.** He swore never to put the store up twice, and he's paying Concord to keep his own loan alive so he can buy the alley if she fails. Now, after learning she hid the condition from him, he draws nearly everything he has to help her succeed. That might be love, but the page skips the step where he decides it, and goes straight to "You'd already done it." Also, I've watched her refuse his free labor ("An invoice goes with the money, Mr. Gagnon"), so I already know she won't take the check. That drains the suspense.
- **Denise's "That night in the pit—".** She almost tells him, then swerves into a run-on about shoes. It's a beat that exists to hold something back, and it holds back something Ray already knows as of scene 1.

---

## 5. Where it sounded like a machine

- **Everybody tells everybody first.** "Thank you for being here. I wanted you to hear this before the committee and before the town." This echoes Chapter 4 ("I wanted you to hear it from me first," and "you'd better have it from me before the post office does") and Chapter 5 ("She wanted it said in this room, to Denise, before..."). Now Ellen brought the Open notice to his store "herself so he'd see it first." It's a tic.
- **Ray hears people like engines.** "He could hear a clutch start to slip from the far end of the lot with the truck running." "It made the sound a starter makes on a cold morning, grinding through to something and catching." Earlier: "the way a motor runs fast before it's warmed through," "the small change you hear in a motor the second before it catches," "when a clutch was about to go." Five or more across the book. It was charming once.
- **"Fella I knew in Meredith..."** is the second "Fella I knew in [town]" story, after Tilton in Chapter 2. A second one makes it a routine.
- **"He knew where she was without turning his head, and then he turned it."** Nearly word for word Chapter 5's "She knew where he was before she turned her head, and she turned it." Mirrored on purpose or not, it reads like a template.
- **The narrator explains the subtext.** "Without Linda, she meant." "Ray knew what *like* meant in her mouth. It meant seven sharp." "The short words had gone out of her again. Ray heard them go, and put it down to the cold."
- **Recycled props.** Adding-machine tape curling "over the edge of the desk" (Chapter 4 and again here). "The lanes lay bright and backward" through the gap (Chapters 4 and 5). Squaring things: pages, score sheets, pins. "The one with the stubs."
- **The wise son.** "Dad. You're allowed to want something that isn't on a shelf." A greeting-card line. Danny had been better as the man with the rock salt held in midair.
- **Every scene ends on a quiet gesture:** "She's got business for your committee." / "crossing the ice." / "Ray bent back down to his laces." / "and zipped the jacket." Four scenes, four soft landings, no hooks.

---

## 6. What I think is going on

Ellen will make her number, or come close, through the Open, using Linda's name. Ray will try to give her the cashier's check, and she will refuse it, or he'll slip it in somehow and she'll send it back with an invoice-style note. The real climax is Ellen doing what she has always done: writing up her own error in public. She'll stand at the Memorial or before the league, tell them Ray vouched for half a sentence because she let him, and give the league its Saturday back even if it costs her the March number. I think her unstated "third item" for buying the alley is Ray himself: she heard his league that night in the vestibule, or heard him. Locker fourteen is Linda's, and Ray will open it with Ellen near the end. Something inside (Linda's ball, shoes) gets used at the Memorial. Denise knows about the condition and will force it into the open if Ellen doesn't. The dead K will be lit in the last scene, by a sign shop, paid for properly, with Ellen's say-so. Dale's teams go to Pine Ridge for a while and come back for the Memorial. Ending: Ellen keeps Kettle Lanes on March first, Ray tears up the Concord hold, and the orange-tag key or a new one ends up back on his ring. Kate's "Is anybody there with you tonight?" gets its answer in a phone call near the end.

## 7. The listener pass

- **Dates the ear can't hold:** "since the ninth," "Saturday the thirtieth," "on the fifth of January," "the half ended Saturday, and tomorrow was a Tuesday." Spoken aloud, I have no idea where we are in the winter.
- **The italic *like*** in "Ray knew what *like* meant in her mouth." A TTS voice can't stress it, so it comes out as "Ray knew what like meant in her mouth," which sounds like a typo.
- **The pages:** "Page one is... Page two is the plan if the ice goes. Page three is the terms." Then later "Plan if the ice goes?" "Page two." It works on paper. By ear I'd lost which page was which.
- **Glove scene, lines 63 to 115.** A long alternating exchange with almost no tags, broken up by long glove-fitting sentences. "Anybody can bowl it." / "Anyone who enters." / "Anybody off the ice." By the time "The league's bowled for it five Februaries" arrives, a listener has to count back to know it's Ray. The anybody/anyone split is the only speaker cue, and the ear won't catch it.
- **Line 89, a 70-word sentence of "and... and... and"** about the glove going on. Read aloud, it's a breathless run.
- **New names in one breath:** "Pine Ridge Lanes... Gil's place, over in Halsey Falls," then "Gil Arsenault." Three proper nouns for one throwaway.
- **"H. PIKE"** on the door. The voice will say "H Pike." Fine, but pointless aloud.
- **Arithmetic scene:** "statement," "bank's number," "paydown," "the line," "cashier's check," "the store's long checkbook, the one with the stubs." Heard rather than seen, this is noise.
- **Sentence shapes:** scene 4 runs "Danny looked... Danny punched... Danny tore... Danny reached... Danny read... Danny looked..." A run of the same subject-verb opening.

---

## 8. The romance

Yes, in the first two scenes, then it disappears.

- The glove: "The left was colder than the right had been, and he held it a second between both of his before the glove went on, which he hadn't meant to do, and then he got on with the glove." That made me want them together, mid-fight, which is the right place for it.
- "He went out bare-handed with his gloves still on her." He's angry, and he still leaves her the gloves. Good.
- She returns the gloves a week later, "warm when he took them." Nice.
- Laughs: Hollis's "Then it won't take long." The license: "Says you bought this at ten past four this morning." / "I was up." The Meredith widow and "the fella who sold him the rake" got a smile.
- After the bob-house, the two of them share no scene for the rest of the chapter. She watches the petition from the register "and didn't come down." He sits alone with an envelope. Half the chapter is the romance offstage.

---

## 9. What I want to know next

- Does he give her the check, and what does she do with it? (I think I know: she refuses. So this pulls less than it should.)
- Will Ellen own up, to Ray and to the league, that she let him vouch for half a sentence? This is the one real question pulling me.
- Do Dale's teams actually leave for Pine Ridge on Tuesday, and do the rest follow?
- What's in locker fourteen? (Planted in Chapter 1, still unopened. I've half stopped caring.)
- What's her third item?

That's a short list, and most of it I can already guess. The page hasn't made me afraid of anything. The worst case is the alley going to a man who'd "have kept it nice."

---

## 10. Best moment

> "Did you know I didn't hear it?"
>
> She didn't answer straight off. Somewhere out toward the far shore the ice let go a long boom that went under the shack and away. Her gloved hands closed on the pages and creased the top one. She looked down at his gloves on her hands, and her eyes stayed there.
>
> "I said it," she said.

This is the only moment in six chapters where I was afraid for them.
