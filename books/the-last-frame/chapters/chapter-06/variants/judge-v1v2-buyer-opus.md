A = v1.md, B = v2.md

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** I'd keep reading B because he puts his gloves on her finger by finger "the way he'd done it last for Danny at the bus stop," says "they're yours," and she "stood and let him." Then the clock story in the bob-house made me like the man before he ever admitted he was courting her. A is the same chapter told more like a list of business.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 8%, in Arlene's kitchen: "A week ago at his own counter, in front of his son, he had said out loud what his no was worth, and on the first of March the store went to Danny, and since then the two had come to be one paper." I had to read that twice to work out which paper. Just before it is "He told her what was in it. A mutual release, one clause..." so I'm in a lawyer's office before I've had any ham. The thumb sped up again at about 52%, where she works out the option to buy: "Arlene would go into The Pines on the first of March, paid. The building would come back to her, in a year or in five." That's a spreadsheet in the middle of the one scene where they're alone together. A did win me back with the actual touch: "the knuckle with the lead on it came against his thumb and stopped there. It was warm." B never gives me that.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 78%, in the bob-house, when Hollis starts on the derby: "Gil Arsenault'd drive over from Pine Ridge in Halsey Falls for it. She beat him in the 'ninety-four open, and he still brings it up." It's a new name, a new town and an old tournament while Ellen stands there holding a fish, and I skimmed down to "Judy's the other vote out here." Then the committee talk with Judy ("The underwriter has never met you") is money again, and I only came back for the gloves. The long Kettle Road paragraph at about 15% runs long too, but "You'd have kept it a museum" carried me through it.

5. **Confidence: low.** The scenes and most of the lines are the same. B wins on the glove scene and the clocks. A has the one real touch between them. I could see another night going the other way.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** The dialogue is almost word for word the same in both, from "like a man buying a boat" to "She had friends."
