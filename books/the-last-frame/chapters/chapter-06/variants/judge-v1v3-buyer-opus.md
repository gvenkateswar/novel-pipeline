3. **Where A lost me:** At about 7%: "He had said it himself a week ago, at his own counter with his son ten feet off: what his no was worth, coming from the man who bought if Ellen missed. On March first the store went to Danny, and the two were one paper now..." I had to read it twice to work out whose paper was which, and my thumb started moving. I drifted again at about 80% on "So the derby's indoor day would come to her on Sunday, for a venue fee," which is bookkeeping. The bowling-alley scene pulled me back in, but it ends with "He took his hand off it" and nothing happening between them, and that let me down.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 5%: "He told her what was in it. A mutual release, one clause, that let all three of them out of the backup contract once all three had signed." That's a lawyer talking, and it comes before I care. Arlene's photograph going face down into the box won me back. At about 78% I skimmed "The derby's indoor day was hers on Sunday, for the house's fee" the same way I did in A. The three "He took that one" lines at the counter came close to a list, but each one hurt, so I stayed.

5. **Confidence: medium-low.** These are the same chapter with a few beats moved around. B wins on the warm knuckle, the photograph, and "the coldest hands on this lake." A is cleaner in places, and its "I'll wait on him" opening at the lanes is good.
