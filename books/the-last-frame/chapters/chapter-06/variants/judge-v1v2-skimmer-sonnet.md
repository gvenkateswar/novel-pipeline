A = v1.md, B = v2.md

1. **Winner:** A

2. **Why:** A opens on a voice line ("They never clean it," in the voice she used for the price of oil) and has Ellen's knuckle, with the pencil lead on it, come to rest warm against Ray's thumb. In B the hand moment is told ("closer to hers than either of them had let it come since the pit at midnight") and the opening is paperwork.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 52%, at "He could see her running it. Arlene would go into The Pines on the first of March, paid. The building would come back to her, in a year or in five. The lanes would stay lit." That is the option-to-buy-back terms laid out as a ledger, and my thumb sped up. It sped up a little earlier too, at the three-times "He took that one." run of objections (~44%). It picked back up at "the knuckle with the lead on it came against his thumb."

4. **Where B lost me:** About 15%, at "He had carried that evening ever since, the evening he drove up Kettle Road with his offer folded in his jacket and saw her office light already on at the top of the drive and took it for a good sign..." That is one long backstory block on a page I had barely started. The thumb sped up again at the Danny/father paragraph (~38%, "His father had stood at that register in his overcoat the week he died...") and at "It was a short paper, one clause long, and it put his hand closer to hers than either of them had let it come since the pit at midnight" (~62%). The Wolfeboro clocks story in the bob-house (~58%) is the best thing in either version, and I'd keep it.

5. **Confidence:** Low. It's close to a coin flip. They share about 80% of the text. B has the better bob-house scene (the clocks story, "She saved her temper for March") and a tighter Ellen scene. A has the better opening and the better touch.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same Danny scene almost line for line, same Arlene, Hollis and Judy beats, same last four lines.
