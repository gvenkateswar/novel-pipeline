A = v1.md, B = v2.md

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** In A the two of them have their hands on the same paper at the lanes and her knuckle ends up warm against his thumb, and that held me through the contract talk. B rushes that same scene to get to the business answer and spends its long paragraphs on Ray thinking in Arlene's kitchen and at the register, which is where a tired thumb starts moving.

3. **Where A lost me:** about 52%, at the lanes, when Ray offers the option and the page lays the numbers out for me: "Arlene would go into The Pines on the first of March, paid. The building would come back to her, in a year or in five. The lanes would stay lit." That reads like a man adding up a ledger in his head, and I skimmed it. Then her landlord line came and pulled me back. (Judy's "March first goes to committee... The underwriter has never met you" at about 85% made me skim a little again in both versions.)

4. **Where B lost me:** about 14%, in Arlene's kitchen, at the long block that starts "He had carried that evening ever since, the evening he drove up Kettle Road with his offer folded in his jacket and saw her office light already on at the top of the drive..." It's a whole paragraph of Ray going back over things after she has already said it out loud, and I skimmed down to the next quote. The same thing happened at about 37% with the Bench paragraph ("Ray could see the next weeks from here: the envelope on this counter at six tomorrow and every morning after..."). The lanes scene in B then goes "Then I keep the paper and write you an option" / "Then you'd be my landlord" / "He had nothing behind that one." That's two people closing a deal, and the hands on the paper never touch. B's best stretch is after that: the Wolfeboro clocks story and the slow glove scene ("two small fingers would go into one hole") are better than A's. But by then I'd already been skimming twice.

5. **Confidence: medium.** B's bob-house and glove scenes beat A's, and on a different night that could swing it. A's opening (each of Arlene's hard lines landing as she tapes up another box) and its lanes scene are the two places a tired reader decides whether to stay, and A wins both.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Same scenes, same order, and many lines are word for word ("like a man buying a boat," "A winter I was winning," "Half out's not out").
