A = v2.md, B = v1.md

1. Winner: A

2. Why: A sounds like people in a town, with the Hollis clock story, the lake that "saved her temper for March", and Ray's gloves-are-yours line. B has the same bones but spends more lines explaining what it already showed.

3. Where A lost me: "At his own counter, in front of his son, he had said what his no was worth coming from the man who bought if she missed, and answered it himself. Nothing." (~8%). I had to reread it to learn it was a callback, and it's the one knotted sentence in an otherwise plain opening. I did not lose interest, only footing.

4. Where B lost me: "He took that one. He had watched Arlene tape a box of it shut this morning..." then "He took that one too." then "He took that one, and it came to him in Arlene's voice..." (~62-66%). It's the same device three times in a row, and it narrates Ray's reaction to each of Ellen's lines when her lines already landed. My eye started skipping here. The closing glove speech ("These went in that hole to the wrist after his fish, and they'd have frozen to the line...", ~93%) is a man explaining his tenderness instead of doing it.

5. Confidence: medium. The two share almost every line of dialogue, so the call rests on texture, the repeated-device tic in B, and A's better scene endings. B's knuckle-against-thumb beat is warmer and more romantic than A's draft lifting the paper. A reader who wants the touch spelled out could prefer B. I'd bet on A, not heavily.

6. Same-author check: Yes. The dialogue is nearly identical, and the differences are in the connective prose.

Calibration note: neither reads like A1. Both have a business scene (Arlene's kitchen), but each is broken up by objects, a third voice, or a touch. B's Ellen/Ray bowling alley scene leans closest to A1, because it is a point-by-point exchange of objections with Ray's reactions itemized. A keeps the objections but cuts the itemizing.
