A = v3.md, B = v2.md

1. Winner: B

2. Why: B gave me the Wolfeboro clocks and the lake that "saved her temper for March," and a glove scene that ends on warmth ("they're dry, and they're yours") instead of two people trading arguments, so I felt more of the two of them.

3. Where A lost me: "Six feet by eight, and all three of them had heard it." (~78%). I didn't know what the six by eight was or who the three were. Just before it comes "So the derby's indoor day would come to her on Sunday, for a venue fee," which is the book explaining its own plot to me, and my thumb sped up there. A second, smaller slip is at the glove scene (~90%). Ray is pulling gloves onto her fingers and says "A cup with nobody on it gets you the ones who were coming anyway. And the bank gets a vote." That repeats Hollis nearly word for word, and it turns the one tender moment into more business.

4. Where B lost me: "He had carried that evening ever since, the evening he drove up Kettle Road with his offer folded in his jacket..." (~12%). It is one long paragraph of backstory about the museum and the lamp. Just before it, "A man who had eaten the same supper at the same hour for thirty years was in no position to say when a ham ought to go in the oven, and he didn't say it" (~4%) made me stop and reread. Neither one made me quit.

5. Confidence: low. It is close to a coin flip. A is cleaner and more physical in the Arlene scene and the hands-on-the-paper scene ("He knew to the inch how far it was" is the best single romance line in either). B has more voice and the better ending. Both stay in rooms with the couple, and neither reads like A1.

6. Same-author check: Yes. They are the same chapter with the same beats and many of the same lines, and only the texture differs.
