A = v3.md, B = v2.md

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** It's the same chapter, but in B Ray has an inside and a sense of humor. There's the ham line ("a man who had eaten the same supper at the same hour for thirty years was in no position to say when a ham ought to go in the oven"), he dodges Hollis with the clock story, he realizes he's kept the store "a museum," and he says "they're dry, and they're yours" over the gloves. In A he mostly moves papers and hands while the other people talk.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 78%, in the ice shack, after Hollis's license / "Plan if the ice goes?" / "Page two." exchange: "So the derby's indoor day would come to her on Sunday, for a venue fee. Ellen nodded once." That's a line from a meeting summary, and I went fast from there to Judy. There was an earlier wobble at about 7%, where "On March first the store went to Danny, and the two were one paper now" made me stop and work out what it meant.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 33%, at the end of the hardware store scene: "His father had stood at that register in his overcoat the week he died, counting Ray's drawer a second time, and Ray had let him. Danny had let him do the same for four years running." I read it twice and still couldn't tell who "him" is in the second sentence. It pulled me out right when Danny's "I'd like it to mean me" should have been landing. B's derby business at Hollis's ("The club pays you for the room. That's my yes.") sags too, but less than A's, because the clock story and the fish come before it.

5. **Confidence: medium.** A is tighter line by line. Arlene taping the box while she lists what she'd lose is better staged than B's version, and A's "He knew to the inch how far it was" is the best single sentence in either text. But B's glove scene and Ray's two jokes are what I'd remember tomorrow. A craft reader who cares more about cutting than about voice could pick A.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** The dialogue is nearly word for word in both, and so are the scene order and the images (the pencils, the steel drawer, "walking ice").
