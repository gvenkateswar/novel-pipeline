4. **Where B lost me:** About 17%, at Arlene's table: "He had carried that evening ever since, the evening he drove up Kettle Road with his offer folded in his jacket and saw her office light already on at the top of the drive and took it for a good sign..." It's one long paragraph of him going over the past while Arlene sits there, and my thumb moved. It happened again at about 40% with the Bench paragraph ("Ray could see the next weeks from here: the envelope on this counter at six tomorrow and every morning after..."). Then the clock story and the ice brought me back, and the glove scene held me all the way to the end.

5. **Confidence: medium.** A is leaner, and its Arlene scene is better: she tapes the box shut while she lists what she loses, so something is happening while she talks. A's hands-on-the-paper moment at the alley ("He knew to the inch how far it was") also beats B's. A tired reader might like A for being shorter. I'm picking B because of how it ends.
