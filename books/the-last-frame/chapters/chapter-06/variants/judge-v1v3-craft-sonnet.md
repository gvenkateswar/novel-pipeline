A = v3.md, B = v1.md

1. Winner: A

2. Why: A keeps the business of paper and signatures inside people's hands and mouths (Arlene taping the box while she says the worst of it, Danny pricing cleats, Ray working a glove down finger by finger), while B stops to explain what its own gestures mean.

3. Where A lost me: "Six feet by eight, and all three of them had heard it." (~80%, in the bob-house after Hollis's Linda's-name speech). I couldn't tell what the measurement was or what "it" was. It reads like a line that meant something in an earlier draft. A smaller slip is the exposition block "He had said it himself a week ago, at his own counter with his son ten feet off..." (~7%). My thumb got slower there, but I didn't stop.

4. Where B lost me: "He took that one. ... He took that one too. ... He took that one, and it came to him in Arlene's voice as much as hers" (~45-50%, the Ellen scene). Ellen's speech is chopped into three pieces, and each piece gets a stated reaction. It is a device, and by the third repeat I saw it working. Earlier, "which was Arlene all over" (~3%) is a stock phrase. Late, "You've got about the coldest hands on this lake, and I'd like them warm." (~92%) says what the glove-fitting had already shown.

5. Confidence: medium. A is cleaner and has more separate voices. B has some good touches that A lacks (the photograph turned face down in the box, "by spring there would be somebody else's feet on them", the warm knuckle against the thumb), so it isn't a rout. Neither reads like the A1 code-and-procedure anchor, but B's Ellen scene is the closest thing to couple-doing-business in either text.

6. Same-author check: Yes. Same Arlene, Danny, Hollis and Judy lines, same "Voyons donc" and boat line, same scene order. This is two passes at one chapter, and the lines are mostly shared.
