1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** It's the same chapter both times, but in B he puts his own warm gloves on her and says "they're dry, and they're yours," and he tells Hollis about the man who filled his house with clocks and then went and sat in his truck, and those were the two places I put the phone down on my chest for a second; A's version of the gloves is lovely but tighter and cooler, more like a job done well than a man giving something away.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Ray took the second envelope out of his jacket, from the same office in Laconia, and laid it on the counter in front of his son: the store, Danny's, on March first." (~28%). I'd just sat through one envelope at Arlene's, and here came another. Then there was a credit-union form out of a drawer, and that was three papers before Ellen had shown up at all. I skimmed until the price gun got taken out of his hand. There was also an earlier wobble at "On March first the store went to Danny, and the two were one paper now" (~6%). I had to read it twice and still wasn't sure what was tied to what.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Ray laid the second envelope from the Laconia office on the counter in front of his son: the store, Danny's, on the first of March." (~30%). It's the same spot and the same sigh. B pulled me back faster, with the father counting Ray's drawer a second time the week he died. That's a feeling and not a form. B's long paragraph at Arlene's ("He had carried that evening ever since…", ~17%) is a big block on a phone screen, but it's a man being sorry, so I read it.

5. **Confidence: low.** These are close to a coin flip. A's bowling-alley hands ("He knew to the inch how far it was") beat B's, and A gets into the room more cleanly. I pick B for the gloves and the clocks, which made me feel more.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. They have the same scenes, the same beats, and whole lines word for word ("A winter I was winning," "a man buying a boat").
