1. Winner: A

2. Why: A keeps the paperwork inside people's mouths and hands (Arlene taping boxes while she lists what she loses, Ellen asking "Tell me what it's for"), so my thumb never had a reason to speed up, while B summarizes the contract for me and then runs Ellen's objections as a tally.

3. Where A lost me: "So the derby's indoor day would come to her on Sunday, for a venue fee." (~75%). It's a ledger line in the Hollis bob-house scene, who yes'd what and for how much, with the Gil Arsenault history right behind it. My thumb moved there. It picked up again at "Judy's the other vote out here" and at the glove scene, which is the best thing in the chapter. The paragraph at ~8% ("On March first the store went to Danny, and the two were one paper now...") also read as recap, but it was short.

4. Where B lost me: "He told her what was in it. A mutual release, one clause, that let all three of them out of the backup contract once all three had signed." (~5%). That's a contract being explained to me at 7 a.m. before anything has happened. The thumb sped up again in the Ellen scene, on the run of "He took that one." / "He took that one too." / "He took that one, and it came to him in Arlene's voice..." (~45-50%). It's four objections in a row, tallied like invoice lines, and by the third I knew the pattern. The warm-knuckle moment after it is good and nearly pulled me back.

5. Confidence: medium. B has the more tender beat (the knuckle against the thumb, "It was warm," and the "coldest hands on this lake" line). A's longing is held back ("He knew to the inch how far it was"), and that restraint could read as less payoff to someone else. It's close. A's dialogue is sharper and its exposition thinner, and for a tired skimmer that decides it.

6. Same-author check: Yes. Same chapter, same beats, same lines ("You're allowed to want something that isn't on a shelf," "She had friends"), and the same habit of ending scenes on a small physical sound.
