## Where the draft departs from the beat sheet, and why

1. **Hollis's money line (s2).** The beats give "Our people come in off the ice to bowl. Not to watch your league." The second sentence is an "X. Not Y." contrast, which the author's rule bans outright. On the page: Hollis, "Our people come in off the ice to bowl." The treasurer adds the fact in plain words: "They'll pay to enter a final, and they'll fill the lot doing it." The money fact still reaches a listener.
2. **"That's walking ice. That's not truck ice." (s1)** is also a contrast, so it's out. Hollis says: "Told her walking ice, and leave the car at the access."
3. **"Working each finger home the way he'd seat a part" (s1, beat 8)** would put a mechanical comparison inside the exchange, which the lead instruction forbids. The gloves are written as literal action: the cuff opened with his thumb, each finger worked down to the web and smoothed flat. Ray's two motor comparisons are in s2 (the spool, "the way a pulley spins when the belt's jumped off it") and s4 (the adding machine, "the sound a starter makes on a cold morning").
4. **Gloves phrasing.** "Pulled off his gloves" and "stripped off her gloves" don't trigger the `gloves? off` pattern. The phrase "gloves off" is nowhere on the page.
5. **Ray's lie-blindness (s1).** He reads "Without the Open I do not make the bank's number" as "Without Linda" in his own head. Nothing else in the scene interprets her pause or her eyes. After "I said it" he gets body only: "He had asked her once," and his bare hands.
6. **Denise's swerve (s3)** avoids "is all," which belongs to Ray (bible). Her swerve line is a run-on in her own register and ends on "you're holding up the pair."
7. **Danny's checkbook line (s4).** Danny doesn't mention his own signature on the credit-union form (bible: he never mentions it). His line is "Store's got a line at the credit union. It's what the line's for."
8. **Tip-up count** is kept off the page. Great Kettle has lake trout, and NH line limits differ on designated trout waters.
