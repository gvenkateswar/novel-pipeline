## Scenes

### Scene 1
**Setting:** Arlene's kitchen above Ambrose Pharmacy, up the steep side stair. Saturday, January 9, seven o'clock. Boxes marked THE PINES in her green-ledger hand stand along the wall and under the window down Main Street to the lake. **Sensory anchor:** the police scanner low on the counter, and Arlene answering it. **New texture detail (one):** one scanner call she comments on. Suggested: a chimney fire out past the Pelletiers' ("They never clean it."). The ham is in the oven.

**Characters:** Ray, Arlene.

**Want:**
- **Ray** wants Arlene to let him out of the backup contract. The reader learns it at his first line: "I'd like you to let me out of that paper." In the way: the paper is her bed. Without a signed buyer on March first she has no sale, no money and no place at The Pines.
- **Arlene** wants to walk into The Pines on the first of March owing nobody and paid to the dollar, and she wants the alley to live. The reader learns it in her plain account and in "I wanted it to live."

**Surface action (P = played in real time; 1L = one line or one clause):**
1. **P.** Ray lays an envelope beside her cup before he sits: "I'd like you to let me out of that paper." Arlene doesn't touch it. She turns the scanner down a notch.
2. **P.** He tells her what's in it: a mutual release, one clause, drawn Monday by a lawyer in Laconia, his own money. "Have your own lawyer read it before you sign anything." Arlene: "You had a lawyer draw it before you asked me." Ray (optional; it can be his only defense): "Seemed a poor way to ask, with nothing in my hand."
3. **1L, Ray's interior.** Why he's here, one sentence, his own words coming back to him: what his no was worth, said at his own counter in front of his son, and the store going to Danny on March first, are one paper now. He won't hand Danny the store as the man the end stool described over the cake. (Normie unnamed; flag 5.)
4. **P, never one line.** Ray asks the adult question: "Tell me what happens to you if she misses, Arlene, and tell it plain." She does, plainly, the way she talks about money, like weather:
   - "If she misses and there's no sale on the first of March, The Pines gives my place to the next woman, and I go back on the list at eighty-four."
   - "I get my money months from now, after I've foreclosed on that woman."
   - "And the man from Concord buys the lot for the land under it."

   Ray takes each of the three without answering. **Something that isn't arithmetic pushes here** (the cold reader's ask). While she says it she does one physical thing with her own packing: tapes a box shut, or turns a photograph face down for the box. That is the bed made visible.
5. **P.** The eight days, as Arlene tells it, unasked, because she has been angry at his anger since the sale: "It sat listed two years, Raymond. You came in eight days after she did. I'd signed with her by then, and I'd have signed with her anyway. You'd have kept it a museum. I wanted it to live." Ray's interior gets one clause of memory: the drive up Kettle Road that December with his offer, and her lights already on.
6. **P.** Her reason to release him anyway: "In nineteen seventy-one I held Normand to a building he never wanted, and he gave it eighteen years. I won't hold a man to a paper he's asked out of." (Normand, never "Normie", anywhere in this chapter.)
7. **P.** Ray signs first, at her table, with her pen, fast and plain. He doesn't read it again. **The laugh:** Arlene, watching: "Raymond, you've signed that like a man buying a boat."
8. **P.** She won't sign yet: "Voyons donc. Bring me her signature. It's hers to risk as much as mine. I sign last." She folds his signed release back into its envelope and puts it in his hand, not in her drawer.
9. **1L, the ending action.** She's already up, the oven door open, the ham out, carving, and sets a plate in front of him without asking whether he has eaten.

**Subtext:**
- Ray wants to be forgiven for being angry about the sale, and wants to be clean in front of his son. Neither is said.
- Arlene wants him to know she chose right and is sorry it cost him. Feeding him without asking is her apology.
- He came to give something away, and he leaves holding it.

**Texture budget:**
1. The scanner call (one line). It shows Arlene runs the town's weather, fires and money in one flat voice, which is why her plain account lands.
2. The THE PINES boxes, one physical beat (step 4). They make her bed a thing in the room instead of a clause.

The ham is the scene's ending action, not a third texture detail.

**Point of view:** Ray. His long patient sentences end on short ones; memory comes in as a clause; he notices wear (the box tape, her hands, the stair).

**Ends on:** consequence, ending mid-motion. The paper that was meant to set him free is back in his hand, unsigned by her, and Arlene is carving. No line under the plate.

**Word target:** 750.

### Scene 2
**Setting:** Gagnon & Son, Saturday, eight o'clock, two hours after Danny had the lights on. The Bench is on its three stools by the front window with the free coffee. **Sensory anchor:** the price gun's click. **New texture detail (one):** Danny pricing a carton of ice cleats with the price gun at the counter. **Want:**

**Point of view:** Ray. His interior is short here. The scene is Danny's.

**Ends on:** reversal, ending mid-motion. Ray came to hand the store over, and the store is handed back to him unsigned while his son works it. No line under Danny's walk down the aisle.

### Scene 5
**Setting:** out on the open ice off the point, at Judy Ostrowski's two tip-ups, the same morning, the sun just up and no warmth in it. **Sensory anchor:** the snow squeaking under boots in the hard cold. **New texture detail (one):** Judy re-baiting a tip-up with a smelt from her bucket and paying the line back down the hole.

**Characters:** Ray, Ellen, Judy.

**Want:**
- **Ellen** wants Judy's yes to the indoor day on Sunday. The reader learns it at her ask, which is one line.
- **Judy** wants her Saturdays left alone and wants to fund a loan she likes, inside the rules. The reader learns it at her first line.
- **Ray** wants Ellen to see what Hollis and Judy both just told her, and wants her hands warm, without giving her the name. The reader learns it at "You heard Hollis."

**Surface action:**
1. **1L, Judy's own want.** Judy, crouched at the hole, smelt in hand: "Saturdays I come out here so nobody talks to me about money."
2. **1L.** Ellen: "Hollis says yes to the derby's indoor day at my place on Sunday. He says it wants yours."
3. **P, never one line: Judy's rule, re-baiting through it.** Judy looks at Ellen's pitch for what it is, a woman close to the line, and doesn't stop working the line: "Close, is it? Then you know March first goes to committee. I like you. The underwriter has never met you."
4. **P, never one line.** Ellen, polite, procedural (her fear register): "The commitment says subject to committee. I read that as a formality." **1L, Ray's interior:** the first thing he has ever watched her read wrong. Her voice goes calmer as it goes, and he has heard her tell him what that calm is.
5. **P.** Judy, line paid down, flag set: "For a winter that clears, it is." A beat, then the vote, plainly: "You've got my yes for Sunday."
6. **1L, the laugh and the tact exit (moved from Hollis; flag 1).** Judy looks from one of them to the other: "Going to check my flags." Ray: "They're both down." Judy: "Then it won't take long." She walks off across the ice.
7. **P, the longing, at least 120 words in real time.** Ellen's hands are white from the wet wool. Ray pulls his gloves off and puts them on her, working each finger down into its place one at a time, the left hand and then the right, and he doesn't stop arguing while he does it. Ray's interior gets one clause of memory: the last hands he did this for were Danny's, small, at a bus stop. He talks about her hands, never about her (his tender register), and the plain word comes last.
8. **P, the argument, said while gloving.** "You heard Hollis. And you heard Judy." Ellen asks the adult question: "Are you changing your answer?" Ray: "No."
9. **P, the cut.** Ellen, gloved in his gloves: "Then your no stays what it is, and I'll go and ask the people it belongs to." Ray: "It belongs to me." Ellen: "It has Linda's name on it. She had friends." Cut. No reaction, no exit, no line under it.

**Subtext:**
- Everything his hands do says yes, and everything he says is no. She takes the gloves and refuses his reasons in the same minute.
- She has just learned the bank can say no to a winter that clears close, and the only thing the size of the gap is a name he won't give. So she goes over his head, openly, and tells him first.
- "She had friends" makes it everyone's: the name belongs to Linda's people too.

**Texture budget:** Judy's smelt and line. Judy keeps working while she delivers the worst news in the chapter, which is the comedy and the coldness of it.

**Point of view:** Ray. His sentences are short through the argument and longer only on the gloves.

**Ends on:** cut, dialogue. This is the chapter's one button: "It has Linda's name on it. She had friends."

**Word target:** 525.
