# Chapter 03 Issues

**Issue 1 — MUST-FIX**
- Problem: The scene 6 tally disagrees with earlier scenes in the chapter. Glow nights are set as Fridays and Saturdays, so by Saturday Dec 19 there have been four, not two. Scene 3 puts six party bookings in one week, but scene 6 puts six parties on the tape across the whole period. Dates, counts, and money must agree.
- Location: "Two glow nights went on the tape, then six parties, then the scavenged parts and the electrician, then Ray's invoices in his square capitals." Compare "Six party bookings for the week, each on its card in its slot." and "Those staying?" / "Fridays and Saturdays."
- Suggested fix: Change scene 6 to "Four glow nights went on the tape, then eleven parties" (or another count that exceeds scene 3's six). Alternatively, make the glow nights Fridays only in scene 4 and keep "two."

**Issue 2 — SHOULD-FIX**
- Problem: Scene 1 has two triplets. The bible allows at most one per scene.
- Location: "the lobby was all marble and brass and a calendar of loons" and "Spring leagues pay for summer, summer people pay for fall, and it all washes."
- Suggested fix: Cut the lobby line to "marble and a calendar of loons." Keep Judy's three-part line.

**Issue 3 — SHOULD-FIX**
- Problem: The "Not X. Y." pattern appears three times, and the bible allows at most one per chapter.
- Location: "Not in his hand. On the bench, beside the coffee can." / "Bowlers, honey. Not money." / "I'm not. I'm doing it at the clipboard."
- Suggested fix: Keep the narration instance. Rewrite Denise's line as "Bowlers, honey, in case anyone's counting." Change Ellen's reply to "At the clipboard."

**Issue 4 — SHOULD-FIX**
- Problem: Denise's answer to who will teach Ellen sounds like she is volunteering. Ellen then texts Ray with no stated reason, so the reader loses the thread.
- Location: "Who's going to teach me?" / "Honey, who do you think?" followed by "LESSONS 0700 MONDAY. RATE PER OUR AGREEMENT."
- Suggested fix: Make Denise's reply point at Ray, for example "Honey, who do you think has been fixing your machines?" Alternatively, add one line of Ellen's reasoning before she texts.

**Issue 5 — SHOULD-FIX**
- Problem: The handshake happens twice. A second paragraph also opens with "He", right after the paragraph before it opened with "He wiped".
- Location: "He wiped his hand on a rag from his back pocket and held it out. She shook it." / "He had not said it back to her. She noticed that he hadn't, and his hand was already out, and she took it."
- Suggested fix: Cut "She shook it." from the first paragraph, and open the interior passage with "Ellen noticed that she had not got a readback" or similar. Keep the "his hand was already out" beat in the interior passage.

**Issue 6 — SHOULD-FIX**
- Problem: Ellen never fudges a number, even to herself. Her own figures put her at 88%, not 90%. Nineteen of 90 days is $6,333 on a straight line, and $5,600 is 88.4% of that.
- Location: "Ninety percent of where a straight line to thirty thousand said she ought to be."
- Suggested fix: Change it to "Eighty-eight percent of where a straight line to thirty thousand said she ought to be." Keep the generic "At ninety percent" idiom in the next sentence, or change it to "At eighty-eight."

**Issue 7 — SHOULD-FIX**
- Problem: Rule 16 and the ban on aphoristic closers. The scene 6 interior paragraph ends on a line that states its own meaning.
- Location: "At ninety percent you either got ahead of the work or the work got ahead of you, and the room stayed perfectly quiet either way, right up until it didn't."
- Suggested fix: Cut everything after "work got ahead of you." Alternatively, replace the sentence with a concrete action, such as Ellen drawing a line under the total.

**Issue 8 — SHOULD-FIX**
- Problem: Denise's crossword clue has the wrong letter count. "Revealed" has eight letters. Denise does the crossword in pen and would not make this mistake.
- Location: "Seven letters, 'exposed.' Revealed. It's a theme."
- Suggested fix: Change "Seven" to "Eight."

**Issue 9 — SHOULD-FIX**
- Problem: Both similes come from outside Ellen's idiom of sequences, intervals, and numbers. In scene 5, Ellen's POV narration uses them.
- Location: "skipped twice like a flat stone on a pond" and "It fit in her palm like a grapefruit"
- Suggested fix: Rewrite the first as "touched down twice, at intervals she could have timed with a watch." Cut the grapefruit comparison and state the ball's size as "four and a half inches across, no holes."
