# Judge: Chapter Two, v1 (A) vs v2 (B) — persona Craft, model Opus

Read: A = chapters/chapter-02/variants/v1.md, B = chapters/chapter-02/variants/v2.md, in that order, in full.

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** In B the two of them actually talk to each other in the one scene where they're alone, and she sounds like her and he sounds like him ("It's eleven minutes past ten." / "She can hear you, you know. She'll go slower out of spite."), whereas A hands me the best conversation in the chapter secondhand.

3. **Where A lost me:** about 65%, the lane scene, when the talk I'd been waiting for since the counter gets summarized instead of said: "She asked what the part was called that he'd just set on the floor, and how he could tell from the lot, and he called every answer up the lane louder than it needed." I wanted the lines. The "saving things up to tell her" that follows is lovely and almost buys it back, which is why this is close.

4. **Where B lost me:** twice, briefly. About 2%, the second paragraph, a stack of pluperfects arranging the scene after the fact: "Ray had been counting a drawer Danny had already counted, twenties first, when the bell went over the door and the cold came in with her... She had pulled one glove off with her teeth, laid the phone flat by his hand and touched it, and she had not said good morning." I could feel the paragraph being built. And about 92%, inside the last minute before five, a flashback to Wednesday noon and a roll of register tape: "at noon Wednesday he had opened the drawer for a roll of register tape and found Danny's name on the line in Danny's careful hand" — the clock is about to jump and the chapter steps out of the room to explain a signature. A does the same information in two sentences at the top of the scene and gets on with it.

5. **Confidence: medium.** Same bones, same best lines, same ending. A has the better wit at the counter ("I'm the top of it and most of the bottom" beats "most of it winters in Florida") and the better breath on the wrist (wind taking it and giving it back). B has the better invoice line ("An invoice is paid in money, Mr. Gagnon"), the better Bench retort ("Score it in the right column"), the plow going by, "I was nine," and above all two people bantering down sixty feet of maple instead of one man being told about it. The lane scene decides it.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes — identical skeleton, dozens of shared sentences, same ear for Normie; these are two passes by one hand, not two writers.
