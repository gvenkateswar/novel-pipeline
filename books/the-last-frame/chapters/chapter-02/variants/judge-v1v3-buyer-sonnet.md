# Judge: Buyer persona, A = v1, B = v3

1. **Winner:** A

2. **Why:** A keeps the two of them in the same small spaces, on the doorstep with the light and in the pit with the envelope. It also has the cleanest laugh (the "for the broadcast" line), and I never had to stop and work out a joke. B is nearly the same chapter, but it slows down where A moves.

3. **Where A lost me:** Near the end, at about 85%, where the key gets explained: "The key would let him in at her front door at any hour without knocking, without her having to come down through her own dark building and turn the lock from the inside, which was the very thing they had stood at her back door about last night." The scene had already shown me this, and I started skimming. Then "What it said past the door he couldn't read. He turned it over the way he'd turn a key somebody brought in off the street" slowed it down more. Smaller dip at about 55%, in the loan talk ("Granite Lakes writes me the loan the day this winter clears thirty thousand...") and the Normand line, which read like a ledger.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 22%: "Fella I knew in Tilton had an alley over there and wouldn't call me on principle, and drove all over three states looking for a pinsetter man, and found one, too, down in Lowell, eighty-some years old..." It's a long tangled anecdote, I couldn't tell who was who, and Ellen doesn't write any of it down. Bigger dip at about 88%, where the key meaning is one solid block: "The key opened her front door. It opened it at any hour, without his knocking on the glass..." I skimmed that block. I also got briefly confused at "He's the youngest of the four of you", because I couldn't tell who she meant.

5. **Confidence:** low. They are close to the same chapter, and I'd call it a coin flip leaning A. B has sweeter moments in the lane ("Thinking it over" / "Depends who's asking", and the Linda coffee can), but the Tilton story and the unclear Bench line cost it.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes, same beats and same dry New England voice; B reads like a re-run of A with the lines shuffled.
