1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** In A the couple is in the building together for almost the whole night scene, with her voice coming down the lane and the invoice fight afterward, while in B he fixes the machine alone and listens for her.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Granite Lakes writes me the loan the day this winter clears thirty thousand, with every dollar it makes still sitting in the account." (~35%) It's a money rule delivered flat, and the "Normand" back-and-forth right after it is more shop talk than I wanted. The other soft spot is the paragraph that explains the key at the end: "The key opened her front door. It opened it at any hour, without his knocking on the glass... and it was a no to selling." (~88%) It tells me what the key means after the scene had already shown it, and the sentence about the back door is tangled. I kept reading both times.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Way I've got it," he said to the cylinder, "you own the building, and Arlene's carrying the note, and if your bank hasn't paid Arlene by March first, that paper you signed sells it to me that afternoon at appraised value..." (~28%) This is a contract read aloud, and my thumb sped up. Then the night scene: "He worked. The first bolt on the old clutch had been on since before Danny was born and didn't want to come..." (~62%) It's a man alone with a machine and a flashlight she left behind, and she isn't there. I'd have moved to my next book around here if I were tired.

5. **Confidence: medium-high.** I'd say high on the night scene alone. The two share most of the morning and the five o'clock ending, and B's ending is a bit cleaner, so the overall gap is smaller than the night-scene gap.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes, same beats and the same Normie and bell closing line, and A reads like the same draft with more people in the room.
