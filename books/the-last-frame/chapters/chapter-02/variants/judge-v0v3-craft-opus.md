1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B the two of them actually talk to each other, and she sounds like nobody else in the book. "Then they had better not get notions," the lane conversation, the invoice from Worcester. A has the finer single sentences, but it spends its middle with him reciting a contract to a lock and then fixing a machine alone in an empty building.

3. **Where A lost me:** around 35%, "Way I've got it," he said to the cylinder, "you own the building, and Arlene's carrying the note, and if your bank hasn't paid Arlene by March first, that paper you signed sells it to me that afternoon at appraised value, so Arlene gets paid either way." I was reading a term sheet, and her answer was "Correct." It never fully came back for me. Scene three, "Her car wasn't in the lot," is a man alone with a machine. Listening for her voice in the empty building is a lovely beat, but she isn't there, and I was waiting for her to walk in.

4. **Where B lost me:** around 88%, "The key opened her front door. It opened it at any hour, without his knocking on the glass and without her coming down through her dark building to let him in, which was the thing they had stood at her back door about last night, and she had answered it without asking him for a thing." The book stops and explains the key to me, down to "it was a no to selling," when the key on the counter had already said it. I also skimmed a bit early, at the Tilton story ("Fella I knew in Tilton had an alley over there…"), which is Ray showing off. It's in his voice, though, so I forgave it.

5. **Confidence: high.** A is better line for line in places ("which was how Danny said most things," "She had told her daughter he was the locksmith, which he was"). But B's lane scene, with her small and lit at the far end, the slow ball that wobbles, and "I asked him. He only does machines," is the scene I'd remember, and A doesn't have one like it. "I bought it to hear all ten going at once. Last night I heard nine" gives her a want. A gives her a loan condition.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same skeleton, same images (the glove pulled off with her teeth, the old pins in the shirt pocket, the rock salt held in the air), and whole sentences shared word for word.
