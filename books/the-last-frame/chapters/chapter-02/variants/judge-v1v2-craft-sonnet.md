# Judge report: Craft persona (Sonnet), A = v2, B = v1

1. **Winner:** A

2. **Why:** A lets the two of them talk in scene, and each sounds like herself or himself ("An invoice is paid in money, Mr. Gagnon. That is how an invoice works." against Ray's "She can hear you, you know. She'll go slower out of spite."). B's best pit-scene moment gets summarized instead of played.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 88%, at the drawer paragraph: "He had left it in the drawer unsigned, and at noon Wednesday he had opened the drawer for a roll of register tape and found Danny's name on the line in Danny's careful hand..." The signature is on the counter before this paragraph and explained only afterward, so the rhythm stalls just as the scene should tighten. A smaller snag at about 82%: "The Bench went quieter than it had been," which is a limp comparative. Also, "and he turned that over and got nowhere with it" (about 85%) is a flat landing after a good sentence.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 58%, in the pit: "She asked what the part was called that he'd just set on the floor, and how he could tell from the lot, and he called every answer up the lane louder than it needed." This is the chapter's one chance for the two of them to be loud across sixty feet of maple, and it comes as a paragraph of summary. Earlier, at about 20%, "He'd said how he took it once, to a shoelace" made me stop and puzzle over it. The doorway paragraph ("nothing in the doorway but the two of them and the cylinder half out of the door and the coffee going cold on the sill") also pads.

5. **Confidence:** Medium. They are close. B has the better lines in places (the parking-space ball, the key "with no lock to go with it," the breath and the wind), but A is tighter and funnier in dialogue, and it keeps the pit scene live.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same cadence, same beats and the same set pieces, and A reads like a tightened pass of B.
