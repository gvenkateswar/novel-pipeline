# Judge report: v0 vs v3, Chapter Two (Buyer, Opus)

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B she stays in the room. She stands at the end of lane seven calling down to him, throws a ball so slow he could read the name on it, and hands him her stupid twice-underlined invoice. In A he fixes the machine alone in the dark and walks out, and I was in bed waiting for her to come back.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 34%, "Way I've got it," he said to the cylinder, "you own the building, and Arlene's carrying the note, and if your bank hasn't paid Arlene by March first, that paper you signed sells it to me that afternoon at appraised value, so Arlene gets paid either way." That's a closing statement, not a man on his knees beside a woman, and I read it twice to follow the money. I came back for the phone call with Kate. Then at about 66%, "He worked. The first bolt on the old clutch had been on since before Danny was born and didn't want to come," and she's nowhere in the building. That's a man alone with a machine, and my thumb started moving faster until the last scene.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 87%, "The key opened her front door. It opened it at any hour, without his knocking on the glass and without her coming down through her dark building to let him in ... and it was a no to selling." I'd already felt that the second she laid the key down, and he walked me through it anyway. It's a short dip, and the breathing paragraph pulled me back. His speech at about 35% ("Arlene had this place listed two years...") is business too, but his father sitting out in the truck in the dark kept me in it.

5. **Confidence: high.** Both end on the same key-and-clock scene, and it's good in both. What decides it is the night scene: B gives me the two of them hollering down sixty feet of maple at each other, and A gives me a bolt and an empty counter.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. It's the same rhythms, the same Bench, and the same "most people can't / most people won't" sentence word for word.
