# Judge report: Buyer persona (Sonnet)

A = variants/v3.md
B = variants/v2.md

1. **Winner:** A

2. **Why:** A gave me more of the two of them in a room, with more warmth and more of him wanting her (the light waiting where his hands were going, "A good many voices had come down that lane at him, Linda's among them, and none of them had come this low"), while B kept more of its attention on the ledger and the clock.

3. **Where A lost me:** The loan-terms exchange in the vestibule, at about 42%: "Granite Lakes writes me the loan the day this winter clears thirty thousand, with every dollar it makes still sitting in the account." My thumb sped up through the money talk, but the lock-confession beat just before it and her "Last night I heard nine" just after pulled me back in.

4. **Where B lost me:** The paragraph at about 88%: "Under his hand the form lay flat. He had left it in the drawer unsigned, and at noon Wednesday he had opened the drawer for a roll of register tape and found Danny's name on the line..." It is a procedure paragraph (form, drawer, slip, phone, minute count) sitting right where I wanted to be looking at her. The same loan-terms stretch at about 40% also read like paperwork.

5. **Confidence:** Medium. They are the same chapter beat for beat and close in quality. B's lane banter ("It's eleven minutes past ten" / "She'll go slower out of spite") is a real point in its favor. A has the better Bench joke ("He's the youngest of the four of you"), the Tilton story, and the Linda line, and its ending is cleaner.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes, same beats, same dry New England deadpan, and many lines are shared word for word.
