3. **Where A lost me:** At about 40%, in the lock scene: "Way I've got it," he said to the cylinder, "you own the building, and Arlene's carrying the note, and if your bank hasn't paid Arlene by March first, that paper you signed sells it to me that afternoon at appraised value, so Arlene gets paid either way." That is a contract read out loud. My thumb sped up there, and the next speech ("Arlene wouldn't carry the note without a buyer signed in behind it...") kept it moving. It sped up again at about 68%, when Ellen isn't in the night scene: "He worked. The first bolt on the old clutch had been on since before Danny was born..." Then comes "He listened for a voice that carried to lane ten... and the building gave him the boiler running down cellar and a ticking motor and nothing else." This is a man alone with a machine, and I skimmed to the final scene.

4. **Where B lost me:** Twice, both small. At about 17%, Ray's anecdote: "Fella I knew in Tilton had an alley over there and wouldn't call me on principle, and drove all over three states looking for a pinsetter man..." It's a digression, and Ellen doesn't write any of it down either. At about 90%, the key paragraph: "The key opened her front door. It opened it at any hour, without his knocking on the glass..." It explains what the key means, and I'd already got it from the image. I slowed there but kept reading.

5. **Confidence: high.** The choice is not close. In A the thing that costs me is the contract paragraph plus a whole scene without Ellen. In B the exposition is shorter and has feeling in it ("the winter his father sat out in the truck", "I bought it to hear all ten going at once. Last night I heard nine."). The lane scene is two people talking in a room, which is what I keep reading for. I'd bet money. The only reason for any doubt is that B's last paragraphs over-explain.
