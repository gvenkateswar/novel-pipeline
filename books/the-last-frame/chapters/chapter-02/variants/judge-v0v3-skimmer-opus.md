# Judge report: Skimmer, Opus

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** In A she's in the room for every scene, and she's funny: she tells the Bench he's the youngest of the four of them, hollers down sixty feet of lane, bowls badly, and pays him with an invoice from a man in Worcester. In B she leaves him alone in the pit with a clutch and a flashlight, and I was tired, so I skimmed it.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 36%, "Arlene had this place listed two years," and through "...the only way I get there is to put the store up behind it." That is a long speech about money said to a lock, and my thumb sped up. It brought me back with "I bought it to hear all ten going at once. Last night I heard nine." It slipped again at about 88%, "The key opened her front door. It opened it at any hour, without his knocking on the glass...", where the book stops and explains the key to me while the clock I actually cared about was running.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 35%, "Way I've got it... you own the building, and Arlene's carrying the note, and if your bank hasn't paid Arlene by March first, that paper you signed sells it to me that afternoon at appraised value." That's the contract, read aloud, and I skimmed it. It never really got me back. At about 66%, "He worked. The first bolt on the old clutch had been on since before Danny was born" begins a man alone with a machine in an empty building. At about 76%, "Concord would lend him what the appraiser said, through the end of the year, and the credit union would lend the rest" is the ledger again. The coffee "to a shoelace" and "Ms." were the only lines I slowed down for.

5. **Confidence: high.** B reads like A1 (procedure, the couple doing business, the man with the machine), and its third scene takes her out of the room altogether. A has the same business in it, but she is always there to answer it.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Same counter, same bell and Bench, same rhythms; A reads like the same writer on a better night.
