A = variants/v3.md, B = variants/v0.md. Persona: Craft. Model: Sonnet.

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A sounds like two people who cannot talk to each other talking anyway (the lock, the light, the hollering down sixty feet of maple), while B has Ray reciting the note and the March first clause to a cylinder and then spends its night scene with him alone in an empty building.

3. **Where A lost me:** The last scene, at about 88%: "The key opened her front door. It opened it at any hour, without his knocking on the glass and without her coming down through her dark building to let him in... and that was her answer, the one she had promised him before the committee sat, and it was a no to selling." The key and the clock already carried this, and the paragraph explains it. It is the one place A stops trusting me. A smaller dip at about 40%: "which sat on her about as well as a wet coat" is a simile that arrives on schedule.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 33%: "Way I've got it," he said to the cylinder, "you own the building, and Arlene's carrying the note, and if your bank hasn't paid Arlene by March first, that paper you signed sells it to me that afternoon at appraised value, so Arlene gets paid either way." "Correct," she said. That is two people doing business, which is the A1 failure. I skimmed the next two speeches too. The night scene (about 55-70%) lost me again: "He listened for a voice that carried to lane ten without being raised... and the building gave him the boiler running down cellar and a ticking motor and nothing else." The longing is real, but it is a man alone with a machine, and Ellen is not in the room. "Noted" is a fine button but a smaller laugh than A's "He's the youngest of the four of you."

5. **Confidence: high.** A has the better voice and the better scene work. The lane-seven scene (Ellen small and lit at the far end, the invoice-versus-envelope argument, "I asked him. He only does machines.") is the best thing in either text. B is cleaner at the sentence level in places, but its middle is procedure and its night scene is a solo.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same Bench, same Normie bit, same clock jump, same "That's the bell" ending, same plain-spoken Ray. They read as two passes at one outline.
