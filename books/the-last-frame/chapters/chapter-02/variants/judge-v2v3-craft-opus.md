# Judge: v2 (A) vs v3 (B) — Craft persona, Opus

**Winner: B**

**Why:** In B the two of them finally sound like two different people: Ray gets to ramble (the Tilton man who drove three states and got handed Ray's number) and Ellen gets to cut him off with "He's the youngest of the four of you," and that gap between how they talk is what I was reading for; A keeps them both clipped in the same register, so the voice is one voice doing two parts.

**Where A lost me:** "Under his hand the form lay flat. He had left it in the drawer unsigned, and at noon Wednesday he had opened the drawer for a roll of register tape and found Danny's name on the line in Danny's careful hand" (about 93%). The minute hand is one click off five and the author stops to explain Wednesday. B puts that where it belongs, before the bell. Smaller drift earlier: "League is at half past six tomorrow." / "I know when league is." / "It's eleven minutes past ten." (about 64%), which is two people reading clocks at each other, though "She'll go slower out of spite" rescues it.

**Where B lost me:** "She kept talking, about nothing that needed an answer, and he answered all of it." (about 69%). That is the one conversation in the chapter I wanted to hear, and B summarizes it. Also a small wince at "his knees had a word to say about it" (about 55%), which is a line I've read in a hundred books.

**Confidence: medium.** These are the same chapter with the bones rearranged, and A has lines B doesn't ("left the other seven standing there to talk it over," "He'd have given her the part and the hour and both his hands for the asking, and she had brought money," and the face going across at *in case you fell*). I'd pick B again, but not for money.

**Same-author check:** Yes. Whole sentences are shared ("held the light steady, which most people can't, and close, which most people won't"), and the clock, the key, and the bell land identically.
