# Judge report: Craft persona (Sonnet), A = v3, B = v2

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A has Ray sounding like a man telling a story and Ellen sounding like a woman filing a report, so the two voices pull apart on the page, while B's two voices drift closer together and it explains its big beats a little more.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Go ahead and look. It's a short list. Fella I knew in Tilton had an alley over there and wouldn't call me on principle, and drove all over three states looking for a pinsetter man, and found one, too, down in Lowell, eighty-some years old..." (about 13%). The anecdote is in character, but it tangles. I lost track of who wouldn't call whom, and I skimmed the rest. I slowed again at the key scene, "Whether it said anything past the door he could not read. He looked at the key, and it told him what a key tells anybody, and he stood there wanting it to tell him more." (about 90%). It is a good line, but it follows a paragraph that has already spelled out what the key means.

4. **Where B lost me:** "She was keeping the building. She had promised him an answer before his committee sat, and this was the answer... It was a no to selling. He could read that as plain as a tag." (about 87%). The scene's subtext is stated outright. The next line, "What he couldn't read was whether it said anything past the door, and he turned that over and got nowhere with it," is a flat summary where A gives a feeling. I also stumbled earlier at "'He declined payment,' she said. 'Score it in the right column.'" (about 14%), which sounds like a writer reaching for a clever comeback. In A, "He's the youngest of the four of you" sounds more like a person talking.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two are close. They share most of their skeleton and many lines. B's opening on the sound is the sharper hook and its prose is tighter. A wins on voice, the wet-coat "owing him" image, the lines about the voice coming down the lane "whole," and a slightly less over-explained ending.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. They are two passes at the same scenes, with the same beats, the same lines and the same rhythm of short declaratives followed by one long sentence.
