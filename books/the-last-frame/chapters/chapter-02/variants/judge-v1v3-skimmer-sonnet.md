# Judge report: Skimmer, Sonnet. A = v3, B = v1

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** B keeps the two of them in the room reacting to each other (her mouth going flat at "in case you fell," the coffee left on the sill, "started saving things up to tell her") and breaks its heavy stretches into short paragraphs, while A packs more of its explaining into solid blocks I'd run my thumb down.

3. **Where A lost me:** The cellar paragraph at about 40%: "He had a coffee can down cellar at home with fifty-some years of the same in it, pins and springs and the insides of locks off houses that had been sold or torn down..." It's a man alone with his own head, rehearsing a speech, and the thumb sped up. It sped up again at about 93% on the wall of text that starts "The key opened her front door. It opened it at any hour, without his knocking on the glass..." That one is a single block of him working out what a key means.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 90%: "The key would let him in at her front door at any hour without knocking, without her having to come down through her own dark building..." It's the same explaining beat, but it is split into three short paragraphs, so I kept reading. The only place I really slowed was the loan exchange at about 35% ("Granite Lakes writes me the loan the day this winter clears thirty thousand, with every dollar it makes still sitting in the account"). That reads like a ledger, though A has the same exchange.

5. **Confidence: medium.** They are close. A has the better jokes ("He's the youngest of the four of you," "Depends who's asking") and the Tilton story, which is fun. B is easier to skim without losing the thread.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. They are the same chapter built on the same beats, with the same ending bit, same bell line, and same voice.
