# Judge: Skimmer (Opus) — Chapter Two, A = v2, B = v1

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** B gave me more of the two of them in the doorway and more jokes to wake up for, and it got the loan paperwork out of the way before she walked in, so the last minute on the clock was just the two of them and I never had to slow down for a drawer.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Under his hand the form lay flat. He had left it in the drawer unsigned, and at noon Wednesday he had opened the drawer for a roll of register tape and found Danny's name on the line in Danny's careful hand, the store put up behind his offer by the man who would have to carry it if it went." (~93%). She's standing there, the hand is a mark off twelve, and I'm being told about Wednesday and a roll of register tape. My thumb moved and I had to go back to catch that Danny signed it. A's pit scene, I'll say, did not lose me at all; the "It's eleven minutes past ten" / "She can hear you, you know" back-and-forth is the best part of either version and I'd keep it.

4. **Where B lost me:** "She asked what the part was called that he'd just set on the floor, and how he could tell from the lot, and he called every answer up the lane louder than it needed." (~67%). That's a conversation I was promised and then got the summary of, with a man kneeling at a clutch housing, and I skimmed to "Throw one." Also, honestly, the first paragraph: "Ray was counting a drawer Danny had already counted at six, twenties first" is a man and a ledger before anything has happened, and I only came back when the bell went.

5. **Confidence: medium.** These are the same chapter with the furniture moved. I'd lean B on a tired night, but if A moved the Danny-signed-it paragraph up to the ten-to-five slot and kept its pit dialogue, I'd flip.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same bones, same lines in places ("Lock's the one I'm working on," "That's the bell"), same man, same clock.
