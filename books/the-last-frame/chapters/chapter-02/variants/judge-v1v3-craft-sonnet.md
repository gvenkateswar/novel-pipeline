# Judge report: Craft persona, Sonnet. A = v1, B = v3

1. **Winner:** B

2. **Why:** B lets the two of them talk across sixty feet of maple ("What's it doing?" / "Thinking it over." / "Depends who's asking."), and it has more sentences with a person behind them ("owing him, which sat on her about as well as a wet coat", "She threw again. It was worse."). A summarizes that exchange in a paragraph and is tighter but flatter.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 88%, when the key scene stops and explains itself: "The key would let him in at her front door at any hour without knocking, without her having to come down through her own dark building and turn the lock from the inside, which was the very thing they had stood at her back door about last night." Then it explains again: "This was the answer. It was no to selling." The scene had already shown this with the key, the clock and her breathing. I also tripped at 3% on the opening simile, "the way the Saturday show out of Boston called a big string", which I couldn't picture.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 15%, in Ray's Tilton story: "Fella I knew in Tilton had an alley over there and wouldn't call me on principle, and drove all over three states looking for a pinsetter man". It is voicey, but it is a digression that goes nowhere while she is waiting for a price. Ellen's comeback to Normie, "He's the youngest of the four of you," also didn't land for me. B repeats the key explanation at about 88% ("The key opened her front door. It opened it at any hour...") and the same "that was her answer" beat, so it is no cleaner than A there.

5. **Confidence:** Medium. They are close, because both are the same scenes with the same bones. A is the better-paced and more dramatic version, with sharper Normie and Ellen exchanges and the pitch broken up by her reactions. B is the better-sounding version. I'd bet on B at around 60/40.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same plain New England diction and the same "and... and..." rhythm, and many of the same lines ("Lock's the one I'm working on", "I bought it to hear all ten going at once").
