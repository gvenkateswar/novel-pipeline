# Judge: Skimmer (Opus) — Chapter Two, A = v3, B = v2

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** B hits me with the sound before it tells me who's sitting on which stool, and down in the pit it lets the two of them actually talk across sixty feet of lane, where A tells me "she kept talking, about nothing that needed an answer" and makes me take its word for it.

3. **Where A lost me:** The thumb started moving at "Fella I knew in Tilton had an alley over there and wouldn't call me on principle, and drove all over three states looking for a pinsetter man, and found one, too, down in Lowell, eighty-some years old" (~14%). A man telling a story about machine men to prove he's the best machine man. I came back for "He's the youngest of the four of you." It went again at "She kept talking, about nothing that needed an answer, and he answered all of it" (~69%), because that is the conversation I wanted and it got skipped. And the big paragraph starting "The key opened her front door. It opened it at any hour" (~94%) I read the first and last sentence of.

4. **Where B lost me:** "It opened it at four in the morning, or at noon on a Sunday, or at ten on a Thursday night with seven grinding" (~91%) and then straight into "Under his hand the form lay flat. He had left it in the drawer unsigned, and at noon Wednesday he had opened the drawer for a roll of register tape" (~94%). Two paragraphs of a man explaining a key and then a form to himself while she stands there. I jumped to the minute hand. Short of that, it didn't.

5. **Confidence: medium.** They are the same chapter with different trim, and the ending drags in both. B wins on the opening and the pit and loses a little of it back at the counter.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes in the same order with the same closing line; these are two passes by one hand, not two writers.
