# Judge: v3 (A) vs v1 (B) — Craft persona — Opus

Chapter Two, read after Chapter One. A = v3.md, B = v1.md.

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A's people sound less alike and its best lines are things nobody else in the book could say ("He's the youngest of the four of you," the Lowell pinsetter man asking for Ray's number, "She threw again. It was worse."), while B keeps stepping in to tell me what a line meant.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 93%, the paragraph that starts "The key opened her front door. It opened it at any hour, without his knocking on the glass and without her coming down through her dark building to let him in, which was the thing they had stood at her back door about last night" — I had just read the back-door scene; this is the page re-reading it to me, and it runs on through "and it was a no to selling" before it earns its way back with "it told him what a key tells anybody, and he stood there wanting it to tell him more." Earlier, at about 6%, "the way she'd been doing since the winter the Legion broke the ball return on four" was shop lore laid on top of shop lore, but I kept going.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 17%, "For the broadcast," she said. "He gave me the diagnosis for nothing and declined the money. Score it however you like." — Normie's bit was already funny; Ellen explaining it to the Bench flattens her into a second announcer, where A's one dry line made her a third kind of person in the room. Again at about 93%: "This was the answer. It was no to selling." — two short sentences hammering a thing the key had already told me. B does have lines A doesn't that I'd miss: "he started saving things up to tell her," the ball "at about the speed of a man looking for a parking space," and the key "somebody brought in off the street with no lock to go with it."

5. **Confidence: medium.** Same skeleton, same scenes, most of the same sentences. A wins on the lines that belong to one voice only; B wins two or three images outright. Not a coin flip, but I would not bet much.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes — whole sentences are shared verbatim ("which most people can't, and close, which most people won't"); this is one writer doing two passes.
