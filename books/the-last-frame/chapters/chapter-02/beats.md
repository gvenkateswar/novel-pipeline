## Flags before the beats

1. **Ray's lender commitment: the log, the outline and the bible disagree.** The log says "not placed in 01; introduce cold in Ch 04." The outline's plant table says "Ray's commitment good through March 1," planted Ch 01. The bible says the Concord commitment is good **through Dec 31** and is extended to March 1 for a fee in Ch 04. **Resolution here (binding from the orchestrator):** plant it in Scene 2 as "a bank in Concord that'll lend me what the appraiser says it's worth, good through the end of the year." Say nothing about renewing, so that Ch 04's renewal stays a choice. **Orchestrator:** after this chapter, change the log's Ch 04 note from "introduce cold" to "renew."
2. **Two Concords in one scene.** The outline puts the Concord lender and the condo man from Concord in the same argument. For a listener the two must not blur. The lender is always "the bank in Concord." The condo man is "a fella up from Concord in a car too clean for the roads, wanting the lot for condos." Keep them at least one exchange apart.
3. **How the at-cost offer works (architect's mechanism, for the domain checker).** The bank in Concord lends against appraisal. Ray's offer is what she paid, which is above appraisal, so the credit union has to lend the difference against the store, and that needs Danny's signature and the Thursday committee. This is the bible's research row ("lending against a store's real estate the gap between appraisal and an above-appraisal price") and the reason the commitment plant falls out of the argument naturally. Flagged `[CHECK]`: whether an SBA commitment written for the backup purchase at appraisal can fund an earlier purchase of the same property at a higher price, with the gap financed separately.
4. **"Then I'll see you March first" has no speaker in the outline.** It goes to **Ray**, after he has heard her swear to Kate.
5. **Danny's signature.** The bible says Danny had signed the form before Ray asked and never mentions it. Ray asked him Wednesday, off the page. In Scene 4 the signed form is on the counter, and Danny says nothing about it. Nothing more is revealed.
6. **Human-gate carry-over** (brief, Ch 01 notes): the readers found the contract muddled, and some sentences ran 70 to 100 words. This chapter gives the contract one plain exchange (Scene 2). Long sentences are kept to the high points and stay under about 60 words, except one per scene at most.

### Register

**Tense.** Ch 01 was comic, at a public party pace: forty-three people, a PA, a ruling. Ch 02 is private, quieter and tighter, and runs on clocks: half past seven, eight, ten o'clock, two minutes to five. The comedy shrinks to the Bench's two lines and Ellen's deadpan. The tension is a deadline Ray set on himself, and both of them know where it is. Ray's POV throughout: longer and looser sentences than Ellen's, with *and*-chains, rounding, machines as *she*, time dated by what broke, and memory slipping in as a single clause. Under fear he talks to the object in his hands. In anger he drops to five-word sentences and "Mrs. Daly." At least three of Ray's interior passages run longer than the dialogue around them (S1 the hand, S3 the empty pit, S4 the minute).

### Scene 1

**Setting:** Gagnon & Son Hardware, bottom of Main Street. Wednesday, December 2, half past seven, cold and clear. The store has been open since six. Sensory anchor: the bell on the door, and the cold that comes in with her and lifts the smell of birdseed and cut brass off the aisles.

**Characters:** Ray, Ellen, Normie and the Bench (three men; only Normie named), Danny (optional, at the register, one action and no line).

**Want:** Ellen wants the name of lane seven's part and Ray's rate, so she can hire it fixed before Thursday's league without asking him a favor. The reader learns it from her line: "I'd like the name of the part, and your rate." In the way: he'll give her the name for nothing and won't take her money, and she won't take anything for nothing. Ray wants her to stand at his counter a little longer. On the surface he wants to stay out of her alley ("Not my alley"). Under it, he'd do the job in a minute if she asked instead of hiring.

**Surface action:**
1. Ray behind the counter, recounting the drawer Danny has already counted (one clause: his want apart from her). The Bench on their stools by the coffee pot. The bell. Ellen comes in with the cold.
2. At the counter she pulls a glove off **with her teeth** and lays her phone down. She says nothing first. She plays the recording: a sound like a **chair dragged across an upstairs floor**, then the knock, and the same interval again.
3. Ray watches her bare hand, not the phone: cold-reddened knuckles, a plain steel watch. The memory slides in as a single clause: the same hand finished the eighth toggle, the ninth and the tenth, pressed the strip flat along its whole length and tore it against the panel edge while he stood knocking. **She finished the tape first.** That's the first thing he knew about her.
4. The Bench calls it like the Saturday show out of Boston, low and play-by-play ("Gagnon's at the counter, folks, and it's quiet in the house"). Do not quote any real catchphrase.
5. Ray shuts his eyes and names it before the recording loops twice. It's seven's clutch, slipping and catching, and the knock is the sweep coming down on it. He gives it as a machine with a temper ("She's not broken. She's sulking."). He adds that there's a spare on a nail behind seven with his writing on the tag.
6. She takes the reading glasses off their cord, gets a pen from the cup on his counter, and writes his answer down **in his words**, on a pad from her vest pocket. Interior, long: he hasn't wanted to watch anybody do anything in six years, and he watches her write it down.
7. "I'd like the name of the part, and your rate." He tells her the name again and says he hasn't got a rate. She says she'll find someone who has. He tells her to go ahead and look, and goes back to the drawer.
8. The Bench: **"And the veteran declines."** (the laugh, at Ray).
9. Ellen turns her head to the Bench and answers it as a literal fact ("Noted." or a dry one-liner), and the Bench takes it the way lane three took the altitude. She leaves the glove off. She picks up her phone.
10. At the door she turns his own line from last night back on him: "Eight o'clock, Mr. Gagnon. I'll hold the light." The bell, and she's gone before he can answer.

**Subtext:** She has come to the one man she won't ask, to get his answer without asking, and she has made a public appointment to kneel beside him. He wants to be asked. She offers to pay instead, and he takes the payment as the insult.

**Texture budget:** (1) **Diagnosis by sound.** Ray names the part with his eyes shut from a phone speaker. It shows the one thing in this town she can't get without him. (No second texture detail. The scene is her hand.)

**Point of view:** Ray.

**Ends on:** **Reversal.** She takes his gibe ("he'll want somebody to hold the light") and books herself into it. Last beat: "Eight o'clock, Mr. Gagnon. I'll hold the light." Then the door bell, and she's gone.

**Word target:** 650

### Scene 2

**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, the front door and vestibule. Wednesday, December 2, from eight o'clock. Cold and clear, the door propped open, and the lake wind coming across the lot to find the gap at the collar. Sensory anchor: the cold off the open door, and the warm fryer-and-coffee air from inside meeting it at the sill.

**Characters:** Ray, Ellen, Kate (by phone, heard only through Ellen's half).

**Want:** Ellen wants her locks changed while the man who held the old key does it, and the building plainly hers. The reader learns it when she takes the flashlight from his bag before he asks. Ray wants her to take one offer, his at-cost buyout, before Thursday at five, so the building comes to him clean and fair and nobody's mistake is bought cheap. In the way: she'd rather lose it on March first than hand it over now, and he will only ask once. The reader learns his want on the line "I'll buy it off you at what you paid. Every dollar back."

**Surface action:**
1. The back door is already done (one clause). Ray kneels at the front door. There's a cup of black coffee on the counter that she has set out for him, and he notices she remembered how he takes it. Optional, one line.
2. He pulls the cylinder. She holds the flashlight without being told how, **steady, which most people can't, and close, which most people won't**, and he feels **her breath on the back of his wrist** like weather coming in off the lake. His hands stop for a second, and she does not move the light. (The longing beat: give it room, three or four sentences, and no joke.)
3. **Texture:** he pushes the plug out and the old pins spill into his palm, small and brass and worn. He tips them into his shirt pocket instead of the trash. He keeps things.
4. **The contract, plainly, in one exchange.** Ray, to the cylinder, the way he'd say it to the Bench: *She owns it. Arlene's carrying the note. If her bank hasn't paid Arlene off by March first, the contract she signed sells the building to him at whatever the appraiser says it's worth, and he pays Arlene. Arlene gets paid either way. Only question's by who.* Ellen: **"Correct."** (Her word, and her only "Correct." in the chapter.) That's the whole restatement. Nothing about it gets repeated anywhere else in the chapter.
5. **The offer, said to the lock, not to her** (his fear register: he talks to the object in his hands). March is appraised value, and that'll come in under what she paid, and the difference comes out of her house. Or she sells it to him now, at what she paid, every dollar back. The bank in Concord is good for what the appraiser says it's worth, **through the end of the year** (the commitment plant; say nothing about renewing). The rest the credit union lends against the store, if Danny signs, and the committee meets **Thursdays at five**. His father put the store up once, in **'seventy-four**, and nearly lost it. Ray has never asked it of the store. **He's asking it once, and this is the once.**
6. She asks who else is coming. Nobody. Arlene had it listed **two years**, and the only other one who came up the drive was a fella up from Concord in a car too clean for the roads, who offered land value for the lot, for condos. (Keep him apart from the bank. See flag 2.)
7. She says no, plainly, and throws the bank at him: **"Granite Lakes writes me the loan the day this winter clears thirty thousand, with every dollar it makes still sitting in the account."** Ray gives the rule one beat so the reader holds it: "Still sitting there." "Every dollar."
8. Ray: **"Nobody's cleared thirty in this building since Normand."** Ellen answers literally and deadpan (for example, that she'll note the date when she does). The dialogue doctor owns the exact line. Ray: "Mrs. Daly." Ellen: "Ms." (optional; it's his anger tell, and the chapter's only "Mrs. Daly").
9. Her phone. **Kate.** Ellen takes it **without lowering the flashlight**. She doesn't leave a position mid-job, so he hears every word. Her half only: she tells Kate she's with the locksmith. Then: "The house." / "Not the rest." / a pause long enough for him to set a pin / **"I swear."** She ends the call the way she'd end a briefing. One clause of Ray interior: she put her house into this and swore the rest to her daughter, and he has just offered her the house back.
10. He seats the new cylinder and works the new key twice. He gives her **every new key**, front and back, counting them into her bare palm; she closes her hand. Then he takes out his own ring, finds the orange tag, and slides the old key into her new lock in front of her. It goes in and turns nothing. He works it off the cylinder and puts it back on the ring, dead.
11. Last line, Ray, standing, the bag in his hand: **"Committee's Thursday at five. After that, I'll see you March first."**

**Subtext:** He is making the one offer that would take her out of his town, and some part of him is glad when she refuses it. He won't look at that part. She is refusing the only safe exit to keep a position she has held for one day, and she does it while letting him feel her breathing on his hand. Neither of them mentions the flashlight.

**Texture budget:** (1) **The old pins into his palm, and into his shirt pocket.** It shows a man who keeps things. (2) **The 1996 key in the new cylinder, turning nothing.** It's the cost of his own work, done in front of her. The rekey is two people at a lock. Use no locksmith vocabulary beyond "cylinder," "plug" and "pins." No licensing line.

**Point of view:** Ray.

**Ends on:** **Dialogue.** "Committee's Thursday at five. After that, I'll see you March first."

**Word target:** 1,050. Do not compress steps 2, 7 or 9. The breath, the bank's number and the oath are the three things this scene exists for.

### Scene 3

**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, the back door and the pit behind lane seven. Wednesday night, ten o'clock, hard cold and clear. Sensory anchor: one caged bulb on over the seven-and-eight pair, and the warmth that comes off the motors when he runs her.

**Characters:** Ray (alone). Ellen is present only in what she has left.

**Want:** Ray wants seven running before Thursday's league, because he has never left a machine down on a league night except Tuesday's, and he wants to be in her building when she is. The reader learns the second want when he stops on the catwalk to listen for her voice, and the building is silent. In the way: she has arranged it so neither of them has to ask or thank. She sent a time, left the door unlocked, and stayed away.

**Surface action:**
1. Her text, read once, in her full stops: "Lane seven. Ten o'clock. The back door is open." No question mark anywhere in it.
2. He drives up Kettle Road. The sign on its pole reads with the K dark, and he dates it the way he dates everything: **the K went out the winter Danny got his license**, and nobody fixed it, because Arlene said the sign still said what it meant. ("dead K" once.) Her car isn't in the lot.
3. The back door is unlocked: his cylinder from this morning, with her key-turn on it. Inside, the pit. The spare clutch has already come down off its nail. It lies on the workbench under the caged bulb, with his old tag still on it and a strip of her tape beside the tag, LANE 7 in black marker. A flashlight lies on the bench aimed at seven's drive, switched on. Under it is an envelope, **R. Gagnon**, in the same marker.
4. He works. Give one physical pleasure room for two or three sentences: the first quarter-turn of the seized bolt giving, which he feels in his back teeth. He swaps the clutch and runs her from the pit, and she sets clean, the sweep coming down and lifting without the knock.
5. Interior, longer than anything said in the chapter so far: the building at night with nobody in it, the lanes seen backward and bright through the gap under the masking, and him listening for a voice that carries to lane ten without being raised, and not hearing it. One clause, no more, that he has been in this building alone a hundred nights and has never once listened for anybody.
6. He leaves the envelope on the bench, unopened. He sets the old clutch on top of it so it won't blow off.
7. He goes out the back. She said the door was open. It's a storeroom latch, so it locks itself on closing, and he pulls it shut behind him and hears the lock he pinned that morning take, and he is on the outside of it.

**Subtext:** She paid him so she wouldn't owe him, and he refused the pay so she would. Neither of them said a word to the other all day, and the whole conversation is on a workbench.

**Texture budget:** (1) **The clutch swap**, one sentence of hands, plus the bolt giving. It shows the only work in town that comes from him. (Nothing else technical.)

**Point of view:** Ray.

**Ends on:** **Consequence.** The latch he cut that morning catches behind him, and he is locked out of her building by his own work. Last beat: the sound of it taking, and his hand still on the cold handle.

**Word target:** 450. Deliberately short; it is a single action and the chapter total is fixed.

### Scene 4

**Setting:** Gagnon & Son Hardware. Thursday, December 3, from about a quarter to five to five o'clock. It has been dark outside since a little after four, and the store lights are on. Sensory anchor: the clock over the key-cutting machine at the end of the counter, its minute hand moving in small jumps.

**Characters:** Ray, Ellen, Normie and the Bench, Danny (silent).

**Want:** Ray wants the phone to ring, or the door, before five. He'd tell himself he wants the yes. What he wants is for her to come. The reader learns it when he squares the credit union form under his hand for the third time and looks at the door, not the phone. In the way: he will not ask twice, and she does not ask at all. Ellen wants the offer to die in front of him, so neither of them can say later that she didn't decide. She also wants to give him the run of her machines without asking him for it. The reader learns this when she sets the key down and looks at the clock, not at him.

**Surface action:**
1. Ray at the counter. The credit union's form lies under his hand with **Danny's signature on it**, and beside it a slip with the loan officer's number. Danny is at the far register and says nothing about it. One clause covers him.
2. Ray's fear register: he picks something up and fixes it, a loose guard screw on the key cutter. He talks to the screw. The Bench is on their stools. One clause: Normie bowled with Lucien in 'seventy-four and knows exactly what the store nearly cost, and he hasn't said a word all afternoon.
3. **Two minutes to five.** The bell on the door. Ellen. **The Bench goes quiet.** Write the quiet: the coffee pot ticking, someone setting a cup down with care.
4. She comes to the counter. She takes a glove off to get it out of her vest pocket (Ray watches the hand, as in S1). She puts **one new key** on his counter: no tag, no ring, no word. It lies between his hand and the signed form.
5. **The minute, in real time.** Do not summarize it. Both of them look up at the clock over the key cutter. Ray's long interior runs while the hand jumps. The key is the most anyone has given him without being asked since Linda, and it is a key to the building he is contracted to buy. The form is under his hand. He could ask her now, and he swore after 'seventy-four that he wouldn't ask the store twice, so he won't. He watches her watch the clock, and her face is perfectly calm. *Show the gap:* he can hear from her breathing that it isn't. Keep it slow. At least 250 words from the key touching the counter to the minute hand reaching twelve.
6. **Five o'clock.** She turns and goes without a word. The bell on the door rings behind her.
7. Normie: **"That's the bell."** Cut. Nothing after it: no reaction beat, no hand reaching for the key.

**Subtext:** She has refused him and given him a key in the same gesture. He has kept his word to his father and lost the building he wanted in the same minute. The town has watched both. Neither of them said one word.

**Texture budget:** (1) **The credit union form with Danny's signature**, one look. It shows what the store would have carried. (No locksmith or machine detail beyond the screw.)

**Point of view:** Ray.

**Ends on:** **Cut.** At five the bell on the door rings as she goes. Normie: "That's the bell." End of chapter and of Act 1. The chapter promise is left hanging: *What will he do with a key to the building he's contracted to buy?*

**Word target:** 650. The minute in step 5 is the chapter's high point. The sentences get longer and slower there.
