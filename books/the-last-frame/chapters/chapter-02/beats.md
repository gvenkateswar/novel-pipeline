# Chapter 02: Right of First Refusal (Beat Sheet)

**POV:** Ray Gagnon, close third, past tense.
**Dates:** Wednesday, December 2 (6:00 a.m.) to Thursday, December 3 (6:10 a.m.)
**Target:** 3,200 words (the scene targets below add up to 3,250)
**Chapter promise (from the outline):** Why is Ray helping the woman whose failure gets him what he wants?
**Money state:** NOI to date about −$300. Cash $41,000 by Dec 3 after restocking. Lane 7 is down two days (Tuesday night and Wednesday) and runs again for Thursday league.

## Flags (read before drafting)

1. **Three doors, nine keys.** In Ch 01 Danny booked "Rekey, three exterior, eight a.m. tomorrow. Three keys a door," with a possible old Corbin cylinder on the bulkhead. The outline says "her lock" and "every new key." Follow the Ch 01 log: Ray does the front door, the back door, and the bulkhead, and hands her nine keys. On the bulkhead he swaps in the new cylinder Danny brought because the Corbin can't be repinned. This adds detail and doesn't conflict with anything.
2. **Who sees the tag.** The outline puts the broken part "on her desk in the morning" with a tag in her strip format. Ray can't see her find it from his POV, so he writes the tag and leaves the part at night (Scene 5), and Scene 6 delivers her reaction by text. Ray writes the tag himself, imitating her five-column format. That imitation is the pleasure the outline wants.
3. **The pool sheet has already been revealed.** Ch 01 showed it through Denise's phone photo, so don't explain it again. In Ch 02 the sheet is simply on Ray's wall behind the register. He sees the empty March 1 square, and Normie offers him the pen. No one explains what the square means.
4. **(Revised by orchestrator.)** The fuel-oil calendar was cut from Ch 01's final text. Don't use it. In Scene 5, Ray's "noticing Ellen" detail is the rooster card file: the M slot is empty, because she kept Arlene's MECHANIC / R.G. card.
5. **Ray never enters uninvited.** At 8 a.m. he waits at the door until she opens it, even though the job is booked. At 9 p.m. she unlocks the back door and lets him in, gives him no key, and tells him it locks behind him when he pulls it shut. She leaves. He never touches her new keys after handing them over.
6. **Kate's call is not a coincidence.** Kate calls her mother every morning when she comes off night shift in Manchester, around 8:15 from the hospital garage, and Ellen says so. Ellen tells Kate about the offer during the call. Ray hears only Ellen's half.
7. **ATC cap is 3 (not a set piece).** Use exactly these, all in Ellen's dialogue as Ray hears it: **"Stand by"** (to Ray when Kate calls, Scene 3), **"Unable"** (her first answer to the offer, Scene 3, followed at once by plain English), and **"readback"** (Ray's word for her habit in Scene 2; Ellen never says the word). Nothing else from the approved vocabulary. Her text messages are not in phraseology. Her strip format on the tag is labeling, not phraseology.
8. **Phrase caps for this chapter.** Key-ring jingle once (Scene 3, during the offer). "Eight days" twice at most (Scene 1 interior, plus one more if needed). Dead K / ETTLE LANES once at most (Scene 5, optional). "The voice" never (that is Ch 01, 07, and 09 only). "The wood stays on the lane" never.
9. **No outline-bible conflicts found.** The outline's "SBA approval letter" wording was already fixed in the outline itself: it is "the bank's commitment letter on an SBA loan." Use that phrase.

### Scene 1
**Setting:** Gagnon & Son Hardware, 6:00 a.m. Wednesday. It is dark outside and the plow drivers' radios crackle from trucks idling at the curb. The store smells of wet boots, cut brass, and burnt coffee. The key cutter whines. Rock salt is stacked on a pallet by the door.
**Characters present:** Ray, Danny, the Bench (three retired regulars by the coffee pot; name only Normie)
**Surface action:** Ray cuts a key for a plow driver (unnamed walk-on) and deburrs it on the wheel. The plow driver pays in exact change and leaves. Danny comes in with his tablet under his arm and sets it on the counter. The 8 a.m. job reads KETTLE LANES / REKEY / ALL EXTERIOR. Danny has already pulled pins and set aside a new cylinder for the bulkhead. Ray checks the box anyway, and Danny lets him. Behind the register is the pool sheet, KETTLE LANES. NEW OWNER. HOW LONG?, full of initials except the last square, MARCH 1. Normie holds out the five-dollar pen toward the sheet: "Last one, Ray." Ray takes the pen, uses it to write a sales slip for the plow driver's account, and puts it back in the cup.
**Interior passage (required, the eight days):** The bank's commitment letter is folded in thirds in his shirt pocket. In two to four plain sentences: the letter's date is eight days after Arlene shook Ellen's hand. He knows the date the way he knows Linda's average. He has carried the letter every day since anyway, like a part for a machine that's been sold. Use Ray's idiom of tolerances and wear. No self-pity.
**Subtext:** Ray wants the alley and won't profit from betting on it. Danny already does everything Ray "checks," and both of them know it.
**Tension source:** Situational and internal. The rekey on the tablet is a job he is booked to do against himself.
**Ends on:** Ray puts the pen back in the cup on the counter, uncapped end down, and goes to the back for his pinning kit. (Action.)
**Word count target:** 500

### Scene 2
**Setting:** The same store at 7:30 a.m. Daylight is gray at the front windows. The door bell rings. Ellen's boots leave salt on the worn wooden floor path.
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen, Danny, the Bench (Normie plus two)
**Surface action:**
- Ellen comes in with a parts list printed from a candlepin forum (the forum header is still on it, and three lines are highlighted). She puts two bags of rock salt on the counter for Danny to ring up. That's the restocking, and one price at most appears on the page.
- She asks Ray to listen to something and doesn't explain. She holds the phone flat on the counter and plays the lane 7 video.
- Ray listens once with his eyes closed. The Bench calls it like a broadcast ("And he's closing the eyes, folks").
- The Bench's rival diagnoses: Normie says it's the motor, the second man says belt, and the third says it's "the new management."
- Ray names it from the sound: "That's not the table. That's the clutch letting go a half-second late." The burned smell she reported last night was the clutch facing, and the motor quit protecting itself.
- He glances at her forum list: half of those parts haven't been made since the 1970s. Clutches get scavenged from alleys that closed.
- He gives instructions. Leave the PINSETTER 7 breaker off. Nobody reaches into the pit for a pin. Don't let anyone "try it once." Ellen reads all three back in order.
- Then he tells her where a spare clutch is: on a nail in her own pit, third from the left, labeled in his capitals, salvaged from a house that shut down years ago. He pauses for the readback. It doesn't come. She has already written it on an index card, and she turns the card so he can read it.
- Neither of them raises who will install it. The Bench notices this out loud.
**Interior passage (required, something specific he notices about Ellen):** While the video plays she keeps her thumb off the screen and holds the phone dead still, the way a person holds evidence. She didn't narrate the clip or apologize for it, and she doesn't fill the silence while he listens. Linda used to talk over machines. Ellen lets them talk. Keep it to two to four sentences and don't compare the two women any further than that.
**Subtext:** She is asking for expertise without asking for help, and he is giving it without offering. Each is careful not to owe the other.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and comic. The Bench turns a private standoff into a public broadcast.
**Ends on:** Danny lifts the store's job box onto the counter and says, "Eight o'clock. You want me to take it?" Ray picks up the pinning kit and the bulkhead cylinder and goes out to his truck ahead of her. (Action.)
**Word count target:** 700. Set piece: protect the eyes-closed diagnosis and the readback that doesn't come.

### Scene 3
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes at 8:00 a.m. Cold comes off the glass. The front door is shut, and through it Ray can see down the dark building to the trophy case. Order of work: back door, then the bulkhead (cylinder swap), then the front door last. Ray kneels on the outer mat at the front door with the plug and follower out and the pins laid in a tray.
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen (Kate on the phone, voice never heard)
**Surface action:**
- Ray knocks at 8:00 by his watch and waits until Ellen opens the door from inside.
- The rekeying shows in specific nouns: plug, follower, pin tray, the new keys he cut at the store. He rekeys the back door and the bulkhead first, out of view in a few lines.
- At the front door he finishes the repin and tests his old key, Arlene's 1996 key with the orange tag R.G. — 1996. It goes in and doesn't turn. He works it out of the lock and leaves it on the ring.
- Still kneeling, he makes the offer. He'll buy her out at exactly what she paid: down payment back, the note assumed, closing costs covered. She loses nothing and walks away clean. He lays it out as plainly as a work order, important part last.
- Her phone rings. "Stand by," she says to him. She takes the call standing up and says it's Kate, off shift, who calls every morning from the garage.
- Ray hears only Ellen's half: "Kate. I'm aware." / "Yes. Exactly what I paid." / "Scale of one to ten, I'm a three." / "I heard you." She hangs up without arguing.
- Her answer to Ray is "Unable," and then plain English. Retiring at fifty-six was federal law, not her choice. This alley is the first thing she has chosen in two years. She says it in short declaratives, without contractions, slowly. That isn't anger. It's the most care she can give a sentence.
- Ray stands. He counts nine keys into her palm, three per door, each door's set on its own split ring, and tells her which is which. He puts his own ring away. **The one key-ring jingle of the chapter** happens here, as he weighs the ring before he pockets it.
**Interior passage (required, what the alley holds):** Through the door glass, with the plug in his hand, he can see the trophy case and the center shelf. In two to four sentences: this is the last room where everything is where Linda left it, the 187 card, locker 14, the settees she complained about. He meant to keep it that way, the way you keep a tool you can't bring yourself to sharpen. Don't name grief and don't flash back.
**Subtext:** Ray's offer is fair, and part of him knows it is also a way to freeze the room. Ellen hears that her daughter agrees with him. Both of them tell the truth for the first time.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and situational. A fair offer that a sensible person should take, refused for a reason he can't argue with.
**Ends on:** Ray, the keys handed over and his kit closed: "Then I'll see you March first." (A line of dialogue, flat and sincere. Not a quip. This is the Act 1 break.)
**Word count target:** 850. This is the longest scene and holds the act break. Don't trim the refusal.

### Scene 4
**Setting:** Gagnon & Son, 5:40 p.m. Wednesday, after close. The till is open, the lights over the aisles are off, and the roof rakes hang overhead in the dark. Ray sits on the stool behind the counter. This is not Linda's chair; Linda's chair stays empty beside it, and it can appear once without comment.
**Characters present:** Ray, Danny
**Surface action:** Danny counts the drawer. Ray has his phone out and the Thursday league schedule on the counter, and lane 7 has a match on it. Danny asks without looking up whether he's going to fix it. Ray answers with a "Fella I knew…" story about something else: a fella in Tilton who kept his neighbor's furnace running all one winter because his own pipes were on the same wall. Danny says that isn't an answer, then reads it as one. Ray types the text and deletes it twice. The version he sends: Thursday league needs lane 7. He can do it tonight if she'll let him in. Her reply comes in under a minute: a time and a door. "2100. Back door. I'll unlock it."
**Subtext:** Ray can't explain to himself why he's helping, and Danny can see it without being told. Ray writes the text so that it's about the league.
**Tension source:** Internal. He won't enter uninvited, so he has to ask.
**Ends on:** Danny pushes the drawer shut with his hip and says, "Take the good flashlight. Not yours." (A quip ending, the first of at most two in the chapter.)
**Word count target:** 350

### Scene 5
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, 9:00 p.m. Wednesday. The back door, then the dark lanes with only the counter lights on, then the pit behind lane 7: low and dim, with the workbench, the coffee can of bolts, and spare parts on nails labeled in his own capitals. The building is cold and the baseboard ticks as it cools. The burnt smell is still faint at lane 7.
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen (briefly, at the door)
**Surface action:**
- Ellen opens the back door from inside at 9:00. In her car keys and coat, she says the counter lights are on, the breaker is off, and the door locks when he pulls it shut. She doesn't give him a key and he doesn't ask for one. She leaves, and he hears her engine start.
- He walks through to the pit. On the way past the counter he flips open Arlene's rooster card file out of habit. Under M there is nothing. She kept his card.
- He passes the trophy case and touches the glass with one knuckle (his tic, once).
- In the pit: the clutch from the nail, the old one off, the new one on. He resets the breaker himself and runs lane 7 empty three times, timing it on his watch: seven seconds a cycle.
- He takes one of her index cards from the counter stack and a Sharpie and copies her five-column strip format off the lane-light switches, block capitals: 07 / 2340 / MX / 1 / CLUTCH — SLIPPING. REPLACED. He ties the card to the burnt clutch with a twist tie and sets both squarely on her office desk.
**Interior passage (required, the last unchanged room, as he sees her changes):** On the switches her tape is over Arlene's. On the pegboard is the LOCKSMITH / 0800 card she never took down. Under M in the card file is an empty slot where his card used to be. In two to four sentences: she changes things only when she has a reason, and she kept the one card that says she needs him. He can't decide whether that makes it better or worse.
**Subtext:** He is alone in the room he wanted to own, doing the work of keeping it alive for someone else, and he's careful to leave it exactly as she arranged it.
**Tension source:** Internal and situational. It is her building at night, with her permission, and he is not the owner.
**Ends on:** Ray pulls the back door shut, tries the handle twice, and walks to the truck. In his pocket Arlene's key opens nothing. (Image and action. Optionally, the sign over the lot can be the one dead-K mention of the chapter.)
**Word count target:** 550

### Scene 6
**Setting:** Gagnon & Son, 6:05 a.m. Thursday. It's dark, a plow truck idles at the curb, and the Bench is already on its stools by the coffee pot.
**Characters present:** Ray, Danny, the Bench
**Surface action:**
- Ray's phone buzzes at the register. It's a photo from Ellen: the tag on the clutch, with one line added under his in her block capitals: REMARKS: INVOICE TO E. DALY.
- He types back: "I didn't fix it for you. Thursday league's got a match." He sends it.
- Normie, reading over the counter, asks whether that's her. Danny, restocking, asks whether he's going to bill her. Ray answers neither of them.
- He looks up at the pool sheet behind the register, at the empty March 1 square. The five-dollar pen is in the cup. He doesn't take it.
**Subtext:** The chapter promise, staged without anyone saying it: he is helping the woman whose failure gets him the building, and he has no answer for why, not even for himself.
**Tension source:** Internal, with the town watching.
**Ends on:** Ray turns from the sheet, takes a key blank from the board, and starts the cutter for the plow driver waiting at the counter. (Action. Don't end on a reflection or an aphorism.)
**Word count target:** 300

## Checks

- **Scene endings:** 1 action / 2 action / 3 dialogue (sincere, not a quip) / 4 quip / 5 image and action / 6 action. That is one quip out of six, well under half. The Scene 6 text line is a quip mid-scene but doesn't end the scene.
- **Interior passages:** four (S1 the eight days, S2 noticing Ellen, S3 Linda and the last room, S5 her changes to the room). The minimum is three.
- **Ray's comic engines:** diagnosis by sound (S2), the Bench (S2, S1, S6), "Fella I knew…" (S4).
- **Ellen's ATC phrases:** stand by, unable, plus the readback habit (3 of 3).
- **Distinct voices:** Danny is quick and transactional, talking in inventory and invoices. The Bench is broadcast patter. Ellen speaks in literal short declaratives and slows down with no contractions in S3. Ray says few words, the important ones last.
- **Clean:** no swearing. Check every pinsetter and ball sentence for accidental double meanings. Ellen is gone before Ray works at night.
- **Every scene advances plot or deepens character.** S4 is the thinnest scene, but it carries the text by which he asks permission (a keys beat from the Revision log) and the "Fella I knew" engine, so keep it.
- **No recap** of Ch 01 at the top. Open on the key cutter.
