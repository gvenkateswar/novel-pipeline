# Epilogue: Ice-Out

There was no gap in the noise anywhere down the house, and Ellen stood in it. Over the door the new K laid its red on the glass block.

Hollis came for his shoes. "Called ice-out yesterday." She handed them across.

"You look rested, honey," Denise said from the register.

"I slept till ten past five."

Denise's bowling bag stood zipped by the vestibule door, where she never left it.

Kate came in from the lot in her coat with her car keys still in her hand. As Ellen came out from behind the counter, Ray moved up into her place. Kate watched him do it, looked at him once, and went past him into the office ahead of her mother.

Ellen left the door open on the lanes. Kate hitched herself up onto the steel desk in her coat with the keys in her lap, and the noise of the house came in after them and filled the room to the shelves. Ellen stayed on her feet.

"April," Kate said. "In person."

Ellen had written this night up once already, in ink, and every line of it had been true, and none of it had gone to Kate. She began it where a write-up begins, with the time.

"First. I was on a midnight shift. At two in the morning, two airplanes came too close to each other, and they were both mine."

"Was anybody hurt?" Kate said.

"Nobody."

It was the top line on every form Ellen had ever filled out, and Kate had gone straight to it.

"Second," Ellen said. "I told one of them to turn, and he turned. They played me the tape before I went home, and you couldn't hear a thing in it. I drove home at the speed limit the whole way. I turned the engine off in the driveway, and then I didn't do the next thing."

The next thing was the door handle, and she had known exactly where it was.

Kate turned the keys over and over. "How long were you out there?"

"An hour and four minutes."

"Of course you know," Kate said.

Ellen knew because the clock on the dash had been in front of her the whole time. She had read it at every change of the minute, the way she read any clock she could see, and it was the only thing she did in that car on purpose.

"Your light came on at twenty to seven," Ellen said. "You came out the front door with your coat open and walked past the car to the bottom of the drive for the bus, and when you heard it on the hill you turned around and came all the way back up to knock on the glass by my head." She still had the sound of it, two knocks, quick and light, from the side of a small fist. "I said I was fine. I said it the way I'd said everything to the airplanes."

"Were you?" Kate said.

"No."

Through the open door she could see Ray at the register with a pair of rental shoes in his hand, and he had seen her face. He held the shoes still by the heels and stayed where he was, and then he handed them across the counter to whoever was waiting.

"Why didn't you come in?" Kate said.

"Because I'd have sounded fine, and you'd have believed me."

Kate closed her hand on the keys. "I learned the voice anyway."

\* \* \*

Kate brought a house ball through on a straight arm and let go late and high, and it came down on the maple with a crack, hopped the board, and rolled off down six. Ellen watched from the end of five's settee.

Ray came down, lifted the ball off the return between five and six, and walked it to her. "Nice arc. Wrong lane."

"She told me you'd say that," Kate said.

He put it in her hands and let go at once, and went back up.

Ellen followed him up. Clipped by the register was Normie's new sheet, with no title across the top and every square empty but one: R. Gagnon, in pencil, in the printing she knew from Ray's invoices, the first square he had ever bought. She reached for a pencil.

"You don't get to bet on your own flight," Denise said, passing for her bag.

Ellen walked her to the vestibule.

"Halsey Falls in the morning," Denise said. "Spring doubles with Gil Arsenault. He handed me the flyer with the cup in my other hand. I kept this one."

"I'm nervous," Denise said, low. "Don't you tell a soul."

She went out.

Back beside Ray, Ellen watched Kate throw again. Under the counter his hand found hers and held it, and neither of them looked down. The pins went, and she did not know how many.

Kate came up to the counter across from them. "I'm thinking about taking days. I haven't decided. Scale of one to ten?"

Ellen held on to Ray's hand under the counter.

"Tell me what you'd miss."
