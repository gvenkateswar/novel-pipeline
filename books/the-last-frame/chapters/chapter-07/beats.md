# Chapter 07 — Loss of Separation (Dark Moment): Beat Sheet

**POV:** Ellen (close third, past tense)
**Dates:** Tuesday, February 2, 5:45 p.m. to about 11:15 p.m.
**Target:** 3,200 words (scene targets sum to 3,200)
**Chapter promise to set up:** Can she save the alley alone, and does she still want to?

## Pre-flight flags (read before drafting)

1. **The outline's opening is Ray's image, but this is Ellen's chapter.** "Ray is sitting in his truck in the Kettle Lanes lot with the engine running" has to come through Ellen's eyes. She sees the exhaust through the glass-block band at 5:45 and goes out to it. The cashier's check on the dashboard is the first thing she reads when she reaches the driver's window. Don't open in the truck.
2. **"Purpose of visit" (Ch 06's last line) gets answered by the offer, never in words.** Nobody mentions Hollis's question, and Ray doesn't explain where he's been since Sunday. The reader works out that the $55,000 is his answer. Don't open with a recap of the bob-house (CLAUDE.md, rule 21).
3. **Where the $55,000 comes from (addition, consistent with the bible).** It's the money Ray set aside for his own purchase: the down payment his SBA lender requires. He says so. That's the plainest way to show "he wants her and still wants the alley and can't separate the two": if the alley is sold to him in March, he needs this money to buy it, and if she takes it, the sale never happens. He doesn't spell out the irony and neither does the narration (rule 16).
4. **How the meeting's mechanics work, so the vote isn't luck (rules 14 and 22).**
   - **The lineage rate is the house's price.** The league doesn't vote on it. Verna rules that from the chair, and that's part of why the room gets angry: they can't vote it down, so they can only leave.
   - **Moving the championship into the Open does need the league's consent.** The trophy and the title belong to the league. Denise moves it, because Ellen asks her to in S2 (that ask is set up on the page). Hollis seconds; he's already backed the derby fallback in Ch 06. The captains vote by show of hands: **carried, 11 to 8, the coach abstaining.** Ray's abstention is "I won't stand up against it" in action, and it's earned from Ch 06. Verna records it as the first abstention by the coach since 1996.
   - The vote passing is **why** two teams walk out. The rate is the grievance and the vote is the last straw.
5. **Which teams quit (rule 9: hurt, not wicked).** **Taylor's Marina** (Tuesday) and the **Highway Department** (Thursday). The Highway Department is earned: the Ch 04 sit-out, when they wouldn't pay for marathon strings, and "22 years of Thursdays." Thursday captains are at a Tuesday meeting because the league meets as one body. Their anchor is **"the 112"** (bible walk-on, unnamed; Ray's Ch 08 side-match opponent). Plant him here: he's the one who asks Ray to come with them.
6. **The "112" collision.** The outline's optional office detail is "Denise's earflap hat on the counter hook with '112' on masking tape." The bible gives the defectors' anchor as "the 112 average." If both appear in one chapter, the reader will connect the hat to the anchor. **Recommendation:** use the hat with no number, or skip it. Keep "112" for the anchor only.
7. **The pool sheet has to be at the alley.** In Ch 06 it hangs behind Ray's register. The outline's "Feb 2 — they split" square needs it on Ellen's counter. **Fix (rule 14):** Denise has it under the counter because Ray took it off his wall Monday and handed it to her. Denise says this in S5, in one line, as her one speech of the chapter. Nobody interprets it.
8. **Who knows they split?** Two early bowlers pulled into the lot at about 6:10 and saw Ellen at Ray's window and saw her walk away. That's enough for the town. Whoever writes "FEB 2 — THEY SPLIT" stays unnamed, and the handwriting isn't Normie's.
9. **Rule 21: don't re-stage the Open plan.** The reader has heard it twice already. On the page: Ellen's lineage line, word for word ($12.50; the house share of the prize fund cut from $5 to $2), and **one sentence of narration** for the Open. Everything else stays on the room's reaction. Verna cuts her off at exactly three minutes, mid-sentence. That's both a rule-20 loss and the reason the plan can't be recited.
10. **Money on the page.** This chapter goes over the bible's "two or three prices" guideline because Ray is required to say his numbers. Keep all of them to three places:
    - **S1:** Ray's figures in one run: fifty-five thousand; forty-eight to the note; "twenty-three thousand nine hundred fifty"; seven to the bank; "about twenty-six." Ellen's answer: "Fifty dollars." (Her $24,000 projection against his $23,950 hurdle. That margin is the joke and the problem. See S1.)
    - **S3:** twelve-fifty and two dollars.
    - **S5:** her tape, one line: income twenty-two, cash seventeen.
    - Keep $15,150 and $17,600 off the page.
11. **Ray waits in the lot (small deviation, flag only).** The outline says "He nods, and goes home." The beat sheet has him nod and go out the back. He's in his truck when her car fails to start on the first try (the Ch 02 detail), and he waits, as he did in Ch 01. He does go home. This doesn't change the plot. It gives the chapter a suspended ending instead of a still image.
12. **No kiss in this chapter (Clean rules).** Kisses 1 and 2 are spent, and Kiss 3 is Ch 10. Rule 22 says no kiss gets forgotten: Kiss 2 is acknowledged once in S1, in Ellen's interior, as a fact (the cellar stairs, six days ago). Not in dialogue.
13. **Ellen never raises her voice at work (bible).** Rule 22's raised voice belongs to the room: the Highway Department captain. Ellen's anger runs slow and loses its contractions. Her loss of composure is the reverse. In S1 she's perfectly composed while ending it, and that's what she owns in S5.

### Scene 1 — THE OFFER
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes lot, 5:45 to about 6:05 p.m. Full dark. About 14°F. The plowed snowbank on the northwest side is higher than the truck's hood. Ray's pickup sits nose-out under the lot light with the engine running, and its exhaust shows white. Behind Ellen the sign at the road buzzes. On the truck's dashboard: a cashier's check from Granite Lakes Savings, and a folded strip of adding-machine tape from the store.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Ray
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late.** Ellen at the counter sees the exhaust through the glass-block band and counts it as a vehicle, not a person: one truck, running eleven minutes. She goes out without her parka (vest only). She doesn't get in. She stands at his window, because she stands for bad news.
- He gets out rather than talk to her through glass. He hands her the check. Payee: Kettle Lanes. Amount: fifty-five thousand. She doesn't take it. He holds it out a while, then sets it on the hood.
- **Ray's numbers, his longest speech in the book, delivered in his order with the important words last.** Forty-eight pays the note down. Debt payments drop, and Judy's bar drops with them, to twenty-three thousand nine hundred fifty. That's under her twenty-four without touching a league rate. Seven to the bank lifts her February twenty-eighth to about twenty-six. "Both numbers." Then, last: "Championship stays the way it was."
- **Comic middle (rule 15): Danny's annotations.** The tape is in two hands. Ray's figures are in pencil. Danny's notes run down the margin in ballpoint, block letters: IT'S EQUITY NOT A GIFT. SHE'D OWN LESS — SAY THAT PART. Ray, reluctantly: "Danny says I have to call it equity. Says if I call it anything else you'll throw it at me." Ellen: "Danny's right." Ray: "He usually is. I don't tell him."
- **Ellen's one dry precision (her idiom, and it costs her nothing yet).** "Fifty dollars." Ray: "Fifty's over." Ellen: "Fifty dollars is inside the error on my tape." It's funny and it's true, and it isn't her reason.
- **The honest admission (outline).** She asks why. He jingles his keys out of his coat pocket and closes his fist on them (rule 17: the chapter's one key ring, mid-scene). He says it plainly: it's his down payment, the money he'd need to buy the place off her in March. He wants her, and he wants the alley, and he's been trying since Sunday to pull those two apart and can't. That's as far as he goes.
- **She refuses, in two reasons, plain.** If she takes it, she'll never know whether she could carry it. And he'll never know whether she wanted him or his check. (She can say "your money." No therapy language.)
- **He's just as honest.** Then he's keeping the right of first refusal. If she goes under, somebody keeps the lights on, and he'd rather it was him than a developer from Concord.
- **"Then we want the same building."** She says it. Then she ends it, in short declaratives, in order, the way she'd hand off a position: they should stop; effective now; she'll see him at six-thirty because he coaches. He doesn't argue. He picks the check up off the hood.

**Interior passage 1 (Ellen, 2–4 sentences), before she answers:** Why she bought it. This is the first thing she chose after a statute chose for her, and it's a building full of people who need putting in order again. If she takes the check, the alley will be something that happened to her, the same way fifty-six happened to her. Then one fact, unadorned: six days ago she was on her cellar stairs with this man, and she has not once since then thought about the boiler first.

**Subtext:** The offer is generous, real, and correct on paper, and accepting it would make him a rescuer. Refusing it is the most Ellen thing she could do, for the right reason, and the method she uses (a briefing) is the wrong one.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Two honest people and one check. Situational: the meeting is in 25 minutes, and two cars pull into the lot (the witnesses, flag 8).
**Ellen loses ground (rule 20):** She turns down the one plan that closes both gaps, and she knows the numbers well enough to know exactly what she's turning down.
**Ends on:** Mid-conversation, cut away. A second set of headlights swings into the lot and lights both of them. Ray, already turning to his truck: "I'll be in at six-thirty. I've got a team." Ellen: "I have the schedule." Cut on her walking, not on him. No last look.
**Word count target:** 750

### Scene 2 — TWENTY MINUTES (THE COST BEFORE THE PODIUM)
**Setting:** The office, 6:07 to 6:27 p.m. Door shut. The steel desk, the printing adding machine, the 2019 fuel-oil calendar, the green ledgers on their two shelves. A space heater, off, because the popcorn machine is on out front for the meeting crowd. Through the door: boots on the vestibule mat, settees being dragged into rows, the PA's hum when someone bumps the gooseneck. On the coat hook: Denise's earflap hat (no number; flag 6).
**Characters present:** Ellen; Denise (from about 6:22, at the door)
**Surface action:**
- **She stands (tic).** She doesn't sit. She checks her watch against the atomic-set time and finds them equal, which doesn't help. Her hands are fine. She looks at them to make sure.
- **The 2 a.m. memory, short and specific (outline; bible write-around).** The end of a rattler, the midnight. Two arrivals for Logan, on the downwind, fifteen miles west. She descended one into the other's altitude. The conflict alert went off. She turned them both and gave the traffic. The closest point was inside the minimum. Her voice on the frequency never changed. Every pilot said thank you. **"She filed the report herself. Two days off the boards. A week of retraining."** (Use the bible's three sentences.) What broke, in one physical fact: afterward, in the employee lot in Merrimack, she couldn't get her key into the ignition for four minutes, and she timed it.
- **"The voice."** Name it once in this scene (allowed in Ch 07), in narration: that night she learned the voice could carry anything, and nobody listening would know.
- **She knows she's doing it again.** No epiphany. Just the matching fact: twenty minutes ago she ended it at a truck window in the voice, and the voice was perfect.
- **She does the work anyway (action, not feeling).** She tears the stub off the adding machine and runs one scenario she hasn't let herself run: if two teams walk. She doesn't read the total. She folds it into her vest pocket. (S5 pays this off; she was ready for the number and didn't look.)
- **Comic middle (rule 15): Denise at the door, one speech.** Verna has arrived with the gavel, the binder, and a seat cushion. She has asked for the meeting to start at 6:29 "because six-thirty is late," and someone has already moved to rename the snack bar, out of order, in the vestibule. Denise rambles and runs her sentences together (rule 13). No "honey."
- **Ellen asks.** Before Denise goes: will she move the Open motion when the floor opens? (Rule 14 setup for the vote. Denise's arc: being asked.) Denise looks at her a moment longer than the question needs. "I'll move it. I'm not saying I like it. I'm saying I'll move it." Hedged, Denise-style. No thanks exchanged.

**Interior passage 2 (Ellen, 3–4 sentences):** What she's afraid of: that her calm hides the failure until it's too late to fix. On that night the aircraft were safe and she wasn't, and nobody on the frequency could tell. Tonight eighty people are about to hear the same voice say twelve-fifty.

**Subtext:** She's grieving, she won't let herself do it, and she knows exactly what she's doing. That's the worst part.
**Tension source:** Internal. The clock (Verna starts early).
**Ends on:** Mid-motion. Through the door, the Grange gavel comes down once, at 6:29, before Ellen has the door open. She's already walking. Cut.
**Word count target:** 500

### Scene 3 — VERNA (COMEDY FIRST)
**Setting:** The lanes, 6:29 to about 6:58 p.m. All ten lanes dark at the far end, and the house lights up. The burnt-orange settees are pulled into rows facing the counter. About eighty people in parkas and league shirts, both divisions. Boots dripping on the approach mats. The popcorn machine running. Verna Tibbetts, 88, in her lavender parka zipped to the chin, sits at a card table at the foot of lanes 5 and 6 with the 1979-onward binder, the Ambrose Grange gavel, and a wristwatch laid flat on the table. Ellen stands at the counter (her post; it's her building). Ray sits with Gagnon & Son on the second settee, Hollis beside him. Denise is at the end of the counter.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Verna, Ray, Hollis, Denise, the 112 (Taylor's Marina anchor), the Highway Department captain, the Rotary man (optional, one line), the room
**Surface action:**
- **Old business: the pending motion of thanks (pays off Ch 04).** Verna takes up the motion of thanks to the house for the night of the storm, pending since January, and reads its full text. It carries by voice vote, unanimous. Ellen is thanked for the ground stop by the same room that will turn on her inside half an hour. **No applause** (the book's one ovation is Denise's in Ch 09). Nobody comments on the irony. Don't point at it.
- **Precedent, read in full (outline set piece).** Before new business, the 112 asks from the floor whether the league has ever changed anything mid-season. Verna says the chair will consult the record. She finds 1987 and reads those minutes in full, in her small, cheerful voice: attendance; a dispute over a ball return; a motion to rename the snack bar ("The Pin Stop") that was moved, seconded, and failed; and, at last, the item that answers him (keep it short and real: 1987, mid-season, the league moved the Tuesday start from 7:00 to 6:45, and two bowlers objected for the record). The room fidgets. Ray doesn't.
- **The snack bar motion, renewed.** A man in the back renews the motion to rename the snack bar, half joking and half stalling. Verna recognizes him in full parliamentary form and notes the motion was seconded in 1987 and failed then, too, "but I don't like to presume." (Adapt the bible line; don't reuse it word for word.) It's seconded. It fails. Verna records the count. The bit gets about four exchanges and no more.
- **Article Four.** "Mrs. Daly, under Article Four you have three minutes." Ellen: "Ms." Verna: "The chair stands corrected. The secretary will so record," and writes it. (This is the chapter's one "Ms.", and it's new because it's Verna.) Verna sets the watch.
- **The three minutes (rule 21).** On the page, word for word, only this: lineage goes to **twelve-fifty a bowler, starting tonight**, and the house share of the prize fund goes from five dollars to two. Then **one sentence of narration** covers the Open (the reader has it). Stay on the room: who stops chewing, the Highway Department captain's cap coming off, Ray looking at the floor between his boots, Hollis looking at Ray.
- **"Negative" (ATC 2).** Someone calls out, mid-speech, "Is this Ray's idea?" Ellen: "Negative." She doesn't look at the second settee.
- **Time.** Verna's gavel at exactly 3:00 cuts Ellen off mid-sentence, on the kids' division bowling free. "Time." Ellen stops. (Rule 20: the procedure she respects most takes the last word from her, and she lets it.)

**Interior passage 3 (Ellen, 2–3 sentences), as she counts the room on the way to the counter:** Eighty-one. This is the reason she bought it: a room full of people who need sequencing, and a night with an order to it. She has never once addressed a room she couldn't see the end of.

**Subtext:** Verna's procedure is a comedy and a fortress. It protects Ellen's three minutes and it also guarantees she gets no more.
**Tension source:** Comic and procedural on top. Underneath, the room's patience running out during the 1987 minutes, Ray's silence on the second settee, and the price that hasn't been said yet.
**Ends on:** Mid-motion. The Highway Department captain is on his feet before the gavel has stopped. Cut before he speaks.
**Word count target:** 650

### Scene 4 — THE WALKOUT (RULE 22)
**Setting:** Same room, about 6:58 to 7:20 p.m. The house lights are still up. The lanes are dark at the pin end. The front door is twenty feet from the counter, past the snack bar, with cold air coming in every time it opens.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Ray, Verna, Denise, Hollis, the Highway Department captain, the 112, a hedging Legion bowler, the room
**Surface action:**
- **The raised voice (rule 22).** The Highway Department captain shouts. They paid in September. Twenty-two years of Thursdays. Arlene never once did this. He climbs onto the end of a settee to be seen. Then the line he can't take back, at full volume: **"Nobody asked you to buy it!"** That's exactly her wound, and he doesn't know it. The room goes quiet for a second because it went too far. He doesn't take it back, and he doesn't sit down.
- **Comic middle (rule 15): Verna in the storm.** "The gentleman from the Highway Department is out of order. He is also standing on a settee. The chair asks him to choose one." He gets down. He's still out of order.
- **Vary dialogue length (rule 20).** A Legion bowler hedges at length: he isn't against it, he just thinks, and his wife thinks, and it's a lot at once, and couldn't it wait till September, which is all he's saying. A short one from the back: "Pine Ridge is forty minutes." Someone asks Ray directly what he says. Ray, from his seat: "I said mine to her. Not here."
- **Lineage ruled, not voted (flag 4).** The 112 moves to reject the rate. Verna rules the motion out of order: the house sets the price of a string, and the league has no jurisdiction. She says it without sympathy for either side. That's when the room understands that leaving is the only vote they have.
- **The Open motion.** Denise moves it, as asked, in one sentence with a qualifier. Hollis seconds it, with his cap off to think and back on to speak. Captains' hands: **11 to 8, the coach abstaining.** Verna: the chair notes it's the first time the coach has abstained since 1996, and so records. Ray keeps his hands on his knees.
- **The walkout.** The 112 stands up. Taylor's Marina is bowling Tuesdays at Pine Ridge as of next week, and Gil has lanes. The Highway Department captain says Thursdays too. They start collecting bags. Then the 112, at the door, to the second settee: **"Ray. It's your league. You coming?"**
- **Ray's choice (earned from Ch 06: won't help, won't stand against).** He gets up. He walks the length of the settees toward the front, and the room, and Ellen, assume he's walking to the door. He stops at the counter, beside her, on her side of it. To the 112, plainly: "I don't agree with her." He stays standing there. He doesn't touch her or look at her, and he doesn't add a word.
- **They leave anyway.** Eight bowlers, two teams, out the door into the cold. The door wheezes shut on its closer. Nobody runs after them.
- **Verna.** "The chair notes the departure of two teams." Gavel. "The meeting is not adjourned." Then she adjourns it properly, at a stated minute, because it is now 7:19 and the Tuesday division bowls at 7:15 and she has never started a league night late.

**Interior passage 4 (Ellen, 2–3 sentences), with Ray beside her:** He's a foot to her left, holding a check he'll put back in the bank tomorrow and a letter that gets him the building if she fails, and he's standing on her side of the counter. She can't afford to turn her head, so she doesn't. She counts the room again instead: seventy-three.

**Subtext:** Ray didn't help her. He also didn't let them make it look like the alley was leaving her. He still thinks she's wrong. She still won't let him carry anything. Neither of them gives an inch, and he stands there anyway.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal, at full volume. A room against one woman, a captain who goes too far, and a man whose next step everyone misreads.
**Ellen loses ground (rule 20):** The vote carries and two teams leave anyway. Her best plan just cost her the league revenue it was meant to fix. She has no answer to "Nobody asked you to buy it," and she doesn't try one.
**Ends on:** A procedural line, mid-motion (the chapter's one quip-adjacent ending). Verna's adjournment at 7:19. Then Ellen, at the PA, is already giving the lane changes for a division one team short. Cut on the first word over the PA, before the sentence. Don't describe her voice; S2 already named it.

### Scene 5 — CLOSE
**Setting:** About 10:50 to 11:15 p.m. Tuesday league done, bowled on nine lanes, with one blind team scored per the bylaws (Verna). Lanes dark, and the sweeps down. Only the counter lights and the snack-bar fluorescent are on. The pool sheet is on the counter under Denise's crossword. Outside, the sign buzzes, and two cars are in the lot: Ellen's, and Ray's truck by the pit-side door.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Verna (briefly), Denise (briefly), Ray
**Surface action:**
- **Comic middle first, then quiet (rule 15).** Verna leaves last of the bowlers, zipped to the chin, binder under her arm. She stops at the counter: Ellen ran three minutes and four seconds, and the chair let the four go. That's all. Ellen thanks her. Verna says the chair doesn't require thanks, only minutes. Exit.
- **The pool sheet (outline).** Denise slides the sheet out to settle the night's cash. A new square in a hand neither of them names: **FEB 2 — THEY SPLIT.** Denise uncaps her crossword pen and crosses it out, one line, and says nothing about it. Then her one speech of the chapter (flag 7), sideways: Ray took the sheet off his wall Monday and handed it to her across the counter, said it wasn't his business to have up, and said it like a fella returning a borrowed hammer. She puts on the earflap hat (no number) and goes. No "honey."
- **The tape.** Alone, Ellen takes the folded stub out of her vest from S2 and finally reads it against tonight's actual. One line on the page, her figures: income projection twenty-two; cash seventeen. Two thousand under where she stood at 6:07, and further from both floors than she was this morning. Ray's offer would have cleared both. She doesn't think that last sentence. The reader does.
- **Ray.** He comes up through the steel door by lane 10 with the drop light. Lane 9's table ran late in the third string, Denise texted him, and he's been in the pit since. He writes his time out on his card on the pegboard. (One card, no comment. This is the chapter's single index card.) He puts on his coat.
- **The ownership line (outline: one sentence, no therapy).** She says it before he reaches the back door, standing: something on the order of **"I ended it at your truck window like a shift briefing, and that was mine, not yours."** One sentence. She doesn't explain it or follow it.
- **He nods.** He says nothing, or one short word at most. He goes out the back. She hears the back door lock behind him.

**Interior passage 5 (Ellen, 2–3 sentences), after he's gone:** The same mistake, the same kind, at the end of the same kind of shift: everything on the frequency perfect and the separation gone. This time she reported it herself within five hours. It doesn't fix anything, and she didn't expect it to.

**Subtext:** She owns her method and keeps her decision. He accepts the one and still disagrees with the other. Nothing has changed except that they've both been honest, and she's alone in the airspace she chose.
**Tension source:** Internal (the tape, the empty building) and interpersonal held at arm's length (one sentence each way).
**Ends on:** Suspended action and an unanswered question (sets up "does she still want to?"). Lights off, door locked. In her car, the engine doesn't catch on the first try (the Ch 02 detail). Across the lot, Ray's truck sits with its lights on and doesn't leave, the way it didn't leave on December 1. Ellen keeps her hand on the key and doesn't turn it again yet. Cut. Don't show the second try. No sign image (the dead K isn't used this chapter).
**Word count target:** 500
