She had seen Hollis's question go down the table to him..." and runs through "as plainly as she had ever watched two slow lights draw toward each other on a dark screen." It replays the whole scene back to me in summary, then states the moral ("He was right, and she had known he was right while she stood at the table"). The radar simile is the author reaching for a motif. I had already felt all of it on the page. Earlier, at ~5%, the single block after Denise's cut-off sentence ("Ellen had worked this counter beside Denise since the first of December... For ten days she had let the mailbox go for her") is a paragraph of lecture before anyone has spoken. Near the end, "None of them would do." closes the scene by explaining the key, where A just puts it in her pocket.

5. **Confidence:** High. The two are the same scene with near-identical dialogue, so the difference is entirely in what happens around the lines, and A is ahead everywhere: the room is blocked (the four-person table, Dale's keys, the zipped jacket), the silence is dramatized (Dale's keys lifted and set down in the same place, Denise's grip on the mop) where B has Ellen narrate her temptation to end it, and the ending is clean. The quiet went on, and she let it."). Neither reads like calibration anchor A1. No codes, no ledgers, and the couple is in the room. That is the only reason this isn't higher than high.
