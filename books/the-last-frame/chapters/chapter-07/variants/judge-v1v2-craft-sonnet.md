1. Winner: A

2. Why: A has a woman whose voice I can hear going wrong ("She noticed that she had made her voice pleasant. Of all her voices it was the one she trusted least."), while B mostly arranges still people around a table and tells me how quiet it is.

3. Where A lost me: the long reflective paragraph in the last scene, "She had watched it close. She had seen Hollis's question go down the table to him and the silence take hold, and Verna's ruling put it all on one chair..." (about 85%). It re-narrates the table scene I had just read, then says "Nobody at the table could have told. Nobody had." and "He was right, and she had known he was right." Ray's line had already landed, so this is the author explaining it to me. A smaller dip came earlier, in the paragraph after Denise breaks off, "She knew every hand on those envelopes..." (about 8%). It is one very long run of accounting for the seven envelopes. My thumb sped up there, but the Gil Arsenault voicemail sentence and Denise stopping mid-sentence had already earned my attention.

4. Where B lost me: "Dale's keys lay where they lay. Verna's hands were folded. Denise sat by her mop. The lights went on burning over the empty lanes, and Ray looked at the table in front of him, at nothing on it, and the quiet went down the table and past the foot of it and out over all ten lanes and did not come back with anything." (about 45%). This is the second of three paragraphs that just inventory hands and keys while the silence is "heavy." After that I was reading blocking, not people. "Stayed where they were" and "did not move" repeat again around 55%. The ending also goes flat: "She put the key in her own vest pocket." The sentence stops there and no longer has any weight behind it.

5. Confidence: medium. A's weak paragraph near the end is a real flaw, and a reader who hates interiority could prefer B's cleaner stage picture. But A is better sentence by sentence, and its people sound more different from each other.

6. Same-author check: Yes. Same Ellen, same radio-voice motif (separation, "Understood," Kate's name), same Verna ("the secretary"), and same dialogue lines almost word for word. A is the same chapter with the interiority turned up.

Craft notes behind the pick:
- A's rhythm varies. It runs from a fast one-line fragment ("Nobody answered. Ray's hand stayed where it was.") to the long rolling sentences. B's rhythm is steadier and slower, and most beats end on a "did not / stayed" clause.
- A gives each speaker something different. Dale speaks in deadpan fact ("Under the scrape it was steady. He had been saying it to himself on his route since December"). Verna rules in the third person, and A has her speak "into the gap." Hollis is flat. Ray's voice "came back shorter," which is a precise echo of the radio-headset image. B states these differences (Hollis "in the voice he would give an ice depth") but A makes me hear them.
- A is fresher on the details: "like a woman signing for a delivery," the cuff of the glove standing up out of the vest, and the key zipped into the pocket. B reaches for the more generic "breath going up white" and "taking each one as long as it took."
- A's closing image does the work of the chapter. B's closing image is the same object with less behind it.
- Calibration check: neither text reads like A1 (flight-strip codes, clipped procedure). Both are scene-in-a-room with a couple, so A1 doesn't decide it. The ledger-and-envelope counting at A's opening is the closest either gets to A1, and A turns it into feeling quickly enough.
