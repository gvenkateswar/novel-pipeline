1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A lets Ellen hear voices the way a dispatcher would: Denise leaving a sentence unfinished, Ray's long voice coming back short, her own "pleasant" voice that she trusts least. And it gives her one paragraph at the table where she knowingly lets the silence do her asking ("She had worked beside people who let a silence do their work for them. She had never thought well of a single one."). That is the best writing in either version. B handles the same scene by listing who hasn't moved.

3. **Where A lost me:** about 90%, the paragraph after the radio line: "She had watched it close. She had seen Hollis's question go down the table to him and the silence take hold, and Verna's ruling put it all on one chair, and each face come round to him in turn…" It retells the scene I read five minutes ago, then lands on "kept her voice where it was" for about the fifth time in the chapter. By then the phrase was a habit and had stopped doing any work.

4. **Where B lost me:** about 45%, after Hollis's question: "Hollis waited with his eyes on him. Dale's keys lay where they lay. Verna's hands were folded. Denise sat by her mop." It's a stillness inventory. It comes back at about 55% ("They stayed where they were. Hollis's elbows stayed where they were. Ray's hand had not moved…") and again in the slow round-the-table look before "Take it." Each time I skimmed down to the next line of dialogue.

5. **Confidence: medium.** Most of the dialogue is the same in both, and B is cleaner in places: Ray's "came out bitter at the far end," and his breath going white on the step. But B shrinks the moral centre of the chapter, Ellen choosing not to rescue Ray, to one sentence, and A gives it room to hurt.
