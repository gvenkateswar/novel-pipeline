# Ch 07 cold-read triage (orchestrator, autopilot)

**Verdict:** "The strongest set piece in the book... But the dark moment is mostly arithmetic." The best moment is Ray walking to Ellen's side of the counter: "I don't agree with her." The second best is the near-miss backstory and the four minutes at the ignition. The problems are four:
- The breakup is too tidy, and Ray accepts it without protest.
- Verna's comedy deflates the meeting.
- The cost of refusing the money is never felt.
- The tics are plain to see.

**Process:** one revision round (a prose-writer targeted pass, then a voice-editor pass).

**Keep unchanged:**
- plot facts: the $55K offer and its numbers, the 11–8 vote, Ray abstaining, two teams leaving, and Ray standing at her left
- the Highway Department captain shouting "Nobody asked you to buy it!"
- the near-miss backstory

**Length:** 3,200–3,500.

## Make the dark moment dark

1. **The breakup (S1).**
   - Ellen can still speak in her briefing voice. But Ray, who shouted at her in Ch 06, protests. He argues back with one or two lines that cost him, and he doesn't fold the check away in silence.
   - Ellen's two-clause reason ("If I take it, I'll never know whether I could have carried it. And you'll never know whether I wanted you or your money.") is too balanced. Break it up so she gets the first half out, stumbles, or says the second half badly.
   - Ray's "I want you. I want the alley. I've been trying since Sunday to pull those two apart" says the theme out loud. Cut it to something smaller and more his.
2. **Ellen hits the floor (S5).** The four-minute ignition story sets up a moment alone, so pay it off.
   - After the meeting she sits in her car. Her hands won't get the key in, the way they wouldn't after the near-miss, and she times it again.
   - Let her lose composure here, alone: something she says out loud in the car, or a sound she makes. No swearing, and no melodrama.
   - Ray's truck is in the lot. He does not come over, because she ended it. His headlights follow her down the drive only after she has started the car on her own. Keep it short.
3. **Ellen raises her voice once,** somewhere in the chapter. The meeting is the obvious place, or at Ray in S1.

## Stop the comedy deflating the meeting

4. Verna reading the 1987 minutes: cut it to the one line that matters (the mid-season precedent, and the vote).
5. Cut the ninety-one-year-old's snack-bar rename motion from the meeting, or move a single line of it to before the meeting starts. Verna keeps her procedural dryness, but nothing gets a laugh once the vote is called.

## Clarity

6. **The numbers.** "Income, twenty-two. Cash, seventeen." should say plainly what it means against Judy's targets, for example: "Twenty-two thousand against Judy's thirty. Seventeen in the bank against her twenty-five."
7. **The $55K speech.**
   - Trim Ray's numbers to what a person would say out loud: the total, the note paydown, and that it gets her over both lines.
   - Cut "Twenty-three thousand nine hundred fifty," or let Ellen say it back as the thing that stings.
   - "Danny worked your income back from the cash you gave me and the interest you pay Arlene" is confusing. Make it plain how Ray knows her numbers, or cut the line.
8. **The pool sheet.** Make clear that Ray brought the pool sheet to the alley on Monday and gave it to Denise. Cut the "FEB 2, THEY SPLIT" square: nobody could know within 90 minutes.
9. **The 2019 fuel-oil calendar.** Either cut it or describe it so it's new information.
10. **"Because you coach."** Make it clear early that Ray coaches the Gagnon & Son team.
11. **Hollis's "Purpose of visit."** Ch 06 ended on it. Add a line in S1 that connects: Ray's offer is the answer he couldn't give Hollis, or Ray says he got as far as the first word.

## The vote

12. The vote is too neat: exactly the two teams Ellen projected losing are the ones that walk. Keep two teams leaving, but make one of them a team she counted on staying. That's one more blow.

## Tics

13. Cut the key ring turning or ringing. It's retired for this chapter.
14. Cut at least half of the pause lines: "He stood with that a long time," "He let that go by," "She kept her hands where they were," "She looked at them to make sure, and they were fine."
15. Cut the "lit the two of them up like a lane" simile.
16. The chapter can still end on Ray's headlights following her car, but only after item 2. Don't let the warm image be the first thing that happens after the meeting.
