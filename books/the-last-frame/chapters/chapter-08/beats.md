**POV:** Ray (close third, past tense) for Scenes 1–5. Ellen for Scene 6, the chapter's one short Ellen section, after a section break.
**Dates:** Monday, February 8 to Saturday, February 20
**Target:** 3,400 words (scene targets add up to 3,400)

## Pre-flight flags (read before drafting)

1. **Ledger location conflict (fixed).** The outline puts Arlene's 1996 ledger open in her apartment. The bible and Ch 05–06 put every green ledger, 1971–2026, on the office shelves at the alley. **Fix (rule 14):** Arlene asked Ellen to lend her the 1996 volume, and Ellen did. On the spine is a strip of masking tape in Ellen's block capitals: `1996 — LENT A. FORTIER 02/05 — DUE 03/01`. Ray recognizes the hand. Nobody comments on the due date.
2. **League-night conflict at Pine Ridge (fixed).** Ray coaches Gagnon & Son at Kettle Lanes on Tuesdays, and Denise never leaves the counter during a rush (bible). The side match is the night of the release (Tue Feb 16), so it starts at **9:50 p.m.**, after both houses' Tuesday leagues are done. Gil keeps one lane pair open past closing for it. Denise leaves Kettle Lanes once the night's cash is settled, and Ellen closes alone (off page). Ray arranged everything by phone with Gil on Sunday, which he hated doing. That gets one line in S4.
3. **Clean-rules problem in the bible.** Gil's sample line "my lane, my balls" breaks the no-double-meaning rule. Use: **"House rules, Ray. Three strings, total pins, my lane, my house ball. You'll forgive the ball."** Check every Pine Ridge sentence for "balls" plural.
4. **The defectors are both Tuesday teams.** In Ch 07's final text, Taylor's Marina and **the Legion** left, and the Highway Department stayed. The Ch 07 beat sheet had it differently, so go by the final. **Rule 14 cause of the return:** the side match terms (S4) bind Taylor's Marina, and the Legion is bound because **the two teams ride to Halsey Falls together in the Legion post's van.** A forty-minute winter drive is the only reason Pine Ridge works for either of them. One van means they decide together. Plant the van in the Pine Ridge lot (S4). Their first Tuesday back is Feb 23, off the page. The Feb 20 money row counts their commitment and Open entries.
5. **What Ray stakes in the match (rule 1: no money).** If Ray wins, both teams come back to Kettle Lanes Tuesdays at $12.50. **If the 112 wins, Ray bowls Tuesdays at Pine Ridge with Taylor's Marina through April.** That answers the 112's Ch 07 "You coming?" and explains why the 112 takes the match. Ray loses something real if he loses.
6. **The release stays private (rule 7).** On the page, only Ray, the lawyer, and the reader know. Nobody tells Danny, the Bench, Denise, or Ellen. The Bench misreads the store handover as Ray clearing his decks to take the alley on March 2 (S5). Denise relays that rumor to Ellen (S6), so Ellen ends the chapter believing the opposite of the truth. Ray tells her himself in Ch 09.
7. **Locker 14 (outline): no speech, no flashback, and no comment then or later.** Ellen and Denise both see him carry the bag out (S2) and say nothing. **Ch 09 and Ch 10 drafters must not mention the empty locker.** The ball goes with Ray to Pine Ridge and sits in its bag at Denise's feet. Gil's house rules mean he bowls a house ball. Leave it unremarked. Whether he bowls it on the 27th is Ch 09's choice.
8. **"The voice" is banned in Ch 08** (permitted only in Ch 01, 07, 09). No kiss in this chapter (Clean rules). Kiss 3 is Ch 10. Neither of the first two kisses gets mentioned in dialogue. One glancing fact in Ray's interior (S2) is allowed, so the stairs aren't forgotten (rule 22).
9. **Arlene's "first crack" said at Linda's funeral (small addition).** The bible says she promised "more than once and loosely." Ray names one of those times, in March 2020, at the funeral, without dating it. It's the edge his anger needs, and it keeps Arlene from being a villain, because she meant it that day.
10. **Ray says "Ms. Daly" (S2).** It's the first time he gets her title right all winter, and it lands as distance. That doesn't interfere with Ch 10's uncorrected "Mrs. Daly". It makes it better. This is the chapter's only use of either title.
11. **Massachusetts vacation week.** The bible's money table says Feb 13–21 and the research notes say Feb 15–19. Sat Feb 13 is the arrival weekend under either reading. Write "Massachusetts plates" and "vacation week" and give no dates.
12. **Plant for Ch 09.** Hollis's plow contract calls him out above ten inches (S6). That's the rule-14 cause of Gagnon & Son being a bowler short on the 27th. Ellen also ends Ch 08 with no mechanic arranged for the 27th, which sets up the D-side ask.
13. **Ch 07 reader questions answered without re-staging:** "Did Ray really take the pool sheet off his wall, and why?" gets the empty nail behind the store register and the Bench's complaint (S5). "Can the Open close the gap?" gets the pace sheet stated plainly against both of Judy's targets (S6). The reader was confused by Ch 07's shorthand, so say "against thirty" and "against twenty-five" outright.

### Scene 1 — UNIT 2B
**Setting:** Arlene's apartment above Ambrose Pharmacy, Monday, Feb 8, about 10 a.m. You get there up the steep side stairs. Boxes are half packed and labeled in green-ledger script, THE PINES — UNIT 2B. Fifty years of photographs lie face down on the table for packing. The police scanner is on the counter, turned low, and wintergreen comes up through the floor from the pharmacy. The 1996 ledger lies open on the kitchen table at a line that reads *key to R.G.*, with Ellen's masking-tape strip on the spine (flag 1).
**Characters present:** Ray, Arlene
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late.** Ray is already inside, setting down two rolls of packing tape from the store. Arlene called Gagnon & Son for three and asked that "Raymond bring them himself." She licks her thumb and counts the rolls.
- She turns the ledger toward him and taps the line with her magnifier. The 1996 page reads *key to R.G.* and *no charge.* She says that's the only thing she ever wrote down about him and that building.
- **Comic middle (rule 15).** The scanner reports a plow truck in the ditch on Dock Road. Arlene names the driver without looking up and gives her opinion of his corners. She goes straight back to the conversation as if the scanner were a third person at the table who talks too much.
- **Ray finally says it (the anger the bible says he has held since the sale).** She told him first crack, more than once, and once at Linda's funeral. His voice doesn't rise, but it's the hardest thing he has ever said to her, and he can't take it back. Arlene doesn't flinch: she said it, and she meant it that day.
- **Arlene's one speech (short, under 60 words).** She knew his offer was coming. She sold to Ellen anyway: **"You'd have kept it a museum. I wanted it to live."** She doesn't soften it with "dear." One "Voyons donc" is allowed if it fits, and no more.
- Ray asks whether Ellen knows why she was picked. Arlene: "She hasn't asked." (This plants Ch 10, where Arlene tells her in one line.)

**Interior passage 1 (Ray, 2–4 sentences), after the funeral line:** What the alley is to him: the last room Linda was in that nobody has rearranged. Since December he has been angry at an 84-year-old woman in a cardigan, and it has done exactly as much good as being angry at the weather.
**Interior passage 2 (Ray, 2–3 sentences), on seeing the tape on the spine:** He knows those capitals from the R.G. card. She lent Arlene the book and gave it a due date of March 1. Don't explain why that hurts.

**Subtext:** Ray holds Arlene to a promise she said out loud and never wrote down, which is exactly what he did to Ellen in Ch 06. The reader should catch it. Ray half does, and the narration never says so (rule 16).
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. A man finally being unkind to someone he loves like an aunt, and losing the argument anyway.
**Ends on:** Mid-motion. Arlene finishes counting and says something like "Two. I asked for three." Ray is already on the stairs going down for the third roll. Cut on him moving. Don't end on the museum line; it shouldn't be the last line of the scene.
**Word count target:** 500

### Scene 2 — LANE 4 (REPAIR, INVOICE, TROPHY CASE, LOCKER 14)
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, Saturday, Feb 13, about 2:40 to 3:30 p.m., the first Saturday of Massachusetts vacation week. The lot is full of Massachusetts plates, and there's a waiting list on the strip board. Nine lanes are running and lane 4 is dark. Denise is spraying shoes at a two-count. The Open entry clipboard is on the counter with nine lines left. The R.G. — AFTER HOURS card is on the pegboard by the pit door. In the pit: the table on lane 4 hangs at the respot.
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen, Denise, an eight-year-old boy from Massachusetts (walk-on, unnamed)
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late, already inside.** Ellen's text, already received, is quoted once on the page as she wrote it: lane 4's table hanging on the respot, work order, **"rate per our agreement."** Ray comes in through the back door and writes his time in on his card (the chapter's one index card; no comment). The reason column says LANE 04 / TABLE.
- **Their only exchange about the machine, at the pit door, flat and exact.** She tells him the table has hung three times since noon and that she has closed the lane. He has already heard which part it is from the catwalk. **One machine simile, the chapter's only one (rule 22 cap).** Neither says anything that isn't about lane 4. Neither mentions Feb 2.
- **Comic middle (rule 15).** A Massachusetts boy waiting for lane 4 lies flat on the approach to watch Ray through the pin deck and asks whether he lives back there. Ray: "Saturdays." The boy reports this to his father, word for word, at full volume.
- Twenty minutes later lane 4 cycles clean. Ray writes his time out on the card.
- **The invoice at the counter.** He hands her a Gagnon & Son sales slip. She reads it, writes a check on the spot, and tears it out. "Thank you, Mr. Gagnon." "**Ms.** Daly." (flag 10). That's the whole conversation. While she writes, he reads the Open clipboard upside down and counts the lines left.
- **The trophy case on his way out (outline: one visit).** He stops between the snack bar and lane 1. He doesn't touch the glass (that tic is retired). He looks at the photo on the 187 card, Linda mid-release, the only way he has ever seen that string (Ch 04).
- **Locker 14.** Beside the case. He lifts the latch, takes Linda's ball out in its bag, and leaves the locker door standing open. He walks out past the counter with the bag. Denise keeps spraying. Ellen is on the phone. Nobody says anything, and the narration gives nobody a reaction (flag 7).

**Interior passage 3 (Ray, 3–4 sentences), at the case:** Linda would have entered the Open the first day the clipboard went up. She'd have paid cash, written her name on line one, and then asked Ellen exactly where the entry fee went. That's the whole beat. No flashback and no memory scene.
**Interior passage 4 (Ray, 2 sentences), at the counter while Ellen writes the check:** "Effective now," and he has kept to it: the card, the slip, the check. She wrote "rate per our agreement" because it's the one agreement they still have. (A glancing fact about the cellar stairs is allowed here and optional. One clause, no description.)

**Subtext:** It's the coldest exchange in the book, and both of them conduct it perfectly. He gets her title right because they're strangers now. Then he takes the one thing in the building he has kept frozen for six years.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal (the strained politeness) and internal (the case). Situationally, her best Saturday is a lane short until he fixes it.
**Ends on:** Mid-motion, cut away. Ray is passing the counter with the bag. Ellen, on the phone to a waiting family and not looking up, says, "Lane four. Three-fifteen. Four bowlers." She's booking the lane he just fixed. Cut on her line. Don't end on Ray, the locker, the door, or the lot.
**Word count target:** 700

### Scene 3 — LECLERC'S (THE RELEASE)
**Setting:** Leclerc's law office, second floor over the barber on Main Street, Tuesday, Feb 16, 9 a.m. **Three details only (outline):** the notary embosser, the radiator under the window, and the framed 1950s map of the lake.
**Characters present:** Ray, the lawyer (unnamed on the page; "Leclerc" can appear only as the name on the door)
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late.** The release is already drafted on the desk. Ray signs. The lawyer asks for ID. His bank commitment letter is folded behind his license where it has been since Ch 02, so it comes out with it. The lawyer reads the letterhead and says nothing. Ray folds the letter into his coat instead of back behind the license. That's a small action, and nobody remarks on it.
- **Comic middle (rule 15).** The embosser is older than the lawyer and needs her whole weight on it. Ray takes a dime out of his pocket and tightens its hinge screw while she gets the date stamp ready. That's his fix-while-talking tic, and the dime is from Ch 04. The radiator bangs at each signature.
- The lawyer says the reason isn't required. Ray: "No." (No "Fella I knew"; that bit is retired after being cut off in Ch 05 and Ch 06.) She notarizes and says **"I'll record it this afternoon."** Once recorded, it's public. Anyone can look.
- She asks whether he wants a copy sent to Ms. Daly. "No." She asks when he means to tell her.

**Interior passage 5 (Ray, 3–4 sentences), as he signs:** What he's signing away: the March 2 plan, and being "the somebody" who keeps the lights on. If she misses now, the building goes to whoever bids, and the developer from Concord is a real man with a real phone number. He signs anyway. Don't state why. The reader has S1 and S2.

**Subtext:** This is the most important thing Ray does in the book, and it's done in a room with a radiator, in four minutes, with the important word ("No") said last.
**Tension source:** Internal. The risk he's now accepting on her behalf, without her knowledge or her consent.
**Ends on:** An unanswered question, cut away early. The lawyer: "When will you tell her?" Cut before he answers. Ch 09 answers it.
**Word count target:** 300

### Scene 4 — PINE RIDGE (THE SIDE MATCH)
**Setting:** Pine Ridge Lanes, Halsey Falls, Tuesday, Feb 16, 9:50 to about 11:15 p.m. Sixteen lanes, cosmic carpet, overhead scoring screens, pop music, and the smell of pizza and the carpet glue that never fully went away. Pine Ridge's league is done and every lane is dark except one pair at the far end. In the lot: the **AMBROSE POST — AMERICAN LEGION** van (flag 4). The lounge is background only, and nobody in the cast is in it. The teams wait at the snack counter.
**Characters present:** Ray, Denise, Gil, the 112, the Legion captain, Taylor's Marina and the Legion (as a group)
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late.** Ray is lacing his own shoes on a Pine Ridge settee (he bowls in nothing else). Linda's ball sits in its bag at Denise's feet by the scorer's table. One line: Ray called Gil on Sunday and asked, which cost him more than the forty-minute drive.
- **Gil's terms (flag 3), over the wireless mic to a nearly empty house.** That's the comic middle, Gil's salesmanship turned on a crowd of eleven. "You'll forgive the ball." Ray takes a house ball off the rack. The stakes are stated once, plainly (flag 5): if Ray wins, both teams are back Tuesday at twelve-fifty; if the 112 wins, Ray bowls here Tuesdays through April. The Legion captain explains the van in one sentence: they came in one van, and they go home in one van.
- **Gil referees scrupulously.** He says Denise's paper sheet is official alongside his own screen. Once, he rules against his own house's interest: he refuses the 112's request to move to a "truer" lane between strings.
- **String one: Ray bowls terribly.** He lofts, chops wrong, and leaves wood everywhere. On one ball: **"Holy mackerel."** It's the only time in the book, and he hears himself say it. Don't interpret it.
- **Denise–Gil (the Book 2 seed, one exchange only, between strings).** Gil tells her he watched her pick up a four-horse with her third ball in 2015. Denise is unimpressed by the overcoat and corrects one detail, then goes back to her sheet. Gil compliments her conversions, not her looks. Two lines each at most, then out.
- **String two.** He goes low. *Low. Let the lane have it.* It's his own December lesson, heard in his head, one line. He climbs back in it.
- **String three, tenth frame, total pins.** He needs a specific number, and Denise tells him the number, nothing else. First ball chops and leaves wood on the deck. Before the third ball, in his head and verbatim: **"The wood stays on the lane. You play what's left."** (Full line, use 2 of 3. Don't say it aloud and don't paraphrase it.) The third ball drives a fallen pin across and takes the last of what he needs. This is skill he taught, not a lucky bounce (rule 22). Describe one fallen pin doing its job.
- **Ray wins by two pins.** Gil reads the totals off Denise's paper, not his screen.
- **Grumbling (outline, Ch 09's sour line comes later).** The 112 accepts it with bad grace. The Legion captain agrees because of the van, and is almost relieved.

**Interior passage 6 (Ray, 3–4 sentences), during string one:** He hasn't bowled with anything riding on it since Linda. League strings don't count; they're Tuesdays. Linda's ball is four feet away in a bag he carried in and can't use under Gil's rules, and he brought it anyway.

**Subtext:** Ray is winning back the teams for the woman whose building he signed away his claim to this morning, and nobody in this room knows that. The 112 thinks Ray is choosing the alley over the league. He's choosing something nobody here has a word for.
**Tension source:** Situational (a match he's losing for two strings) and interpersonal (the 112's grievance, Gil's courteous rivalry).
**Ends on:** A grumble, the chapter's one quip-adjacent ending. The 112, coat already on, heading for the van: some version of "Tuesday. Twelve-fifty. Don't expect me to like it." Cut before Ray answers. If the drafter wants Gil's "They drove here. I just kept the lights on," it goes mid-scene, not here.
**Word count target:** 850

### Scene 5 — THE BENCH (THE STORE HANDOVER)
**Setting:** Gagnon & Son Hardware, Wednesday, Feb 17, about 6:20 a.m. The bell on the door, the key cutter, and plow radios crackling from trucks out front. The Bench is on three stools by the coffee pot. The cracked green stool beside Ray's at the counter (Linda's, Ch 02) has been empty for six years. Behind the register is the **empty nail** where the pool sheet hung until Feb 1.
**Characters present:** Ray, Danny, the Bench (three regulars; name Normie only)
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late.** Ray has already said it. Danny is standing with his tablet under his arm and asks: **"Is this you giving me the store, or you leaving the store? Because those are different invoices."** Ray: giving. Outright, with the papers to follow.
- **The Bench's broadcast (Ray's comic engine; rule 13: funny through what it gets wrong).** They call the handover like the Saturday show out of Boston. They argue over who they report to now. Normie says they report to the coffee. They ask Danny whether the coffee is included. Danny, transactional: it's been a quarter since 1990, and nobody has paid it since 1990.
- **They get it wrong, which keeps rule 7 intact (flag 6).** One of them works it out loud: Ray gives up the store, so Ray is freeing himself up for March second, so he's getting the alley. Another says he heard about Tuesday at Pine Ridge and that proves it. Ray doesn't correct them, because he doesn't lie, and he goes quiet instead.
- **The pool sheet answer (Ch 07 reader question).** Normie complains there's nothing to bet on in here anymore since the sheet went up the hill. He points at the empty nail. Nobody explains why Ray took it down.
- **Danny's one real line:** "You're allowed to want something that isn't on a shelf." Ray doesn't answer it.
- Ray tells Normie to sit on the green stool, because the Bench needs a seat for the broadcast. Normie sits and keeps calling it. Nobody pauses, and the narration doesn't pause either.

**Interior passage 7 (Ray, 2–3 sentences):** The store was handed to him above this floor in 1964, along with everything in it, and he has kept it the way his father left it and the way Linda left her stool. Danny modernized it four years ago, and Ray has been "checking" Danny's work ever since.

**Subtext:** It's Ray's third letting-go in two days, done in front of the three loudest men in Ambrose, and they read it backward. The reader knows, the town thinks the opposite, and Ellen will hear the town's version.
**Tension source:** Comic on the surface. Underneath, the dramatic irony of the rumor, and Danny's patience finally being rewarded.
**Ends on:** Mid-motion, cut away mid-broadcast. Ray is going out the door into the cold, and Normie, from the green stool, is still calling it ("and the old man's heading out, folks, and nobody knows where…"). Cut mid-sentence. Don't end on the door, the bell, or the truck.
**Word count target:** 450

### Scene 6 — PLAN E (ELLEN)
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, Saturday, Feb 20, 7:15 a.m., before opening. A whiteboard is propped on the burnt-orange settee at lanes 1–2, divided into columns A through D in Ellen's capitals. The pace sheet on graph paper is taped by the register. On the sandwich board, brought in to dry: **OPEN — FULL**. Hollis has a printed forecast, his waterproof notebook, and his blaze-orange cap. Denise has the crossword in pen.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Denise, Hollis
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late, mid-plan.** The plans are already on the board:
  - **A:** the ice holds, the derby runs outdoors, and the Open runs as planned.
  - **B:** storm. The derby's indoor events move here, and fish caught Friday get weighed Saturday on **lane 10** (plants Ch 09's coolers).
  - **C:** roads closed. Open with whoever can get here, and Hollis's route keeps Kettle Road passable.
  - **D:** the power goes. Hand-set pins from the pit, as on Jan 7. The Ch 04 callback is one clause.
- **The money, stated plainly (Ch 07 reader confusion; rule 15).** Ellen reads the pace sheet: twenty-four thousand three hundred to date. Projected **twenty-nine against Judy's thirty.** Cash projected **twenty-three thousand seven-fifty against her twenty-five.** Both are still short. The Open sold out Thursday, Massachusetts week was strong, and both Tuesday teams are back on the sheet at twelve-fifty. The 27th has to close both gaps. Keep the sentences this plain.
- **The forecast (weather is the plot here, so a weather line is allowed).** Hollis, with measurements and no speculation: a coastal low in the long-range models for Friday night into Saturday. "Don't know yet." He'll know more Thursday, and he'll know at 4 a.m. Saturday. If it's over ten inches, his plow contract has him on the back roads from three a.m. (flag 12). Denise works out loud that Gagnon & Son would be a bowler short in the final. Hollis: Ray'll find somebody.
- **Comic middle (outline set piece).** Hollis, cap off to think and back on to speak: **"What's Plan E?"** Ellen: **"Plan E is we all go home."** Hollis writes it in the notebook (his one notebook use this chapter).
- **The rumor (flag 6), Denise's one speech of the chapter, talky and run-on.** She covers the Bench's version of Wednesday (Ray gave Danny the store, so he's clearing his decks for March second). Then Tuesday at Pine Ridge, which she saw: Ray bowled terribly and then didn't. Gil's overcoat. The van. She doesn't mention the four-horse. Ellen tries to keep order with **"Stand by"** (ATC 1). Denise doesn't stand by.
- **Ellen loses the round (rule 20).** Hollis looks at the board and asks the question she has no answer to: **who's on the machines?** Under every plan, A through D, Ellen has written `MX:` and nothing after it. On a day with 150 people in boots, two tables will act up, and D needs people in the pit. She has no answer and doesn't pretend to have one.

**Interior passage (Ellen, 3–4 sentences), after Denise's account:** He won her two teams back on Tuesday. Two weeks ago he stood at her left, and he still holds the first refusal. If she misses by fifty dollars, he gets the building those teams came back to. She can't make those facts sit on one line of a tape. (The reader knows about the release. Ellen doesn't. Don't wink at it.)

**Subtext:** Ellen built a contingency for every failure except the one that's a person. The only qualified answer to `MX:` is the man she ended it with, and she believes he's positioning himself to take the building.
**Tension source:** Situational (the storm, a dollar margin on both numbers) and internal (the blank `MX:`, a gap she can't sequence).
**Ends on:** An unanswered question, which sets up both halves of the chapter promise: the weather and Ray. Denise, capping her crossword pen: "You calling him, honey, or am I?" (the chapter's one "honey"). Cut before Ellen answers. No gesture with the marker, no type-and-delete, and nothing on the board changes.
**Word count target:** 600
