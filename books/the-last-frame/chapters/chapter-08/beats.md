*Plot architect, Oct 29, 2026. Built from outline.md rev 3.1 (Ch 08, with Ch 07 and Ch 09 for hand-offs), continuity-log.md (the Ch 06 and Ch 07 entries, their flags, the master flags and the plant ledger), story-bible.md, genre-maps/romance.md, Ch 07 `beats.md` and `final.md`, and the orchestrator's seven requirements. Ray's POV throughout. Warm register. Act 3 opens at about 70%.*

**The lead, picked against the beats.** The couple is apart for the first two scenes and together only for the last two, so the lead is **lead with the couple**. In every scene where Ellen is absent, Ray is doing something because of her and holding something of hers: her letter at Pine Ridge (s1) and her signature on Arlene's copy of the contract (s2). In the two scenes where she is present, the pressure is a third party the reader can see. In s3 it's the Bench coming through the door at six while she's mid-sentence. In s4 it's his word to Dale, Hollis on the phone, Arlene's signature already on the page, and Concord at nine in the morning. Every scene is acted, nothing is narrated, and there are no glosses after a gesture.

## Flags (where the log, the outline and the orchestrator's requirements meet, and how each is resolved)

That date is the verification: she put it in writing before anyone knew how Friday would go. **Repair order still holds:** accountability is on the page already (the Ch 07 confession, and the letter written the next morning), then verification (s1), then her coming to his ground (s3), then the condition (s4), then the ordinary beat (Ch 09 s1). **Calendar for the log:** Feb 4 (Thu), the letter is read to the Thursday division, offstage. Feb 5 (Fri), Ray reads the committee copy. The Feb 11 event is struck. The Feb 17 lapse is stated as intent in s4.
3. **The third telling is cut.** Ellen does not re-confess in s3. She says only what is new: the teams are back, Denise told her, she came after she knew it, and he has no reason to believe she would have come anyway. She also says she has no way to give him one. He has one already, in the date he read in s1. He says nothing in s3. He gives her the date in s4 (World rule 20: the fact reaches her in the chapter it arises).
4. **New, from the Ch 06 flag, and a causal link, not a plot change.** At Pine Ridge, Dale asks the question that took his crew away ("And Linda's cup?"). Ray answers for himself: **"You won't pay for it twice. That one's mine."** That promise is why Ray's condition in s4 exists, and it is also why the condition is a real ask: if she says no, his word to Dale breaks. It also turns Ch 07's "That's mine" (which she refused) into a debt that is truly his. Orchestrator: confirm it is inside the approved outline (F9's "bowl the Memorial", plus the Ch 06 flag on the double payment).
5. **When the release is signed.** On Feb 6 there is no paper yet. Arlene agrees on her condition, her lawyer puts it on one page, and she signs during the week. On Feb 16 Ray carries it with Arlene's signature and his own, and a line left for Ellen's. 6. **Normie / Normand.** Arlene says "Normand" in s2, so per the master flag **"Normie" does not appear anywhere in Ch 08.** In s3 "Coffee's on" belongs to **the man on the end stool**, not to a name.
7. **The author's contrast rule (zero tolerance) against the outline's wording.**

9. **Retired, and not used anywhere in this chapter:** Ellen's list collapsing into *and*s; "he did not raise his voice" (and "raise" near "voice" in any form); "the voice she kept for the minutes"; the second chair in any form (it stays off the page); the Worcester page; the receipt behind it; Ray's shirt pocket as a filing place.

### Scene 1

**Setting:** Pine Ridge Lanes, Halsey Falls, Friday evening, February 5. Sixteen lanes, pop music over the speakers, overhead scoring screens nobody in this match will look at, and the "cosmic" carpet. **Sensory anchor:** the warmth of a building somebody has the money to heat, which Ray resents enjoying (one clause). Pizza and the carpet glue that never went away. Then the parking lot, cold, and the dome light in Ray's truck.

**Characters:** Ray, Gil Arsenault, Denise, Dale Hutchins, Hollis (silent until the lot), Gil's anchor (unnamed walk-on; a lineman half Ray's age), the Legion's captain (silent), a few of the Highway crew (a room).

**Want:**
- **Ray** wants Dale's crew and the Legion back on Ellen's lanes before Linda's night, and he wants it done without her asking. The reader learns this at the terms, then at **"That one's mine."** In the way: Gil's anchor, Gil's house ball, Ray's own first string, and Dale's pride.
- **Dale** wants a way home that doesn't make his walkout wrong. A match gives him one. The reader learns it at "And Linda's cup?"
- **Denise** wants to be at Ray's elbow without saying sorry. The reader learns it at the score sheet she brought from Kettle Lanes.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Open on Gil's hand.** Gil, in the good wool overcoat with a bowling glove he has no need for, holds out a house ball. **"House rules, Ray. Three strings, total pins, my house balls. You'll forgive the house balls; they're older than you."** (The laugh, early.) Ray answers in his mock-solemn register or takes the ball without answering. Writer's choice; one line at most.
2. **The terms, said by Gil as referee, short.** If Ray takes it, the Highway Department and the Legion go home to Kettle Lanes on Tuesday at the season rate and bowl the Memorial. If Gil's man takes it, Ray pays their lineage here the rest of the season and bowls for Pine Ridge. Dale is on the settee with his coat on. **Dale: "And Linda's cup?"** **Ray: "You won't pay for it twice. That one's mine."** Dale looks at him a beat. "Bowl, then." *(No figure. No reference to the Open's entry beyond "pay for it twice.")*
3. **Denise is already at the scorer's table.** She has a Kettle Lanes paper score sheet and a pencil from Ellen's cigar box, because she doesn't keep score on a screen. Nobody explains why she's there, and nobody mentions Tuesday. Ray takes out his pocketknife, sharpens her pencil to a point without asking, and hands it back. That is the whole mend.
4. **Ray starts badly.** The house ball is lighter in the hand than his own and rides wrong. His first ball takes three. **"Holy mackerel."** (Once in the book.) Gil's anchor is clean and young and doesn't talk.
5. **Gil, scrupulous (one sentence, optional, and it decides nothing).** In the second string Gil calls a foul on his own anchor's toe, on a line only Gil was watching. The anchor looks at him. Gil doesn't look back. Ray is still behind going into the third.
6. **Denise at Gil, within Ray's hearing:** **"He's a hundred-and-four average with a hundred-and-forty heart. Don't tell him I said either."** Gil, to Denise: **"Mrs. Lavoie. I watched you pick up a four-horse with your third ball in twenty fifteen. I still think about it."** Denise's pencil stops for one second over the sheet, then goes on. No line from her. *(Book 2 seed: shown, not said.)*
7. **Interior passage 1: the tenth.** Ray needs every pin on the deck. First ball: the front goes down, and a pin lies across the deck in front of three still standing on the left. Second ball: one more falls. Then the third ball in his hand, small and heavier than it looks. Pop music. The lane is longer than it looks. He rolls it into the wood lying on the deck, not at the standing pins, and the wood takes them. **Nobody names a rule, and the narration doesn't either.** Long sentences, landing on a short one. Ray's body, the ball, the sound of the pins (not wooden spoons; that image is spent). No memory of the lesson and no Ellen in his head. The reader supplies Ch 04.
8. Denise's pencil is moving before the last pin lies down. Gil keys his wireless mic and gives it to the visiting side in two words. **Dale: "Tuesday, then."** He picks up his bag.
9. **The lot.** Cold. Hollis, who has said nothing all night, stands at Ray's truck. **"You read it yet?"** Ray: "No." Hollis takes a folded sheet out of his waterproof notebook and holds it out. Committee copy. Ray reads it in the dome light, himself.
10. **The letter, quoted, only what is new** (World rule 8; the reader has heard the confession). Two sentences and the date. Model:
    > *The silence and the name are mine. I ask the secretary to strike "That's mine" from the minutes.*
    
    One clause of longing that isn't about the plot: her printing is the same hand he watched write his answer down at his counter in December. Then the foot of the page.

**Subtext:** He wins her teams back for a woman he isn't speaking to, and makes a promise about her Open he has no right to make, which is the old habit of speaking for other people. Then he finds out she had already put herself in writing two days before, when she couldn't have known he would win anything.

**Texture budget:** Two. The side-match format (three strings, total pins, house balls), which shows Gil's fairness and puts Ray on borrowed equipment. The tenth won on the wood, which shows Ray playing what's left without anyone saying so.

**Point of view:** Ray. Comic and dry in the room, and slow and physical in the tenth.

**Ends on:** **Cut.** The date at the foot of the page, in her printing, under the dome light: *the third of February.* No reaction from Ray, no line from Hollis, and the truck not started.

### Scene 2

**Setting:** Arlene's apartment above Ambrose Pharmacy, Saturday, February 6, about noon. Up the steep side stair. Boxes labeled for The Pines in green-ledger script, and photographs face down for packing. **Sensory anchor:** wintergreen and cough drops rising from the pharmacy under the smell of the ham. The police scanner low on the kitchen counter, and the radiator clanking.

**Characters:** Ray, Arlene.

**Want:**
- **Ray** wants off the March first contract, so that he stops being the man waiting at the bottom of Ellen's drive. First, though, he wants the answer he has been angry about since the sale: why Arlene chose her. The reader learns the second want at his first line and the first at "I want off the March paper." In the way: Arlene's bed.
- **Arlene** wants to be paid on March first, to the dollar, and the alley alive after her. She also wants Raymond to hear exactly what he's asking.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Open on Arlene's hands.** She puts a plate of ham in front of him without asking whether he's hungry. The bible pleasure: being fed by someone who doesn't ask. He eats.
2. The exchange runs as written below. The scanner can carry one dry Arlene comment on a call, timed to break a silence. Optional, one line, and never Hollis.
3. **Interior passage 2: Ellen's signature.** After Ray's ask and before Arlene answers it, Arlene has her copy of the contract out of a Pines box before he's finished, and she lays it flat on the oilcloth. Ray's hand on it. Ellen's signature, printed more than written, every letter separate, from the morning of the first of December, six hours before he knocked. Her voice in the doorway in one clause: *By me.* His own signature under it. One sentence about her hand that isn't about the plot.
4. The cost, the reason and the condition, as written.
5. No paper is signed today. Arlene's lawyer will draw it; that's said in Ray's ask and nowhere else.

### Scene 4

**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, the front door, then the trophy case and the counter, Tuesday, February 16, after league. The lot has emptied and the lanes are dark. **Sensory anchor:** the front door's glass rattling in its frame when he raps (Ch 01), and wet wool and road salt in the vestibule. Inside, the light over the counter and the snack bar's tick as it cools.

**Characters:** Ray, Ellen, Hollis (by phone).

**Want:**
- **Ray** wants three things, in his order. He wants to name his own wrong first. He wants to keep his word to Dale by asking her for Linda's night back, which is the first time in the book he asks her for something to her face. And he wants her signature on the release, so that he is out of the way. The reader learns the second want at "I told Dale at Pine Ridge…" and the third at "I'm asking you to sign it."
- **Ellen** wants him inside, and she wants to give him the key back. The reader learns it when the door opens before his third rap, and again when the key comes out of her vest. What's in the way: a key handed over is a briefing, and he is waiting to be asked.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Open on Ray's hand at the door.** He raps twice, and the glass rattles in its frame. The door opens before the third. She was already on the other side of it. **The longing.** (Never "knuckle" and "glass" in one sentence.) One line somewhere in the first beats, no gloss: **he had called the sign shop in Laconia at noon and ordered a K.**
2. She stands back and he comes in. Nobody says "come in." **He goes right instead of left, to the trophy case.**
3. **Interior passage 3: locker fourteen.** His key ring comes out, heavy. The dead key with its orange tag swings. There's a gap on the ring where hers was. The small locker key, which he finds by feel. Linda's ball has been in fourteen since the spring she died; one clause, no years. He fits the key into the brass padlock and **doesn't turn it**. He takes it out and puts the ring away. Ellen watches from the counter and says nothing. Then he goes to the counter. *This has to carry for a reader who hasn't thought about the locker since the storm.* Make the padlock, the key and his hands specific.
4. **He names his wrong.** He's standing, and so is she (she stands to take bad news). Ray, short, plain: **"What I said about the radio. I picked it because I knew it'd land."** Ellen, exact and true, no absolution: **"It did."**
5. **He gives her the date** (flag 3). Ray-shape, with the important word last: *"You stood at my counter and said I'd no reason to think you'd have come anyway, and Hollis had already shown me what you wrote Verna, and it's dated the third."* Her hand on the counter, one beat. She has no answer in order, and that is all the narration says, through her hand.
6. **The condition**, Ray's long chain: Linda's name comes off the Open. The league bowls its own Memorial at two on the last Saturday, on all ten lanes, the way it always has. Her Open gets the morning and the evening, and a cup of its own. Then: **"I told Dale at Pine Ridge his crew wouldn't pay for Linda's cup twice. I said that one was mine."** That's the pressure: his word, in her hands.
7. **Hollis by phone.** She picks up the counter phone and dials. Ellen asking a plain question is a small move toward Ch 09, and the scene doesn't mark it. Hollis is up; he plows at night. She asks whether the committee voted on Linda's name. **Hollis: "We voted on an Open. Nobody voted on a name."** One more flat line from him is allowed (the derby's people want to bowl, mornings and evenings suit them). She hangs up.
8. **She accepts and pays. The chapter's money fact, Ellen to Ray, no figure:** "Agreed." Then: **"Nine teams wrote Linda's name on their entry form. In the morning I will call all nine and offer their money back, and all nine will take it. After that I have no room at all."** She may add, once: "That was my room." Ray hears the cost and doesn't offer anything. His hand passes the blank envelope inside his jacket and leaves it there.
9. **The release.** He takes a single page out of his jacket. **One line, no legalese:** Arlene's lawyer had taken a week and a half to get it onto one page, which let all three of them go from the March first sale. Arlene's signature is on it, and his, with a line left. **"I'm asking you to sign it."** (Ray asks once, never twice.)
10. **Ellen: "You have asked an eighty-four-year-old woman to bet her bed on me."** **Ray: "I know what I asked."** Silence. Neither of them forgives it, and the page doesn't soften it.
11. **She says what her signature does, then her own reason, then signs.** "If I miss, Arlene loses her place, and it will be my name that put it there." Then the chapter's one Ellen contraction: **"If I ever ask you to stand beside me, I won't have you standing at the bottom of my drive as well."** She signs. Ray watches her hand on the pen, slow and real-time, the same hand that signed Arlene's copy. One sensory detail, and no gloss.
12. **The turn, said.** Ray: **"I'll call Concord at nine and let it go. They'd want ninety days to write it again."** Ellen: **"There are thirteen."**
13. She takes his key out of her vest pocket, where it has been since the night he gave it back, and holds it out across the counter.
14. **Ray: "Not yet."**
15. **He goes.** He crosses the vestibule, and at the outer door he looks back through the glass. **She is still holding out the key.** End.

**Subtext:** He comes to her door the way he came on the first night, with no key this time. He says the cruel thing was chosen, asks her for Linda's night, and asks her to make his word good. Then he asks her to tie Arlene's bed to her number so that he can stop being the man who profits if she fails. She pays for all of it out loud and signs for a reason that is a promise to ask. Then she hands him the key instead of asking, and he waits for the asking.

**Texture budget:** Two. The release, one line: it shows that nothing stands between her and March first except her own winter. The ninety days against thirteen: it shows he has made it impossible to be her buyer, in a figure she computes before he can.

**Point of view:** Ray. Tender register for the knock, the locker and her hand on the pen. Plain and short for his wrong. Long and chained for the condition.

**Ends on:** **Question.** "Not yet." He's at the vestibule door, and she's still holding out the key. No sign, no headlights, no line after.

**Word target:** 1,050   **Word floor:** 850 (the repair. Play it from the knock to "Not yet" in real time, with no summary of the phone call, the reading of the release or the signing.)
