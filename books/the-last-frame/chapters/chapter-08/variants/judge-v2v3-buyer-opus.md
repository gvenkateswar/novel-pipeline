Then the league room goes quiet one lane at a time while he says it again. Those two scenes held me tighter than anything in B, even though B's trip to Arlene's is the nicer visit.

3. **Where A lost me:** around 75%, on Presidents' Day morning, when the talk turns into lines with nobody's name on them and I had to scroll back up to work out who was speaking. "He heard the chair go back on the dry floor, the second of February, and his own voice over the top of it." Then at Arlene's, "With every lane of the Open full, I clear by six hundred. One storm takes that," and from there it's the two of them doing a deal at a kitchen table. Nobody reacts to anything until the signing. I read it quickly to get to the stair, and the key on his glove brought me back.

4. **Where B lost me:** around 77%, the same deal at Arlene's: "If I sign this and I miss, it's a bank sale. You wait for your money and go back on the list, the condo man gets the lot for the land..." B kept me better than A did here, because Arlene polishes her glasses, opens the drawer full of string and rubber bands, and calls him "Raymond," and Ellen's mouth nearly smiles. But it's still money talk, and it goes on longer. Before that, the apology at the door comes out as one solid paragraph, so I read it straight through in a couple of seconds and it was over before I could feel it.

5. **Confidence: low.** The dialogue in the two is nearly word for word the same. A has the better romance scenes (the door, the league room) and B has the better ending stretch (Presidents' Day, Arlene). I picked A because the door scene is the reason I'd keep reading.
