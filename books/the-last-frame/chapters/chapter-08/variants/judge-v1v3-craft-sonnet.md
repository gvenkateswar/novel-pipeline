A = v3.md, B = v1.md

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** In the two scenes that carry the chapter, the glass and the settees, A lets her body answer him line by line ("That one she took at the mouth. It pressed shut and held." and "A ball came up the Highway crew's return and sat there, and nobody reached for it."), so the couple is in the room and the lane goes quiet. B says the same thing in one block and tells you the room is quiet.

3. **Where A lost me:** ~88%, "He was all the way out of her paper now. It was in his inside pocket, and at six tomorrow he would lay it on the store counter beside Danny's envelope." That is the ledger coming back after the glass and the Open. The Laconia order after it ("ordered the K from the shop in Laconia at the quote he'd given her... date the bill the first of March") is a second one. I also winced once earlier, at ~30%: "He had put the worst of it first, so he couldn't stop short of it." It explains a move the dialogue already makes.

4. **Where B lost me:** ~2%, the opening paragraph's single long run: "Hollis had phoned the store at five, on a Thursday in February with first ball an hour and a half off... and Ray had left the truck round by the dumpster, where nobody would read anything into it." It's seven facts before anyone speaks. Second, ~50%: "this room would have her two in the morning by the weekend, and the Bench would have it with their coffee by Monday, and every one of them would know where to aim." The line is good, but it spells out why he can't say the quiet thing, so I skimmed. B's apology (~28%) is one paragraph of speech, "I said you sounded fine on the radio that night. You'd told me... I'm sorry." Nobody talks in a single breath like that, and Ellen's reply is another block. That is where B went flat for me.

   B has the funnier narrator: "though he'd had some hopes", the radiator "had heard the news and had views on it", "one letter of a sign." I'd keep those. A cut most of that wit for staging.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two are about 90% the same text, so they differ by pacing and paragraphing. A's broken-up, one-line-paragraph style is a mannerism that could wear on a different reader, and B's jokes are better.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same chapter, same dialogue, same Hollis, the same invoice joke and the same "Not yet" ending. They differ only in how the beats are paced.
