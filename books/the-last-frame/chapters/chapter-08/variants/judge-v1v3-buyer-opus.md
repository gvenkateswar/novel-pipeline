1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** In A the apology at the door comes one line at a time, and I get her face between the lines: her chin coming up, her mouth pressing shut, her hand still on the glass while she makes her own confession. That's the scene I'd stay up for. B hands me the same words as two speeches, and the scene is over before I've felt it.

3. **Where A lost me:** At the Presidents' Day paper, about 68%. "He heard the chair go back on the dry floor, the second of February, and his own voice over the top of it." I had to read that twice to work out we'd jumped back a couple of weeks. Then at Arlene's table, about 78%, my thumb started moving: "If I sign this and I miss, it's a bank sale. You wait for your money and go back on the list, the condo man gets the lot for the land, and the house money my daughter let me spend goes with it." That's a loan officer talking, not a couple. The key on the stair brought me back.

4. **Where B lost me:** At the same table, a little earlier, about 75%: "With every lane of the Open full, I clear by six hundred. One storm takes that." From there to the stair it's money and signatures. The radiator that "had heard the news and had views on it" was the only thing keeping me in that room. B also nearly lost me at the door, about 40%, and I wasn't bored. It went by too fast. "He had put it in order in the truck, and he said it in that order," and then the whole apology comes in one paragraph. I read it in one breath and wanted to be made to wait.

5. **Confidence: medium.** B is warmer in Ray's head. "Though he'd had some hopes," the fries "they'd be sorry about," the gloves "gone shiny at the fingertips from a month of her hands in them," and her saying "Ray." on the stair are all better than A's versions of the same moments. If the door scene weren't the heart of the chapter, I'd pick B.
