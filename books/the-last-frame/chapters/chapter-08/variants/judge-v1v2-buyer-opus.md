1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** It's the same night and the same words. But in B, Ray is funny in his own head ("though he'd had some hopes," the door closer he leaves alone, the radiator that "had heard the news and had views on it"), and the stair ends with her saying "Ray." and his gloves gone shiny from a month of her hands. That's the moment I'd turn the light off smiling about, and A's version of it left me working out who said what.

3. **Where A lost me:** At Arlene's table, about 72% in: "With every lane of the Open full, I clear by six hundred. One storm takes that." From there to the signing it's money and a bank sale and the condo man. A slows it down even more with business, Arlene polishing her glasses and opening the drawer of "string, rubber bands, a ledger pencil," so the ledger gets more room, not less. I got my attention back on the stair, but then "She didn't say his name. He turned anyway" and an unattributed "Not yet." made me go back and check whether she said it or he did.

4. **Where B lost me:** The same table, about 78% in: "If I sign this and I miss, it's a bank sale. You wait for your money and go back on the list, the condo man gets the lot for the land..." My thumb went faster there too. B gets through it quicker, with short back-and-forth lines and no stage business, and the stair pulled me back in. I also skimmed a little in the first paragraph at "a carbon worn to a stub and scored across its face," but he's on his knees fixing her machine while she says Linda's name down the lane, so I stayed.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The dialogue is almost word for word the same in both. A has better pauses in places, like the fogged glass that "faded, and came back, and faded, and came back," and Ellen looking at him "and kept looking, and said nothing" when he gives up the bank sale. I still picked B for the warmth and the ending.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Both have the same scenes, the same lines of dialogue and the same voice. The difference is how much Ray jokes to himself.
