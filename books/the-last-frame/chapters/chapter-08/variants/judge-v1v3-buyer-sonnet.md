1. Winner: A

2. Why: At the glass door I got to watch her take each piece of his apology (the chin coming up, the mouth pressing shut, "Understood.") instead of hearing him read it all out in one breath, so it felt like two people in a room.

3. Where A lost me: the start of the Monday scene, about 28%: "at five he had turned the burner off under the pot on School Street, which made him late for his own supper at a quarter past five, and so far he hadn't billed her for a single supper, though he had the line worked out." I read it twice and still didn't know what the joke was. Later my thumb sped up at about 90%: "He was all the way out of her paper now. It was in his inside pocket, and at six tomorrow he would lay it on the store counter beside Danny's envelope." That's paper and errands again, with the couple out of the room.

4. Where B lost me: the long paragraph in the league-night scene, about 50%: "The words he'd said to her in the dark were about a night she had told him by flashlight with a pin in her hands, and that night was hers. If he said them out here, this room would have her two in the morning by the weekend, and the Bench would have it with their coffee by Monday..." That is a man thinking through the logistics of an apology while forty people wait. B also gives the whole apology as one block ("I said you sounded fine on the radio that night. You'd told me... And I walked out... And you were telling me... I'm sorry.") and her answer as another. It sounds recited, and she has nothing to do while he talks.

5. Confidence: medium. The two chapters are almost the same text. B is a bit funnier and clearer in places, for example the radiator "had views on it" and "which was about what he'd have expected of paper". A's staggered glass-door scene is what I'd stay up for, so I'd pick A on that.

6. Same-author check: Yes. Same scenes and same lines, with the same Verna ruling, "Holy mackerel" and "Not yet". They differ mostly in how the lines are broken up.
