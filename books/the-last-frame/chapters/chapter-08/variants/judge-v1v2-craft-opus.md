A = v1.md, B = v2.md

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** The dialogue is almost word for word the same in both. B gives the money scenes room for people to breathe and lets Ray think between the lines, so Arlene's table and the release read like two women and a man pushing on each other. In A those same lines come at me like minutes from a meeting.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 80%, at Arlene's table. "If I sign this and I miss, it's a bank sale. You wait for your money and go back on the list, the condo man gets the lot for the land, and the house money my daughter let me spend goes with it." In A this follows the six-hundred line with nothing in between, and then Ray's offer, Arlene's answer, Kate and the drawer come one line after another with no faces attached. I skimmed down to the stair. It doesn't help that "his hand on your drawer" points at a drawer A never shows us. The jokes I did love in A come earlier: the door closer he leaves alone even though he has a screwdriver in his jacket, the radiator that "had views on it," the "poor time to bring up one letter of a sign." A has the funnier narrator. Its narrator just goes quiet right when the paperwork starts.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 88%, the signing. "Ellen took the paper out of her coat and opened it on the oilcloth and signed it standing, with her own pen, on the second line, her name square and plain under his. She turned it round and held out the pen. Arlene took it and signed last, once, sitting, the pen going slow across the third line..." Second line, third line, pen capped, pen handed back: it's the procedure done out in full, and my eyes sped up. Just before it there's a run of single-beat stage directions ("Arlene gave that one nod." / "Ray had nothing to put against that, and didn't try.") that starts to tick like a metronome. Even so, B had already won me over in the release scene with "she had put the if on the end of it herself, where he would have left it off," and with Arlene calling him by his whole name "never once to let him off anything."

5. **Confidence: medium.** Most of the text is shared, and A's best asides are better than anything B has to replace them. I'm picking B for the back half, and the back half is where a reader decides whether to keep going.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The dialogue is the same nearly line for line, and so is the clipped, Yankee, comma-chained narration.
