# Reader notes: Chapter 10, "Ice-Out"

**Draft read:** `chapters/chapter-10/final.md`, Oct 29, 3,398 words (the tournament winner), as named by the orchestrator. For context I read `chapters/chapter-01/final.md` through `chapters/chapter-09/final.md` in order, and nothing else in the folder.

---

## 1. Pull score: 5 / 10

The scene behind lane seven and the locker are the best ending material in the book. The rest of the chapter is a 3,400-word wind-down that ticks off every plant in turn, and none of it is in doubt. Ch 09 settled the plot. Kate said "Ten", so I knew before this chapter started that the money would be found, the K would light and the key would go back. Inside this chapter the only open question was who would win the Memorial, and the book settles that in two sentences.

**Recommend the book: No.** I'd hand it to one kind of reader, someone who likes Haruf or Strout, wants a quiet book and will put up with bylaws. If a friend asked me for a romance to read next, I wouldn't name this one. The middle of the book, from Ch 04 to Ch 06, is lineage rates, bylaw clauses, invoices, a vote and a cashier's check. That's where a reader with other books on her phone puts it down.

**Buy the next book by this author: No, not blind.** I'd download the sample, because the sentences are good and Ray's voice is real. On the evidence of this book I wouldn't pay for it on the author's name alone.

**Where I'd most likely have quit the book for good:** Ch 04, in the office scene with the lineage notice ("First... Second... Third... I asked Verna for them the day after I closed"). A close second is the vote in Ch 05, where Verna reads the bylaws aloud.

## 2. Where I got bored (thumb sped up)

- **The opening paragraphs.** "Four inches of slush on top at the public access, and water up through every hole I cut. Derby's off for Sunday." (paragraphs 2–3). The derby doesn't matter any more. I'm waiting for Kate's call, and the chapter gives me pencil shavings and a fishing report first.
- **The Memorial, which is the event the whole book was built toward.** "He bowled ordinary strings with it." (middle of scene 2). Three strings and a championship go by in one sentence. "It came down to lanes five and six, Lakeview Diner against Gagnon and Son" is the first I knew anything was close. I had no idea Denise bowled for the diner until she picked up the cup. In Ch 08 a single spare got a full page. Linda's Memorial gets a summary, so I felt nothing about the result.
- **The bank.** From "Down the line, cellophane." to "The underwriter has never met you." (start of scene 4) my thumb was moving fast. A notary problem shows up on the last afternoon and is solved by walking two blocks. Then "Eighty-eight hundred, out of your savings and onto Arlene's note" asks me to remember the eight-to-one ratio from Ch 07 and the $1,715 from Ch 09 and do the subtraction myself. I didn't. I skimmed until "It's gone."
- **The sign hardware.** "seated its electrode in the housing, with the disconnect open on the pole below him and the shop's sheet in his jacket pocket." This is procedure at the exact moment I wanted the picture.

## 3. Where I got confused

- **Kate's call: "Like this."** Who says it? It's Kate, I think, meaning "call me first, like this," but it sits by itself after a sentence about Ray holding the knife, and I read it as Ray at first.
- **"Is he the one who followed you out as far as the plow had been?" / "The call ended."** Did Ellen answer, and who hung up? It's a nice button, but I lost a beat trying to work out whether I'd missed a line.
- **"I said I'd sleep on it."** Ch 09 ended on "Ten." Kate never said she'd sleep on it on the page, so for a moment I thought I'd skipped something.
- **"You're in the other chair."** I had to read it twice. I didn't know where Ray was until "The Bench sat on their stools." The phone is lying on a chair at the bank, Ray is at his store listening through it, and the chapter cuts straight there from the iron step with no anchor.
- **"The committee had sat at two."** Which committee? I worked out that it was the bank's, and that Judy is the Aunt Judy from Ch 09, but only after a pause.
- **"he put it in Danny's hand ... Danny didn't open the envelope."** This is the $48,000 cashier's check that has ridden in his jacket since Ch 06. What does handing it to Danny mean? Is it going back into the store? Is it Danny's now? Did Ray just answer "buying in or getting out of the way"? The book has carried this envelope for four chapters and lets it go without a word, and I don't know what happened.
- **"on her blotter, in her hand, a slush depth a shade under the one Hollis had given the radio."** No idea. Is Judy in the derby? Did she have a bet on it? It reads like a reference to a scene I never saw.
- **The gooseneck announcement.** "the gooseneck was already bent across to him. He leaned to it." On a first pass I wasn't sure whether Ray or Ellen was speaking. It's Ray, giving Linda's name to the Open. That's a big decision, and it's over in one line.
- **"Twice behind lane seven and once under the sign."** I had to stop and count the kisses (Ch 03, Ch 05, now) to check her count. It's correct. A listener won't stop to check.

## 4. Where I stopped believing

- **The notary snag.** "The paper you signed in February ... was never notarized, so you sign it again at this desk in front of me before four, or the wire doesn't go." It's a ticking clock built for the last scene. It costs nobody anything, and it's gone in a paragraph. Arlene's lawyer drew the release, so why did nobody catch this in three weeks? I could see the author's hand.
- **Gil turns up with the spring-doubles flyer the moment Denise wins.** "Mrs. Lavoie." He held out the spring-doubles flyer. That's staged. Gil came over from Halsey Falls with a flyer in his coat, ready for exactly this.
- **Every side character gets a bow.** Hollis says "Committee never voted on a name," then writes it down. Dale "unzipped his town jacket and sat back to watch." Arlene says "You two done sitting in cars?" Judy says "Is that Ray Gagnon on my chair?" Each is fine alone. Together it feels like curtain calls.
- **The neon.** In Ch 04 Ellen says, "I hire out what I am not qualified for... You are a pinsetter man. That is a neon tube." In Ch 10 Ray is up the ladder seating the electrode while she holds the foot of it, and the page never mentions her rule. I'll accept that she's softened, but the book doesn't show her choosing it.
- **Kate's yes.** "Then do it." Her mother is spending nearly all her savings on a losing bowling alley, and Kate puts up no resistance beyond "call me first." The fourth-grade memory in Ch 09 sets up some of this, but the risk the book has been building since Ch 02 ("I swear") disappears with a car door slamming.
- **Choices under pressure.** Ellen's choice to use her own money follows from what she knows, and the safer option (Ray's $48k) was closed off in Ch 07 with "I'll never know which of us carried it." That holds. Ray walking to the bank is the only option he has. I have no complaint about the logic. My complaint is that nothing in this chapter is under pressure for more than a paragraph.

## 5. Where it sounded like a machine

- **Every scene ends on a closing image, and you can feel them coming:**
  - "The call ended, and the screen went dark between them."
  - "...with Ellen watching from the counter and his hand still on it."
  - "...and lay flat on it, and came away with nothing in it."
  - "...turned toward The Pines."
  - "Both words."
- **The chapter is assembled from callbacks:** "That's not asking." / "Throw her, Mrs. Daly. He waited to be corrected." / "She licked her thumb and counted it." / "Kettle Lanes, this is Gagnon. Request." / "gave it a quarter-turn until it sat" / "You two done sitting in cars?" / the minute hand "most of the way round to the top." I caught every one, and that's the problem: by the fourth I was ticking them off instead of feeling them. It reads like a ledger closing.
- **Two hands on the key:** "He put his hand over hers on the key. It went under the two of them, stiff and then sudden, and the shackle dropped." This is a symbol in the costume of a stuck lock.
- **Generic kiss prose:** "Then Ellen was kissing him, and he was kissing her back." / "She tasted of the urn's coffee, burnt and sweet."
- **The feeling explained after the gesture:** "Ellen took her time. He had never once seen her take it over anything, and she took it now, slow and plain."
- **The book-wide tic of silence as a response.** In this chapter alone: "said nothing about Manchester," "Denise sat down on the settee and said nothing at all" (the same phrase as Ch 03's "said nothing at all, which was rarer than the money"), "gave it to him without a word," "He didn't answer it," "He kept his hand on his knee," "Danny didn't open the envelope." Characters in this book answer by not answering, and by Ch 10 it's a house style.
- **The ticking motif:** the case glass ticked, the motors ticking as they cooled, "a small steel tick," "the watch went on," Kate's turn signal ticked. Five in one chapter.

## 6. What I think is going on (prediction tracker, committed)

After Ch 09 I expected the following. Kate says yes, and Ellen covers the gap from her own savings. The Memorial is bowled Sunday and Denise finally wins the cup. Locker fourteen gets opened and Linda's things come out. Ray fits the new K and Ellen gives his key back by asking him to take it, since she "doesn't ask." The third item from the storm turns out to be "I love you." Arlene gets her bed at The Pines. **All of it came true.** The surprises were small. Ray loses his last box and the Memorial by two pins. Linda's score sheet shows a poor middle string. Linda's ball goes onto the house rack. Ray gives Linda's name to the Open from next year. He returns Arlene's 1996 key. And there's the notary snag, which is the only real surprise and the cheapest one. Where it ends: Ellen owns Kettle Lanes outright with almost nothing left in the bank. Ray is her D-side, not a partner, with a key and no stake. Denise has the cup and maybe Gil as a doubles partner. The alley is a living place and no longer Linda's museum. There's no secret left. The book had no mystery beyond the clause Ray didn't hear, and that came out in Ch 06–07. An ending I could predict this completely is a finding in its own right.

## 8. The listener pass

- **Kate's call:** speaker tags are thin and the sound effects stand between lines: "Keys came out of an ignition." / "Her car door slammed." Aloud, "Like this." and "Then do it." could belong to anyone. Two women, one phone and a man in the room, and I lost who was speaking twice.
- **Kate and Denise sound alike.** Both talk in breathless run-ons ("it's medieval" / "so don't you go bowling it sentimental on me today, because I'll beat you regardless and then I'll have to feel bad about it"). Heard through the same narrator voice, they blur together.
- **The bank scene can't be followed by ear.** Ray's store, the Bench, a phone on a chair, Judy, cellophane, "the drive-up tube came down with a thunk," and then a walk up Main Street. A listener won't know where the camera is until "Judy's glass office." "Down the line, cellophane." read aloud is a non-sentence.
- **Numbers:** "Eighty-eight hundred" is fine to hear, but the arithmetic behind it (eight to one, $1,715, the snack bar) is invisible by ear.
- **Repeated words:**
  - "cord" five times inside one kiss paragraph and its neighbours ("the cord stopped her short," "the cord biting," "over the cord," "the cord stayed where it was," "the cord drew tight").
  - "key" all through the chapter.
  - "ring" / "split end" / "thumbnail" / "a quarter turn at a time": the key-on-ring choreography happens twice in this chapter (off for the locker key, on for her key) and once in Ch 07. Aloud it's the same sentence three times.
  - "iron" three times in the lane-seven scene ("iron step," "cold iron," "came up off the iron").
  - "gooseneck" three times.
- **Sentence shapes:** long "and... and... and" runs keep repeating ("He opened the split end of the ring with his thumbnail and worked the key on a quarter turn at a time, into the place Arlene's had hung thirty years, and she watched every turn."). Read by a voice, the chapter falls into one rhythm.
- **First paragraph:** "The pocketknife took the cedar off in long curls" doesn't say who is holding the knife until the second paragraph. A listener starts the last chapter not knowing whose head they're in.
- **"Lakeview Diner"** arrives aloud as a team name with no face attached.

## 9. The romance

**Yes, this chapter made me want them together, and twice it made me laugh.**

- "And one you left off. I've loved you since about the first night, when you kept me knocking at your door to finish your tape." "About" is Ray's hedge, and the line rewrites Ch 01's opening image from his side. It landed.
- The readback: "Third item," he said, and heard her words come out in his voice, and went on with it. "You love me." This is the right move for these two people, and nobody else in the book could do it.
- The reading-glasses cord catching on his zipper: "Ray laughed against her mouth." This is the only clumsy, physical, funny touch in the book's three kisses, and it's better for it.
- I laughed at Kate: "I'd have said no." / "You weren't asked."
- I laughed at Arlene: "That tag's about worn through." / "So am I."
- "Twice behind lane seven and once under the sign. I've kept count." Good, it's her. I had to count to check.

## 10. What I want to know next

**Nothing is pulling me forward.** The book closes every door it opened, so I don't want a next chapter and nothing here makes me want a next book. The only loose threads are ones I didn't care about until they were dropped. What did Ray's handing the $48,000 envelope to Danny mean? Will Denise bowl the spring doubles with Gil? Will the Highway crew stay past this season? None of those would make me buy a sequel. If the author plans a series, nothing in this chapter plants it.

## 11. Best moment

> It was the last Tuesday Linda had bowled. Three strings in her own hand, and none of the three anything anybody would put in a frame. She'd had a poor box in the middle string and written it down as careful as the good ones.

It's the only payoff in the chapter I didn't see coming, and it does the book's argument (a building that is used, not preserved) in three sentences, without a callback. Close second: "I've loved you since about the first night, when you kept me knocking at your door to finish your tape."

## 12. The last line

> "Both words."

**It works, and it's the right size for Ellen: clipped, exact, a report and not a feeling.** It needs the listener to remember "Ettle Lanes" from Ch 01. Most will, because the dead K comes back in nearly every chapter. My objection is that I knew it was coming. The K was ordered in Ch 08, so the last scene is one switch being thrown, and the last line confirms something I already knew. It's a neat line ending a book of neat lines. It closes the book, and it didn't move me.
