# Judge report: Ch 10, v2 (A) vs v3 (B)

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A gave the two moments I'd waited nine chapters for, the daughter letting her go and Ellen saying it on the iron step, enough room to land, so I put the phone down with my throat tight. B got through the same things a beat too fast, and I had to reread the daughter's call to work out who said what.

3. **Where A lost me:** Briefly, at the bank, about 71% of the way through: "Your contract with Arlene is on record at the county, and the release from February was never notarized, so title won't insure over it." It's paperwork, and my eyes slid until Arlene showed up on the sidewalk counting her key. "Is that Ray Gagnon on my chair?" / "In it." pulled me straight back. Nowhere else.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 10%, in the call: "which for me is a— It doesn't matter." The ticking stopped. "Then do it." It came so quick that I wasn't sure Kate had actually let her go. In A she says "I did the subtraction anyway. Without the dollars," and I felt it. My thumb also sped up at about 33%, at "Hollis's golf pencil had him back in at anchor on the Gagnon and Son sheet," which is a scoresheet line. At about 69% the same notarized-release passage as A lost me again. On the step, B puts "The night of the storm… It is now. Third. I love you." into one paragraph. A makes me wait through the watch ticking and "a heading with nothing yet under it," and the waiting is the whole pleasure. I'll give B its kiss, though. "Her mouth was cold from the pit, and warmed" and his arm going round her inside the open jacket are warmer than anything in A's kiss, and Ray answering Denise ("you've watched me bowl my own thirty years") is a line I missed in A.

5. **Confidence: medium-low.** The scenes, the order and most of the sentences are the same. A wins on the call and the declaration, which are the two places a woman reading this in bed is waiting for. B wins the kiss. If B had A's call, I'd flip.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes, same props and many identical lines, like two drafts of one chapter.
