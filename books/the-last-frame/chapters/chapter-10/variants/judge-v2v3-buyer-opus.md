A = v2.md, B = v3.md

1. **Winner:** A

2. **Why:** In the pit, A lets him stand in the quiet and listen to her breathe "until he was sure she had finished, because she had always given him every number whole." Then she laughs into his chin over the snagged cord. That's the scene I'd read in bed and turn the light back on for, and B rushes past it to get to the bank.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 10%, in the ping-pong of phone calls to Judy: "Ten tomorrow. They don't vote on a winter till it's over, and they vote on money that's arrived. Bring it in by nine and I'll hold it here till they vote." That's three calls about a hold and a wire, and my thumb moved until the trophy case. I came all the way back at the locker, on "his hand had the weight of it before the rest of him did."

4. **Where B lost me:** About 80%, the bank wait, which B stretches out longer than A does. Judy works "somebody's furnace loan by the look of it," then a whole paragraph begins "He laid out a no for himself, plainly, since nobody else in the room was going to. Arlene's note part paid…" That's a money scene sitting on top of another money scene, and I skimmed down to "Approved." Earlier, at about 5%, two Kate lines in a row ("If it goes in, I'm your bad winter." / "Yes," Kate said) made me read the phone call twice to work out who was talking. The bowling crowd at about 35%, one long sentence of "and… and… and…," looked like a list and I skimmed it.

5. **Confidence:** low. Most of the dialogue in the two is the same. B has better single lines ("folded the knife shut over it," "her mouth came up to his before he got there") and a lovelier ladder. A wins on the two scenes I'd care about, the locker and the pit, and its bank is shorter.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. It's the same scenes, mostly the same dialogue word for word, and the same habit of letting a small object (pencil, cord, key) stand in for feeling.
