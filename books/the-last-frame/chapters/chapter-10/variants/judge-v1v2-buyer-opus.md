A = v1.md, B = v2.md

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** The scene I'd been waiting the whole book for, the two of them behind lane seven, goes on longer in B, and I get to stand there with him while she breathes and the furnace knocks and she laughs into his collar. In A I'd barely got there before it was over.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 18%, in the call with Judy: "Your own money lowers the line slowly. It takes a good many of your dollars to buy down one of the winter's." Kate had just made me sit up, and then it was a loan officer and a committee and three phone calls back and forth. My thumb kept moving until the trophy case. It slipped again at about 91%, where the sign scene opens on "The shop's man had set the transformer that morning and taken out the jumper Arlene's sign man put across the K the winter it went". By then it's the last page, and I wanted Ellen on that ladder, not wiring.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 85%, the bank wait: "a note part-paid with her savings in it and no bed bought, a foreclosure sale in her own lot, the man from Concord taking it at land value". It's one long paragraph of him working out the money while she stands at the glass, and I skimmed it to get to the phone ringing. The same Judy paydown call is in B too, but it's shorter ("Ellen told her Kate's answer"), so I got through it faster.

5. **Confidence: medium.** Most of the words are the same in both. B wins on the pit scene, with the long quiet before "Third item," her laugh with no sound, and "now that he was let he meant to be thorough about it." It also wins on the last frame, where each ball cuts to a face: Ellen's basket stopped above the oil, the Ostrowski boy with his cap off. A has a few lines I'd miss ("He had done a good deal of waiting on this woman... and he had come to like it"), but they aren't enough to beat B.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** It's the same chapter with the same lines, cut a little differently.
