1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A has the lines I'd read aloud to someone, like "That's what twice is for," "He was fairly sure it was him," the sofa carried up a narrow stair, and "here was the same voice, polite and exact, with this in it." B is the cleaner chapter, but it's quieter, and in B Ray mostly watches where in A he gets the jokes.

3. **Where A lost me:** "She came, round the Memorial cup she had set on the scorer's table at five and six to stand for the final." (~30%). I read it twice and still couldn't tell where she was. Two lines later "Ellen stayed at the open locker, standing" and then she "stepped back" from the gooseneck at her counter, across the house. I lost track of the room for half a page. "Hollis had one of hers for the derby weekend" (~3%) tangles the same way. Earlier, the Judy paydown call (~17%) sags in both versions. The bank's "A no came to this. A note part paid..." (~85%) is an inventory, but it's short and I kept reading.

4. **Where B lost me:** "He laid out a no for himself, plainly, since nobody else in the room was going to." (~81%). The whole paragraph after it (the list behind a woman packed since August, the foreclosure sale, the man from Concord, Ray at the edge of the lot) is longer than A's version of the same list, and my thumb sped up. The ladder's "He made up the two ends in their boots, as the sheet said, and gave each one a push to be sure" (~92%) is a man with a machine, though "he knew where she was by the ladder alone" won me back right away. B also drops the line I most wanted at the count. "Same number twice." / "That's what twice is for." isn't there, and the scene reads as a man reading envelopes.

5. **Confidence: low.** The dialogue is nearly identical, and B is better at the ladder and has the better sentence for Ray swallowing advice ("he folded the knife shut over it"). A is better at the strike ("he waited for the sound, and none came"), the count, the I-love-you and the zipper. A wins on voice even though its sentences are rougher.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** The dialogue is word for word the same, and so are the gestures, the ending and the cadence. Only the narration's sentences differ.
