A = v2.md, B = v3.md

1. **Winner: A.**

2. **Why:** A slows down at the two moments that matter, the last ball and "I love you," and gives Ray the best sentences in either version ("because she had always given him every number whole, and he meant to give her this the same"; "he meant to be thorough about it"; "he was fairly sure it was him"). B shortens the bowling final to a scoreline and has the stronger bank scene, but by then I had already been won over by A.

3. **Where A lost me:** around 83%, at the bank wait, where the prose turns into a list of what's at stake: "There was time to think a no all the way through, so he did, plainly: a note part-paid with her savings in it and no bed bought, a foreclosure sale in her own lot, the man from Concord taking it at land value, and himself kept off that lot by his own word." That's a ledger behind a colon, and A's bank scene around it is thin ("Judy listened a long time and wrote nothing"). It got me back with the chair: "Ellen sat down when a thing was decided."

4. **Where B lost me:** around 25%, at the roll call of the final squeezed into one breath: "Then all ten lanes went at once for three strings, and once Ray saw Ellen at the fryer with her eyes shut for the length of one crash; he saw the Highway crew on ten with plow sand in their cuffs, in on the Saturday envelope Dale had laid back on her counter with the flap still stuck, and the Lewiston morning team out of the money by the second string, and Gil Arsenault..." My eyes started skimming there. B then rushes the climax ("His first ball brought the rack down to four"), so the two pins standing land as a result, not as a moment. A's "neither one so much as rocked" made me hold my breath. B's didn't.

5. **Confidence: medium.** About 80% of the dialogue is the same. B does several things better: the tagged "Yes," Kate said, which A leaves unclear after "The speaker hissed"; "folded the knife shut over it"; Judy sitting down in the chair Ray fixed and noticing it; the ladder he "knew where she was by the ladder alone." A wins the pit and the lanes, and those are the scenes I'd remember.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same dialogue nearly word for word and the same plain, Maine-flat cadence; one is a cut of the other.
