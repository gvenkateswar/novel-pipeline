1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B the people talk like different people. Kate rattles on about the garage and the charge nurse ("it's medieval"). Ray gets a joke about his crooked headlight. Ellen stops and restarts her "third item" like a woman reading her own list back. In A everyone speaks in the same clipped hush, and the catwalk is mostly furniture and ticking.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 55%, on the catwalk: "Down the catwalk the motors were giving up the day's heat one at a time, a tick from behind lane four and a while later one from behind lane nine, while the warmth went out of the kickback steel at their backs. Seven ticked once, close by." It's a catalogue of the room while I'm waiting for her to speak, and the ticks keep coming ("Behind lane two a motor ticked and was done," "another motor gave up a tick") until they read like a habit and not a heartbeat. I'd also stumbled earlier, at about 7%: "and she let it all go down the line to her. 'Like this.'" I had to read it twice to work out who "she" and "her" were. A got back some of my goodwill with "He was still laughing against her mouth when she kissed him, and she kissed him through it until he quit". That's the best single beat in either version.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 78%, in the bank: "and on her blotter, in her hand, a slush depth a quarter inch under the one Hollis had given the radio." It's a callback for the sake of a callback, the writer showing me the town's bookkeeping when I want to get to the signature. Right after it, "Judy picked up her phone and called the wire room and gave it to them, and waited, and put the phone down," is the couple doing business. It's short enough that I didn't put the book down.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two share most of their skeleton and many of their sentences. A's locker scene, with the hands on the key and the bones shifting, and its ladder gust are better written. B wins on voices, and on the confession being allowed to take its time.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. It's the same scenes in the same order, with whole sentences shared and the same fondness for objects that tick.
