A = v2.md, B = v1.md

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** Both have the same bones and mostly the same lines. A trusts the big moments to play out in silence and furnace knocks. B keeps stepping in to tell me what the moment means: "the one form she had ever trusted to arrive," "in his own words," "which was some work." In the pit, which is the whole point of the chapter, I wanted the version that keeps its mouth shut.

3. **Where A lost me:** the night count, about 50%. "He read every figure off them to the cent, which he had never once done for anybody, and she wrote each under the last, drew her line, added the column down and then added it again from the bottom up, and her pencil stopped." It's envelope, envelope, envelope, add, add again. There's no joke in it and nobody talks, so my eye slid down to "Nine hundred dollars under." The roll call of teams at about 38% ("The Lewiston team, through from a morning squad, was out of the money by the second string. Gil's four from Pine Ridge…") is a smaller drift of the same kind.

4. **Where B lost me:** the pit, about 57%. "He gave it back to her in the one form she had ever trusted to arrive." A few lines later comes "Then he said his own, in his own words." The narration explains the echo before I've had a chance to hear it. It's a pity, because B's count scene just before this is livelier than A's ("That's what twice is for," the two men carrying a sofa up a stair), and Kate's "Don't. I'm fine. It's a good garage" is the best Kate line in either version. Smaller snag: the sign scene opens on the transformer and the jumper across the K before it gets to the people, and "she pulled off his right glove" made me stop to work out whose hand was in which glove.

5. **Confidence: medium.** B has more small voice moments and A has better big ones. A wins because "he meant to be thorough about it," the laugh "with no sound in it," and "his hand had the weight of it before the rest of him did" are the lines I'd remember. The last frame also builds better in A, with the crowd going still lane by lane.

6. **Same-author check: yes.** They share the same dialogue almost word for word, and the narration has the same long "and… and…" sentences. One draft is tighter than the other.
