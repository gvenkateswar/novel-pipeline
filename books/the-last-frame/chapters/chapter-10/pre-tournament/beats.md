### Scene 1 — "Then do it."

**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, the counter, Sunday morning before anyone else is in the building. **Sensory anchor:** cedar pencil shavings curling off Ray's pocketknife onto the counter. Hollis's flat voice comes out of the snack-bar radio on the shelf over the fryer.

**Characters:** Ray, Ellen. Hollis, as a voice on the radio.

**Want:**
- **Ray** wants Kate's answer and won't ask for it. He asked once, Saturday ("And if she says no?"), and he doesn't ask twice. He also wants, though it isn't his to want, for her to keep the rest of her money. **In the way:** his rule, and the fact that the money is hers. The reader learns the want in his interior as the knife keeps going over the same pencil past the point where it needed to: he had asked her once, at this counter.
- **Ellen** wants to tell him before he has to ask. It is Kate's rule, the first morning it applies, and she gives it to him. The reader learns this at her first line.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open on Ray's hands:**
   - his pocketknife taking every pencil in her cigar box to a point, for the ten lanes' scoresheets;
   - the shavings on the counter.
2. **Hollis on the radio**, in measurements and in his own voice (one or two sentences): four inches of slush on top at the public access, water up through every hole he cut, and the derby's Sunday is off.
3. **One clause, Ray's, no comment:** she let him in at half past six, and the whole time she turned the bolt his key was in her vest pocket.
   - **Optional, one sentence:** a free Sunday for every bob-house on the lake, and none of it is coming up her drive. No rescue.
4. **Ellen comes to the counter beside him.** Without being asked, in one paragraph, she gives Kate's words as Kate said them, with nothing around them:
   - **"Kate said, 'Then do it. And call me first next time. Like this.'"**
5. **Ray's knife stops.** Her hand comes to rest on the counter an inch from his on the knife, and neither of them moves it (the longing). One sentence at most.
6. **Ray, to the pencil (his fear and tender register talks to the object, with the important word last):**
   - **"I'd have said no."**
   - One clause of his interior, so the reader can't misread it: it was the rest, the part she swore to keep, and he'd have kept it for her.
7. **Ellen: "You weren't asked."** Cut.

**Subtext:** She brings him the answer before he has to ask. That is the Ch 09 ask paid forward, and Kate's rule handed on. He wanted her savings kept. Her line tells him the money and the risk are hers, and it tells him she's teasing him, which tells him they're fine. It also seeds Monday, when he keeps himself out of the money on purpose.

**Texture budget:** One. Hollis's slush figure, in his voice.

**Point of view:** Ray. Comic. Ellen's contractions are free with him.

**Ends on:** **Dialogue (laugh).** "You weren't asked." No reaction beat after it.

**Proposed:**

> "How's she read?"

**Why it works:**
- It is the outline's open image: the sign comes on with every letter lit. It is Ray's want (to be the one who asks), Ellen's comedy (the exact, literal answer), and the book's readback in shape, without the word.
- The listener needs one setup: Denise's "Ettle's" earlier in s5 makes "both words" mean the whole name is back.
