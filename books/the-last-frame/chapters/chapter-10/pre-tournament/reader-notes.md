# Reader notes: Chapter 10, "Ice-Out" (last chapter)

**Draft read:** `chapters/chapter-10/final.md`, Oct 29, 3,429 words. For context I read `chapters/chapter-01/final.md` through `chapters/chapter-09/final.md` first, in order. I opened nothing else in the folder.

---

## 1. Pull score: 4 / 10

The only open question coming in (does Kate say yes?) is answered in line 11. After that the chapter is a victory lap: every secondary character gets a final bit, every object gets put back on its shelf, and nothing goes wrong. I wasn't pulled through it. I was walked through a checklist I could have written myself after Ch 09.

**Would I recommend the book?** No. Not to a general romance reader who has other books on her phone. Maybe to one specific friend who reads Haruf or Strout for the weather and the hardware store, and I'd warn her about the middle.
**Would I buy the next book by this author?** No, not blind. I'd download a sample, and the sample would have to move faster than this one did.

---

## 2. Where I got bored / where my thumb sped up

- **Around 20%:** *"You said four inches on the radio. I had three and three-quarters at my holes." / "Where were your holes?" / "Where I put them."* A cute bit, but it's filler. The Judy/Hollis exchange is a vaudeville spot in a chapter whose one real question has already been answered. I skimmed it.
- **Around 22%:** *"The winter's cash stays where it is, every dollar it made still sitting in the account. Anything you put on the note is new money, and I'll want to see where it came from."* Bank procedure again. I've heard the "every dollar still sitting in the account" rule since Ch 02. My eyes slid off it.
- **Around 27%:** *"So moved." / "Second."* / Verna writing it into the binder. A motion and a vote, again, in a book that has already run a full league meeting (Ch 05). It feels like a ceremony.
- **Around 35%:** *"He bowled three ordinary strings with it and was glad of every one."* The Memorial, the event the whole book has been building toward, gets one sentence of summary for our hero. Then Denise's four-horse, which Ch 08 had already set up for me ("pick up a four-horse with her third ball eleven years back"). I knew she'd make it before she picked up the ball.
- **Around 45%:** *"Twenty-eight thousand nine hundred." … "Tomorrow I sign eighty-eight hundred of mine onto Arlene's note, and when the committee says yes, the bank's number comes down to meet it."* I had to stop and redo the eight-to-one rule from Ch 07 to see why 8,800 closes an 1,100 gap. That's homework, not suspense.
- **Around 70%:** *"Not buying in, not getting out of the way. Neither invoice, then."* Danny's line exists to close Danny's line from Ch 06. My thumb sped up.
- **Around 90%:** *"Who won? Nobody. She didn't quit and she didn't lose it, so the pot goes to the derby kids' division…"* and then Gil's spring-doubles flyer in Denise's pocket. This is the last page, the key moment is waiting, and the book stops to close two more side threads first. I skimmed straight to the key.

## 3. Where I got confused

- **Line 3:** *"He had asked her once, at this counter, what she'd do if her daughter said no, and he didn't ask twice."* I read it twice. I thought it meant he'd been told and was refusing to ask again. It actually means he's waiting and won't ask her what Kate said.
- **Line 5:** *"Over the fryer the radio had Hollis on."* Until now, "the radio" in this building has meant the Pit/Counter two-ways. Is Hollis on the walkie? On local AM? It only clears up when Judy says "You said four inches on the radio."
- **Judy.** Ch 09 mentioned her once ("My Aunt Judy from the bank"). Here she turns out to be the person who decides the whole book. I had to work out that she's the Granite Lakes loan officer, and that "Judy's desk, Monday morning" is the closing.
- **The butterscotch.** *"Judy only offered from that bowl before bad news"* sets up bad news, and then it's "The underwriter has never met you. Committee sits at two." Was that the bad news? Is there more? It resolves offstage with "It funded," so the fake-out never gets explained.
- **Verna's January vote.** *"She voted yes on the owner's rate in January, and it has been folded in this binder since."* I only got this because I happened to remember the folded slip in Ch 05. A reader who doesn't remember it will be lost.
- **The second pool sheet.** Ellen asks for "a square" on "the other sheet," and Denise says she can't bet on her own flight. The book never said what that sheet was for. I inferred it's about the two of them, but I'm guessing.
- **Locker fourteen, why now?** Scene two opens with the key already turning. Nothing on the page shows Ray deciding to open it after six winters.
- **Arlene's drive-by.** She's 84 and the back seat is full of boxes. Is she driving? Who is? *"the window came down"* doesn't say.

## 4. Where I stopped believing

- **Dale seconds the motion.** *"Dale put his burger down. 'Second.'"* This man walked his crew out of the league over this exact cup ("My crew paid for that cup in September"). Now he seconds opening Linda's name to anyone who pays, with no beat showing him change. That reversal happens off the page.
- **The derby treasurer moves it.** *"So moved."* The day before, Ellen killed his six o'clock raffle and he said "The committee's going to hear about this." Now he's the one who moves to honor her Open. Another offstage reversal. Everyone in town has quietly switched to warm.
- **Timing parade at the sign.** Arlene drives past at the exact minute, wire confirmation in hand. Then Denise walks down the drive with both pool sheets and the money. Three characters converge on the foot of the pole as if they were called for a curtain call. It's a convenience, and it reads as one.
- **The bank officer at the Memorial.** The one person whose desk decides the money just happens to be at the counter on Sunday, so Ellen can brief her before the first ball. Small-town plausible, but it's convenient.
- **Choices under pressure:** there's no physical danger in this chapter. Ray staying out of Judy's office follows from what he knows (Main Street gossip, Dale's "losing pays you"). That holds. Ellen spending her savings follows from Kate's yes. Nothing safer was left open.

## 5. Where it sounded like a machine

- **Callback saturation.** Almost every line of the last third is a return: "Charge me for the snowstorm." / "That's twice." / "Don't you go bowling sentimental." / "That's not asking." / "Under the clock." / Arlene licking her thumb to count. / "Neither invoice." / "the veteran turns out his pockets." / "you don't get to bet on your own flight." / "Both words." Any one of these is a pleasure. Ten in a row feels like a closing checklist.
- **Every scene ends on a neat line:** *"You weren't asked."* / *"Dale Hutchins moved his boots to let him by."* / *"He didn't ask."* / *"Committee sits at two."* / *"Both words."*
- **Mirrored declarations:** *"I love you, Ellen."* / *"…It is in order. I love you."* They're built as a matched pair. The third-item payoff was set up in Ch 03, and it arrives exactly where I'd have put it.
- **A thesis in dialogue:** *"Thirty years I've kept everything just how it was, and I'd let go of the lot to be the one you ask."* That's the book's theme statement put in Ray's mouth. Nobody says that sentence.
- **Feeling explained after the fact:** *"He had fixed every machine up that drive twice over, and he couldn't fix a committee on Main Street, so he'd come down here and put up a K."* / *"He had no idea what Ellen had come back here to say."* / *"He didn't know what she meant by it this time."*
- **Recycled body business from earlier chapters:** *"he felt it go in his back teeth"* (Ch 02, word for word). *"It came up out of him all at once"* (the Ch 03 laugh). The boiler "come up through the floor" (Ch 07). Squaring things: *"squared the stack under his hand again, though it was square"* (pins in Ch 03, gloves in Ch 08, fork in Ch 08). Hands *"bare and cold, as they always were"* (nearly every chapter).
- **Talking to objects, a tic by now:** *"He said it to the pencil."* / *"he said, to her hands"* / *"Arlene said, to the tag."* Earlier he told the lock, the check, and the deposit slips.
- **Stock romance close:** *"he went on kissing her until the building was quiet all round them, and a while after that."*
- **Everyone talks in the same dry button register:** Hollis, Judy, Denise, Arlene, Danny, Normie, Ellen. Everyone gets a zinger and everyone lands it. Denise's run-on is still her own. The rest blur.

## 6. What I think is going on: predictions after Ch 09 vs. what happened

**What I predicted after Ch 09:** Kate says yes. Ellen covers the gap from her savings. Ray's check never gets cashed. The "third item" from the pit is "I love you," and she says it here. Ray opens locker fourteen and bowls Linda's ball. Denise wins the cup with a third-ball four-horse. The K gets lit and the sign reads Kettle again. Ellen's key goes back on Ray's ring. She pays Arlene on March first and Arlene gets her bed.

**Came true:** all of it. Every one.

**Surprised me:** Ray himself moving Linda's name onto the Open (the "museum" arc from Arlene, paid by him rather than imposed on him; a good turn). Verna's secret yes in January (a nice grace note, if you remember the slip). Ray refusing the chair at the bank, and Ellen putting her phone face up on it so he's "in the other chair" (the best structural idea in the chapter). Arlene taking back the orange 1996 key (I expected Ray to keep it as a keepsake; giving it back is better). Denise and Gil as a seed.

**Did not happen:** anything going wrong. The committee could have said no, the Memorial could have cost something, Kate could have said something hard. None of it. The book ends exactly as forecast, and it ends at full warmth for every character.

## 8. The listener pass (TTS)

- **Numbers back to back:** "Twenty-eight thousand nine hundred" and "eighty-eight hundred" come two lines apart. A listener can't do the eight-to-one math by ear and won't know whether this is good news.
- **"Kettle Lanes, Gagnon. Request." / "Gagnon, say request."** Read aloud, the inverted call sign makes it unclear who's speaking. Both lines open with a name.
- **"He waited for the Ms."** A TTS voice will say "M S," "miz," or "Ms" as in manuscript. The best beat in the last scene depends on the listener hearing *Ms.* versus *Mrs.*, and they may not.
- **"Voyons donc"** will be mangled.
- **"Ettle's"** spoken aloud sounds like a mispronunciation, not a joke, unless the listener is holding Ch 01 in their head.
- **"four-horse"** is jargon, never explained in nine chapters.
- **The phone scene** has five voices in about forty lines (Ellen, Judy, Ray, Danny, Normie), two of them coming through a phone lying on a chair. *"I can hear you, Ray."* Judy or Ellen? I had to check.
- **"Ray" opens paragraph after paragraph:** "Ray had set…" "Ray was looking…" "Ray's first box…" "Ray picked…" "Ray squared…" "Ray's hands stopped…" "Ray squared the stack…" "Ray sat down…" "Ray looked at her hands." "Ray took his hand…" "Ray looked at it." "Ray came down…" "Ray stood…" Aloud it becomes a drumbeat.
- **Clause pile in the kiss:** *"It came up out of him all at once, the same laugh she'd pulled out of him in the pit behind lane five in December, the first one in six years, and he felt her start to smile, and she kept hold of his face…"* A listener loses who "she" is and what "the first one" refers to.
- **The motors sentence:** *"one and then the next one over and then one away down by lane two, and the warmth going out of the kickback steel behind them a little at a time, and her breathing next to him, slower than his, and under all of it lane seven, sitting quiet on the clutch he'd put in the first week of December."* Lovely on the page, but it's an "and" chain that a voice can't shape.

## 9. The romance

Yes, in places. These made me want them together or made me laugh:
- *"Something caught. Her reading glasses had swung out on their black cord and hooked the pull of his jacket zipper, and when she came up the last inch they jerked her short, and Ray laughed against her mouth."* The one moment in the love scene that isn't composed. It's the best thing in the scene.
- *"Twice behind lane seven and once under the sign. I've kept count."* In character. Neat, but hers.
- *"That's not asking." / "Ray. Will you take it?"* The book's arc in two lines.
- *"She's got the butterscotch out." / "I can hear you, Ray."* Made me laugh.
- Denise: *"You came all the way from Halsey Falls in that coat to count to two?"* Made me laugh.

The two "I love you"s don't move me. They're too symmetrical, and the third-item setup told me exactly where hers would come.

## 10. What I want to know next

Nothing. The book is over and it closes every door. I have no question about Ellen and Ray. The Denise/Gil spring doubles is the only thread left loose, and it isn't enough to make me buy a sequel. That's the most important finding: there's no ache at the end, nothing unsaid and nothing it cost them that's still sitting on the page.

---

## 11. Best moment

> *"He bowled three ordinary strings with it and was glad of every one, and each time he looked up, with all ten lanes going at once, Ellen was behind the counter with her eyes shut."*

It quietly pays off "I bought it to hear all ten going at once" from Ch 02, without a callback line or a speech. It's the only payoff in the chapter that trusts me to remember. Close second: *"He waited for the Ms. She threw it."*

---

## 12. The last line: "Both words."

It works, in Ellen's voice: terse, exact, a readback. It lands the Ch 01 Ettle joke. But it's the ending I'd have written for this book after Ch 01. It's a button, not a surprise, and it's the fifth neat button in one chapter. The beat two lines earlier (*"He waited for the Ms. She threw it."*) is the stronger close and gets buried under it.

## Context: the whole book as it reads at this point (brief)

Where I'd most likely have quit for good: **Ch 04's office scene** (the lineage notice, "First… Second… Third," the bylaws Verna won't release) running straight into **Ch 05's league meeting**.
