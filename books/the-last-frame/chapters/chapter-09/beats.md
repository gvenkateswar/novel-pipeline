### Scene 1

**Setting:**
- **Place:** Ray's back step on School Street, then the yellow kitchen: oilcloth table, one dented aluminum pot, the radio on top of the refrigerator.
- **Time:** Wednesday, three days before the Open. One minute before a quarter past five. Full dark is coming, about ten minutes after sunset.
- **Sensory anchor:** the kitchen's heat after the cold step. It smells of hamburg, green pepper and tomato cooked down.

**Characters:** Ellen, Ray.

**Want:**
- **Ellen** wants to sit at his table as someone asked, with nothing to carry and nothing to brief, and to still be there when his house would go quiet.
- **In the way:** she doesn't know how to arrive anywhere without a task. And the key is still in her vest from the stair ("Not yet"), so she doesn't know yet what she is in this kitchen.
- **Ray** wants her at the table at a quarter to six, and wants to say himself why he waited.
- **The reader learns Ellen's want at:** "I wanted it from you."

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Open on her empty hands (one line, then a short interior).**
   - Nothing in them: no pad, no pen, no clipboard. He said *"Wednesday. Quarter past five. Bring nothing."* at her counter on Monday, and she has.
   - She is a minute early, as she is for everything, and stands on his back step deciding whether a knock a minute early counts as early.
   - One line places his gloves and his key: the gloves in her coat pocket, the key in her vest. She leaves both where they are.
2. **The door opens before she knocks (played).** Ray: *"Heard you being early."* He heard her car a minute ago and has been standing on the other side. Ray reads by sound. The line is his, and it's the first laugh of the chapter.
3. **The kitchen (one line).**
   - The yellow nobody has argued with in forty years.
   - The radio on the refrigerator gives the weather on low: a few inches Saturday morning, over by noon. Ray turns it down. Nobody remarks on it. This is the forecast s2 turns on.
4. **The second bowl (one line; observable; no comment).** The second bowl comes from the back of the cupboard, a different pattern from his. He rinses it before he fills it. The narration says nothing about why.
5. **The ordinary pleasure (played, three or four sentences, no comment).** The hot bowl. She is hungrier than she knew. Elbow macaroni, hamburg, tomato and green pepper, eaten at a table built for two people who don't mind knees. Let her enjoy it on the page.
6. **The laugh (never one line).** Ellen, looking at the pot, which holds about four suppers: *"What do you make the second night?"* Ray: *"That one."*
7. **The longing (played).**
   - Under the oilcloth his boot comes against her right knee and stays there. She doesn't move it.
   - Ray, who reads people by how they wear things: *"You favor one on a stair. Which?"* / *"The left."* / *"What happened to it?"*
8. **The ski fall, in order (played).**
   - She tells it the way she'd write it up, by time and not by ordinals (the chapter's one ordinal list is s3's): December of two thousand and nine, the hill, Kate fourteen, the beginner trail.
   - The toboggan: three boys, crossing from the left. *"It had the right of way, so I gave it the right of way."* Then the snow fence, and the knee.
   - He laughs at the toboggan and at nowhere else in the story. One sentence of hers registers exactly where he laughed, the way a controller hears where a voice changes. Never "she noticed he didn't laugh at the knee."
9. **A quarter to six (never one line; the chapter's first long interior passage, then dialogue).**
   - She knows the time without a clock. The narration says only that it's a quarter to six. Never "without looking", which is a capped motif.
   - Ray, plain: the house goes quiet about now. Supper's done, and it's the better part of four hours to bed with nobody in them. He says it shorter than he said it to Hollis, and in his own words.
   - Ellen: *"Denise told me. In January."* / Ray: *"Denise would."* / Ellen: *"I wanted it from you."*
   - Ray: *"I told myself I'd go up that drive the winter Danny was ready. You went up it."*
   - No narration explains that he means her in his kitchen and not the building. The clock and the second bowl do it.
10. **"Supper" (one line).** Ellen: *"It's a good supper."* It is her first time with the word in the book. He looks at her for a second over the pot. Nothing else.

**Subtext:**
- Nobody says Saturday, the Open, money, the league or Linda. All of it is in the room: the forecast he turned down, the key in her vest.
