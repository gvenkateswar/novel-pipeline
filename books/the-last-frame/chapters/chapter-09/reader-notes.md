# Cold read, Chapter 9: The D-side

## 1. Pull score: 4 / 10

Almost everything in this chapter is something I had already predicted, and it all arrives in the order I predicted it: the storm, the refunds, coming up short, the call to Kate. The only thing pulling me into Chapter 10 is Kate's last line.

**Would I have stopped?** Not inside this chapter, but only because I'm about 85% of the way through and want to see how it lands. That's sunk cost, not pull. I came closest to putting it down at the opening of the Arlene scene, "I'm under," Ellen said. "By more than Sunday can make." That's the third time in about two pages I'd been told she's under, and the scene had nothing new to give me.

## 2. Where I got bored

- **Scene 2, second paragraph:** "Wednesday through Friday the forecast had said a few inches Saturday morning, over by noon. On that she had kept both evening squads; snow that quit at midday never touched either of them. On Friday night, with page two of the terms in her hand, she had held Sunday's empty lanes spare anyway. Four squads, thirty teams." This is where I started skimming. It's operations data, and the third time I've heard the forecast phrase in one chapter.
- **Scene 3, opening:** "At lane one the derby treasurer was tipping the raffle stubs out of a bait bucket..." and "their anchor was Judy's brother's boy, an Ostrowski, by the entry form Ellen had read." I didn't know why I was being shown a raffle or a nephew. The nephew comes back with one arm in his coat and then goes nowhere.
- **The readback** ("Three's down till I tell you she isn't..."). I understand what it's doing, but I heard the same list twice in a row, and the second time I skipped it with my eyes.
- **The Arlene scene, all of it.** Known stakes, known answer, a neat exit line.
- **The first half of the Kate call.** Ellen recaps the release, the fifteenth, Arlene and the house, all of which I watched happen last chapter.

**The bigger boredom problem:** this was meant to be the Open, the day the whole book has been building toward with Linda's name and Gil's grudge. It never happens on the page. What I got instead was a queue of people collecting refunds at a counter. The deflation is clearly deliberate, but it means the climactic chapter is mostly administration: phone calls, briefings, envelopes, a register tape.

## 3. Where I got confused

- **"The phone lit a pale square on the ceiling over the bed."** It comes straight after a supper with his foot against her knee and him refilling her bowl. I assumed she had stayed the night at Ray's. It took me two paragraphs to work out that it's Saturday and she's in her own bed. "Ten to four was when she woke" reads like a habit, not a date.
- **"The camp."** This is the first time I've been told Ellen lives in a lake camp. I had pictured her in a house, or in the building itself.
- **Who bowls Linda's final on Sunday?** All fifteen evening teams took refunds and left. If the evening squads were qualifying, the final is missing half its field. I don't know what's left of the Open.
- **Where is Gil Arsenault?** Last chapter he was the fourteenth team, the man who'd wanted "a second look" since 1994. He isn't mentioned at all here. Did he come? Did he take his envelope?
- **The money.** In Chapter 5 Ellen said her own money "lowers the line, slowly, and all of it wouldn't buy back half of January." Now "more than half of the rest" covers fourteen hundred dollars. I can't do that arithmetic and I don't believe the book wants me to try, but Ellen, who knows everything to the minute, answers "How much is more than half?" with "Judy writes the figure Monday morning." That isn't her.
- **"She had said harder things on a frequency, and could not think of one."** Could she not think of a harder thing, or not think of a reply? I read it three times.
- **"I skied down for the patrol once."** It took me a beat to connect this to the knee story at supper. It does connect, but heard aloud it sounds like nonsense at first.
- **"Road agent, Kettle Lanes."** I worked out that it's radio order (who you're calling, then who you are), but only on a second pass.

## 4. Where I stopped believing

- **All fifteen evening teams refuse Sunday morning.** The out-of-towners (Lowell, Saco, Massachusetts) I accept. But "Every evening team but Dale's was already in the building," and some of them must be local, and Linda's final is Sunday at one, in town. Every single one taking the cash and none moving to Sunday is a convenience, and it exists to make the shortfall as large as possible. The page never addresses the locals.
- **Refusing Ray's name at the bank.** The safer option is right there: Ray's name behind hers, Arlene's bed protected, Kate's money untouched. Ellen refuses on "beside, not behind." The page does close it in character, and Kate even says "Then he's got more sense than you," which helped. But I didn't fully buy that a woman who protects Arlene this fiercely would gamble Arlene's bed and her daughter's safety money on a preposition. It held, just.
- **Arlene's calm.** She signed a release twelve days ago against her own judgement ("I didn't say I'd like it"), and now that the thing she feared has happened she says one sentence and turns her hat. No edge at all, not even at Ray, who isn't in the room. Too gracious for an eighty-four-year-old who has just lost her place in the queue.
- **Ray and Linda's day.** Linda's Memorial, the thing he quit his league over, gets snowed out, and Ray has no reaction on the page. He becomes a very good dispatcher. Where's the man who couldn't say his wife's full name aloud?
- **Choices under pressure:** sending everyone home at four is clearly right. Ray's reason ("a hundred and fifty with kids in it, and the heat goes when the power does") closes the obvious objection, and asking Dale first follows from Chapter 3. I believed every safety decision. The disbelief is all on the money side.

## 5. Where it sounded like a machine

- **The chapter is built out of callbacks**, one after another: "Nobody ever refunded me for a snowstorm either" (Ch 3's "charged me for a snowstorm"), "Scale of one to ten" / "Ten" (Ch 5), "You're not doing the voice" (Ch 5), "Readback correct" (Ch 4), "beside me" (Ch 8), "packed since August" (Ch 1), the passenger seat with the dash lit green (Ch 5), "nothing in her hands" at the start and "nothing in her other hand" at the end. Each one is fine. Together they feel like a book tying its own ribbons instead of anything happening for the first time.
- **Explaining the gesture after it lands:**
  - "He had rounded every number she ever gave him. He gave her these exact, in the low voice she had worked whole midnights hoping to hear on a frequency."
  - "Dale watched her ask somebody."
  - "Ellen knew six true sentences that would fill the quiet, each one ready and in its place, and she said none of them."
  - "She had no list. She said it in the order it came."
  - "He went out, and he had not said no to Sunday."
- **Every scene ends on a crisp button:** "the ladle went into the pot." / "You'll have it." / "He was already moving down the catwalk." / "That I'm under." / "I've been packed since September." / "The line went dead."
- **The "ticked" habit again:** "The baseboard heat ticked along the wall of the camp" and, a page later, "The baseboard heat ticked." The whole book ticks: coffee pots, grills, trucks, glass.
- **List of three:** "No pad, no pen, no clipboard."
- **Everyone talks alike.** Hollis, Dale and Ray all speak in the same clipped, dry, two-beat lines ("Ice I can call. The roads are Dale's." / "Roads hold till the middle of the afternoon. After that they go. I'll call the hour."). Kate's echo of Ellen is intended and works. Denise is the only voice I could identify with my eyes shut.

## 6. Did the storm cost something I hadn't priced in? Did Kate's call go as I expected?

**The storm: no.** I'd priced in nearly all of it by the end of Chapter 8, and the radio at supper confirmed it on page one. Refunds were promised back in Chapter 6 ("Cash back if weather stops play"). Coming up short was the obvious result. The money she swore to Kate has been loaded since Chapter 2 ("The house." / "Not the rest." / "I swear.") and was pointed at in Chapter 8 ("It isn't in this"). The only costs I hadn't priced in were small ones: the Saco woman who "drove up here to hold her cup one time" going home with her envelope in her glove, and the father with two boys and one ball. Those stung for a line each. The cost I expected and didn't get was Ray's: Linda's day is snowed out and he loses nothing on the page.

**Kate's call: mostly yes.** I expected her to be hurt at not being asked first, and she was. I expected the scale-of-one-to-ten callback the moment Ellen said "I call Kate," and it came. What I didn't expect, and what is the only real turn in the chapter: Kate doesn't say yes. "Let me sleep on it. And I'm going to want something for it." That and "I'd have said no, and then I'd have driven up" are the two lines that kept me going.

## 9. The listener pass

- **Clock times all through the chapter:** ten to four, ten past four, a quarter past four, by five, half past ten, half past twelve, twenty to two, four o'clock (five or six times), a quarter to four, Sunday at one, six Monday, till three, plus Wednesday, a quarter past five, a quarter to six in scene 1. A listener drowns. By the Dale scene I couldn't tell by ear whether it was two o'clock or four.
- **"At twenty to two Ellen turned before she answered him"** comes right after Dale says "Four o'clock." The time stamp arrives after the dialogue, so the ear has already filed the scene as four o'clock.
- **The briefing then the readback:** the same logistics list twice in a row, read in the same narrator voice. Aloud it's a minute of déjà vu.
- **"Four squads, thirty teams" / "fifteen teams" / "a hundred and fifty" / "fourteen hundred dollars":** numbers stacked without anything to hang them on.
- **"The D-side":** said aloud, "they call it the D-side" sounds like "they call it the decide." The chapter title has the same problem.
- **"Hamburg":** a TTS voice will say it like the German city.
- **"Road agent, Kettle Lanes":** meaningless heard cold.
- **Same-shape sentences in the refund line:** "The captain from Lowell had..." / "A woman from Saco came next, with..." / "After her came a father from Massachusetts with..." / "Dale came last, to..." Four in a row, same rhythm.
- **The Kate call:** mostly untagged short lines, and Kate and Ellen are written to sound alike. "How much is more than half?" / "Judy writes the figure Monday morning." / "What's left?" / "One bad winter." / "One." With one narrator voice I lost who said "One." It's Kate, but I had to count back.
- **"I skied down for the patrol once. I'm not doing it from Manchester."** Too compressed for the ear.
- **The scanner, the radio, the PA, the pit radio, the phone:** five devices in one chapter all carrying voices, and the narrator has to announce each one.

## 10. The romance

Yes, quietly. The supper is the closest this book has come to two people simply liking each other:
- "What do you make the second night?" / "That one." This made me laugh.
- His stocking foot against her knee under the oilcloth, and "She did not move hers."
- "He laughed at the toboggan, with his spoon halfway up."
- "It's a good supper," she said, and he gets up and refills her bowl without a word. That's the best wordless yes in the book.
- The D-side: "Today and after, whatever Monday says." / "Readback correct." It's a proposal in her language, and it worked on me.

What I missed: she rides down the hill in his passenger seat. That's the seat Denise described in Chapter 5, the one Ellen "put herself in... long enough to know she would like it." It happens offstage, in a subordinate clause ("the passenger seat she had come down in"). It's the one image the book had me longing for, and I didn't get to sit in it. He then drives her home and goes back down without a goodnight on the page either.

## 11. What I want to know next

- **What does Kate want for it?** This is the only real question pulling me forward.

Those are the only three. Whether she clears, whether Ray takes the key back, whether the K gets lit: I'm not asking those, I'm waiting for them. If Kate's line weren't there, I'd have no question at all.
