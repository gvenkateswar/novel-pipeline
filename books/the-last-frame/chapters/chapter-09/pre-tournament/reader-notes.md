# Reader Notes: Chapter 9, "The D-side"

**Draft read:** final.md, Oct 29 (read chapter-01 through chapter-09 final.md in order, nothing else)

**Warning first:** I'd keep reading, but only because I'm near the end and I've already spent the time. I don't need to know what happens next. I can see it. By the last line, the chapter's own question (can she cover the shortfall?) has been answered: she has the money, Kate says "do it." What's left is how neatly it wraps up.

---

## 1. Pull score: 4 / 10

The chapter has the book's biggest money crisis, $1,715 short with Arlene's bed riding on it, and it settles that crisis on the last page with a yes from Kate. I close it with nothing open that I'm worried about.

## 2. Where I got bored

- **The readback, scene 2, about 45% in.** "Ray put one hand up on the beam and gave it to her. 'Hollis at ten past four. Ice is closed, and worse after two. Three was late at the top of her table...'" I had just read the four-part briefing (Weather, Equipment, Traffic, Special conditions), and here it all comes again, nearly word for word. I skimmed it. I knew it would end on readback from the moment she said "relief briefing," because the book set "no readback" up in Chapter 3. The only new thing is the extra item at the end, and I had to sit through the full repeat to reach it.
- **The supper, scene 1, opening to "She went on looking."** It's pleasant and nothing pushes against anything. They've already made up in Ch 8, the food is described, his hands are described, and then the narrator tells me she has never looked at anyone's hands at a table before. Nothing is at stake until the radio comes on in the last paragraph. The town-dock story ("That's the winter the ice got under the end of the town dock...") is in character, and it's still the point where I checked how much chapter was left.
- **The refund paragraph, scene 3.** "The envelopes stood on end in the cash box, ten of them..." It's one long paragraph about handing envelopes back, built on the "level voice, and under it she was..." pattern the book has now used at least six times. I knew what she was feeling before the paragraph said it.

## 3. Where I got confused

- **When is the supper?** No day or date. Somewhere in the 13 days after Ch 8. I worked out that it comes before Saturday only because the forecast says "Saturday."
- **"Three's been coming round slow since your party Saturday."** Which party Saturday? The December one from Ch 4, where three was half a second late? Or a different one? I don't know which.
- **"Whatever Monday says."** I had to work out for myself that Monday is March first. The page never says so.
- **"Hollis called at ten past four"** and then "off the roads by four" and "By a quarter to four the lot was empty." I took the first as a.m. and the others as p.m., but on first pass I thought she was briefing in the future tense.
- **"he says it gets worse after two."** Does "it" mean the ice or the storm?
- **The radio dial.** Ray reaches up and closes his fingers on the dial during the storm forecast, and that ends the scene. Did he turn it off to keep the evening, or to keep the forecast from her? It's never mentioned again. It reads like a plant that went nowhere.
- **How her savings actually close the gap.** In Ch 7 Ray's mechanism was money paid on Arlene's note at eight to one. Here she's going to use "nearly all of the rest," and I can't tell whether it counts toward the bank's thirty thousand, goes on the note, or what. In Ch 5 she said her money was "not enough to matter." Now it is enough. I believe there's arithmetic behind it, but the page doesn't give it to me.
- **"What do you make the second night?" / "That one."** I had to read it twice to see she was asking what he cooks on other nights.
- **"Is anybody there with you?" / "No. I wanted to ask you first."** Ask Kate first before what? Before letting Ray help? Before Ray is with her? Where did Ray go after "Ask who?" He's at the counter with her, and then she's alone at the camp.

## 4. Where I stopped believing

- **Ray turning off the weather.** All book long this man listens to every machine. This is the week of the Open, the event the whole season rests on, and with a storm coming he turns off the forecast. If it's tenderness, the page doesn't say so. If it's hiding the news, that's out of character and never followed up.
- **Ray at the cash box.** She has just made him her D-side, "today, and after today," and in the crisis he does nothing: "He waited." He closes the lid and asks "What'll you do?" He has a $48,000 cashier's check in a blank envelope inside his jacket. Ch 7 gave her reason for refusing it, and that reason still stands, but Ray doesn't mention it, doesn't offer, doesn't decide not to. The page avoids the envelope instead of closing it off.
- **The storm decision.** This one I believed. She knows the boiler dies with the power, there are 150 people and 41 children, and the town wants everyone off the roads by four. Hollis's arithmetic rules out running the evening squad early, since it would run past four too. The Massachusetts teams are driving home tonight either way. Holding them was Denise's option, and Ellen answered it in one line. It follows from what she knows.
- **The relief briefing as a declaration.** It's in character, and it's also exactly what the book has been building toward since Ch 3. It felt engineered: the motif arrives right on schedule.
- **"Nobody had heard it from her before. Kate had been there for it, and the surgeon in Concord had read the rest off a picture."** The narrator is telling me the anecdote matters. The anecdote was already doing its job.

## 5. Where it sounded like a machine

- **"Her voice went out over the ten lanes level and courteous... Under it she was already out on the drive."** Then, in the same scene: "every word of it courteous and exact. Under it she was taking each one off a number." "Level and courteous, and under it she was counting" was used in Ch 3, Ch 5 and Ch 7. It's a template now.
- **"Ray waited for a fifth item with the wrench in his hand."** Ch 7 had "Ray waited for a fourth item." Same beat, number bumped by one.
- **"with her weight even on both feet."** Fourth or fifth time in the book (Ch 6, 7, 8, 9).
- **"the whole room smelling of wet wool and road salt."** Third time (Ch 1, 8, 9).
- **"the lanes ran backward and bright."** Ch 4 and 5 have "bright and backward." "One caged bulb hung over the pair": Ch 2, 5, 9.
- **"a sound she could have stood in all afternoon if she had had the afternoon."** Ch 7 had "She could have stood in it all winter."
- **"and heard whose word that was, and did not take it back."** Ch 5 had "she did not take them back." Same move.
- **"She had sat at a great many tables and could not remember looking at anyone's hands at one. She went on looking."** The narrator explains the feeling right after showing the look.
- **"the voice of a good pilot at three in the morning with a long night ahead of him and nothing in it he could not handle."** The comparison suits her career, but the sentence is close to 90 words and wears out its welcome.
- **Every scene ends on a placed line:** "his fingers closed on the dial." / "Readback correct." / "Ask who?" / "Then do it. And call me first next time. Like this." All four land like button endings. By Ch 9 I can feel the chapter organizing itself around its exits.
- **Denise and Ray share a rhythm.** Both use long comma-chained run-ons ("Then keep them. Keep them like December, thirty-three of them slept on those settees and paid for strings at midnight and they're still telling it at the diner, and is this lot any different? It is not."). Without the tags I couldn't always tell who was talking.

## 6. What I think is going on

Ellen breaks her promise to Kate, puts most of her own savings in, and makes the number on Sunday with almost nothing to spare. Possibly the Memorial crowd turns out bigger than anyone expected, with Dale's crew coming in off the plows, the Bench, the town coming out "for Linda" now that Linda's name is back on the league's own day, so the savings end up partly unnecessary. Arlene keeps her bed at The Pines. The K comes back from Laconia and gets lit on or around March first, so the sign finally reads Kettle Lanes. The front key goes back onto Ray's ring at the end; she held it out in Ch 8 and he said "Not yet." Locker fourteen gets opened, probably at the Memorial, probably with Linda's ball inside, and Ray bowls with it or hands it on. The Bench's pool pays off: the March first square left blank in Ch 1, and Normie's mystery dollar-a-square sheet in Ch 4, with Danny on Valentine's, which I think is a pool on Ray and Ellen. Ray's $48,000 envelope never gets cashed and ends as a gesture, maybe torn up, maybe turned into a partnership share once she has made the number on her own. No secret is left. The book is now a delivery schedule.

## 7. The listener pass

- **The briefing and the readback:** four headed paragraphs (Weather. Equipment. Traffic. Special conditions.) followed straight away by the same content again in Ray's voice. Aloud, that's the same list twice in about a minute, with the numbers too: "a hundred and fifty in the building, forty-one of them children," twice. A listener will think the file skipped back.
- **The briefing paragraphs open on quotation marks with no tag.** A TTS voice reads four untagged quoted paragraphs from one speaker, then Ray's untagged reply. It works only because Ray's reply has one tag.
- **Unclear speaker in the Kate call:** "'Are you hurt?' / 'No.' / 'On the second of December I swore to you. The house, not the rest.'" "No" and "On the second of December" are both Ellen, in separate paragraphs. Heard aloud, the second line sounds like Kate answering.
- **Times:** "ten past four," "after two," "by four," "a quarter to four," "two," "one o'clock," "six," "seven." The listener can't tell a.m. from p.m. or briefing time from clock time.
- **"Seventeen hundred and fifteen dollars under thirty thousand"** is fine aloud, but it arrives without the $1,600 refund ever being stated, so a listener has to do subtraction they never heard the parts of.
- **"Two thousand nine"** said twice, by both of them, back to back.
- **The chapter title "The D-side"** is read aloud before the term is explained. It sounds like "the decide."
- **Long sentences:** the "good pilot at three in the morning" sentence and the refund paragraph will run out of breath in a TTS voice.
- **Sentence-shape run:** "Her voice went out... Under it she was..." / "every word of it courteous and exact. Under it she was..." / "Her voice did not change once, and she listened the whole way for it to." That's three in one scene.

## 8. The romance

Yes, in places, and mostly in the supper.

- "You finish the run?" / "All of it. Some of it standing." / "How much of it standing?" / "To the third turn." This made me laugh.
- "I'll have to warn the pot. Six years she's cooked for one, and she's never had a repeat customer." / "Then I'll be a minute early for supper." This made me want them together.
- Ray's extra item at the end of the readback, "And you haven't sat down since Hollis called," right after she thinks "at the treasurer she would have liked another item." That's the one place the ATC device gave me something besides structure.
- The auger man ("Which one of you's got my auger?... toward a lake that was closed") is the chapter's best laugh, and it has nothing to do with the couple.

In the crisis scene, the relationship goes slack: he waits, she decides, he asks a question.

## 9. What I want to know next

Not much, and that's the finding.

- Will Sunday's Memorial bring in anything? (Mild interest. I assume yes.)
- How exactly does her savings money count against the bank's number? (I want this answered, but it's a clarity question, not a pull.)
- What's in locker fourteen? (It was planted in Ch 1 and I'd forgotten it until I made my prediction list. It isn't pulling me now.)
- What happens to the blank envelope in Ray's jacket?
- Does the K get lit?

Nothing here is a question I'd stay up for. The chapter answered its own cliffhanger before ending: "Then do it."
