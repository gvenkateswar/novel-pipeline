4. **Where B lost me:** at about 62%: "Ray's money had been closed since January; Judy's rule counted none of it." It's a ledger line dropped into the quietest moment in the chapter, right after the tape. The readback at about 38% dragged here too, but B breaks it in the middle with "in the low voice she had worked whole midnights hoping to hear on a frequency," and that kept me in it. Also, B's Arlene is a prop ("folded her hands on the table and waited for the rest"), where A's Arlene has the Pelkey culvert and "I don't need the figure, dear. Go on." That scene is thinner in B, but I didn't stop reading.

5. **Confidence: medium.** They share most of their lines. A wins the supper ("She told it by the calendar, the only way she told anything"; "she heard exactly which word had done it") and wins Arlene outright. B wins the radar call (A never says who speaks "Road agent, Kettle Lanes"), the green shirts still creased from the package, the tape, and the whole Kate call ("I say what he fixed"; "Is it your knee?"). The call is the climax, so it decides it.
