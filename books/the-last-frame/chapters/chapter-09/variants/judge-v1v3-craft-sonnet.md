A = v1.md, B = v3.md

1. Winner: B

2. Why: In the one scene that matters, the phone call, Kate finally sounds like a different person from her mother ("Two minutes. It's my mother." / "Then he's got more sense than you."), and the silence is written leaner and lands harder ("The hospital filled it for her").

3. Where A lost me: The silence paragraph on the call, about 92%. It starts "Ellen held the phone and waited. Nothing came down the line, no breath and no words, for so long..." and runs on past the point. Somewhere in the middle, at "She had the length of the quiet to the second, as she had every length, and she let it run," my thumb sped up. The paragraph is a catalog of hospital noises plus a speech about how restrained she is being, so it announces the restraint it is supposed to show. The "as she had every..." move is also a tic by then. It shows up as "as she had once known to the word," "as she had every minute of every day," and "as she had every length." Also, in A's call Ellen delivers the whole backstory in one tidy block ("There's a man who was behind me on the building...") and Kate mostly reacts, so the two women sound alike: short, flat, level.

4. Where B lost me: The Dale section opening at about 40%. "'These say four o'clock at the dock,' the derby treasurer said, with the raffle stubs already tipped into lane one's ball return." Then "A child in a snowsuit drew." I couldn't tell who the treasurer was, what dock, or what the child drew. It is a detail added for texture that makes me stop and decode. The Hollis exchange before it ("Nine inches at the access since midnight. Two more since three. Page two says I call the outdoor day.") is a mild skim too. Both versions share the relief briefing and readback at about 45%. That is the closest either gets to the A1 problem, a couple doing business in lists. I skimmed it in both.

5. Confidence: medium. The two share most of their text. A has the better kitchen: "at a speed that left no room for discussion," the snow-fence run, "this one would not take a slot," and the closing "Ellen opened her mouth to answer it." If the kitchen were all I weighed, I'd lean A. B takes it on the phone call, which is the scene I'd remember.

6. Same-author check: Yes. Same sentences, same tics ("the best sound she knew," "yellow nobody had argued with in forty years"), and the same scenes in the same order. They differ only in trims and in how much Kate gets to say.
