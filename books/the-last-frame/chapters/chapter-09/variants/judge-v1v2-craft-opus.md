3. **Where A lost me:** at about 61% my ear snagged on Denise's run-on, "Keep them, like December, and they're still telling that one at the diner, so bowl Linda's at two and the evening after, and I'll run this fryer till morning, and who pays for coffee? Nobody." I couldn't tell what December was until Ellen's reply, and even then only half. I drifted for real at about 74%, on "Ray's forearm lay on the counter by the box, and each captain came to her past his arm." That was about the fifth time his sleeve, arm or hand had been parked against hers in one scene ("his sleeve against her arm," "her hand lay flat on the counter by Ray's," "his arm still against hers," "his shoulder shook once against hers," "his hand flat on the counter beside hers"), and it had turned into a tic. The next line, "Thirty thousand stood where Judy had set it, every envelope went the wrong way from it, and Monday was two days off" (~77%), I had to read twice. A still has the best single lines across both texts: "they've buried him since, and I'm still thinking," "the only line on any bill of his she had let stand without a correction," and the jaw "shaved that morning and missed in one place." B has nothing as good as the doctor joke.

4. **Where B lost me:** at about 73%, when the envelopes turned into a count: "Two, three, four. Through the door glass the lot was showing gaps…" and then "Six, to a team of sisters from Nashua who argued over which of them would carry it. Seven, eight, nine." (~77%). It is a ledger, and Ray isn't in it. He drops out of the scene entirely while she hands out money. The sisters from Nashua saved it from being a list, but my thumb moved. Earlier, at about 11%, the stove-clock arithmetic ("The clock on his stove said fourteen past. That left sixteen minutes to the weather.") read like her procedure brain intruding on the date. It's in character, but I noticed it as a device the second time ("there were ten minutes yet").

5. **Confidence: medium.** The chapters share their skeleton and most of their lines. B wins on rhythm and clarity: the bare final exchange at the counter, the furnace-and-spoon beat before she stands, "it'll be my name that took it from her," and "There's someone" to Kate, which is the best romantic beat in either version. A wins on Ray's voice and the longing details. A judge who weights the couple being physically together in the counter scene could flip this.
