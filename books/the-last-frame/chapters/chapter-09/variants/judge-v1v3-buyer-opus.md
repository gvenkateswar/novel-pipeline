A = v3.md, B = v1.md

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** B is warmer where I care. His boot shifts against her knee when he laughs, she has told the knee story only "to an orthopedist and an insurance form," and on the phone she hears that her daughter is frightened and says "Nobody's hurt." A's Kate is sharper, but the call turns into a cross-examination about money and I read it with my thumb.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Weather's parked over the lake and Dale calls the hour, and the morning's off the lanes by half past twelve and the families have the settees, and the two evening squads are fifteen teams and every one of them is in the building." (~46%). That's the whole list read back to me a second time. My thumb had already started at Hollis's "Page two says I call the outdoor day" (~27%). It kicked in again at Kate's run of money questions near the end: "How much is more than half?" / "What's left?" (~90%). Then "Then he's got more sense than you" pulled me back.

4. **Where B lost me:** at the same spot, the readback starting "Weather's the band parked over the lake and Dale calling the hour, and the morning's off by half past twelve..." (~46%). I skimmed it like I skim it in A. But B got me back faster with "Half past twelve, fifteen, one: he had rounded nothing," and nothing after that lost me. The long silence on the phone, where she checks the screen to see if the call is still counting and thinks of Kate at fourteen skiing down for the patrol, held me the whole way.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two share nearly every line and lose me at the same briefing. B wins on the supper and the phone call, which are the two scenes I'd be reading for. A's storm crowd (the woman who came to hold the cup, the Lowell men who "never got a line in the water") is a little livelier than B's.

6. **Same-author check: yes.** Same scenes, same lines, often word for word. Only the trimming differs.
