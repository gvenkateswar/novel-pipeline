1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A keeps giving me a person to look at (Ray's "I varied the bowl," Dale who "had entered her evening," Kate's tenth-birthday story) in the places where B gives me a man's arm resting on a counter, so my thumb stayed slower in A.

3. **Where A lost me:** the relief briefing in the catwalk scene, at about 33%: "Traffic. A hundred and fifty in the building, forty-one of them children. Both morning squads are done. The Memorial at two, on all ten lanes." It is a list, and I skimmed it. I came back at "At the Center they call it the D-side," because that is the first line that sounds like her. I also slowed down in the envelope run at about 65% ("She said it to the second captain, and the third, and the fourth, in the same words"). Then HUTCHINS woke me up, and the "empty cash box, sixteen hundred" math was quick enough to pass.

4. **Where B lost me:** the same briefing, in almost the same words, at about 33%, so it is a wash on that one. B is worse at about 62-66%, in the counter stretch: "Ten envelopes stood on end in the cash box, the team names in Denise's round hand. Ray's forearm lay on the counter by the box, and each captain came to her past his arm. To each she said the same thing." Then comes "Thirty thousand stood where Judy had set it, every envelope went the wrong way from it, and Monday was two days off." That one is a ledger sentence, and my thumb moved on it. The repeated "arm against hers / hand flat on the counter beside hers" stage business reads like the couple being placed in the room, not being in it.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two share most of their skeleton and many identical lines, so it is close. A's extra warmth (the birthday story, Dale's line, the funnier chop suey bit) tips it for me. The call to Kate is also better in A, where it has something to say beyond the ask. B's phone call is tighter and cleaner, and a reader who wants speed could prefer it.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes, same lines, same mannerisms (the "I'll allow it" exchange, "Scale of one to ten" ending), so these are variants of one draft.
