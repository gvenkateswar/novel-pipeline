### The money, on the page
The reader holds exactly three money facts after Ch 1. **All three arrive inside the doorway argument between Ellen and Ray**, as things they throw at each other, never as an explanation:
1. **She owes Arlene $250,000 on March 1.** Ray: "You put your house down and Arlene carried the rest." That answers the sample reader's "why does she owe a quarter million on a building she bought this morning."
2. **The bank lends it to her if the winter clears $30,000.** Ellen says it herself, at him, as a boast: "Granite Lakes writes me the loan the day this winter clears thirty thousand." He: "Nobody's cleared thirty in this building since Normand."
3. **If it doesn't, Ray buys the building that afternoon.** Ray tells her he's Arlene's backup buyer: "Arlene's paid on March first. By you or by me." He tells her so that she hears it from him.

After Ch 1, each chapter carries **one** money fact, said in a scene between two people, and never added up by Ellen alone. Not on the page at all: Judy's cash test, monthly interest, the meals-tax account, pace percentages, net-operating-income language, slash-codes. Ellen never reads a tape alone.
