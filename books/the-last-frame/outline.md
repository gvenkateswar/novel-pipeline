## Open for the author

Each item is closed in the outline but needs the author's yes, because it changes a v1 approval or makes an exception to the brief.

1. **Ray's "first crack" becomes a backup sale contract that Ellen herself signed.** Arlene would carry the $250,000 only if the buyer also signed a contract to sell the building to Ray on March 1 at appraised value, if the refinance hadn't funded by then. Ellen signed it at the Dec 1 closing; it was the price of the note. Ray signed as buyer and keeps a lender commitment good through March 1. This replaces the recorded right of first refusal. Releasing the contract needs all three signatures (Ch 8). **Default: yes.** The domain-checker must confirm a conditional sale contract of this kind is plausible in NH. Fallback: an option agreement Ellen grants Ray at closing, exercisable March 1 on default.
2. **Ellen closes the last gap with her own savings.** About $15,000 paid onto the note at closing lowers the bank's required number below what the winter made. The bank requires the winter's cash to stay in the account at closing (planted Ch 2, restated Ch 10), so the paydown has to be new money. **Default: yes.** The domain-checker must confirm the paydown effect on the coverage bar and a same-day wire from savings.
3. **No ownership stake for Ray, kept** from v1 author decision 5. He never puts money in.
4. **Exception to the brief: one withheld fact (audit F12).** Ellen keeps from Ray that she watched him miss "unless the numbers say otherwise" from Ch 3 (Dec 17) to Ch 7 (Feb 2), and lies about it to his face in Ch 6. The reader knows throughout; it is dramatic irony and the book's central wrong. The brief's "no withheld secrets" is otherwise obeyed: nothing is withheld from the reader, and every other fact either lead holds reaches the other in the chapter it arises. **Default: approve the exception.**
5. **The ending: the winter doesn't clear the bar on its own (audit F14).** The winter makes about $28,900 against the bank's $30,000; Arlene's best winter was about $21,000. Ellen pays the difference down with her own money. Ellen says the figure to Ray in Ch 10 s3, so the reader judges the win knowingly. The angle promised that she makes the alley pay. She does, by a third over Arlene, but she doesn't make the bank's number without paying for it. **Default: approve.** Alternative: cut the storm refunds' size so the winter clears $30,000 by a few hundred, and drop the savings entirely. That loses the Act 3 cost the last audit required.
