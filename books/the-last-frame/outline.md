## Shape

### The book in one line
A retired air-traffic controller buys a failing candlepin alley she has one winter to make pay, and the widowed hardware man who lost it to her by eight days is the only one who can keep her machines running. Every week she succeeds is a week he doesn't get the building he planned his life around.

### The money, on the page
Money is the stakes, so it stays. It reaches the reader only as decisions people make with each other in scenes. Ellen never does arithmetic alone on the page. No tape-reading paragraphs, no pace sheets, no "two lines". Each chapter carries **one money fact**, named in its table, and no other figure.

The reader has to hold three things all book, and Ch 1 gives all three in dialogue:
1. Ellen owes Arlene $250,000 on March 1.
2. Granite Lakes Savings will lend it to her only if the winter clears $30,000. ("Up here the winter is the year.")
3. If it doesn't, she has to sell, and Ray gets first crack.

What is cut from the page: Judy's cash-on-hand test (it stays true in the bible but never binds, because the boiler is gone; Author decision 1), monthly interest, the meals-tax account, net-operating-income language, every pace percentage.

### Condemnable choices (map: at least one by a lead, with a cost)
1. **Ellen, Ch 3.** She sees that Ray didn't hear the second half of her promise ("unless the numbers say otherwise"). She doesn't repeat it, because he has just told her about Linda. She writes the lapse on a strip against herself, NO READBACK, files it, and kisses him at dawn. In Ch 6 she says "I said it" and leaves out that she watched him miss it. **Cost:** Ray, the key, a public confession in the league minutes (Ch 8), and the paid gate on the final (Ch 8's condition), which is the margin she needs on storm day.
2. **Ray, Ch 7.** He takes the worst night of her career, which she told him in the pit on storm night, and uses it on her: "You sounded fine on the radio that night too, I bet." **Cost:** he has to name it to her face in Ch 8, and he knows what kind of man says that.
3. **Ellen, Ch 3 (lesser).** She charges the stranded bowlers for bowling on storm night. Sleeping is free; strings are not. **Cost:** the Highway Department team's goodwill. It is half of why they walk in Ch 7.
4. **Arlene (registered, not a lead).** She sold a building that loses money two nights a week, behind summary statements, against a 90-day balloon. Ellen says so to her face in Ch 5.

### Subplots Ellen does not resolve
- **Denise and the Linda Gagnon Memorial.** Denise wins it herself, Sunday (Ch 10).
- **The two teams that walk** (Highway Department, the Legion). Ray brings them back at Pine Ridge (Ch 8). Ellen learns of it from Denise.
- **The derby through the storm.** Hollis makes the calls, both days (Ch 9, Ch 10).
- **Kate.** Left open. Kate thinks the alley was a mistake and says so in Ch 2, Ch 5 and Ch 10. She never comes round on the page. By Ch 10 she is still saying it, and she asks when ice-out is.

### Mystery fairness
The book withholds one thing: **why Arlene sold to Ellen.** Everything else is dramatic irony: the reader sees the NO READBACK strip written (Ch 3) and the release signed (Ch 8).
| Clue | Ch |
|---|---|
| Arlene on opening night, watching Ray re-rack his box: "He'd have kept it nice." She says nothing else | 01 |
| Ray, at the lesson, proud of it: lineage is what it was when Linda bowled | 04 |
| The party Saturday is the alley's best day. Denise, at close: "Now do a Tuesday." | 04 |
| Reveal: Arlene, Jan 1 | 05 (mid) |
An attentive reader can guess the league loses money by the end of Ch 4, one chapter before the reveal. The specific reason ("you'll charge them; he never would") stays unguessed until Arlene says it.

### Texture
One or two pieces of trade, sport, money or procedure per scene, chosen because they reveal character or move the romance. No slash-codes in the prose. Strips are described, not transcribed, except the one strip the plot turns on (NO READBACK), which is quoted once when written (Ch 3) and once when found (Ch 7). ATC phraseology set pieces: Ch 1 (PA), Ch 4 (radios, heard from Ray's side), Ch 9 (storm day). Everywhere else Ellen gets one phrase per scene at most.

1. **Cut the boiler (v1 Ch 6).** Default: **cut.** The midpoint reverses on Arlene's information, not a new cost, and Judy's cash test leaves the page (true in the bible, never binding). The basement kiss is replaced by the New Year's kiss.
