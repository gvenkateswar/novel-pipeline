# Reader Notes: Chapter 09, "The Last Frame"

## Where I got bored

- The weigh-in. "He wrote thirty-eight in the waterproof notebook with the golf pencil. Then he wrote the whole fish again on the next line, weight, length, depth, point, and smelt." By now I know Hollis writes everything down, so this tells me nothing new. Judy in the snowmobile suit is a nice payoff, but the scene stalls as soon as the fish is on the scale.
- The departure logistics. "Families with children, you're first... Halsey Falls, you're number two. Six o'clock tickets, Denise Lavoie has your money at the counter." Then the envelopes, then Kate on the refund sheet, then the phone. This is the climax, and a lot of it reads like a shift log.
- The treasurer's outburst. He goes over the same complaint Hollis's committee already raised in Ch 06. I could see "She had nothing to give him" coming.

## Where I got confused

- "At 4:10." Is that a.m. or p.m.? I only worked out it was morning when "Hollis on the back roads since three" turned up a page later.
- "Kettle Lanes, Pike." Hollis has been "Hollis" for eight chapters. For a beat I thought a new character was on the radio.
- "Halsey Falls, you're number two." Who is that? A party, a team, a town? Later Gil is "the last Halsey Falls coat," so I guess it's Pine Ridge's town. The book never told me.
- "At the Center they call it the D-side... We borrowed their words when ours were not good enough." Whose words, and borrowed from whom? This is the most important speech in the book, and I tripped on the middle of it.
- "I want to be asked," he said finally. Asked what? To stay, to marry her, to come back as the mechanic? She answers by putting the key in his palm, so I suppose it means "ask me back in." But I had to stop and decode it, at the moment I most wanted to feel something.
- "02/27. 0640. LANE 09." The scene takes place after 4:40 p.m. Is 0640 his time in from this morning? I read it twice.
- "Fryer first," Kate says, and then she goes straight to the coffee urn. I never found out about the fryer.
- "where he had stood on the second of February." I had to work out that this meant the league meeting.

## Where I stopped believing

- The money. At the end of Ch 08 she was $1,000 short on one line and $1,250 short on the other, and the 27th "had to do the rest." Then the 27th lost its evening, she refunded $1,140, Sunday closed, and she still came out at $30,850 and $25,600, clear on both. Nobody showed me where that money came from. After a whole book of careful arithmetic, the one sum that decides everything comes out right with no explanation.
- The bylaw that requires a fourth bowler, Danny being conveniently stuck at the store, and the sanction card from Ch 03 sitting in the drawer behind the first-aid kit. I liked the card as a callback. But three conveniences stack up to put Ellen on the lane.
- Ray giving the Memorial to the Open. He has resisted any change to that tournament for eight chapters. Here it happens in one PA exchange, with no scene where he decides. It felt like a gift that arrived on time, not a change he went through.

## Where it sounded like AI

- "She had bought the building for days like this one, a room that would fill with people who needed putting in order." This is at least the fourth time the book has told me that (Ch 01, 05, 07, now 09).
- "It came out in the voice. Every item stood in its place, and nothing in it shook. She had given a relief briefing at the end of every shift for thirty-one years and had never once left anything out." This explains a moment the dialogue had already shown me.
- "She had turned down his fifty-five thousand dollars... After today she would never know." Ch 08 already told me this.
- "There was something he meant to say to Ellen. It would keep until she had slept." This is a coy teaser line, written to set up the last chapter.
- Everyone is quippy in the same register. Kate: "I've been on a refrigerator." Denise: "That's the first thing you've said all winter I'd have said myself." Kate: "You were annoying all day, and you were great." Each of these is a polished little button.
- The chapter ends on "Count it twice." The ending is cute and neat, and so are most of the scene endings in this chapter.
- "Her eyes had gone wet." At the payoff moment I'm told her eyes are wet, but I'm not shown anything.

## What I want to know next

- Does Judy's underwriter accept it on Monday? "On paper" is doing a lot of work.
- What is the thing Ray "meant to say"? What does "I want to be asked" actually mean?
- Does Ellen ever say him the second half of the sentence she got wrong at the truck? (The car scene in Ch 07 is still the best thing in the book.)
- The March 1 square on the pool sheet. Arlene moving to The Pines. Arlene's brass key on its orange tag, which opens nothing. The dark K on the sign. Danny's "OCT" square on the wedding row.

The pull is real, but it is mostly "tie up the bows." The question of whether she survives is already answered, so the suspense is gone. What's left is curiosity about what the two of them say to each other.

## Best moment

> "You can't hold the floor by yourself today," he said. "I share the position. Today, and after. Whatever Judy says Monday."

This is the readback, the device the whole book has been building since Ch 02, and this time it is Ray reading her words back to her. Just before it is "Lane 9's sweep came across the deck on the last four words... She did not say them again." That pays off the December 11 promise being lost under the sweep in Ch 03. It is the best-built moment in the book.

Runner-up: Denise with the trophy. "Well, it's a nice enough—" She stopped and tried again.

## Did the climax belong to Ellen, or did she receive gifts?

It's split. The pit scene is hers. She asks for help, and that is the one thing she couldn't do all book, so it's an earned turn. Almost everything else that resolves the plot is handed to her. Ray won back the teams (Ch 08). Ray signed the release. Ray gave the Memorial to the Open. Kate took over the refunds. Denise won the trophy. The money closed without me seeing how. Her tenth-frame shot is nice, but her team loses, and that loss is the most honest beat in the chapter.

## Did anything feel too neat?

Yes. The money clearing both lines after the refunds. The sanction card turning up for the missing fourth bowler. Gil's "I counted it right this time." Kate approving of Ray in two lines. The final "Count it twice."

## Did the release reveal land?

Partly. I already knew Ray signed it (Ch 08), so the only new information is the date, the 16th. That date means she asked for help believing he could still take the building. That idea is strong, but the scene never lets her or me register it. She reads the stamp three times, her eyes go wet, and then "I want to be asked" muddies it. The emotional peak goes by too quietly to be sure of.

## What does the last chapter need to do?

It needs Judy's real verdict, with a chance it could fail. It needs Ellen saying the full sentence to Ray out loud, the one she got right only alone in the car. It needs a plain answer to "I want to be asked." And it needs to close March 1: the pool square, Arlene's key, the K on the sign. Do it fast and without more speeches about putting rooms in order.

## Verdict

The pit readback is the best scene in the book, and Denise's broken speech nearly matches it. Around them the chapter is crowded with logistics and conveniences, and it explains itself too much. The release, which should be the big emotional reveal, comes out muffled. Ellen earns the turn in her character, but the plot outcome is given to her. I'd finish the book, but I'd be reading the last chapter for warmth, not suspense.
