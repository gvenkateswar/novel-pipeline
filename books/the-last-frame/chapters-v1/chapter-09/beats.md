# Chapter 09 — The Last Frame: Beat Sheet

**POV:** Ellen (close third, past tense) for Scenes 1–5. Ray for Scene 6, after a section break. This is the chapter's one Ray section, and it ends on the key.
**Date:** Saturday, February 27, 4:10 a.m. to about 5:20 p.m.
**Target:** 3,600 words (the scene targets add up to 3,600)
**Chapter promise to set up:** Did she make it, and will one of them finally say it?

## Pre-flight flags (read before drafting)

1. **Where Ellen learns of the release (decided).** She learns in S6, after close, as the outline places it. Three conditions keep this inside rule 23:
   - (a) She has already made the D-side ask in S2, believing Ray gets the building if she fails.
   - (b) Her team has already lost the final.
   - (c) Her tape already shows both of Judy's figures (end of S5) before she sees the paper.

   The release therefore can't save anything and can't pay for anything. All it proves is that the building stood on its own, which is the reason Ray signed it (Ch 08). Judy's decision is still two days off, so this isn't the finish line either. Nobody on the page says any of this (rule 16).
2. **The forced choice: a new mechanism, inside the outline.** Plans A–D covered ice, a storm, roads closed before opening, and no power. In S3, Hollis brings word that **the town is pulling its trucks off Kettle Road at 4 p.m.** At that point there are about 150 people inside, children among them, and the final is on the strip for 6 p.m. No column on the board covers a road closing while the house is full. Ellen makes the decision herself:
   - the final moves to **2:30** on lanes 5–6
   - the raffle is drawn at 2:00
   - departures run in sequence from 2:15, and the building is empty by 4
   - **every 6 p.m. final-night ticket is refunded in full**, because the ticket says six and nobody is getting six
   - **Sunday is closed**

   Before she decides, the full day clears both lines on her tape. Once she gives up the evening and Sunday, she can't tell which side of either line she'll land on. That is the cost: she gives up a sure thing for an uncertain one. Who wins and who loses is unchanged, so this is not an outline-plot change.
3. **Why the numbers close on Saturday (new fact; check against Ch 10).** With Sunday Feb 28 closed for the roads, Saturday's close is the end of the quarter. The S5 tape is the Feb 28 figure: **$30,850 / $25,600**. The arithmetic: the Ch 08 sheet stood at $29,000 / $23,750 without the 27th, and the day as run nets $1,850, which lands on both. The evening and Sunday Ellen gave up were worth about $1,900. Ch 10's "books closed Sunday night from the register reports" still works, since Sunday is when she does the paperwork. The margin is $850 and $600, which is not a cute margin.
4. **Denise's team: needs confirming (default supplied).** The log never names it. **Default: the Lakeview Diner** (a Thursday team; it slept on lanes 5–6 in Ch 04, where Denise bowled by flashlight). The championship draws from both divisions, so a Thursday team is legal. The continuity-checker should confirm this against the Ch 04 final. The final is **Gagnon & Son vs. the Lakeview Diner on lanes 5–6**, the same pair as Ellen's first ball in Ch 01. Leave that echo unremarked.
5. **The substitute ruling needs one invented bylaw (rule 14).** Ray could otherwise take a blind score, as the Tuesday league did in Ch 07. So Verna's binder says: **no blind scores in a championship final, and a substitute must be a sanctioned bowler on no other roster this season.** Everyone sanctioned in the house is rostered except Ellen (Denise's Ch 03 plant). The choice is Ellen bowls or Gagnon & Son forfeits. Ray offers to forfeit. **Ellen refuses the forfeit and puts her card on Verna's binder.** That decision is hers, and it costs her: she bowls in front of the house and leaves the counter in a rush. Keep handicap arithmetic off the page. Verna assigns "the league's average for a bowler with none, 1991," with no number given.
6. **Judy at the weigh-in.** The bank's title rundown for a March 1 closing could plausibly surface the recorded release. Judy says nothing about the loan or the title. "I'm here as a fisherman" is the most she says to Ellen. Don't wink at it.
7. **The key versus rule 17.** Ray's key ring appears once, in S6, when he takes the back-door key off it. It is mid-scene and never the closing image. The chapter ends on dialogue after the key, so "ends on the key" holds without breaking rules 17 and 18.
8. **Linda's ball (Ch 08, flag 7).** Ray bowls the final with Linda's ball from the blue bag. Give it one clause and no reaction from anyone. **Never mention locker 14.**
9. **Banned this chapter:** the word "love," from anyone, Kate included (save it for Ch 10); "Mrs." or "Ms. Daly" from anyone (Verna calls her "the substitute"; Ray's "Mrs. Daly" belongs to Ch 10); Hollis's cap tic (the reader flagged it); "Fella I knew"; scenes ending on a question or a cut-off line (the reader flagged both as tics); any kiss or embrace; anyone being impressed by a readback (retired, rule 17).
10. **Rule 21.** Ellen's Feb 20 call to Ray happened off page. It gets one clause in S1 (she made it herself and booked him by work order, rate per agreement). Don't quote the call. In S6 the release is a sentence and a stamp. Don't re-stage Leclerc's office.

### Scene 1 — HOLD SHORT
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes counter, 4:10 a.m. The radar loop is on Ellen's phone. Snow blows sideways under the lot lights, and the glass-block band is going white from the bottom up. The whiteboard from Ch 08 leans on the lanes 1–2 settee. Under every column, `MX:` now reads `R.G.` in her capitals. One of the four handheld radios sits on the counter on channel 2. Weather is the plot here, so a weather opening is allowed.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Kate, Hollis (on the radio only)
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late.** Hollis's voice comes from the plow cab. He's been out since three (his contract, Ch 08). Wind takes half a sentence. Ellen: **"Say again."** (ATC 1). He calls it in measurements: inches down, inches forecast, gusts, whiteout on the flats. The outdoor day is off. Indoor events go to Kettle Lanes, and Friday fish get weighed at lane 10. Plan B. He'll stop in to run the weigh-in "between passes" and won't be bowling.
- Ellen circles B. One clause covers the board's MX line: she made the call herself on the twentieth and booked Ray by work order at the agreed rate. That's all.
- **Comic middle.** Kate is up because her body runs on nights. She came up Friday on her days off "to see the thing" and is staying at the camp. She triages the building out loud in order of severity, starting with the fryer. Then she reads the board: "Who's R.G.?" Ellen tells her exactly, and in the fewest words possible.
- Kate's doubt, sideways: she asks her mother, on a scale of one to ten, how much of today is one person.

**Interior passage 1 (Ellen, 3–4 sentences):** Why she bought the place. A room that would fill with people who need sequencing, and it was hers by choice. The fear goes underneath: on a day like this her calm will sound perfect right up until it's too late. Plain, and in her own idiom.

**Subtext:** Kate is asking the question Ellen's whole winter has been built to avoid.
**Tension source:** Situational (the storm) and interpersonal (Kate's doubt, gently put).
**Ends on:** Mid-motion. Kate starts the 40-cup urn without being asked and asks where the filters are. Ellen gives her the shelf and its position, exactly. Cut there.
**Word count target:** 400

### Scene 2 — RELIEF BRIEFING (THE D-SIDE ASK)
**Setting:** The floor at 10:40 a.m., then the pit. Ellen counts 147 people in snow boots on the way through. Coolers sit by lane 10, and wet wool and road salt are everywhere. The Open's early rounds are running, and the waiting list on the strip board is three cards deep. Two tables are acting up, lane 3's clutch and lane 9's table. In the pit: the drop light, the coffee can of bolts, and Ray's tape labels.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Denise, Ray, the 112 (walk-on), Taylor's Marina (as a group)
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late, at the counter.** The returning Tuesday teams pay their $12.50 at the counter for the Open's league round and grumble about it. **The 112 gets the chapter's one sour line**, about paying twelve-fifty to bowl in a blizzard. **Denise answers it** in one salty line and doesn't look up from the register. (That's the comic middle. Denise's line should be the sharper one.)
- The fryer is three tickets behind, and the popcorn machine and space heater are about to trip the breaker again (Ch 03/04). **Ellen: "Ground stop on the snack bar."** (ATC 2). No new orders for fifteen minutes while Denise clears the backlog. Denise approves, which is a first.
- Lane 9's table hangs again. Ellen goes through the steel door by lane 10, which sticks (Ch 02).
- **The pit.** Ray has lane 3's cover off. She gives him the **relief briefing** (ATC 3, in narration or dialogue) in **the voice** (its one use this chapter): 147 in the building, lanes 3 and 9, the snack bar stopped, Hollis out since three, the weigh-in at noon, the final at six. It's complete, ordered, and perfect.
- **Then she stops briefing and asks.** The voice goes, and she can't get it back. When she's upset she slows down and drops her contractions. She says she cannot hold the floor by herself today. That's the line she can't take back (rule 22, the quiet half). **"At the Center they call it the D-side. We borrowed their words when ours weren't good enough."** (ATC 4; keep it at least two sentences away from the briefing phrase.) She asks him to share the position, today and after, **whatever Judy says Monday.**
- **Lane 9's sweep comes down on the end of her sentence**, the way the sweep covered her tail in Ch 03. She doesn't repeat it.
- **Ray's readback is his yes.** He reads her back every part, in order, **including "whatever Judy says Monday."** Nobody comments on it and the narration doesn't explain it (rule 16). He does not mention the release.
- Practical, untidy close: he names the order of work, lane 9 and then lane 3, and hands her the drop light to hold.

**Interior passage 2 (Ellen, 3–4 sentences), before she asks:** What asking costs her. She turned down fifty-five thousand dollars to find out what she could carry, and after today she will never know. If she misses Judy's lines by any amount, the man she is asking gets the building, and she is asking him anyway. Don't state the release or hint at it.

**Subtext:** She is giving up the one thing she refused his money to keep, which is knowing she did it alone. He is keeping back the one thing that would make the asking cheaper.
**Tension source:** Internal (her want against her need) and interpersonal (the first real words since Feb 2).
**Ends on:** Cut away mid-motion. Ellen holds the drop light on lane 9's table while Ray works, and the PA hums. Denise, over the house: the fish are here, Ellen to lane 10. Cut on the PA. Don't end on the readback, because it's the tidiest line in the scene (rule 18).
**Word count target:** 750

### Scene 3 — WEIGH-IN (THE FORCED CHOICE)
**Setting:** Lane 10, 12:20 to about 1:20 p.m. Coolers of lake trout and cusk sit on the approach, and the derby scale stands on the scorer's table. The derby treasurer is there with his gray cash box (Ch 06). The raffle woman has her ticket roll. The kids' division bowls free on lanes 7–8 (Ellen's Ch 06 concession). Hollis is in from the plow with snow on his shoulders. This is ATC set piece 3 of 3: the derby weigh-in at a bowling alley.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Hollis, Judy, the derby treasurer (walk-on), Denise, Kate; Ray on the radio
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late, mid-weigh-in. The Hollis–Judy seed (Book 3).** Judy sets her Friday lake trout on the scale. They speak only in weights, lengths, and depths. Hollis writes her fish in the waterproof notebook (its one use this chapter), and then writes it again. Neither of them remarks on it. That's the comic middle.
- Judy, to Ellen, one line: she's here as a fisherman today (flag 6).
- **The news.** Hollis's truck radio: drifting at the Dock Road bend, and the town is pulling its trucks off Kettle Road at four. He gives it in measurements. He'll be back for her lot at five-thirty and not before, and he doesn't want anyone in a car after four. No crash and no accident motivates this (rule 8).
- Ellen looks at the whiteboard. No column covers it. One sentence, with no "none of the plans" narration.
- **Everyone has an answer, quickly.** Denise wants to keep the evening tickets, since half the holders are already in the building. The treasurer wants the raffle drawn at six as advertised. Kate says the kids go first, now. **Ray, on the radio, gives information only:** lanes 3 and 9 will hold till four, not past. He offers no opinion on money.
- **Ellen decides (flag 2).** The final at 2:30, the raffle at 2:00, departures from 2:15, empty by four, every evening ticket refunded in full, and Sunday closed. She crosses out 1800 on the 02/27 LGM strip and writes 1430. REMARKS stays blank.
- **Rule 22, the loud half.** The treasurer loses his composure. The derby's door sales between five and six are its year. He shouts at Hollis that he called their ice at four in the morning and now he's calling their draw. That line hits Hollis's wound. **Ellen takes it in public:** the draw time is her building's call, and Mr. Pike called the ice. (This closes Hollis's thread: nobody blames him.) She has no answer to the treasurer's point about the door money, because he's right (rule 20).
- Optional, once only: on his way back out to the plow, Hollis says, "That's Plan E." Ellen says it's Plan E with a final in it. Drop it if it reads as a checklist.
- **The PA.** Ellen sequences the departures: families with children first; **Halsey Falls, "you're number two"** (ATC 5); and so on. It's precise and quick.

**Interior passage 3 (Ellen, 3 sentences), at the decision:** The tape in her head. With the evening and Sunday she clears both of Judy's lines. Without them she can't say which side of either line she lands on. She chooses anyway, because the tickets say six o'clock and she doesn't take money she didn't earn. Don't state that last reason as a moral. Show it in what she says over the PA.

**Subtext:** She's spending her margin on other people's safety and on a promise printed on a ticket, with nothing to back her up.
**Tension source:** Situational (the clock and the road) and interpersonal (the treasurer, Denise's objection).
**Ends on:** Mid-conversation, at the counter. Denise is already filling refund envelopes and says roughly how much is walking out the door (one figure, said by Denise). Ellen tells her to label the envelopes. This can be the chapter's one quip ending.
**Word count target:** 700

### Scene 4 — SAY REQUEST
**Setting:** 2:00 to 2:30 p.m. The raffle is drawn from the ball return on lane 7–8: the ticket roll goes in and a kids'-division bowler pulls the winner off the return. Families are leaving in sequence and boots are going out the vestibule. Every lane but 5 and 6 is going dark. The counter has the gooseneck PA.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Ray, Verna, Kate, Denise
**Surface action:**
- **Enter on the raffle**, two or three lines of texture at most (trim this first if the chapter runs long).
- **The trophy, by PA request.** Ray comes up the side and stands at Ellen's counter, on her side, as he did in Ch 07, and leans to her gooseneck: **"Kettle Lanes, Gagnon. Request."** Ellen, a foot away, into the same mic: **"Gagnon, say request."** (ATC 6, the last one.) The comedy is two people a foot apart talking over a PA. Ray asks that the Linda Gagnon Memorial go to the Open, this year and every year after, for anyone who pays the entry. He gives no reason. Ellen answers yes in plain English. Verna records it as a gift (an echo of her Ch 04 ruling; one clause).
- **The substitute.** Verna, in her lavender parka, with the binder: no blind scores in a final, and a substitute must be sanctioned and on no other roster (flag 5). She reads the precedent from the minutes in her small, cheerful voice. That's the comic middle. Ray says Gagnon & Son will forfeit.
- **Ellen's move.** She refuses the forfeit and puts her sanction card (Ch 03) on Verna's binder. Verna checks the date and calls her "the substitute" from then on. Ellen knows exactly what she is: an owner with an average no one has written down, about to bowl in front of everyone still in the building, with the counter in a rush.
- **The counter.** Denise won't leave the counter in a rush (bible). Kate takes it: she goes behind the counter and tells Denise to go win something. Ellen hands Kate the departure list.
- **Kate–Ray, one exchange (bible).** Kate reads him like a patient, approves of him by her second sentence, and then says something embarrassing about her mother and the strip board. **No "love," no ex-husband.** Ray goes quiet.

**Subtext:** Ray gives the thing he froze to the change he fought. Ellen puts her own pride on the lane rather than let his team forfeit Linda's first Open.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal (Verna's procedure, the forfeit) and internal (Ellen is about to be bad at something in public).
**Ends on:** Mid-motion. Ellen walks down to lane 5 with a house ball while Kate, behind the counter, picks up the ringing phone and answers it in her mother's exact words. Cut there.
**Word count target:** 550

### Scene 5 — THE TENTH FRAME
**Setting:** Lanes 5–6, about 2:30 to 3:55 p.m., then the office at 4:40. Thirty-odd spectators are left on the burnt-orange settees, and most of the town is already on Kettle Road in sequence. The Linda Gagnon Memorial sits on the scorer's table. Paper score sheets; Verna keeps the official one.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Ray, Denise, the Lakeview Diner and Gagnon & Son (as groups), Gil, Kate, Verna
**Surface action:**
- **Compress strings one and two (a paragraph each).** Ellen lob-fouls in string one, the foul light comes on, and the pins don't count. Denise, on the next lane, grades her out loud. That's the comic middle. In string two she goes low on her own. Don't repeat "Low. Let the lane have it." Ray unzips the blue bag on the settee and bowls with Linda's ball (flag 8; one clause).
- **Ellen's tenth frame, string three.** First ball chops. Second leaves wood. Before the third she says it aloud under her breath, to no one: **"The wood stays on the lane. You play what's left."** (Full line, use 3 of 3. Nobody reacts, and it isn't framed as a readback.) The third ball drives a fallen pin through what's standing. Describe one pin doing its job, different from Ch 08's gate.
- **Gagnon & Son still loses**, by a clear margin (twenty-some pins with handicap). Ellen's frame doesn't decide it either way. Nobody wins on luck (rule 22).
- **Denise wins the Linda Gagnon Memorial.** Ray hands it to her. **The book's one ovation**, from the thirty people left. **Denise loses her composure:** she starts a compliment with its usual qualifier and can't finish either half. Rule 22, the third and warmest instance.
- **Gil's one line**, coat on, last of the Halsey Falls departures. It should land on her spare conversions and, ideally, call back her "Second ball" correction from Ch 08. Then he's gone.
- **Kate comes around**, at the vestibule, the last of the departures, riding out with Denise, who lives past the narrows. Ellen sequenced her own daughter like everyone else. Kate: "You were great. You were annoying, but you were great." (bible).
- **4:40, the office, alone.** Ellen runs the tape with the refunds out and Sunday at zero, twice, and gets the same figures both times: **$30,850. $25,600.** Over by $850 and $600 on paper. Paper isn't Judy. She stays standing. One sentence covers both numbers. No relief speech.

**Interior passage 4 (Ellen, 2–3 sentences), on the approach before the third ball:** One plain thought about this being the first thing in the building she has done badly in front of everyone, and doing it anyway. No epiphany.

**Subtext:** The day's outcome belongs to Denise and the tape belongs to Judy. The only thing that's Ellen's tonight is what she does next.
**Tension source:** Situational (the final, the clock) and internal (the tape).
**Ends on:** A placed object, the chapter's one still ending (rule 18). The 02/27 LGM strip has had REMARKS blank since Ch 03. She writes a plain factual remark (the moved time, the weather, the winner's name, nothing sentimental) and pins it back at the end of the board.
**Word count target:** 700

### Scene 6 — THE KEY (RAY)
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, about 5:05 to 5:20 p.m. The building is empty, the lanes are dark, and snow is still coming. Ray has spent the last hour putting lane 9's table right properly. The pegboard by the pit door holds the R.G. — AFTER HOURS card. In the office: the steel desk, the adding machine, the tape torn off, and the door half of the raffle cash in a stack. Hollis is due at 5:30 for the lot (his contract), and nobody else is coming.
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late. Comic middle, early.** Ray logs himself on the card: date, time in, lane 09. He stands with the pencil over REASON and leaves it blank. Ellen, from the office door, notes that his reason column is blank. He doesn't have one that fits. (This plants Ch 10's "WAITING.")
- **The key and the release.** He takes the back-door key off his ring, the ring's one appearance (flag 7). Arlene's orange-tag key stays on it, unremarked. He lays the back-door key on the desk with his copy of the release, which carries the registry's book-and-page stamp and is dated **February 16, eleven days ago**. He tells her what it is in one sentence (rule 21).
- **She reads it the way she reads everything:** the date and then the stamp, twice. Her voice goes slow, with no contractions. She asked him this morning believing he would get the building. **Ray: he knows. He read it back.**
- **Tears at the key (outline), with restraint.** In Ray's POV: she takes her reading glasses off by the cord. Her eyes are wet and her voice stays level, and he has heard all day what holding that voice level costs her. Don't use the phrase "the voice." Ray goes quiet, which is what he does.
- **"I don't want to own it anymore. I want to be asked."** (outline/bible). The important words come last.
- **Her answer is an action.** She puts the key back in his hand and closes his fingers over it. It's his own deciding tic, done for him by someone else. Leave it unremarked.
- **She sits down**, in the desk chair, for the first time all day (bible: she sits only when the decision is made). Ray notices only that she hadn't sat all day.

**Interior passage 5 (Ray, 2–3 sentences):** Why he didn't tell her in the pit. She had a hundred and fifty people to get down Kettle Road, and the paper wouldn't have moved one of them. Write it directly, with no "it wasn't X" construction.
**Interior passage 6 (Ray, 2–3 sentences):** Linda's trophy is in Denise's car on Kettle Road, and he is the one who handed it over. One plain observation. Don't explain what it means (rule 16). Optionally, one clause noting there is something he means to say to her and that it will keep. Don't name it, and don't use the word "love."

**Subtext:** She asked before she knew. He signed before she asked. Neither of them says so, and the release date and the S2 readback carry it.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. He held something back from her all day, and she asked him at full cost.
**Ends on:** Mid-motion dialogue, deliberately less tidy than "I want to be asked." She pushes half the raffle stack across the desk to him: the derby's half goes in the HELD bag, and he should count it twice. (This plants Judy's Ch 10 approval of the HELD account.) The last line is hers, about the money. It isn't a question, a cut-off, a light, a car, or a lock.
**Word count target:** 500
