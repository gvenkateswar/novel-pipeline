# Reader Notes: Chapter 01, "Cleared to Bowl"

Read as a Kindle buyer who knows only the premise: a retired air-traffic controller buys a failing small-town bowling alley.

## Where I got bored

- **The opening page.** "The deed had carried Ellen's name since nine-forty that morning" is a good first line. Then I spend four paragraphs watching her re-label switches, write flight-strip columns ("04 / 1830 / LG / 4 / —"), put up pegboard and fix foul-light labels. It tells me who she is, but nothing is happening yet and nobody else is in the room. My attention picked up only when Denise stamped her boots.
- **"In the office she lifted a fuel-oil company calendar off its nail... She looked at the number for a while."** This is set up as meaningful, but I don't know why it matters, so it reads like a pause where nothing happens.
- **The lane 7 timing paragraph.** "Ball, crash, the thunk of the sweep, the table down and back up. Seven seconds, never more than seven and a half. Lane 7 took nine. Its next cycle took eleven..." I get that she notices things. But after a whole chapter of exact times (9:40, 4:30, 5:15, 8:10, 8:11, 10:30, 10:55, "one second by her watch"), the numbers stopped telling me anything about her and became a habit I was skimming past.

## Where I got confused

- **What kind of bowling is this?** This was my biggest problem. The ball has "no finger holes" and fits in her palm. People throw three balls a frame. Pins stay lying "across the deck." The pins are "as thin as their name." I eventually guessed this is some regional variant (candlepin?), but the book never says so. I spent the whole ball-throw scene picturing ten-pin bowling and getting corrected. "Seven gets its lineage back" made no sense to me at all.
- **"Two," Ellen said, under her breath.** Two what? Denise stamps four times. Is Ellen counting arrivals? Making a bet with herself? I reread it and still don't know.
- **Who Denise is.** She's been there 22 years and she does the shoes, so I guess she's staff, but the chapter never says so. Is she Ellen's employee now? Did Ellen hire her or inherit her?
- **The locksmith call.** "She looked at the stitching on his jacket and dialed." That made me think she knew she was calling Ray's business on purpose. Then "Say again." and "He's here." make it sound like she's surprised. I couldn't tell whether she was making a point or got caught out, and it changes how I read her.
- **"Who invited her?" the big man said, and answered himself. "I did."** I had to stop and work out who "her" was. The Auxiliary captain's line clears it up, but only on a second read.
- **The note and the right of first refusal.** "If you miss, you have to sell, and Ray gets first crack." So Arlene financed the sale and Ray gets first crack? Why Ray? This is the main stakes of the book and it arrives in two lines of dialogue near the end that I mostly had to piece together.
- **The refund.** "Counted it out into three small piles." How much? I couldn't tell whether this was a big gesture or a small one, and the chapter treats it as big.

## Where I stopped believing

- **The ball jumping into lane 6 and clearing exactly Ray's leftover pins, including the one lying down.** It's slapstick, and I'd accept it in a comedy, but it's very convenient. It was clearly built so the ball would land on *his* lane.
- **The town's one locksmith being the man standing on her mat.** In a small town that's plausible, and Danny's handling of it is funny, so I mostly went along with it. But between that and the ball, Ray keeps turning up wherever the plot needs him.
- **Everyone talks the same way.** Ellen is clipped because she was ATC. Fine. But Ray is clipped ("Some." "Sold you." "Not my alley."), Danny is clipped, Hollis is clipped ("You fly?" "Lane five?"), and Tibbetts is clipped. Denise is the only one with a different rhythm, and even she mostly speaks in short deadpan zingers. A whole town of laconic people makes Ellen's precision stand out less.
- **Ellen bought a bowling alley having never bowled, and we get no reason why.** I'll accept the premise, but I didn't get even a hint of why *this* building or *this* town, so her whole project feels arbitrary to me so far.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Every scene ends on a button line.** "Twenty-two years. They've had numbers on them." / "He's punctual." / "Ms." "He was already sitting down." / "I'll note it in the minutes." By the third scene I was waiting for the zinger, and it always came.
- **The ATC jokes come at a steady pace and feel planned:** "Roger, tower," "say our altitude," "Kettle Tower, requesting a hot dog," "Negative," "Lane seven is unable," "Cleared to bowl." Some are funny (the altitude reply made me smile). Together they feel like a premise being worked through in order.
- **"When she spoke, she used the voice. Thirty-one years of it: low, level, every word given the same room as the one before."** This is a nice line, but it's the narrator explaining the character trait the whole chapter has already shown me.
- **"Nobody made a sound for one second by her watch. Then forty bowlers did, and her ears went hot."** That last clause is the only physical feeling Ellen has in the chapter, and it reads as if it was added to make her seem human.
- **Ellen's inner life is almost absent.** We get her lists, her times and her labels. We don't get one thought about her past, her loneliness, her fear of failing, or anything she wants besides making it to March 1. For a romance I expected at least a flicker of feeling, and there wasn't one.

## What I want to know next

- **Ray and Linda.** The knuckle on the trophy-case glass and the LINDA GAGNON MEMORIAL plate hit me. Is he a widower? Is the bowling alley where she's most present for him? That would explain why he won't hand over the key.
- **Who fixes lane 7 now** that the only mechanic just said "Not my alley"?
- **Why won't Ray buy a square in the pool on his own wall?** Good hook.
- **Who has Christmas Eve?** Denise offered, Ellen said no, and I want to know.
- **Why did Ellen do this at all?** Where did she come from, and is anyone waiting at home?
- **The fuel-oil calendar.** Unpaid bills? Arlene stopped turning pages in January 2019?
- **The ending pulls me forward.** Ray waits in the lot with the engine running until her car starts, then leaves. That's the first sign he's anything other than an obstacle, and it's the closest thing to romance in the chapter.

I do want to turn the page. But mostly it's for Ray's story and the town. I don't have much investment in Ellen yet, and I'm not yet sure this is a romance at all.

## Best moment

The phone call:

> "Gagnon and Son." A younger voice, level.
> ...
> "Ms. Daly, the locksmith's my father."
>
> She didn't answer.
>
> "If you need him sooner than tomorrow," the voice went on, "try your front door."

Then Ray is standing on the mat, "examining the corkboard of lost mittens," and finally says "He's punctual." This was the funniest exchange in the chapter, and it set up the father, the son, the key and the small town all at once.

The close runs a near second: "Across the lot, the pickup's lights came on. It rolled down to Kettle Road and turned toward town, and its taillights got smaller until the trees took them." It's quiet and it isn't over-explained, and it changed how I felt about Ray.
