# Chapter 04 — Ground Stop: Beat Sheet

**POV:** Ray (close third, past tense)
**Dates:** Thursday, January 7, 8:50 p.m., to Friday, January 8, about 6:30 a.m.
**Target:** 3,200 words (scene targets sum to 3,200)
**Chapter promise to set up:** What happens to these two when the snow stops?

---

## Pre-flight flags (read before drafting)

1. **Style Sample 4 overlaps with the outline's set piece.** The bible's Sample 4 already writes the pin-in-the-lap moment ("Traffic, twelve o'clock, one mile," then the laugh). The bible says not to reuse the samples' lines or details, but the outline requires this beat. **Resolution:** keep the outline beat (a deadwood pin rolls into Ray's lap, Ellen makes the traffic call, Ray laughs). Do **not** reuse anything else from Sample 4: not "You're terrible at this," not Arlene hand-setting in 1972, not "Linda would have liked you / hated how you rack," not the hot dogs sold at an unapproved price, and not the hand that doesn't let go. Sample 4 is written from Ellen's POV. This chapter is Ray's.
2. **Pace figure.** The bible lists Ch 04 at "~95%." A straight line to $30,000 on Jan 7 is about $12,670, so $11,800 is about 93%. **Don't put a percentage on the page.** The reader complained about the math in Ch 03. The only money figure on the page is **$11,800**, and Ray reads it upside down (S5). Cash ($36,650) stays off the page, and so does lineage (the bible keeps the $7.50 figure for Ch 05 and Ch 07).
3. **The two closed days.** "Storm closes the alley for two days." Mechanism: a pine across the line on Kettle Road. The utility's recorded estimate, heard on Arlene's rotary phone (copper landline, which works in an outage), puts restoration at Saturday evening. That kills **Friday's and Saturday's glow nights**, the two best nights of her week. The alley reopens Sunday.
4. **Verna and Hollis.** The outline doesn't list Verna in Ch 04, but she is league secretary and the Thursday league is in session when the storm hits. She gets **two short beats only** (a bylaws ruling in S2, adjourning the session at dawn in S5). No gavel, no reading minutes from 1987, no Robert's Rules routine. That is all saved for Ch 07. Hollis appears in S1, S4 (briefly) and S6. **Save his license-check questioning and "You courting her" for Ch 06.** Here he only measures and asks plain questions.
5. **Head count.** 31 bowlers stranded (nine Thursday bowlers who live in the center left at 8:15, when the town radio said the trucks were pulling off). In the building: 31 bowlers + Ray (coaching; Thursday isn't his team's night) + Hollis + Denise + Verna (secretary, not bowling) + Ellen = **36**. Ellen counts 36. Her number to the town is 36.
6. **No secrets carried.** Ray's lender email (S6) isn't a secret from Ellen. She has known since Ch 01 that he has financing and the right of first refusal. It shows the reader his want is alive. Nobody hides anything.
7. **Ch 05 must stay Ellen's discovery.** S3 may carry **one** breadcrumb ("Arlene never charged the league for a lot of things," which Ray means as praise). No numbers, no "lineage," no "frozen," no "2004." Ellen glances at the ledger shelf. Nobody explains it.
8. **Save for later chapters:** "relief briefing" and "the D-side" (Ch 09); "the voice" (banned in Ch 04); the loss-of-separation memory (Ch 07). Ellen's "last day on the scope" is her retirement shift, a different night entirely.
9. **No invoice haggling.** The hand-setting isn't a mechanic call. If money comes up, Ray says "Wasn't a call" once and it's dropped. The Ch 03 reader called the invoice routine sitcom.

### Scene 1
**Setting:** The bottom of the Kettle Lanes driveway at Kettle Road, Thursday, Jan 7, 8:50 p.m. A nor'easter coming in sideways off the lake. Hollis's plow truck sits jackknifed across the foot of the drive with the blade down and dug in. Its amber light turns the snow orange, then not. Up the hill the sign buzzes in the cold, its dead K the one mention this chapter.
**Characters present:** Ray, Hollis, Ellen (arrives mid-scene)
**Surface action:**
- **Open on the truck.** Hollis's plow is stuck sideways with the blade down, snow coming faster than he can push it. Ray is already out there with no hat. He heard it from lane 6: the blade scraping, then the engine pitch going up and nothing moving. He diagnosed it by sound before he got his coat.
- **Why Hollis is there (rule 14).** Ellen contracted Hollis in December to keep the lot open on league nights. He came at 8:40 to clear it for close. The drive runs downhill to Kettle Road, and the truck came round on the slope.
- **Comic middle.** Ray asks if he's hurt. Hollis checks the sky before he answers. Then he lays his tape on the hood: "Eleven. Two an hour, and it hasn't made up its mind about stopping." He writes it in the waterproof notebook with the golf pencil (first appearance of the notebook, which pays off in Ch 06 and Ch 09). The town pulled its trucks off Kettle Road at 8:30 until the wind drops. Nothing tows tonight.
- **Ellen comes down the drive** in her vest under a parka, with a flashlight, and doesn't slip. She and Hollis settle the whole situation in about four exchanges, in measurements and yes-or-no. Is the road closed? It is. Is anyone leaving? Not past him. Ray stands outside the conversation holding a shovel nobody asked for. That's the beat: two safety people who don't need him, and he minds it for a reason he doesn't name.
- **Required plant (the derby).** Ellen asks what this much snow does to the ice. Hollis: snow on new ice insulates it and slows the making. He's got a derby the last weekend of February. "Same weekend as your championship." Ellen asks how many people come to a derby. "Twelve hundred, a good year." She doesn't write it down. Ray watches her not write it down, and that's how he knows she kept it.
**Interior passage 1 (Ray, 2–4 sentences):** He has stood in this lot in every storm since 1996, opening on league nights with Arlene's key. Linda drove herself to league in worse than this and called him from the counter to tell him the roads were fine, which they never were. Keep it in tolerances and weather, not grief.
**Subtext:** Ray is in her driveway in a storm, and there is no machine to fix. Ellen has a man she pays for plowing and a man she pays for pinsetters, and is deciding how much of the night belongs to either.
**Tension source:** Situational (36 people, one blocked drive, two inches an hour). Interpersonal undertow: Ray is a spare part in a conversation between two professionals.
**Ends on:** Cut early, mid-conversation. Ellen asks Hollis how many inches by morning. Hollis takes his cap off to think. Cut before he puts it back on to answer.
**Word count target:** 500

### Scene 2
**Setting:** The counter and the floor, 9:20 p.m. All ten lanes lit and every one of them idle. Thirty-one bowlers in coats on the orange settees, wet wool and road salt on the mat, the 40-cup urn going. The PA hums before anyone speaks. Ellen stands at the gooseneck mic. Denise is behind the snack bar.
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen, Denise, Verna, Hollis; the Thursday league (Highway Department and Lakeview Diner bowlers are the only teams named)
**Surface action:**
- Ellen counts the room. Ray watches her lips move: thirty-six. She calls the town's emergency line on the rotary phone and reports 36 people sheltering in a heated building with food and water. The town says stay put. Keep it to one line. No Red Cross, no cots.
- **ATC 1:** Over the PA: "As of now this is a **ground stop**. Nobody leaves the building." One bowler heckles in fake pilot-speak (one line only; it's texture and doesn't count against her cap). She doesn't answer him.
- **Comic middle (assignments; the chaperoned overnight is made plain and funny):** She assigns sleeping places down the lanes in order, by pair. Couples get lane 3 and 4's settees, the Highway Department gets the far pairs, and Ray and Hollis get the lane 10 settee by the pit door, "in case." Ellen gives herself the counter stool and doesn't plan to sleep. Verna, in the lavender parka, takes the scorer's table at lane 1 with her binder: "The secretary does not sleep during a session." Denise announces the snack-bar rations as a rule with numbers in it (hot dogs per head, the last two bags of fries held back until midnight, coffee free all night, Ellen's call). Denise keeps each speech to three or four sentences at most; the Ch 03 reader skimmed her monologues.
- **First "Fella I knew…"** A Lakeview Diner bowler asks Ray whether a four-wheel-drive could make Kettle Road. Ray answers with a story about somebody else. One or two lines, deadpan, about a fella who was sure his truck could. Ellen, from the mic, without inflection: "Which fella?" (use 1 of 3). Ray names him and his town. Two bowlers laugh at Ray, not at the story. The engine has started.
- **The marathon (money moves on the page).** Ellen announces it. The lanes stay open all night as a marathon, strings at the usual rate, snack bar at the usual prices. Sleeping, coffee, water and the phone are free. The house adds a set amount per string to the league prize fund (no figure on the page). Verna checks her binder and rules from the scorer's table: marathon strings aren't league play and don't count toward averages, but the prize-fund contribution will go in the minutes as a gift of the house. **The town's opinion moves:** the Highway Department lines up at the counter with their wallets out first, and the rest follow. One grumble from somebody, unanswered.
- **Ray says nothing in public** (bible: he never speaks against her in public). He stands at the lane 10 settee with his coat still on. Denise, ringing up strings, sees him say nothing.
**Subtext:** Ellen has turned a disaster into a business night in fifteen minutes, and the league is paying her for it. Ray watches his league choose her plan without being asked what he thinks, and doesn't like how good it is.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal, held in. Ray's disagreement is visible only in what he doesn't do (he doesn't buy a string or take off his coat).
**Ends on:** A line of dialogue (this chapter's one quip ending). Ellen thanks Verna for the ruling. Verna, already writing: "The chair will be here at six, Mrs. Daly. So will the minutes."
**Word count target:** 550

### Scene 3 — THE FRICTION SCENE (rule 19)
**Setting:** The office behind the counter, 9:50 p.m. The steel desk, the printing adding machine, Arlene's green ledgers on two shelves, the 2019 fuel-oil calendar back on the wall. The space heater is running. The door is half-closed. Through it come the first marathon strings, a drawer of wooden spoons hitting the floor over and over.
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen; Denise at the door at the very end
**Surface action:**
- Ellen is standing at the desk, running the first marathon strings on the adding machine. Ray comes in and, while he talks, tightens a loose hinge screw on the office door with a dime from his pocket (his absent-fixing tic).
- **Ray:** "You're charging them." **Ellen:** she isn't charging anyone to be stranded. Sleeping, coffee, water and the phone are free. Bowling costs what bowling costs.
- **Ray's case, from decency:** In the February storm of '78 Arlene opened the snack bar and the lanes and nobody paid a dime. The league still talks about it. "Arlene never charged the league for a lot of things." **This is the chapter's one breadcrumb for Ch 05.** Ray means it as praise. Ellen's eyes go to the ledger shelf and come back. No narration explains it.
- **Comic middle:** Denise starts the popcorn machine out front, and the office space heater trips the breaker. The office goes dark, and Denise calls "Sorry!" from the counter. Ellen keeps talking, reaches the panel without looking, resets it, and finishes her sentence as the heater ticks back on. Ray notices she knew where the breaker was in the dark. (This also gets the reader used to the idea that the power can fail before it does.)
- **Ellen's case, with stakes:** The storm will take Friday and Saturday from her, and those are her two best nights. Tonight is the only thing the storm is giving back. Ray: "Off people who can't leave, Mrs. Daly." "Ms." (once this chapter).
- **She gets angry the bible's way:** slower, quieter, no contractions. "Nobody has to bowl. I am not charging anyone to be stranded. I am charging for bowling."
- **The opposition made plain.** Ellen: "If it were yours tonight, what would you do?" Ray, honestly: open it all for free and eat the cost, because that is what the place is. Ellen: "And in March you will still be open." Ray: "Yes." Ellen: **"On whose money?"** (He has the store, Danny, and a bank commitment. She has a pension and thirteen weeks.)
**Interior passage 2 (Ray, 2–4 sentences, during the silence after "Yes"):** He knows exactly what he would do with the building on March second: nothing. The settees, the league, the trophy case, all of it the way it is. He has been planning for six years to change nothing, and here she is in the office changing things while he watches. Plain words, no therapy.
**Subtext:** Both are right. Ray's free storm night is the same generosity that has been quietly losing the alley money for years (the reader will learn this in Ch 05). Ellen can't afford Arlene's kind of decency, and Ray can afford to recommend it because he expects to be the one who owns it.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Opposite wants, out loud: a paid night against a free one, an alley that pays against an alley that stays the same.
**Ends on:** Cut away on an unanswered question. Ray hasn't answered "On whose money?" when Denise pushes the door open: the Lakeview Diner wants to know if Ray's going to do one about a fella and a moose. Ray goes out. Ellen is left standing at the desk. Don't describe her face.
**Word count target:** 600

### Scene 4 — SET PIECE: "Candlepin by candlelight" (Ray's comic engine: "Fella I knew…")
**Setting:** 1:52 a.m. onward. The power goes out, and then the scene moves to the pit behind lane 5. The exit signs are on battery for about 90 minutes, and flashlights take over after that. The baseboard stops ticking. Breath shows by 3 a.m. Thirty-one bowlers in parkas are awake or half awake. Denise bowls at the foul line with a flashlight held on the pins from the settee.
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen, Denise, Hollis (opening beat only), the bowlers as a heckling chorus
**Surface action:**
- **The cause is shown (rule 17).** Hollis has just come in from measuring ("Nineteen.") when his scanner says a pine is down across the line on Kettle Road. The lanes go dark mid-string. Ray hears the boiler's burner and circulator quit below the floor before anyone else notices the heat (diagnosis by sound). The tables stop wherever they were. (Get right: a Bowl-Mor stops dead in a power cut; the danger is that it starts again with hands in it when power returns.)
- **Ellen's call, not Ray's:** the paid marathon ends at the last full string, because people paid for machines. Anything bowled after this is free: "The house is closed. This is a building with people in it." Ray hears his own argument come back on her terms. Neither mentions it.
- **Ray asks, he doesn't assume** (anchor: never uninvited). "You want lane 5 set by hand?" She says yes, and that she's going back too. He says the pit is no place for somebody who's never been hit by a pin. She says it's her pit. He doesn't argue twice.
- **Why lane 5 (rule 14):** Ray picks it. He says its table stopped high enough to clear the deck by hand. (The real reason comes later in the scene.) He throws its breaker off so it can't start with them in there. Ellen tapes a strip over the breaker: HANDS IN PIT. On the way past the trophy case in the dark he touches the glass with one knuckle (the chapter's one use).
- **Hand-setting, specific:** ten marked spots on the pin deck. Deadwood stays down between balls and gets cleared after the third. They sit up on the side boards out of the way during each ball, then drop down and set. Ellen sets a rule: the bowler calls "Ball!" before every throw (plain English, not ATC). Pins fly. Out front the bowlers boo every delay.
- **Comic middle (the engine at full speed):** Between strings the bowlers shout requests by topic down the lane, a dog, ice, a wedding, and Ray answers each with a "Fella I knew…" of one to three lines, deadpan, important words last. Use **two** quick ones. After the first, Ellen asks "Which fella?" (use 2 of 3) and wants the year as well. He gives it.
- **The turn.** A Highway Department voice asks for "one about a fella who bought a bowling alley." The building gets quieter. Ray says, "Don't know him," and calls for the next ball. Ellen, low, only to him: "Which fella?" (use 3 of 3, changed). Ray: "Nobody you'd know." **That is the last "Which fella?" in the book unless a later chapter has a reason.**
- **The laugh (outline beat).** The next ball sends a deadwood pin off the deck. It rolls along the boards into Ray's lap. Ellen, without looking up from the spot she's setting: **"Traffic, twelve o'clock, one mile."** (**ATC 2.**) Ray laughs hard enough that he has to sit back. It's their first real laugh together, and it lets the room out of the moment before. Don't say any of that in narration.
- **Confessions between strings (keep to one page; rule 15):** Ray tells her why lane 5. February 1998, a Thursday: lane 5's table quit in the ninth frame of Linda's string. Ray went back and set her tenth by hand, so he never saw the 187. He heard it from back here, by the sound of the wood. Then Ellen tells him about her last day on the radar scope: the month she turned fifty-six, a day shift, worked to the minute; she signed off position, handed over the headset, and there was a sheet cake in the break room with her name spelled right. Two or three plain lines. No "relief briefing," no "D-side," no loss of separation. **Ray does not answer her with a fella story.** Don't point it out.
**Interior passage 3 (Ray, 2–4 sentences, inside the 1998 story or right after it):** This pit is the closest he has been to that night in twenty-eight years: the same spots on the deck, the same dark behind the cushion. He has never told anyone he didn't see it. Plain, in grain and wear, not grief.
**Subtext:** Ray brought her to the one place in the building that's most his and Linda's, and he picked it on purpose. Ellen asked to come back because she doesn't send people where she won't go. Neither is courting, and both are.
**Tension source:** Physical (flying wood, cold, the dark). Interpersonal (the bowling-alley request touches the open wound in front of 31 people). Internal (what Ray is choosing to tell).
**Ends on:** Mid-motion, cutting away. Denise's flashlight swings onto the two of them at the deck. "You two setting pins or visiting?" "Ball!" is already coming from the foul line, and they both go for the side boards. Cut.
**Word count target:** 900 (set piece; it carries the laugh, the turn, and both confessions)

### Scene 5
**Setting:** The counter, 5:40 a.m., Friday, Jan 8. Gray light through the glass-block window band. A battery lantern from Hollis's truck on the counter. The adding machine is dead (it's electric), so Ellen works the tape in pencil on the back of a score sheet. Bowlers asleep in parkas down the settees. Cold enough that the coffee in the urn has gone lukewarm.
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen, Denise, Verna
**Surface action:**
- **Comic beat (Verna's payoff, short):** Verna is upright at the lane 1 scorer's table exactly where she sat down. She looks at her watch, writes one line, and says to the room, which is mostly asleep: "The session stands adjourned at five forty-one." Then she closes the binder.
- **Comic beat (Denise):** Ellen adds the night's columns by hand. Denise, putting her coat on, corrects one figure from memory without looking at the sheet ("The diner bought fries twice, honey, the second time was after the lights"). Ellen changes it without arguing.
- **Money moves on the page.** Ray stands at the counter and reads her pencil tape upside down (forty years of reading invoices across a hardware counter). The night made money: more than a Saturday, before the lights went. The running total at the bottom reads **$11,800**. It's the one dollar figure in the chapter. No percentage.
- **Ellen stands for the bad news.** She calls the utility on the rotary phone. Recorded estimate: Kettle Road restored Saturday evening. She sits down when she has decided. She takes the Friday glow-night strip and the Saturday strip off the board, writes CLOSED on each, and pins them back. Then she asks Denise for the Friday party list and starts dialing.
- **Dramatic irony, unremarked:** while she pins the CLOSED strips, Ray's eye goes along the board to the far end. 02/27 / 1800 / LGM / 10, REMARKS blank. He reads the blank as nothing to remark on. The narration doesn't comment.
**Interior passage 4 (Ray, 2–4 sentences): the outline's "Ray knows."** He does her arithmetic without meaning to, two good nights gone, and minds it. He tracks back and finds he has been doing this for weeks: counting cars in her lot on glow nights from the store window, and saying nothing to anyone. Don't use "realized" or "found himself." State it as a fact he notices about his own habits, and stop there.
**Subtext:** Ray wants her to make it, and he wants the building. Both are true, and in this scene neither cancels the other.
**Tension source:** Situational (two best nights lost). Internal (Ray's rooting against his own interest).
**Ends on:** Mid-motion. Ellen is already dialing the first Friday party on the rotary, finger in the wheel, while Ray takes his coat off the settee. Don't let the CLOSED strip be the last image.
**Word count target:** 400

### Scene 6
**Setting:** The bottom of the driveway, about 6:25 a.m. The snow has stopped. A low pewter sky. The lake is invisible under new white across Kettle Road. The town plow is finally working the road. Hollis's truck sits where it was, drifted to the door handles on the lake side.
**Characters present:** Ray, Hollis
**Surface action:**
- Ray and Hollis dig the blade out. Hollis lays the tape on the hood one last time: "Twenty-two. Done." (Hollis's snow total is a clock through the chapter: eleven, nineteen, twenty-two. This is its third and last use.) The town plow comes by on Kettle Road, doesn't stop because the drive is private, and its driver lifts two fingers off the wheel (municipal versus contract plowing, shown rather than explained).
- **Required plant (rule 19: Ray still plans to own the alley on March 2):** Ray's phone, unchecked since yesterday afternoon, has an email from his lender: the commitment letter extended through March 31, **as he requested on December 14**, the Monday of the first lesson. He reads it standing in her driveway with the shovel under his arm. One line of narration says when he asked for it. No commentary.
- **Comic middle (Hollis, plain questions, no tone):** "That the bank?" "Yep." "Still want to lend you a bowling alley?" "Through March." Hollis checks the sky. "She know?" "She knows." (She does. It's not a secret.)
- Hollis gets the truck free and swings it around to start on her lot, because that's what she pays him for.
**Subtext:** The snow has stopped, and with it the excuse. The paper in his pocket and the night in the pit point in opposite directions, and Ray hasn't chosen.
**Tension source:** Internal, with Hollis's questions pressing on it.
**Ends on:** Cut away early, with the question left open (the chapter promise). Through the window, over the blade's scrape, Hollis asks whether Ray's going home now. Ray doesn't answer. He puts the phone away and starts up the driveway on foot toward the building, the shovel still under his arm. Cut. No reflection, and no last look at the sign.
**Word count target:** 250
