# Chapter 10 — Ice-Out: Beat Sheet

**POV:** Ellen (close third, past tense) for Scenes 1–4, Monday, March 1. Section break. Ray for Scene 5, the April coda.
**Dates:** Monday, March 1, 8:30 a.m. to about 4:15 p.m. Coda: Saturday, April 17 (calendar checked: March 1 is a Monday, so April 1 is a Thursday and April 17 is a Saturday), late afternoon into dusk.
**Target:** 2,000 words (Monday 1,550, coda 450; the scene targets add up to 2,000)
**Chapter promise:** Resolved. The final line must be dialogue or action, not a reflection.

## Pre-flight flags (read before drafting)

1. **Judy's wobble: a new fact, and an honest one (priority 1).** Ellen's tape shows $25,600 in cash. Her **bank statement for February 28 shows less**, because Saturday's take never reached the bank. The roads closed at four, Sunday was closed, and the receipts sat in the floor safe until Ellen put them through the drive-up tube at 8:00 this morning. Without Saturday she is under $25,000. Judy's committee wrote "cash on hand on February 28," and the underwriter has to decide whether cash he couldn't see on Sunday counts.
   - **Where it comes from:** Ellen's own storm decision in Ch 09 (empty by four, Sunday closed). Nothing is manufactured, and nobody is a villain (rule 9). The bank is the bank.
   - **What resolves it:** the HELD. NOT MINE. account from Ch 03. The underwriter's one question, relayed by Judy, is how much of what was in the safe belonged to someone else. Ellen made two deposits at 8:00: the house's take, and the derby's half of the raffle into HELD. Because of that split, the count is believable. This is the outline's "Judy notices the HELD account with approval," turned from a pat on the head into the deciding fact. The deciding fact is one Ellen set up on day one (rules 22 and 23).
   - **What it costs her:** approval comes with a condition. Ellen must keep **$25,000 minimum on deposit, reported monthly, through next February.** The floor she just scraped over becomes her floor for a year. She accepts standing up. Rule 20: she has no answer to the underwriter's point, and she pays a price.
   - Figures: statement **$22,140**; Saturday's receipts **$3,460** (together, $25,600). Don't put $30,850 or $25,600 on the page again (rule 21). Refer to "both lines" or "the circles."
   - **Continuity-checker:** confirm nothing in Ch 09 says Saturday's take was deposited. The final says Ray zipped the derby's half "into the HELD bag tonight" (a bag, not a deposit), so this is consistent.
2. **Arlene's brass key: new off-page fact.** Ch 09 ends with the orange-tag key still on Ray's ring (Saturday, about 5 p.m.). In this chapter, **Ray gave it back to Arlene on Sunday, February 28**, when he brought the last of her packing tape (Ch 08 plant). We see it only on Arlene's own ring, next to her new THE PINES 2B key, as she leaves Leclerc's. She says so in one line ("Raymond brought it back yesterday. He said it was a loan."). That way **Ray's key ring appears zero times** this chapter (rule 17). Log the Sunday return in the continuity log.
3. **"Does she know why you picked her?" / "She hasn't asked."** (Ch 08). Ellen asks. This is her move, and it costs her (bible: she is "a little afraid to ask"). Arlene answers in **one line, which must be new**. Don't reuse "museum" or "I wanted it to live" from Ch 08, and don't talk about rooms or order. Suggested content: Ellen asked for the name of Arlene's boiler man before she asked the price, and Arlene wrote it on the back of the offer. Avoid a contrast construction ("Everybody else asked X, you asked Y" is banned). The line is about the handover, which fits Ellen (she "would never leave a job without a proper handover").
4. **The declaration (priority 2).** The order follows the outline. Ray goes first: "Mrs. Daly," uncorrected; then "I love you," with the important words last. **Ellen's answer is the heavier beat (rule 23).** At the truck (Ch 07) she said: *"If I take it, I'll never know whether I could have carried it. And you would always— Whether it was you I—" / "That came out in the wrong order."* In the car she said it right, to nobody. Here she says it to him in the right order, which is **him first, then the building, then why she couldn't take the money**, and ends on a plain "I love you." Use no contractions, because that is how she talks when it matters. In her own words, roughly:
   > *"At your truck I had it in the wrong order. It was you first. I wanted you before I knew whether I could carry this place, and if I had taken your money you would have spent the rest of your life wondering which of the two I wanted. I love you."*
   The prose-writer should find her words. These are requirements: (a) it names the truck; (b) it puts him first, explicitly; (c) it names what he would have wondered; (d) "I love you" closes it, plain and unhedged. **Banned in the declaration:** contrast constructions ("not the money but you"), ATC phrases, "rooms," "in order" as a life metaphor, therapy-speak ("I've been afraid to…"), and "the first thing I ever chose."
5. **The kiss counts.** Kiss 3 is in S4, the pit behind lane 7 (firm). **Kiss 4 in the coda: default OMIT.** The HEA is carried by the declaration, the uncorrected "Mrs.," Ray's standing MX strip, his "spoken for," and Ellen trying to buy a square she means to win. If the drafter includes kiss 4, it goes before the sign lights, never as the final beat, and it uses no detail from earlier kisses.
6. **Kiss details already used. Do not repeat (rule 22):** a mitten on the GAGNON & SON stitching; up on her toes or her bad knee going up; her hand fisted in his canvas jacket; standing so they're level; setting a flashlight down; "who started it, unclear"; Denise watching. **New for kiss 3:** Ray is sitting on the kickback down in the pit, and Ellen crouches to him on the catwalk step, so for once she is the one looking down. Show it through context and consequence: the drop light on its hook, lane 7's ten pins standing on the deck, who speaks first afterward. One embrace at most. No bodies or mouths.
7. **The HEA made explicit (priority 4), all on the page:** both declarations (S4); "Mrs. Daly" uncorrected (S4); in the coda, a standing strip on the board, `MX / R.G.`, with no end date, in her capitals; Ray telling his lender he is "spoken for"; Ellen trying to buy the wedding-date square because **she means to do the asking** (the book's theme, kept in her hands); Denise refusing; Danny's OCT square in plain view. **No on-page proposal, no date named, no engagement stated.** Author check: this implies a coming proposal by Ellen. It doesn't change who ends up together, so I'm treating it as inside the outline. Flag it if the author disagrees.
8. **The sign repair and rule 2.** In Ch 05 Ray said, "Tube on the K's fine. It's the transformer." Ray bought a like-for-like transformer from the sign shop in Laconia, which bench-tested it. He swaps it at the sign's disconnect, as he has done on the store's sign for decades. Ellen asks once, deadpan, whether the transformer is certified. This is the only callback to Ch 06's "Is that wrench certified?", and Ray answers with the shop's tag. She lets him. Don't name a license he doesn't have. **Don't use the style sample's "DEAD K" switch label** (style samples may not be reused). The sign switch is just "the sign switch."
9. **Banned this chapter:**
   - "the voice"
   - the wood line, in any form, and "deadwood" as metaphor

10. **"The dead K / ETTLE" once in the chapter: the coda only.** S4 can't say "K." If S4 seeds the sign at all, say "the sign."

### Scene 1 — CASH ON HAND
**Setting:** Granite Lakes Savings, Judy's glass office, 8:30 a.m. Monday. The butterscotch bowl and the framed lake trout (11 lb 4 oz) are where they were. Out in the lobby, the drive-up tube thunks, along with heels on marble. Ellen's books are squared on the desk: closed Sunday night from the register reports and the bank statements, with the Ch 03 sheet's two circled numbers on top. Ellen came in through the drive-up at 8:00 with two deposit slips.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Judy
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late.** Judy is already in the papers, glasses down off her head. On the Ch 03 sheet she puts a check mark beside the first circle, then puts her pen beside the second circle and leaves it there.
- **Comic middle:** Judy, plain: she's a banker today (the reverse of Ch 09's "I'm here as a fisherman"). One line, unremarked.
- **The wobble.** Judy turns the February statement around: **$22,140** on the twenty-eighth. Ellen explains, in the fewest words: **$3,460** of Saturday's receipts went into the safe at four, stayed there through the closed Sunday, and went in through the tube at 8:00 this morning. Judy explains the rule exactly (bible: she tells you exactly what the rules are). The commitment says cash on hand on the twenty-eighth. The underwriter reads "on hand" as money he can see, and he couldn't see her safe on Sunday. Ellen has no rebuttal (rule 20). She says so in her slow, contraction-free register.
- Judy takes it to the underwriter by phone, **door closed**. Ellen stands on the other side of the glass and can't hear. She watches Judy's pen. She counts the butterscotch (tic: she counts, and on a bad day under her breath). Keep this to a paragraph.
- Judy opens the door with the receiver against her cardigan. One question from the underwriter: how much of Saturday's money belonged to someone else? **Ellen: "Negative. None of it."** (ATC 1). The derby's half went into HELD on its own slip at the same window, the meals tax has been in HELD since December, and Judy has both slips. Judy looks at the HELD statement and says one plain approving line (outline beat). She goes back in and shuts the door.
- **The verdict.** Approved, with the condition (flag 1): $25,000 minimum, reported monthly, through next February. Judy puts it plainly and doesn't apologize. Ellen accepts standing. Closing at one o'clock, at Leclerc's.

**Interior passage 1 (Ellen, 3–4 sentences), through the glass:** Her fear, plainly put. Her calm works fine right up until it's too late, and Judy can't see any of it either. She knows what $3,460 is to the dollar, and she can't sequence a phone call she isn't on. Don't use "the voice."

**Subtext:** For thirteen weeks every number was hers to move. This one isn't.
**Tension source:** Situational (the rule and the underwriter) and internal (waiting with nothing to brief).
**Ends on:** Judy's line, which the outline mandates: **"Congratulations. Don't celebrate until it funds."** End on the dialogue, with no reaction from Ellen and no sitting.
**Word count target:** 500

### Scene 2 — FUNDED
**Setting:** Leclerc's law office, second floor over the barber, about 1:00 to 2:20 p.m. Three details, no more: the radiator under the window, the framed 1950s map of the lake, and the embosser, whose hinge Ray fixed with a dime in Ch 08 (it works, unremarked). A van from The Pines idles at the curb on Main Street.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Arlene, Judy, Arlene's lawyer (walk-on, unnamed)
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late, at the funding.** Skip the signing. Judy's phone buzzes, and she says the one word that matters ("Funded." or a close equivalent, with the time). The lawyer prints the wire confirmation and hands it to Arlene.
- **Comic middle:** Arlene licks her thumb and counts the confirmation, which is **one page**, and says the count out loud. Then she sets the 1996 ledger on the table, **due 03/01** on Ellen's loan strip (Ch 08), on time to the day. ("Don't be sweet to me, dear… Be on time" is in her character. Don't quote it.)
- **Ellen asks.** "Why me?" or an equivalent in her idiom. Before she asks, see Interior passage 2.
- **Arlene's one line** (flag 3). Blunt, about the handover. No "museum," no "live." She doesn't explain it.
- Arlene gets into her coat. Her keys come out of her cardigan pocket with the tissues: the new THE PINES 2B key, and beside it the brass key with the orange tag, "R.G. — 1996" in her handwriting. Ellen looks at it. Arlene: Raymond brought it back yesterday, and he said it was a loan (flag 2). No reaction shot and no meaning stated (rules 16 and 17).
- Optional, one only: a "Voyons donc" at the lawyer or the van driver.

**Interior passage 2 (Ellen, 2–3 sentences), before she asks:** What she has been afraid the answer is. Maybe Arlene picked her for the cash down. Maybe she picked her because she wasn't Ray. Either answer would make the winter something that happened to her. Keep it plain and short.

**Subtext:** Arlene closes her account with both of them in objects, the way she always has.
**Tension source:** Internal (asking a question with a possibly bad answer) and Arlene's bluntness.
**Ends on:** Mid-motion. Arlene goes down the steep stairs one step at a time with her keys still in her hand, and doesn't take Ellen's arm when it's offered. Cut before the van door. Don't end on the key.
**Word count target:** 400

### Scene 3 — NO WINNER
**Setting:** The Kettle Lanes counter, about 3:50 p.m. The lanes are dark until six, with only the counter lights on. Denise's crossword is out, with the pool sheet under it (it has lived at the alley since Ch 07). The strip board is behind the counter. The pegboard by the pit door holds the R.G. — AFTER HOURS card.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Denise
**Surface action:**
- **Enter on Denise closing the pool.** She doesn't look up. She reads the dates for when Ellen would fold: DEC 1 (nobody), the crossed-out "Feb 2 — they split" (Ch 07), and MARCH 1, which **nobody ever held**. She rules there's no winner and the pot goes to the derby's kids' division (outline). She writes it across the sheet in pen. Ellen tells her it goes in HELD. One clause, no more.
- **Comic middle:** Denise's talky, salty account of who bought which date and what that tells you about them. One short speech at most (rule 17). She uses "honey" once. She says nothing about Ray and the March 1 square, because the reader already did that arithmetic in Ch 01.
- Denise **folds the sheet so the WEDDING DATE row stays on the outside** and slides it back under the crossword. She says nothing about it. (This sets up the coda.)
- **The card.** Ellen goes to the pegboard. Ray's newest line, in his square capitals: `03/01 / 1540 / LANE 07 / WAITING`. Nobody in the scene says what it means (this pays off Ch 09's blank REASON).

**Interior passage (optional, 2 sentences):** Lane 7 was the first machine that died on her, on night one. One plain fact, no meaning stated. Cut it if Monday runs long.

**Subtext:** The town's bet on her failure is closed, and the town's bet on the two of them is still open.
**Tension source:** Mild and situational. The card tells her he's in the building and has been waiting fourteen minutes.
**Ends on:** Mid-motion. Ellen is already heading for the steel door by lane 10. Denise, still not looking up, says he hasn't touched a thing back there, because she'd have heard it. Cut on Denise. This is information, not a quip.

### Scene 4 — LANE SEVEN
**Setting:** The pit behind lane 7, about 3:55 p.m. The drop light hangs on its hook. Lane 7's ten pins stand on the deck under the raised table, with nothing wrong with any of them. The catwalk boards, the kickback, the cushion and curtain. The building is quiet enough to hear the baseboard tick. Everything screwable within an arm's reach of lane 7 has been tightened (Ray's tic: he fixes things absently while he waits).
**Characters present:** Ellen, Ray
**Surface action:**
- **Enter late.** Ray sits on the kickback, down in the pit. Ellen comes along the catwalk. **Ray: "Fourteen minutes, Mrs. Daly."** Ellen corrects the number, exactly, and **doesn't correct the "Mrs."** Neither of them notices out loud, and the narration doesn't either (rule 16).
- **Comic middle:** Ray asks a question he already knows the answer to (bible tic). Arlene counted it? Ellen: one page, with her thumb. He has also tightened the lane 7 settee bolts, the drop-light hook, and a hinge, and she lists them back to him in order (with no ATC framing and no "readback" naming).
- **Ray, important words last.** A short run of plain practicalities first. He'll still invoice her. He wants Tuesdays on the lanes with his team, not in the pit. He is sixty-one and set in some ways, and he names one of them, concretely. Then **"I love you."** End his speech there, with no tag after it. He doesn't stand up.
- **Ellen crouches to him on the catwalk step** (bad knee, unmentioned for now). **She says the truck sentence in the right order** (flag 4), slowly and without contractions, and ends on "I love you." This is the declaration. Give it room. Interrupt it with nothing, not even a machine sound (Ch 09 used the sweep).
- **Kiss 3.** She moves first, looking down at him for once (flag 6). Show it through context: the drop light, the standing pins, and who speaks first afterward. **Never repeat an earlier kiss sentence.**
- **The laugh** (outline emotional beat). He gives her a hand up off the step, and her left knee cracks loud enough for both of them to hear. Ellen, deadpan: it's the knee, it's from 2009, and they don't discuss it (bible: the knee she will not discuss). Ray laughs, the first full laugh since the pit in Ch 04, without saying so.

**Interior passage 3 (Ellen, 3–4 sentences), on the catwalk before she speaks:** The car in the Merrimack lot, then her own car on February second, six minutes and twenty-eight seconds. She said this sentence once already, to a windshield. This is the only place she reaches back to Ch 07, and it gets one sentence of recap at most (rule 21).

**Subtext:** Both of them have already given everything up. What's left is saying it, which is the one thing neither of them does well.
**Tension source:** Internal. Ellen has to say out loud the sentence she could only say alone.
**Ends on:** Ray laughing, on the catwalk, with her hand still in his. Cut there, mid-laugh. This is a quip ending (1 of 2).
**Word count target:** 400

### Scene 5 — ICE-OUT (RAY)
**Setting:** Saturday, April 17, late afternoon into dusk. **Open on the lake, per the outline:** Great Kettle has gone dark at the edges and the ice is the color of old pewter. The bob-houses have been off since April 1. Then the counter, then the road sign: a pole at the road, KETTLE LANES in red script under a white candlepin, an extension ladder against it. On the counter: a boxed transformer from the sign shop in Laconia with its test tag, and the strip board.
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen, Denise
**Surface action:**
- **Lake, then the ice-out pool, in two or three sentences.** The town's ice-out pool has replaced the fold pool. Invented method: **Hollis declares ice-out on the morning he can row from the town dock to the public access without touching ice.** He called it Thursday at 6:10. Ellen bought a square and lost (give the exact margin; she would know it).
- **The counter.** Ray has the transformer box under his arm. On the strip board, among the parties, hangs **a standing strip in Ellen's capitals: `MX / R.G.`**, with no end date. He sees it and doesn't comment.
- **Comic middle:** Ellen asks whether the transformer is certified, the single callback to Ch 06's wrench line. Ray shows her the shop's tag, and she reads it, every line.
- **The square.** Ellen asks Denise to sell her a square on the WEDDING DATE row (it's still folded outermost, from S3). The row reads: NORMIE, JUNE 12; D. LAVOIE, NEVER, PAID IN FULL; DANNY, OCT. **Denise refuses: "Honey, you don't get to bet on your own flight."** (outline). Denise points out that Danny has had October since January. One line about Danny, in Denise's mouth, in the language of inventory.
- **Ray hears all of it.** He asks a question he already knows the answer to (bible tic): whether she means to ask him first. Ellen answers in one dry line about getting the order right this time. That is the only use of "order" in the chapter, and it's in dialogue, not narration. Ray goes quiet, which is what he does, and picks up the ladder.
- **The sign, at dusk.** Ray is up the ladder at the disconnect. Ellen is at the foot, holding the ladder. She hires out everything she isn't qualified for, and she lets him. The transformer goes in.
- **The bank letter** (interior passage, below) comes while he's working.
- Ray radios the counter to throw the sign switch (Ray's phrasing doesn't count against the ATC cap). Denise throws it. Up close against the sign, Ray can't read it.
- **The dead K / ETTLE, once in the chapter (flag 10):** use it in the lead-in to the switch, for example "seven years of Ettle's," or in the town's name for the place. Once only.

**Interior passage 4 (Ray, 2–3 sentences): the bank letter's expiry.** The commitment letter ran out on March 31 in his coat pocket. On April 1 a woman from the lender called to ask whether he'd like another extension. He said no, thank you. She asked if there was a reason, for her form, and he told her he was spoken for. This pays off the REASON column (blank in Ch 09, WAITING in Ch 10) without naming it.
**Interior passage 5 (Ray, 2–3 sentences): what the alley holds for him now.** Linda's 187 card is still in the case. He asked Ellen once whether it stayed, and she told him a record is a record. Say it plainly, with no "museum" and no meaning stated.

**Subtext:** He's changing the last thing that was the way it was. She is letting someone do something for her building, in public, at the road.
**Tension source:** Light and comic: Denise's refusal and the certified question. The only stake left is whether it lights.
**Ends on:** The sign lights. Ray, against it, can't see it, and calls down, **"How's it read?"** Ellen, from the road, reads it back, exact and dry: **"Both words."** That is the last line of the book: dialogue, earned by eight chapters of "Ettle's," and by the book's readback device, now turned around so Ray is the one asking for it. It isn't a moral. Ray caused the light (rule 17), and nobody reacts with a reaction shot. **Alternate, if "Both words" reads too neat in the draft:** end on action. Ray starts down the ladder and she keeps both hands on it until his boots touch the gravel. The dialogue ending is preferred.
**Word count target:** 450
