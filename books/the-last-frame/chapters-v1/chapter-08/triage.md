# Ch 08 cold-read triage (orchestrator, autopilot)

**Verdict:** "A busy chapter that moves the plot well." The best moment is the Pine Ridge finish, where Ray uses Ellen's lesson in reverse; keep it exactly. The main problem is that Ray's three big moves (the release, the teams, the store) arrive without us watching him decide any of them.

**Process:** one revision round, a prose-writer targeted pass and then a voice-editor pass.

**Keep unchanged:**
- plot facts and money
- the match and its finish (84/117/133 against 108/115/109)
- the boy's "Do you live back there?" / "Saturdays."
- Ellen's MX section and the empty columns
- the "When will you tell her?" hook
- "Thank you, Mr. Gagnon." / "Ms. Daly."

**Length:** 3,300–3,500.

## Show Ray deciding

1. **The release (S3, Leclerc's).**
   - Name it before he signs: first-refusal release, so the reader knows what is on the desk.
   - Give Ray one interior passage of 2–4 sentences where he decides. He still believes the Concord developer line, and he signs anyway, because a safety net under her means she never finds out whether she carried it. That is her own reason from the parking lot, coming back to him in his own words. Don't state the theme. Let it be his logic, in his terms.
2. **Arlene's line (S1).** Replace "You'd have kept it a museum. I wanted it to live." with something as sharp and specific as the rest of her dialogue, in her voice (blunt, money-minded, maybe "Voyons donc"). Keep the meaning. Lose the pull-quote balance.
3. **The store handover (S5).**
   - Cut the Bench coffee bit to one exchange at most, and cut the gossip recap ("He's clearing his decks").
   - Danny's "You're allowed to want something that isn't on a shelf" is a therapist line. Danny talks in invoices and part numbers, so rewrite it in his transactional voice.
4. **The bet (S4).** Give the reason it works.
   - Why would Gil host a match that could cost him two Tuesday teams? Give him a reason in one line. For example, he has wanted to beat Ray on his own lanes for thirty years, or the Marina were slow payers.
   - Why would the 112 agree? Pride: he has wanted Ray head to head since 1996. Give it a line.

## Clarity

5. **The ledger loan.** Add one clause saying the ledgers came with the building, so Ellen owns them and lent Arlene her own 1996 book. Make the direction of the loan unmistakable.
6. **Ray at Arlene's.** Make it clear early that he's helping her pack for The Pines (she moves March 1).
7. **The Pine Ridge opening.** Give one clean anchor: which night, why both teams are there, and the time. Cut the three clauses about other places, or reduce them to one.
8. **Denise's score report.** She says all three: 84, 117, 133.
9. **"You picked her."** Make the "her" unambiguous. The 112 means Ellen, or the alley over the league.

## Trims and tics

10. **The whiteboard (S6).** Compress plans A–D to one sentence or one glance and go straight to MX. Don't read the pace sheet aloud number by number. Give one line on where she stands (about $1,000 and $1,250 short).
11. Cut Hollis's cap routine in this chapter.
12. Cut the narration's recap of the breakup ("Effective now, she had said at his truck window, and he had kept to it: the card, the slip, the check.").
13. Cut the tape-as-feelings metaphor ("She could not make those facts sit on one line of a tape...").
14. **Scene endings.** No more than one scene may end on a cut-off line or a question. Vary the rest.
15. Shorten Gil and Denise's four-horse exchange so it doesn't interrupt the match.
