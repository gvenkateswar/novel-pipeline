# Chapter 03 — Deadwood: Beat Sheet

**POV:** Ellen (close third, past tense)
**Dates:** Tuesday, December 8 to Saturday, December 19
**Target:** 3,300 words (scene targets sum to 3,350)
**Chapter promise to set up:** Will the numbers say otherwise, and what happens when Ray finds out what she actually promised?

---

## Pre-flight flags (read before drafting)

1. **Price cap vs. money on the page.** The bible allows two or three prices per chapter, but rule 15 and the Money notes require the $30K/$25K logic to be explicit and the money to move in a scene. **Resolution:** the only dollar figures on the page are $30,000 and $25,000 (S1), one rounded loan-cost figure in Judy's explanation (S1), and the December 19 position (S6: NOI $5,600; cash $34,300). Ray's per-call rate, the $3,400 scavenged parts, the $5,400 glow setup, and the appraisal fee happen on the page **without amounts** ("she wrote the check without looking up," "Ray named a number; she doubled it").
2. **Cash bridge.** Dec 3 cash $41,000 → Dec 19 $34,300 works only if the Dec 16 appraisal draw is about $3,800 (after parts −$3,400, glow −$5,400, NOI +$5,900). The bible doesn't give that split. Keep the appraisal amount off the page.
3. **Setting up the promise for Ch 05/06.** Ch 05 says Ellen "knows which half of that sentence Ray heard," and Ch 06 says both of them are telling the truth. S4 therefore plants that **Ellen notices she got no readback and lets it go.** S2 has a full readback of the key deal, so the missing one in S4 stands out without comment. Don't explain it.
4. **Ch 02 reader complaints that apply here.** Ellen's "why I bought it" interiors must **not** repeat the "thirty-one years / federal law / first thing I chose" wording (it has been said twice already). Use new angles (S1: clear criteria; S3: a room that needs sequencing). Limit repeated stage business: the knuckle on the trophy glass at most once, the key-ring jingle at most once (S2), and no door-handle double-check.
5. **Key.** Ellen holds 3 rings of 3 keys (front/back/bulkhead). She gives Ray one **back-door** key, the door he used in Ch 02. Every visit, including the S4 visit when Ellen is in the building, goes on the strip.
6. **Technique terms.** Named leaves and the lob-foul penalty are marked Confirm in the bible. Use **one** leave name, spoken by Ray, with no pin numbers. Use "chop" once in a general sense. For the lob foul, use the write-around: "the foul light came on and the pins didn't count." The 300/245 line is verified; use the bible's wording and never name record holders.
7. **ATC cap: 3 phrases**, allocated as follows: S2 "Readback" (dialogue), S4 "Affirmative" (dialogue), S5 "Negative" (dialogue). Use none in S1, S3, or S6, and none in Ellen's narration. Her interior voice uses order and numbers, not jargon.
8. **Christmas stays off the page.** "December office parties" and "the holiday bookings" are fine. No season magic.

### Scene 1
**Setting:** Granite Lakes Savings Bank, Judy's glass-walled office. Tuesday, Dec 8, 9:05 a.m. Floor wax and radiator heat. The pneumatic tube thunks at the drive-up. A bowl of butterscotch and the framed lake trout.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Judy Ostrowski
**Surface action:**
- **Open on the sheet.** Judy slides a single sheet across the desk with two numbers circled in pen: $30,000 and $25,000. ("Two numbers. I'll circle them so neither of us can pretend later.") Ellen stays standing (she stands for bad news). Judy waits until she sits. She doesn't.
- Judy explains the logic in plain words. The other nine months net out to about nothing, so the winter is the year. The new loan costs about $24,000 a year in payments, and the committee wants a quarter more than that coming in. That makes thirty thousand in net operating income, Dec 1 through Feb 28. Twenty-five thousand in the bank on Feb 28 so one bad month doesn't sink it. "I like you. The underwriter has never met you. He's met Arlene's last three winters."
- **Comic middle:** Ellen reads the criteria back, exactly, including the dates. Judy stops with a butterscotch half unwrapped: in nineteen years nobody has read her criteria back to her. Ellen asks the weight of the lake trout in the photo. Judy knows it to the ounce without looking ("Eleven pounds, four ounces. The underwriter didn't believe that either."). That is also a quiet seed for her Ch 09 talk with Hollis in measurements.
- **Required plant (appraisal and environmental review):** Ellen asks which item has the longest lead time. Judy says the appraisal, plus an environmental review because it's a 1958 building with an oil tank. If Ellen wants to close on March 1 she should order both now, so the only thing left open on March 1 is the winter numbers. Ellen tells her to order them today. Judy writes it down and says she'll call the appraiser before lunch.
- **Required plant (separate account):** On the way out, at the teller window, Ellen opens a second business account for money she's only holding: meals tax from the snack bar, league prize-fund money. The teller asks if she'd like a nickname for it on the statement. Ellen prints one in block capitals on the form: HELD. NOT MINE.
**Interior passage 1 (Ellen, 2–4 sentences):** Two circled numbers are the clearest thing anyone has handed her since she turned in her headset. Criteria you can meet or miss, written down, with a date. She has missed the work of being measured, and she doesn't like knowing that about herself.
**Subtext:** Ellen likes Judy because Judy can't be charmed, and some part of her wants exactly that kind of judge. Judy is rooting for her and is careful not to show it.
**Tension source:** Situational. The bar is now concrete, and Ellen is already behind it after one week and a refunded lane.
**Ends on:** An action, not a quip. Ellen slides the account form under the glass, and the teller turns it around to read it. No comment.
**Word count target:** 600

### Scene 2
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, the pit behind lane 10 and then the counter. Wednesday, Dec 9, 4:15 p.m., before Wednesday open play. Warm belts and grease. Coffee can of mismatched bolts. Parts on nails in Ray's square-capital tape.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Ray
**Surface action:**
- Ray carries two cartons of Bowl-Mor parts through the sticking steel door. They came out of a house that closed (he heard about the sale through the hardware trade and drove there Monday). He has already hung half of them on nails, with tape. Ellen pays for the parts by check, there in the pit, writing it against the workbench. Leave the amount off the page.
- **The deal.** He keeps the machines running and she pays a fair rate per call on proper invoices. Ray says he never charged Arlene. Ellen: that is the problem. Arlene's books look better than they were by exactly what he's worth, and she won't carry a number she knows is wrong.
- **Comic middle:** Ray has never written an invoice for this building. He produces a Gagnon & Son sales slip and writes LABOR — LANE 7 CLUTCH with a number so low it's insulting. Ellen crosses it out and writes a higher one. He objects. She raises it again. It's a negotiation run backward, with Ellen bidding against herself, and Ray finally says, "You're bad at this." "I'm accurate at this."
- **The key.** She takes one back-door key off a ring and puts it on the bench, not in his hand. Then the strip: an index card pinned to the pegboard by the pit door, headed R.G. — AFTER HOURS, with columns DATE / TIME IN / TIME OUT / LANE / REASON. Every visit after close gets logged in his own hand. She doesn't need to be there, and he doesn't need to call first. He only has to write it down.
- She asks for a readback (**ATC 1: "Readback."**). He gives it, complete and in order, in his own words, a little grudging ("Back door. After close. Write it on the card. Every time."). Ellen nods once, because it was right.
**Subtext:** The key means she trusts his hands. The strip means she doesn't have to trust anything else, and he understands that. Ray is relieved to be paid, because it gives him a reason to keep coming that isn't the one he won't name.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Pride on both sides. Paying him makes him an employee, and he still holds the right of first refusal.
**Ends on:** An action. Ray threads the new key onto his ring beside the orange-tagged one that opens nothing (this is the chapter's one jingle) and closes his fist around the ring.
**Word count target:** 600

### Scene 3
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes counter. Thursday, Dec 10, 2:30 p.m. A licensed electrician (walk-on, unnamed) is on a ladder over lane 6 running black-light fixtures for Friday's first glow night. Shoe-spray hiss. The pegboard strip board, now half full of December party strips.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Denise (electrician as unnamed background)
**Surface action:**
- Denise, doing the crossword in pen, never looks up and talks for Ellen and herself both. She runs from the black lights to Roger and the dental hygienist in Laconia ("she does his teeth now, which, fine, somebody should") and lands the point: the league won't respect an owner who can't bowl. "Who's going to teach me?" "Honey, who do you think?"
- **Required plant (sanction card):** Denise slides a league sanction-card form across, with the pen. "Fill it out. So you're legal if they're ever short." Ellen: "Short of what?" Denise: "Bowlers, honey. Not money. Well." Ellen fills it out in block capitals. Denise grades her handwriting the way she grades rental shoes ("You'll live").
- **Comic middle:** The electrician tests one fixture. Under black light, Denise's 2011 polo turns out to have a history nobody knew about, the menu board's missing S shows up as a pale ghost, and every bean-supper flyer in the vestibule glows. Denise carries on a running inventory of what the light has exposed while still filling in 14 Across.
- **Ellen takes Denise's advice and credits her** (her arc seed). Looking at the party strips, Denise says office parties want the lanes from 5 to 7 so the spouses can come after. Ellen re-sequences two strips on the spot and writes PER D. LAVOIE in the REMARKS column, in front of her. Denise looks at it longer than she looks at anything else in the chapter, and says nothing kind.
- Ellen texts Ray: lessons, 7 a.m., starting Monday, rate per our agreement. His reply: "I don't charge for bowling. Tell you my price Friday."
**Interior passage 2 (Ellen):** The party strips in their slots. Six bookings, two overlapping, one with a count that can't be right. She knows why she bought this building: it is full of people arriving at once who need someone to put them in order, and nobody else here wants the job. (Write it in her idiom of order and intervals. No "first choice" wording.)
**Subtext:** Denise is testing whether this owner listens. Ellen, who briefs people, is learning to ask, and she is good at it once she sees it as procedure.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal (Denise's skepticism) and a ticking clock (glow night is tomorrow and the lights aren't done).
**Ends on:** An image. The electrician brings the whole bank of black lights up for a test, and down the building the trophy case goes violet. Linda's 187 card glows white in its frame. Denise stops writing. Cut there.
**Word count target:** 500

### Scene 4
**Setting:** Friday, Dec 11. First glow night, then after close. Opens 8:50 p.m. at the counter: black lights, sound system, all ten lanes going. Cuts to 11:25 p.m. in the pit behind lane 3, with the catwalk, a work light, and the sweep's thunk.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Ray, Denise (first beat only)
**Surface action:**
- **Money moves (keep it brief, about 120 words):** All ten lanes running and a waiting list on a strip. Denise corrects the register from memory and tells Ellen, without looking up, that the snack bar has done a league night's business by nine. She can't help adding that the popcorn machine and the space heater can't both be on. Ellen walks the floor counting heads under her breath: 52.
- Lane 3 starts dropping its table late. Ellen pulls it from service and moves the party to the waiting list. She doesn't call Ray. At 11:20, after close, the back door opens. Ray signs the strip first (DATE / 2320 / — / 03 / TABLE LATE) and then goes into the pit.
- **Comic middle:** Ray in the pit under leftover black light. His canvas jacket, his pencil, and the tape labels he's written over twenty years all glow. "Nobody told me the tape did that." Ellen: "It's been doing it the whole time. You never had the lights for it." He asks, sincerely, whether the lights are permanent.
- **Ray's price.** On the catwalk, with his hands in lane 3's table, he names it. He'll teach her. In return, the championship stays as it's always been: last Saturday of February, all ten lanes, the Linda Gagnon Memorial, nothing glowing. He reaches for the motor switch to cycle the machine.
- **The promise, word for word, with Ray mid-motion.** Ellen, from the pit doorway: **"You have my word. The championship stays as it is, the last Saturday in February, all ten lanes, unless the numbers say otherwise."** On "all ten lanes" he throws the switch. The table drops and the sweep comes down with its thunk over the tail of the sentence. He's watching the table timing, counting the cycle under his breath. "That's all I'm asking," he says to the machine. He wipes his hand on a rag and holds it out. She shakes it.
- She says **"Affirmative"** (**ATC 2**) to his "Monday, then. Seven." Clean, not cute.
**Interior passage 3 (short, 2 sentences, no explanation):** She did not get a readback. She noticed that she didn't, and his hand was already out, and she took it. (Stop there. No commentary on what it means.)
**Subtext:** Ray is buying the one thing he can't buy, a promise that something stays the same. Ellen, who never fudges a number, attached the only honest condition there is, and she knows people hear what they came for.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and dramatic irony. The reader hears both halves.
**Ends on:** An action. The table rises on its seven-second cycle. Ray watches it come up level, and Ellen goes back out front to turn off the black lights, bank by bank. No quip and no reflection.
**Word count target:** 650 (warranted: the money beat plus the required promise)

### Scene 5 — SET PIECE: 7 a.m. lessons
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, lanes 5–6, house lights only on that pair. Monday, Dec 14, 7:00 a.m., with a quick montage tag for Wednesday and Friday. The building is still cold. Baseboard ticking. Glow-night confetti in the gutter of lane 6.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Ray
**Surface action:**
- Ellen arrives with a clipboard, a protractor-style sketch of entry angles to the 1–3 pocket, and her reading glasses on the cord. Ray, in his own shoes, looks at the clipboard. "Stop doing math at the pins."
- **Comic middle:** Her lofted balls touch down past the lob line and skip like flat stones. The foul light comes on and the pins don't count. Three in a row. She records each one on the clipboard anyway. Ray: "You writing down the misses?" Ellen: "The misses are the data." The 300 question: she asks the maximum. "Nobody's ever bowled a 300. Best anybody's done is 245." Ellen considers a system designed so no one completes it. Ray: "That's the part people like." Ray asks whether she wants him to show her or tell her. **"Negative,"** she says (**ATC 3**). Then she corrects herself: "Show me."
- **First touch.** Ray corrects her release. He stands to her right, takes her wrist, turns her hand so the palm faces the pins, and lowers it six inches: let it go low, let the lane have it. Hands only, about three sentences, no heat language. He lets go first. The next ball stays on the lane and chops the front, leaving wood standing behind.
- **The deadwood lesson.** She has fallen pins lying on the deck and a cluster standing. She starts to ask for a re-rack. Ray: **"The wood stays on the lane. You play what's left."** (Full line, use 1 of 3. Nobody comments on it. Ellen doesn't repeat it.) He tells her to aim at the fallen pin, not the standing ones. He names the leave in passing, once ("That's a spread eagle"), with no pin numbers.
- Montage tag (2–3 sentences): Wednesday she leaves the clipboard in the car. Friday she brings it and leaves it face-down on the settee.
**Interior passage 4 (Ellen):** Her fear, as a memory. She was never bad at anything in front of a witness, not in thirty years. Her calm was so good that nobody ever saw a mistake until it was already a report. Being visibly bad at something, with a man watching, is new. She keeps her face level and finds she doesn't want to.
**Subtext:** Being taught is a form of being relieved, and she hates it. Ray is teaching the game the way Linda bowled it, which costs him something. Neither says so.
**Tension source:** Internal (her need to be competent) and interpersonal (proximity, the touch).
**Ends on:** An action and an image. Friday: she uses the deadwood and takes down the last standing pin with her third ball. Ray says nothing and steps to the ball return to wait for her ball. The clipboard stays face-down on the orange settee.
**Word count target:** 650

### Scene 6
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes office and counter. Saturday, Dec 19, 11:40 p.m., after the Highway Department party (the last of the week's six). Printing adding machine. Arlene's green ledgers on two shelves. The 2019 fuel-oil calendar. Fryer cooling.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Denise (first half)
**Surface action:**
- **Comic middle (Denise, talky):** Denise, coat on and not leaving, reads the R.G. — AFTER HOURS strip aloud like a gossip column. Dec 11, lane 3, TABLE LATE. Dec 15, lane 9, BELT. Dec 17, lane 3, TABLE LATE AGAIN. Dec 18, lane 6, "GLOW LIGHT BRACKET — NOT MINE, CHECKED ANYWAY." She has opinions about all of it, including how square his capitals are and what Linda used to say about his handwriting. She leaves with one hard "honey" line about the town's pool. Someone added a new row this week, and "they're betting later now. Don't let it go to your head."
- **Money moves on the page:** Alone, Ellen runs the adding machine. The appraiser came Wednesday and she paid it. Glow nights, two weeks of parties, and Ray's invoices all go on the tape. **Net operating income to date: $5,600. Cash: $34,300.** About ninety percent of where a straight line to thirty thousand says she should be. Behind, but closer than the week before. She takes no salary; her pension covers her (state this once, in one plain line, if Ch 01 and 02 haven't).
- She projects forward and notices the league nights are the thinnest columns on the tape. She doesn't know why yet. She looks up at Arlene's ledgers and writes a strip for January: RECONCILE / ARLENE / LEAGUE. (Seed for Ch 05. No conclusions.)
**Interior passage 5 (short, optional if S1–S5 already carry three):** Being at ninety percent is a number she knows. It's the point where you either get ahead of the traffic or it gets ahead of you, and the room stays quiet either way until it doesn't.
**Subtext:** Her promise has a condition, and she has just started measuring it. The reader feels the chill. Ellen doesn't name it.
**Tension source:** Situational. The gap is closing, but the column she can't explain is the league's.
**Ends on:** An image. She writes the championship's strip and pins it at the far end of the board: 02/27 / 1800 / LGM / 10 / with REMARKS blank. She leaves the pen uncapped beside it and turns off the counter lights. No closing reflection.
**Word count target:** 350 (keep the solo stretch under a page)
