# Reader Notes: Chapter Three, "Deadwood"

Read chapters 01 to 03 in order. These notes cover chapter 03.

## Where I got bored

- **Judy's criteria, then Ellen reading them back.** "Net operating income of thirty thousand dollars, for the period December first through February twenty-eighth, inclusive. Cash on deposit..." I had just read the numbers in Judy's words, and now I read them again in Ellen's. Then Judy is amazed by the readback. This is the third chapter where someone is struck by Ellen reading something back (Ray at the counter in ch 02, the Bench going quiet, now Judy). The bit has stopped surprising me, and I skimmed.
- **Denise's crossword monologue at the electrician's ladder.** "Eleven letters, 'mutual agreement.' Roger had a ladder. Roger had a lot of things..." Her voice was a pleasure in ch 01. Here I hit a wall of it, broken up with crossword clues, and I started reading only her first and last sentences.
- **The bookkeeping paragraph.** "Four glow nights and eleven parties went on the tape, then the scavenged parts and the electrician, then Ray's invoices..." This is a ledger written as prose. Then comes "Eighty-eight percent of where a straight line to thirty thousand said she ought to be." I had to stop and check the math to see whether I should be worried. I'm not sure I should be.
- **The office-party rescheduling.** The dentist's head count of 140, the move to 1700, PER D. LAVOIE. I could feel this was meant as a small warm beat between Ellen and Denise. My eyes slid off the strip codes before I got to it.

## Where I got confused

- **The glow bowling just shows up.** In chapter 2 there was no glow night. Chapter 3 opens with an electrician already on a ladder installing black lights. When did Ellen decide this, and what did it cost? It's a big change to Arlene's building and Linda's trophy case, and the decision happened off the page. Because of that, Ray's "Nothing glowing" has less behind it than it should.
- **"Stop doing math at the pins." / "At the clipboard."** I read this twice and still don't know what Ellen means. Is she correcting him? Is it a joke?
- **The cash target looks like it's already met.** Judy says $25,000 in the bank on Feb 28. At the end of the chapter Ellen has $34,300. So one of the two numbers she's racing seems fine already, and that takes air out of the stakes. If the cash is going to drain, I wasn't told why.
- **"Less behind than last week."** We never saw last week's number, so I can't feel the progress.
- **The timeline in the second half.** It runs glow Friday, then a Monday lesson, Wednesday, Friday, then "twenty to midnight on Saturday," plus R.G. card dates of 12/11, 12/15, 12/17 and 12/18. I lost track of which Saturday we were on and how much time had passed. It felt like roughly ten days squeezed into fifteen paragraphs.
- **Roger.** I worked out that he's Denise's ex. One line saying so would have spared me a sentence of guessing.

## Where I stopped believing

- **The promise drowned out by the sweep.** Ellen says "unless the numbers say otherwise," and right on cue the machine comes down "with its thunk across the end of the sentence." That's a very convenient pinsetter. Then the narration tells me exactly what to notice: "Ellen noticed that he had not said it back to her. Not one word of it." The bigger problem is character. For three chapters Ellen has been the woman who requires a readback for a breaker setting and a door key. Here she knowingly shakes hands on a promise she knows he didn't hear. That isn't her. It's the plot needing a misunderstanding for later, and I could see it being set up.
- **Ray thaws fast.** Chapter 2 ends with "Then I'll see you March first," and he has a bank letter in his wallet. A few days later he's scavenging parts for her up past Plymouth, accepting her key, logging his hours on her card, and teaching her to bowl. The fixing I can believe, because he does it for Thursday league and for Linda. The cheerful cooperation I can't quite believe this soon.
- **The invoice haggling.** Ellen raises the price every time he objects: "Object again and it goes up again." / "You're bad at this." / "I'm accurate at this." It's a familiar rom-com routine, and these two people became sitcom characters for half a page.
- **The black light shows Linda's card.** The light comes on and the one thing glowing white in the case is LINDA GAGNON. Then "Denise stopped writing." Too neat. The building hands the author the symbol.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Every scene ends on a placed object or a neat line:** "HELD. NOT MINE." / "Then he closed his fist around it." / "Denise stopped writing." / "turned off the black lights, one bank at a time." / "waited for her ball to come back." / "turned off the counter lights." Across three chapters I can now predict the shape of each scene's last line.
- **The key ring jingles again.** It's the third chapter in a row with the orange-tag ring jingling and a fist closing on it. I've got the symbol.
- **The crossword answers are on theme.** "Concordance." "Revealed. It's a theme." Denise literally says "It's a theme." That's the author winking through her.
- **Explained feelings:** "She had missed being measured. A bank was an awkward place to find that out." "She knew exactly why she had bought this building." Then she tells me why, and chapter 1 already told me.
- **Everyone talks in quips.** Judy ("I like to say it while I do it," "The underwriter didn't believe that either"), Denise ("A call is a favor. A text is just information."), Hollis, and Normie in the last chapter are all dry, quick and aphoristic. The town is one voice in different coats.
- **"So the system is designed so that no one ever completes it." / "That's the part people like."** A nice line, but it's the book explaining its own theme.

## What I want to know next

- **Why are league nights the thinnest columns on the tape?** This is the best hook in the chapter, a real mystery with numbers attached. Is Arlene undercharging, comping, or hiding something in those green ledgers?
- **When will "unless the numbers say otherwise" blow up?** I'm fairly sure it will, and that Ellen will have to touch the Linda Gagnon Memorial. I want to see it, but more out of dread than curiosity, because I can see it coming.
- **The 02/27 strip with REMARKS blank.** Sure, I'll follow it.
- **Who now holds the later squares in the pool, and does Ray ever buy one?**

The pull is real but modest. The league-night question is the only thing I didn't see coming.

## Best moment

> The electrician had run one fixture over the masking at the lane ends, and it lit the catwalk violet through the machines. Ray's canvas jacket glowed, and the pencil behind his ear. Every strip of tape he had ever written on, twenty years of PIN ELEVATOR 4 and BELT, SPARE and DON'T, hung glowing on its nail along the wall.
>
> He stood looking at them.
>
> "Nobody told me the tape did that."
>
> "It's been doing it the whole time," Ellen said. "You never had the lights for it."

This is the one place where the symbolism earns itself. It's concrete, it's specific to him, and nobody explains it. Runner-up: "The wood stays on the lane. You play what's left."

## More energy than chapter 02?

More motion, less tension. Chapter 3 has six scenes, a bank, a glow night with 52 bowlers, and lessons, so it moves faster than chapter 2's quiet hardware store. But chapter 2 had a man trying to buy her out, a key that no longer turned, and a stand-off. In chapter 3 the antagonist becomes a cooperating mechanic and coach within a week, the cash target looks safe, and the only friction is planted, not felt. Busier, but less alive.

## Verdict

I'd keep reading, for now. The setting, Denise, and the candlepin details are carrying it. But by chapter 4 I need Ray and Ellen to actually want opposite things again, on the page, and I need the scene endings to stop landing like tidy little bows. If chapter 4 is another chapter of pleasant competence, that's where I'd put it down.
