## Structural

1. **The promise and the sweep (S4).** The approved outline says Ray doesn't hear the tail ("unless the numbers say otherwise"). Keep that, but stop the machine from arriving on cue.
   - Earlier in S4, establish that lane 3's table is cycling every seven seconds while Ray tests it. The sweep is loud, and both of them have been talking around it and pausing for it. One short, concrete beat is enough.
   - Ellen gives the promise while it's cycling. The noise covers the end of the sentence because it has been covering things all scene, not because the timing is cute. Cut "as she got to all ten lanes."
   - Make not asking for a readback Ellen's own choice, shown and not explained. She has asked for readbacks all week (door, key, breaker). Here she could ask, and she looks at his hands in the machine, or the glowing tape, and doesn't. She knows she is letting it go. Do not explain why. Replace "Ellen noticed that he had not said it back to her. Not one word of it." with something in that register, 2–3 sentences.
   - This keeps Ch 06 honest: both are telling the truth, and she owns the lapse.
2. **Ray thaws too fast.** Keep his opposition alive on the page.
   - In S2 (the key deal), he takes the per-call deal and the key because the machines need someone who knows them, and he says so in a way that makes clear he still plans to own them on March 2. One or two lines, for example that he's keeping them running for whoever holds the building after March first.
   - He still won't buy a pool square. The invoice haggling should stay friction, not banter: cut the escalating-price routine ("Object again and it goes up again" / "You're bad at this" / "I'm accurate at this") to one exchange at most.
3. **Glow night decided off the page.** Add a short beat, in S1 or S2, where the decision and its cost show: $5,400 for black lights, LED lane lighting and sound, out of cash. Ray's reaction ("Nothing glowing" later) should have this to push against. A line or two of dialogue, not narration.
4. **Stakes in S6.** Make the worry legible.
   - Ellen is at 88% of a straight-line pace to $30,000, and December had the parties and the holiday weeks. January has none booked yet.
   - Cut "less behind than last week," since we never saw last week.
   - In S1 Judy can give one plain line that the cash test is the one people pass in December and fail in February. No foreshadowing of the boiler beyond the appraiser looking at it, which is already there.
   - Keep the ledger paragraph to two or three short sentences.
