## Pre-flight flags (read before drafting)

1. **The outline's folding option conflicts with Ch 05. Don't use it.** Ch 05 ends with Ellen's text "*Sunday 1400. Here. About the league.*" and Ray's "*ok*." "Here" is Kettle Lanes. If Ellen walked out to the bob-house that morning instead, she would be breaking her own appointment. So the ledger talk is its own short scene at the alley at 2 p.m. (S2), and the bob-house dawn (S1) is kept short. **Fallback if the chapter runs long:** cut S1 to about 300 words, and open S3 inside the phone call (the failure heard in one paragraph). Never trim S4.
2. **Ledger talk versus argument: how they stay separate without Ellen keeping a secret.** In S2 Ellen tells Ray everything she knows on January 17: the $700, the projection, and that a mid-season raise doesn't close the gap. He leaves.** That is his flaw on the page (a quiet exit instead of a hard conversation at noon) and her rule-20 loss. She isn't hiding anything; he chose not to hear it twice. The real argument over the condition comes in S5, when she has actually changed the championship and needs him. Rule 7 holds.

   She lays this out in S5 term by term. It is open (she tells him first, she has asked Verna for time at Tuesday's meeting, she pitches the derby in public) and it is made for a reason (the tape).
6. **Both of them are telling the truth (S5).** Ray: she gave her word, he never heard a condition, and she let him repeat the short version to the Rotary. Ellen: she said it, out loud, in her own pit, and the numbers now say otherwise. She also admits, plainly, that she saw him not hear it and didn't ask. **That admission is her price for being right (rule 20), and it is not the Ch 07 ownership line.** Keep it to a fact ("I watched you not hear it. In Ray's interior he replays December 11, finds the sweep coming down where her sentence ended, and can't swear she didn't say it. That makes it worse, not better.
7. **Rule 2 at the boiler.** Ray hears the failure and **calls her before going downstairs**. He goes down only after she says "Look. Don't touch." Ellen calls the emergency number on the **2019 fuel-oil company calendar** (Ch 01–02 plant: she put it back because the numbers are right; rule 14). He reaches once for a pipe wrench out of habit and she stops him (comic middle).

### Scene 1
**Setting:** Hollis's bob-house, about 300 yards off the public access on Great Kettle Lake. Sunday Jan 17, 6:50 a.m., gray first light. Six by eight, plywood, blaze-orange door with his name stenciled on it. The propane heater hisses, and the weather radio is turned low. Two holes in the floor are skinning over; Hollis clears one with a ladle. It's warm at head height and freezing at the ankles. Through the small window, one tip-up flag is down (no fish, just the flag) and the others are standing up. The ice booms somewhere toward the far shore.
**Characters present:** Ray, Hollis
**Surface action:**
- **Open mid-task.** Hollis is skimming ice out of the hole with a ladle and doesn't look up. Ray is on the overturned bucket that has been his since about 1971. Sundays at dawn are his standing time. Mention this once, in passing, because S6 depends on it.
- Hollis takes off the cap, thinks, and puts it back on: "You courting her, or just fixing her machines a lot?"
- **The fella story (the chapter's one).** Ray: "Fella I knew in Wolfeboro—" Hollis: "That was you." Ray: "It was a fella." Hollis: "It was you, and it was 1983, and she married a dentist." (Comic middle. Hollis keeps a perfect record and isn't being unkind.)
- Hollis checks the sky through the window before he asks the next one (sky check 1 of 2): "She know you're keeping the letter?" Ray: "Told her last night." Hollis writes something in the waterproof notebook. Ray doesn't ask what.
- **Rule 14 plant for S6.** Hollis says the derby committee still has no indoor fallback if the ice goes bad on the last weekend of February. The Legion hall is booked for a wedding, and the town hall holds sixty people. He says it as a fact, in his "Don't know yet" way. Ray says nothing. It isn't his building to offer, and both of them know whose it is.
- Ray says he has to be somewhere at two. Hollis: "Where?" Ray: "Kettle Lanes." Hollis: "On a Sunday." He doesn't make it a question.

**Interior passage 1 (Ray, 2–4 sentences):** What the alley holds for him. The Sunday after Linda's funeral he sat on this bucket and Hollis didn't ask him a single question for four hours. He has kept that Sunday the same every week since, the way he has kept the league the same: the same lanes, the same night, her trophy on the center shelf. Plain words, in his idiom (wear, grain, tolerances).

**Subtext:** Ray has kissed a woman for the first time in six years. The best friend who asks him everything is the one person he can't dodge, and he tries anyway with the fella.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and comic. Hollis's license-check questioning against Ray's sideways answers. Underneath: Ray already suspects what 2 p.m. is about ("About the league") and has said nothing to Hollis.
**Ends on:** Mid-conversation, cut early. Hollis: "What's she want at two?" Ray starts, "League bus—" and a flag pops outside. Hollis is already up and reaching for the door. Cut before the fish or the answer.
**Word count target:** 400

### Scene 2 — THE LEDGER TALK
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, Sunday Jan 17, 1:58–2:20 p.m. The alley is open on a slow Sunday afternoon: two lanes going (a father and two kids on 3–4), Denise at the counter with the crossword. Then the office: three green ledgers open on the steel desk (1979, 2004, 2025), the folded adding-machine tape, the space heater running, the 2019 fuel-oil calendar on the wall. Ellen is standing (she stands for bad news, including bad news she's delivering).
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen, Denise (one line, at the counter)
**Surface action:**
- **Payoff for "She's going to say something."** Ray comes in at 1:58. Denise doesn't look up from the crossword: "Office. She's been standing since noon." That's her whole contribution. It isn't about the kiss, and the reader knows it is.
- **Ellen leads with the money, in order, no preamble.** "Your league costs me seven hundred dollars a week." (The mandated line, word for word.) She shows him the green ink: 2004, "per league"; Normand's 1979 prize-fund line; the banquet, trophies, and free strings, every spring. Then the margin: "R.G., no charge, wouldn't take it." Ray reads the ham line. He doesn't laugh.
- **Ray takes it hard, in his idiom.** He picks up the adding-machine tape and runs it with his thumb the way he'd check a belt for wear. Then he makes his offer, which is a real one: he'll take a rate increase to Verna and the captains for next season. He'll stand up and argue for it himself in September.
- **Ellen: "I need it now."** He says the league paid in September for a season at the September rate, and you don't change the price in the middle. People quit over less, and Pine Ridge is forty minutes away. (He's right, and Ch 07 proves it.) She tells him what the raise is worth if nobody quits: close, and still short.
- **The strip.** She unpins 02/27 / 1800 / LGM / 10 from the board she brought in, sets it on the open 2004 ledger, and says it's the next line on the tape. Ray: "You gave your word." Ellen: "I did. All of it."
- **He doesn't ask.** He puts the tape down square on the ledger, says he has a lock to look at for the Lakeview Diner at three (rule 14: real, and true), and goes. She doesn't stop him.

**Interior passage 2 (Ray, 2–4 sentences), as he reads the margins:** Arlene wrote him down for twenty years and never told him. The league as Linda bowled it, Tuesday and Thursday, the same price since the year Denise came to the counter, was a thing he thought he'd been keeping up, and it turns out somebody else was paying for it. He hears "All of it" and knows there's a second half to something, and he would rather fix a clutch at midnight than ask what it is at two in the afternoon.

**Subtext:** She tells him the hardest thing first, the day after she kissed him, because she won't let him keep believing in a building that doesn't exist. He leaves because the only answers available are ones he can't stand.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Her number against his loyalty. The championship strip on the desk between them.
**Ellen loses this round (rule 20).** She has no answer to "You don't change the price in the middle," because it's true. And the man she needs walks out before she can finish.
**Ends on:** Mid-motion. Ray going back through the alley past the counter. Denise, still not looking up, holds out a pencil. He takes it without breaking stride and is out the door before he notices he's taken it. Cut. (Not a quip, not a still image.)
**Word count target:** 550

### Scene 3 — THE BOILER
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, Tuesday Jan 26, 11:10 p.m. to about 12:50 a.m. An ordinary winter night, about 18°F, clear. Ray is in the pit behind lane 3 on a logged call: R.G. card, 01/26 / 2310 / 03 / CLUTCH. The drop light hangs on its hook, the coffee can of bolts is on the workbench, and the building is empty. Then the office and the top of the steep basement stairs. Then the basement: the cast-iron oil boiler (older than Kate), the oil tank, pipes in fraying insulation, and water spreading across the concrete toward the floor drain.
**Characters present:** Ray; Ellen (by phone, then in person from about 12:30)
**Surface action:**
- **Diagnosis by sound (Ray's comic engine, played straight).** At 12:08 he has the clutch nearly done when the baseboard stops ticking. Under the floor there's a pop he has never heard from this building, then a hiss like a kettle somebody forgot, then the burner cutting out. He knows what a cracked section sounds like from the store's own boiler in 1994. He doesn't go down.
- **He calls her first (flag 7).** 12:10 a.m. She answers on the second ring. He tells her what he heard, in the order he heard it. Ellen: "Say again the second sound." (**ATC 1.**) He says it again. She says, "Look. Don't touch." He goes down with the drop light, and from the third step he can see the water and a dark seam down the third section. He tells her that. He tells her the burner is off, and he doesn't guess why.
- **She works a checklist, not a toolbox.** Over the phone, while she's evidently pulling on boots, she gives it in order: stay off the floor water; she's eleven minutes out; the emergency number for the oil company is on the 2019 calendar in the office, top left, will he read it to her. He reads it. She reads it back.
- **12:30, in person.** She comes down the stairs in her navy vest over a sweater, with a flashlight. On the technician's phone instructions she throws the red emergency switch at the top of the stairs and closes the feed valve, reading each step back. **Comic middle:** Ray, out of forty years of habit, takes the pipe wrench off its nail. Ellen, without looking up: "Is that wrench certified?" He hangs it back up.
- **The checklist, said plainly.** A certified oil-heat technician is two hours out, maybe more; he's on a no-heat call across the lake. Heaters are booked from the rental yard for 7 a.m. Tomorrow is closed: she'll call Wednesday's two bookings herself at 8. She writes all of it on an index card in strip format (no comment on it). Ray watches the vestibule thermometer on his way up for the second flashlight: 61.

**Interior passage 3 (Ray, 2–3 sentences):** He has a key to this building because of the machines. He has never been in the basement without Arlene's say-so, and tonight he called a woman out of bed rather than walk down eleven stairs. He isn't sure whether that's manners or something he doesn't want to name.

**Subtext:** Every instinct Ray has is to fix it, and the one thing in the building he isn't allowed to fix is the one that's broken. Ellen is perfectly calm. From his side, he sees that she's doing it at 12:30 a.m. in a sweater put on inside out, and he doesn't mention that either.
**Tension source:** Situational (heat lost, the clock against the cold) and internal (Ray's hands with nothing to do).
**Ends on:** Mid-conversation, cut away. Ellen, phone to her ear, on hold with the technician's dispatcher, holds out her free hand for the second flashlight without looking. Ray puts it in her hand. The dispatcher comes back on. Cut before what the dispatcher says.
**Word count target:** 500

### Scene 4 — 3 A.M., KISS 2 (PROTECT ABOVE ALL)
**Setting:** The basement, Wednesday Jan 27, about 2:50 to 3:25 a.m. Only the two flashlights. The boiler is dead and cooling, and the crack shows as a darker line in the cast iron. The water has stopped spreading and is filming over the low spot by the drain. The oil tank is in the corner. The building above them is silent: no baseboard ticking, no machines, the sign off. Their breath shows faintly at the bottom of the stairs. Ellen sits on the third stair from the bottom; Ray stands, then sits two stairs above her.
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen
**Surface action:**
- **Nothing left to do.** The checklist is done. The technician called at 2:40: forty minutes more. Ellen has run the vestibule thermometer twice (58, then 55) and reports both numbers to the room. She keeps her flashlight on the crack because it's the only thing in the building that's her responsibility right now, and she says so in fewer words.
- **Comic middle (rule 15), from cold and tiredness.** She tells him what the technician will say, in order, before he says it. Ray tells her that's a sure way to make a technician say something else. She asks which fella told him that. He says no fella, he's a hardware man; people come in with the diagnosis already written down and get offended when the part doesn't fit. (No "Fella I knew" here. The S1 story was the chapter's one.)
- **Rule 19 in the dark.** Ellen, plainly: "This is good for you." Ray doesn't dress it up: "On paper." She asks how much on paper. He knows roughly what a replacement costs, and he tells her the range he knows, with no hedging. Then he says he still has the letter. She says she knows.
- **The flashlight (seed for Ch 09; don't explain it).** Her beam starts to wobble on the crack. She's cold and doesn't say so. She moves the flashlight to her other hand instead of handing it to him. Ray sees that, and doesn't offer to take it.
- **The 3 a.m. talk.** One exchange that's private and new, not the 187 and not her last day (both used in Ch 04). Suggested: she asks why he called her before he came down. He answers, in his order, important words last: it's her building. She says that's the first time he's said so without "for now" after it. He says he didn't say "for now" this time either. Neither of them adds anything.
- **Kiss 2.** Ray is two steps up, so for once they're level. **She kisses him; or he asks again, in fewer words than in Ch 05 ("Ellen."), and she answers by putting the flashlight down on the stair, beam still on the crack.** The drafter chooses one. Show it through context only: the beam lighting the cracked iron and the cold coming off the floor; her mitten off this time because she was working the valve; one hand of his on her coat sleeve. One embrace at most. **Ray speaks first afterward**, something exact and unromantic in his idiom (the floor drain is slow and someone should look at it in daylight, someone qualified). It must not be the tidiest line in the scene.
- **The technician arrives.** Headlights cross the basement window well at 3:22. Ellen is up the stairs before the knock.

**Interior passage 4 (Ray, 2–4 sentences), before the kiss:** The boiler is going to cost her what he figures it costs, and every dollar of it moves the building a little closer to him. He has the letter in his wallet upstairs, dated through March 31. He's sitting on her cellar stairs at three in the morning holding a flashlight on the crack, and if anyone asked him which of those he wants, he would go quiet. State it as fact, in his idiom (wear, tolerances). No "torn," no "conflicted."

**Subtext:** Their only private time before the break. They're honest with each other about the one thing that should keep them apart, and they kiss anyway, knowing it. Again, it's a choice made with the facts on the table, not instead of them.
**Tension source:** Internal (Ray's two wants on the same stair). Situational (the cold, the clock). Interpersonal (she won't hand him the flashlight).
**Ends on:** Mid-motion. Ellen goes up the stairs toward the knock, already giving the technician the checklist from the top before the door is open. Ray picks up her flashlight from the stair, where it's still pointed at the crack, and follows her up. Cut before the door opens. (Not a still image: he's moving.)

### Scene 5 — THE ARGUMENT
**Setting:** Gagnon & Son Hardware, Thursday Jan 28, 5:35 p.m., just after closing. The Bench stools are empty and the coffee pot is off. The pallet of rock salt is by the door, the key cutter is at the end of the counter, and roof rakes hang overhead. Behind the register is the pool sheet, with a new row in Normie's handwriting: WEDDING DATE, three squares already bought. On the counter: Danny's tablet, left charging, and the store's printing adding machine. Ray has his coat on to go coach the Thursday league, which is bowling tonight in parkas with rented heaters (off the page; one clause at most).
**Characters present:** Ray, Ellen
**Surface action:**
- **Ellen comes in as he's turning the sign.** She has the technician's invoice ($24,500, paid by check Wednesday; the new boiler goes in tomorrow) and a four-page printed plan, stapled. She puts them on the counter in that order. She doesn't take off her coat.
- **She tells him before anyone else, and says so.** She has told no one else: not Denise, not Hollis, not Verna. She has asked Verna for three minutes at Tuesday's meeting without saying what for. The cash on Feb 28 now projects at "nineteen thousand," and Judy's floor is twenty-five. The income was already short. The boiler took the margin she didn't have.
- **The Kettle Open, term by term (flag 5).** She walks him through it the way she'd brief a shift change, and every line she keeps is from her December sentence. Saturday the 27th. All ten lanes. The Linda Gagnon Memorial, bowled for by the league's top teams, as the final round. Nothing glowing. What changes: the public enters the early rounds, spectators pay at the door, a raffle is split with the derby, and the derby's indoor events come to the alley if the ice goes. Then she asks him plainly: will he help her sell it to the league on Tuesday?
- **The argument (both true).**
  - Ray: She gave her word. Last Saturday in February, all ten lanes, as it is.
  - Ellen: She gave it with a condition: unless the numbers say otherwise. She says the numbers on the counter.
  - Ray: He never heard that part.
  - Ellen: She said it, in her pit, December eleventh, with the sweep running.
  - Ray (his best point): She let him say the short version to the Rotary on the sixteenth, three feet from her, and didn't say a word.
  - Ellen (her price, flag 6): "I watched you not hear it. I let it go. I let it go again on the sixteenth." Slow, no contractions (she's angry at herself, not at him).
  - Ray: "Mrs. Daly." Ellen: "Ms." The one use in the chapter, mid-argument. It's the one moment it isn't funny.
- **He says no.** He won't help her sell it. He won't stand up against it either; he says that as a fact about himself, not a promise to her. He jingles his key ring while he decides, then closes his fist on it (rule 17: once, mid-scene). She asks one more time, exactly: is that his answer? He says it is.
- **Comic middle (rule 15), small and dry, before the turn.** When she lays out the raffle, Ray asks, as a hardware man, who's printing the tickets, because the derby bought theirs from him in 2019 and still owes for half. Ellen writes it down. It's the last moment that's easy between them.
- **She sees the pool sheet** over his shoulder while he's answering. She doesn't mention it. He sees her see it. Neither does anything.
- **Ellen: "Then I'll start with Hollis."** She leaves the plan on the counter.

**Interior passage 5 (Ray, 3–4 sentences), when she says "I said it":** He goes back to December eleventh and finds it the way he'd find a sound in a machine. Lane 3's sweep was coming down, he was counting the cycle, and her sentence ran under it. He has her words up to "all ten lanes" and then the sweep, and he can't swear there was nothing after it. That's worse than if she were lying, and he doesn't tell her so.

**Subtext:** Both of them are right, and both know it, and it changes nothing. He can't help her take Linda's night and make it something else. She can't keep a condition she stated and then let him enforce half of it. The wedding-date row hangs behind them, and the town has bought squares on something the two of them can't agree on.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal, at full strength. Rule 19 (opposed wants, said out loud). Rule 20 (she's right, and it costs her: she has to confess the skipped readback, and she still loses him).
**Ends on:** A still image (1 of 2). Ray doesn't read the plan. He leaves it face-down on the counter under the pool sheet, turns off the counter lights, and goes out to coach a league in parkas. The plan is the last thing in the dark store. Don't describe it beyond its position.

### Scene 6 — THE DERBY PITCH (Hollis's set piece)
**Setting:** Great Kettle Lake, Sunday Jan 31, 6:55 to about 8 a.m. Hollis's bob-house again (same sensory anchors as S1, plus five people in six by eight feet). Low gray sky. The trucks Hollis disapproves of are parked out on the bob-house row. The public access at the end of Dock Road is visible from the window. On the floor: the bait smelt bucket, a cooler, and two overturned buckets for the committee. Ray is on his own bucket in the corner, because it's Sunday.
**Characters present:** Ray, Hollis, Ellen, two derby committee members (unnamed: the Sportsmen's Club treasurer, a man with a cash box he won't put down; the raffle woman, with a roll of tickets)
**Surface action:**
- **Rule 14, set up in two lines.** Thursday night Ellen called Hollis and asked for fifteen minutes with the committee, without saying why. Hollis gave her Sunday at seven at his shack, knowing exactly who sits on that bucket on Sundays. At 6:55 Hollis tells Ray, "Committee's coming. So's she." Then: "You can go." Ray stays. He doesn't explain why to Hollis or to himself.
- **Hollis measures before he lets her off the access** (watched through the window, from Ray's POV). Hollis walks to the shore to meet her. Every twenty yards he chips a hole with the spud bar and puts a tape in it. Ray doesn't need to hear the numbers; he knows them by the way Hollis stands. At the door Hollis reads them out for the record in two sentences at most (for example, eleven inches: eight of black, three of white, walking ice and sled ice, not truck ice on this side, whatever the trucks think). Don't reuse the bible's sample line verbatim.
- **The license check at the door.** Hollis's comic engine, played completely straight. Name. Address. Purpose of visit. Number of lines in the water. Fishing license? She has one, bought Saturday, and hands it over with the plan. Hollis reads the date and writes it in the notebook. Then: "Plan if the ice goes?" She hands him the printed plan, with contingencies. Nobody in derby history has done that. Hollis doesn't say so. He turns the page.
- **The pitch, short.** Ellen stands; there's nowhere to sit anyway. She uses the same terms she gave Ray, compressed. The derby's indoor events come to Kettle Lanes if the ice is unsafe: weigh-in on lane 10, the kids' division, the raffle drawn indoors. The raffle is split fifty-fifty, and the derby's half goes into a separate account the day the tickets are sold (plants HELD. NOT MINE. for Ch 10). Spectators for the Open buy at the door.
- **The town pushes back (rule 20).** The treasurer: the weigh-in has been at the town dock since before Hollis. The raffle woman: fifty-fifty of whose tickets? Ellen concedes the dock. The weigh-in stays there unless Hollis calls the ice, and she doesn't argue it. The raffle stays fifty-fifty, and the raffle woman says she'll think about it. She doesn't come around in this scene.
- **"Stand by."** Mid-pitch, two of Hollis's tip-up flags pop outside the window, one after the other. Ellen: "Stand by." (**ATC 2**, the outline's one line.) She's out the door before Hollis has his cap off. Through the window, Ray watches her bring a fish up hand over hand on a line she's never touched, exactly the way Hollis must have told her on the phone Thursday, or else she read it.
