## Where I got bored or skimmed

- **Ch 04, hilltop.** "Any boat here could be theirs, I can't judge one in that sea, and a wake across that bay leads straight to her," Dane said. This was a logistics exchange about why they can't leave, and my attention went. The Russ cow-lying-down gag pulled me back.
- **Ch 06, Marr's store.** "She opened the green ledger to his page. Beth read it upside down: night lines down the column, each one paid off in hundreds and never rung up as cash." From here to Arsenault leaving I was reading a ledger, a curtain, a safe, a key ring, a hook and Ned's phone all at once. It felt like the clues were being laid out for me on the counter. I skimmed.
- **Ch 06, the Wednesday walk.** "Turn round now and he walks us home," she said. "Walk." Fine idea, but it's the third scene in a row of watching the dragger watch them. I'd stopped feeling the dragger as a threat because it never does anything.
- **Ch 06, third sex scene.** "He stayed there a long time. When she pulled at his shoulder he kissed her knuckles and put her hand back where it had been, and went on." It's tender, but it's the third time we've done this, and the yes/yes structure was now predictable. I read faster.
- **Ch 07, the notebook entries.** "*D6 am. Q: CU written rpt. Pouch: two bags. Seal noted: red.*" I read this paragraph three times, then gave up and kept going, hoping the book would explain. It did, two chapters later.
- **Ch 10, Quist and Tess.** "An impeller bearing your price sticker was taken from that shelf at noon on Monday. Was it missed?" I skimmed the whole interview. It's a list of clue resolutions spoken in passive voice.

## Where I'd stop

No outright stop, but the closest call was **Ch 06 into the first half of Ch 07**. If I'd been reading on a weekday I'd have put the book down at the end of the Ch 06 store scene and maybe not picked it up again for a few days. The cliffhanger at the end of Ch 06 ("The float lights were dark.") is what would have got me back in.

## "I'd keep reading, but…" warnings

- **Ch 03:** I'd keep reading, but the generator-shed sex comes on day two with a woman who has just read "Do not disclose to witness" on his phone that morning. I went along with it because the engine scene is vivid, but it's fast for someone this guarded.
- **Ch 06:** I'd keep reading, but nothing in this chapter changes until its last page. Four scenes, all waiting.
- **Ch 07:** I'd keep reading, but I don't believe Beth leaves a shot man asleep, alone, behind a door that can't be barred from outside, so she can carry his notebook to an unheated church. Her reasoning ("Awake, he'd have the book back inside his jacket") is thin for a woman who has been careful about everything for 200 pages.
- **Ch 09:** I'd keep reading, but Ned's confession comes out in one tidy run in front of Tess, every clue explained in order: money, Ashby, the fish house, tonight. It plays like the last ten minutes of a TV mystery.
- **Ch 10:** I'd keep reading, but the series hook (the *Dorothy M.*) is a boat I've never been on, run by nobody I've met. I don't care who was at her wheel.

## Where I got confused

- **Ch 01, end.** "He was already moving, through the kitchen toward the back of the house, his right hand gone to his hip." I didn't immediately get why the cancelled ferry plus the empty skiff sent him for the gun. I worked it out in Ch 02 (the hood is now ashore).
- **Ch 02, opening line.** "She had sent Reyes out through the mudroom to look at the back steps, and now the kitchen door kept him out." I had to reread to see that the door is just sticky, not locked against him.
- **Ch 03.** "*Firm at thirty-one. Saturday noon.*" I thought for a moment this was a bribe. Then it turned out to be the boat, which was a good bait-and-switch.
- **Ch 04.** "She still couldn't say *Put my name in*, with the red book pressing her ribs." I didn't know what the red book was or what name, or what job. It was explained in Ch 06 ("Hundred-ton"). It held me as a question, but only just.
- **Ch 05.** "Dane's phone talked to the sky and nothing else. Beth kept her eyes on the pot." I don't know what this line is telling me.
- **Ch 07, the conclusion she draws.** "They missed the one they'd paid." I couldn't tell if she now thinks the shooters were aiming at her, at him, or at both, or whether she thinks Dane believes she's dirty. It's a big moment, and I was guessing at it.
- **Ch 07.** Q, Quist, Pike, Portland, Arlington, the county, the credit union. By this point I was losing track of which offstage authority knew what.
- **Ch 09, the confession.** "I told them Russ was on the pier in the light and your marshal aboard, so they'd come by water, where you'd see them first." So Ned sold her out and also set up the trap for them? I believed that he's torn, but I had to reconstruct what he wanted that night.
- **Ch 10.** "Your spare truck key was left on the back step the night the bridge came down, and again Monday noon." Only here did I work out that Ned left Tess's key out so the truck could be used at Ashby. That's a big reveal buried in a subordinate clause in someone else's interview.
- **Ch 10.** Two men in the Marine Patrol stern. One is the boy. I assume the other is Arsenault, but he was last in Russ's truck. I also never learned for certain who fired the shot at the float.
- **Ch 10, ending.** The book jumps from Beth running down the courthouse stairs to Dane on the car deck in December. I filled in the reunion myself. That mostly worked, but some readers will want the scene.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Arsenault, Ch 06.** "The men I run her for in Portland don't wait on the state, Tess. I owe them a boat that's sailed by then." A drug runner announces his employers and his deadline at a store counter, next to the witness. No one talks like that.
- **Beth leaving Dane, Ch 07.** See above. This is the biggest one.
- **Ned's reckoning.** Ned gave them the float key, and that got Dane shot and could have got Beth killed. On the page Beth gets one line about it ("You gave them my marshal"), and then the scene turns into a marriage proposal. I believed Ned. I didn't believe Beth's reaction was the whole of it.
- **The proposal on the night of the arrest.** It's lovely, but it's too neat: ham planted, Thanksgiving planted, "Ask me sober" planted, all paid off in the same five minutes as the betrayal.
- **Quist's voice.** "That will need to be repeated, Ms. Marr. Which question is being answered?" Nobody talks in that passive register, and the "Goddamn" before it doesn't fit the same person.
- **Tess as red herring.** The fold of hundreds (Ch 02), the truck (Ch 05), the ledger, the keys. It was heavy enough that I felt steered, and by Ch 08 I'd stopped believing it was Tess.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Every scene ends on a cut line or an image.** "She bent to the next coil." / "Nobody who had been in this house made that sound." / "Up the slope the yellow windows stood lit for nobody." / "and her hand was up in the cold for nobody." / "She found the blanket on the planks by feel and pulled it back up round her shoulders." Each one is good alone. All together the rhythm becomes a drumbeat, and I started waiting for the button.
- **"For nobody" appears twice** as a closing cadence (Ch 03 and Ch 08).
- **The dragging left heel** is in almost every chapter, described the same way each time: "a right boot ... the left heel dragging after it." By Ch 07 ("a step. A drag. A step.") it had become a sound cue, not a character.
- **Dane's under-fire monologue** happens three times (Ch 02 cable, Ch 07 shot, Ch 09 deck), always the same shape: position report, apology, a dry joke with "which." The first was charming ("You're very good at lying down"). By the third ("I've changed my mind about getting up") I could hear the template.
- **Tess's price-tag comebacks**, every appearance: "Wrong's free, Marshal. Right goes on your tab." / "Mop's a dollar. Floor's free." / "Restroom's customers only. Coffee's two." / "Free with purchase." It's a strong voice, but it only makes one move.
- **Consent beats in the sex scenes** are the same structure every time: he stops, says "Beth?", she says "Yes," he goes on. By the fourth scene it read like a checklist rather than these two people.
- **Counting in the shed scene.** "Four strokes. Eight. ... Twelve. Sixteen." and "She was the set. Dip, hold." The metaphor got mechanical in a way that pulled me out.
- **Ch 10, the climax line.** "One slow pull took Beth whole, head bowed, both palms braced across Dane's chest." This reads like a summary of an orgasm, not one. It's also the only scene that uses "cock," and the register jump surprised me.
- **Explaining feelings.** "Somewhere in there it had turned into two people on a cold deck holding each other by the clothes, and she couldn't have said which of them was supposed to move first." / "Nobody had waited on her like that since her father." These are a beat more explained than the rest of the book, which mostly trusts me.
- **Repeated phrasing.** "the cold took the air out of her" (Ch 06) and "the cold took the air out of him" (Ch 09). "Nobody but me and God" works as a deliberate plant. The others look accidental.

## The prediction section

What I guessed, and when:

- **Ch 01:** Ned is good and solid, the father figure. Tess is the gossip. Dane will turn out to be compromised somehow. The phone he turns away from her is the hook.
- **Ch 02:** "Who knew you were here?" My suspects were Dane (the phone glow on the front step), Tess (the hundreds in her vest), and Ned or Tess because she told them "Home." I leaned Tess.
- **Ch 03:** "Q's request approved. Log daily. Do not disclose." I guessed Dane is investigating Beth. I was right, sort of.
- **Ch 05:** Tess's truck at the fire. The book wanted me to think Tess. I half did, and half suspected it was too obvious.
- **Ch 06:** Ned's forepeak confession about the "shorts" made me trust him more, which I now see was the point. The float question to Ned made me nervous immediately.
- **Ch 07:** The float lights are dark, and Ned set the timer. I suspected Ned from here.
- **Ch 08:** "I'd still put Haskell on the list" plus "He didn't look back." I was sure by the end of Ch 08. The reveal in Ch 09 confirmed it rather than surprised me. I think that's acceptable: it felt fair, and the red seal and the two bags in Ch 01 are honest plants I'd missed.
- **Did the solution feel cheated?** No. It felt over-explained at the point of delivery.
- **Ending guesses:** I predicted Beth gets the wheel and Dane comes back for a boat. Both happened. I didn't predict Ned's proposal or Tess's "Tell him yes," and that's the one true surprise in the book.

## The listener pass (if I were hearing this as audio)

- **Hard to follow aloud:** the notebook entries ("D5 0150 report. No indication." / "D6 am. Q: CU written rpt.") will be gibberish spoken. The Ch 08 "Teresa Marr" sentence and the Ch 09 deck-crossing sentence are long polysyndeton runs that will lose a listener halfway.
- **Untagged fast exchanges:** the Ch 08 Beth/Dane interrogation over the book ("Walked the bar." / "A lie..." / "You looked me in the eye." / "I did") and the Ch 10 Quist/Tess interview. In audio I'd lose track of who's speaking.
- **POV switch:** Ch 09's "Dane sat in the dark lane..." is the first time we're in his head, after eight chapters in hers. On the page I caught it. By ear I wouldn't have known for a paragraph or two. The final scene does it again.
- **Jargon density:** binder, D-ring, brow, dolphin, transfer bridge, sill, gut, impeller, governor, bleed screw, Giglio, hundred-ton. Readers can skim past these. Listeners can't.
- **Names that blur by ear:** Q and Quist. Ashby and Arsenault. *Margaret Ann* and *Dorothy M.*
- **What plays well aloud:** dialogue with Dane, Tess and Russ is distinct enough to follow without tags. Ned's weather lines ("Glass is going down") work as a spoken refrain.

## Would I buy book 2?

**Probably not on this hook.** The setup for book 2 is Russ on Quist's task force and the *Dorothy M.*, a green forty-footer nobody can put a man to. I've never seen the boat, and the question has no face. If book 2 is Beth and Dane on the island with Beth running the ferry, I'd buy it. If it's Russ and a mystery boat, no.

## What I want to know next

- What happens to Ned? Does Beth testify against him, and how does she live with having named "Edward Haskell" to a grand jury?
- Does Tess actually marry Ned, and what is that marriage with him inside? This is the strongest pull in the book, and it's a side character's.
- Does Dane keep his badge after Arlington? The Giglio problem follows him.
- Does the district make Beth's command permanent?
- Who ran the *Dorothy M.*? I'm told to care, but I don't yet.

## The book as a whole

**Did the ending feel earned?** Mostly yes. Beth taking the *North Reach* off the slip alone is planted from the first chapter (she has the correction "a breath before Ned made it") and pays off cleanly at Pruett ("nobody stood beside her to make it after her"). The red book, "Put my name in," and Ned's "You put it in" all land. The romance ending is earned in the sense that the book did the work. "I love you" after ten days is fast, but "Since when?" / "You jumped." made me smile. The last image of the ramp rising and the black water opening mirrors the opening and closes the book well. What didn't feel earned was Beth's emotional reckoning with Ned. The plot closes, but she never really breaks over him on the page.

**Which character will I remember?** Ned, first: the man who can't swim, steps off the float on Dane's one arm, and proposes sober on the night he sells everyone out. Then Tess's "Tell him yes." Beth is solid and competent, but she's more of a lens than a person to me. Dane's voice I'll remember, but as a cadence, not as a man.

**Where would I most likely have stopped reading?** Chapter 6, after the Marr's store scene, around "Good till next August." It's 55–60% into the book, the sex scenes are starting to repeat, the dragger has watched them for three chapters without doing anything, and the clues are being laid out on a counter for me to inventory. If I'd made it to the "float lights were dark" line, I'd have finished. Many readers wouldn't get there that day.

**Other whole-book notes:**
- The heat level came as a surprise from the title and the first two chapters, which read like a clean suspense novel. The first explicit scene, in Ch 03, changed what kind of book I thought I was holding.
- The prose is the strongest thing here: specific, physical, local. It also tends to resolve every paragraph on a cadence, and over 31,000 words that sameness is what made me skim, more than any slow scene.
- The middle (Ch 04–07) circles. Hide, get found, move, repeat. Ashby, then the fish house, then the church, then the boat. Each move is justified, but the shape repeats.
