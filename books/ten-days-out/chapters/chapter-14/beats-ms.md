2. **The phone (B7).** These amendment lines are retired:
   - the call-log claim, "First switched on Saturday, twenty to one";
   - "From the point they can see the pier all day. In the dark they can't see it leave."

   **What replaces them:**
   - **Its job:** to tell the big boat when the ferry leaves the pier in the dark.
   - **How Dane knows it went in on Saturday:** the battery is near full, and the bag is dry with no ice on it. Friday night the car deck took spray all night, and everything on it wore ice by morning.

## Scene 1 (Beth)

**Setting:** The town office front room, a little after ten on Friday night. A lantern on the clerk's desk and the stove door open. Then the harbor road and the stone pier, with snow blowing sideways, and the captain's cabin up the steel stair. **Sensory anchor:** the sound through the wall, steel grinding on stone, a long shriek and then a thud each time the sea lifts the ferry and drops it.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Tess (one line, to Dane). Russ, Ned and the kid are silent images.

**Surface action:**
1. The binding opener, then the sound in plain words. Beth is on her feet with her coat half on before she has decided anything. "I'm taking it off."
2. **Dane on the job, four sentences of action, no talk.**
   - He runs a thumb under each of the kid's cable ties and adds a fresh one. The kid's face is to the wall, as it has been since the man from the big boat stood at the step. That's one clause.
   - Ned sits cuffed at the pipe, listening to his boat grind on the stone.
   - Russ is on the couch with his leg up, holding.
   - Tess holds the shotgun out to Dane: "It's loaded, Marshal. I loaded it." He breaks it open, looks, closes it and hands it back.
   - Dane tells Tess they'll be on the ferry till light.
   - **Plant for Scene 2:** Tess puts the island radio in her coat pocket, on top of her keys.
3. **Longing (shown).** Beth watches Dane's thumb go under the ties, slow and careful, the same hands as on her knee on Tuesday. She wants those hands on her. Then she makes herself look at the door. Two sentences at most, with an object and an action.
4. **The pier.** The ferry rolls on bare granite. Gloss the float in one clause: the floating dock that used to sit between steel and stone, gone in the first storm.
   - Beth: "By light it's holed, and a holed boat takes nobody to Portland." She'll hold it off the pier inside the harbor till the wind goes back round.
5. **The argument, both partly right (keep the old text, trimmed).**
   - Dane: the steering has never had the sea on it. Out there it's the rocks in the dark; here it only grinds.
   - Beth: "Here it's holed for certain. Out there it might hold."
   - He doesn't tell her no. "What do you need?" / "Two hands on the ropes that know what they're doing."
   - **The laugh (binding):** "I worked the back of a lobster boat two summers. I'm good at exactly this."
   - **The undercut and the payoff of Ch 12:** she takes the flashlight out of her coat and puts it in his hand, and Ned's knife after it. "Tonight you hold the light." He looks at the flashlight a beat longer than a flashlight needs. Fold the two old handings into this one (N10).
6. **At the wheel, one line on the rod (it replaces the whole hatch scene).** She spent Thursday and Friday at the co-op vise on the rod that runs from the wheel down to the blade under the boat that steers it. It's back in, as straight as the vise could get it, and it might stick with the sea on it. She says the last part out loud to him before he goes down. One line of dialogue at most.
7. **The father beat (alone, non-romantic):** in eleven years she has never been in charge of this wheel with nobody beside her. Her father stood at a wheel every working day of his life, and her hands go to this one as if they already know it. Then straight into the ropes, with nothing of Dane in that paragraph.
8. **The ropes and the cut (keep the old text, trimmed).**
   - She calls the ropes off one at a time from the cabin window, and he lifts each off its post and throws it clear.
   - The last one holds the whole boat, so tight it hums.
   - The dread, a breath long: in the first storm a rope like that parted like a gunshot.
   - "It won't come off the post. Cut it?" One second. "Cut it."
   - He stands to the side of the rope's line and cuts it with Ned's knife, and the end whips back across the empty pier. She watches him do it right.

**Subtext:** "Tonight you hold the light" is the first thing she has given him since Wednesday that isn't an order, and both of them hear it. He asks "What do you need?" because asking is the only thing he can do right now that isn't the hold.

**Tension source:** Situational: the boat dying on the stone, minute by minute. Interpersonal: an honest disagreement, decided by her. Internal: wanting a man whose request still stands against her.

**Turn:** The last rope is cut. The boat is off the stone, and in this wind it can't go back.

**Longing:** his thumb under the ties; the flashlight held a beat too long.

**Laugh:** "I'm good at exactly this."

**Texture budget (2):** a loaded rope cut, and where to stand for it; the straightened rod, in one line.

**Plants:** Tess's radio on her keys (→ Scene 2); the rod might stick (→ Scene 2); "Tonight you hold the light" (← Ch 12; → Scene 5, where he held the light at the vise all Saturday).

**Ends on (ending type: image):** The boat swings free into the dark and snow, and the stone falls away from its side. Dane stands on the car deck with the knife in one hand and her flashlight in the other, looking up at her window. No dialogue after "Cut it."

**Word count target:** 380. **Floor 350.** If it runs long, cut Ned at the pipe, then Russ. Never cut the opener, "What do you need?", the laugh, "Tonight you hold the light", the rod line, or the cut.

## Scene 2 (Beth)

**Setting:** The captain's cabin, inside the harbor, all night. The harbor mouth is white water all the way across. Below the harbor road on the right are the rocks where the lobster boats went up in the first storm, and she can't see them. **Sensory anchor:** the wheel's wet spokes in her bare palms. After the jam, the wheel is sticky with her own skin.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane (from step 3). Tess, seen only as a light and the red shape of the fire truck.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open in simple past, on the event** (never "She had held…"). An hour in, the wheel goes heavy at one point in its turn. She feels it in her palms before she understands it.
2. **The jam, plain for a listener (keep the old text word for word, from "The wheel stopped." through the rope scar).**
   - The rod has stuck with the blade turned all the way to one side, so the boat keeps turning, slowly and then faster, toward the rocks.
   - "Come on. Come on, damn you."
   - She throws her whole body on the wheel. The rod comes free, the sea spins the wheel back through her hands, and it takes the skin off both palms. The white rope scar opens with the rest.
   - She could have let go. She holds on until it stops, and then she holds it there.
3. **Dane in the doorway.** He felt the boat turn wrong through his feet and came up the stair. He looks at her hands and says nothing.
4. **The surprise: Tess's light (keep).** A white light comes on along the shore, swings, and stops on the rocks. Behind it is the shape of the red fire truck on the harbor road.
   - The light shows the danger first: the rocks are far closer than she guessed.
   - Turning all the way might jam it again, so she turns most of the way with her raw hands and holds it. The rocks go by a few yards off.
   - One line: Tess has put the light on the rocks for her, and still won't say one word to her on the radio.
5. **Who has the prisoners (keep, two lines).** "Who's on the prisoners?" / "Russ. She'll have given him the shotgun."
6. **The shirt (keep; cut "quick and sure").**
   - The first-aid box is empty, said in one clause.
   - Coat off, shirt off, coat back on over his bare chest. He holds the shirt up without a word.
   - She takes her left hand off the wheel: "Hold it here. Don't turn it." He holds the spoke exactly where she put it. He stands on one sleeve, splits the shirt down its seam, and wraps her left palm and then her right while she steers.
7. **The night, cut to one exchange plus the laugh (keep, trim).**
   - "That was my good shirt." / "You don't have a good shirt."
   - "Open and close them for me. Every few minutes, or they'll set." / "Stop," she tells him, and he stops.
8. **Longing (replaces the cut "She wanted him…" line; binding to replace).** His coat hangs open over bare skin in a cabin cold enough to show her breath. She looks at the hollow of his throat and wants her mouth there, and she's angry enough to keep both hands on the wheel. Specific, physical, hers. No "Both were true." No father.

**Subtext:** She has never let anyone help her with a job. Tonight two people do: Tess, who won't speak to her, and Dane, whose request still stands. She lets them, because the boat matters more than being right. He says nothing about her hands because the last thing he decided about her life was the hold, and he knows it.

**Tension source:** Physical: the jam, the rocks, the pain. Surprise: the light. Internal: wanting him while the hold stands.

**Turn:** Her palms are torn by her own choice, and Tess lights the rocks. The reader didn't see Tess coming.

**Longing:** the hollow of his throat; the wordless shirt held up.

**Laugh:** "That was my good shirt." / "You don't have a good shirt."

**Texture budget (2):** the jam (the rod sticks, the wheel spins back); the fire truck's light on the rocks.

**Plants:** the palms (→ Scene 4's wrists over his shoulders, Ch 15–16); Tess's light (→ Scene 3, Ch 15, Ch 17); the rod stuck once (→ Scene 5's "If it sticks, I'll feel it first," and fear only in Ch 15).

**Ends on (ending type: a small private action):** Later, when he has turned to watch the light on the rocks, she opens and closes her wrapped hands on the wheel, as he told her to, where he can't see her do it. No line after.

**Word count target:** 560. **Floor 550 (amendment floor).** This is the danger scene, so never compress steps 1–4 or the shirt. If it runs long, step 5 folds into one sentence of narration.

## Scene 3 (Beth)

**Setting:** The captain's cabin at first gray, Saturday, about six. The snow is thinning and the wind is starting to go back round. Dane's phone is propped on the shelf under the front windows. **Sensory anchor:** her hands on the wheel in his torn shirt, gone stiff and brown. Down on the harbor road the fire truck's light goes off, and the truck drives away up the hill. All night Tess never once swung the light up to the cabin.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Dane's boss (voice on speaker).

**Surface action:**
1. **Open on the sight.** Past the white water at the harbor mouth, off the point, the big boat lies showing one light, closer in than it was last night. Neither of them says what it is. They both know since Thursday. The fire truck leaves in one sentence.
2. **Dane calls his boss on speaker** in front of her: the big boat has come in closer overnight.
3. **The boss, short (it replaces the old seven-fact block; binding).**
   - **The license (B6):** "Your Hal Mercer comes back to nobody. There's no license out of Gloucester in that name. That's a lie to a federal officer, and in this sea it still doesn't put a boarding team aboard."
   - **The one plain line:** "Nothing flies before Tuesday. The Coast Guard's on the storm's distress calls. If that boat comes at you, that's a distress, and they'll come from an hour off."
   - *Optional, first cut:* "And a text from a phone in nobody's name won't arrest that lawyer."
   - Dane (N1): "The harbor mouth is white water till Sunday evening, and the next storm's in before light Monday. Sunday night or not at all." / Beth: "Sunday."
4. **"Cancel it" (binding).**
   - Boss: "And the hold stands for Monday."
   - Beth, to Dane, not to the phone, her wrapped hands on the wheel: "Cancel it."
   - He looks at her hands. Then into the phone: "Withdraw it. Tell the prosecutor this morning. She doesn't get held, and she doesn't go into the program unless she says so. Write that down."
   - Boss: "You asked for it Wednesday." / Dane: "I was scared, and I called it the job." A breath. **"And I'm involved with her. Write that down too."** (B8)
   - A silence on the line. Then: "Written."
   - Boss: "Then she's on her own after Monday." / Dane: "Not all of her. I've put in for the Maine office."
5. **The boss speaks to Beth (keep).** He holds out the phone. "Say it to me." / "After Monday nobody from my office stands between you and those people. Do you understand what you're choosing?" / "I know what I'm choosing. I'm the one standing here." The call ends.
6. **To her face (N4: the reason first, then what made him see it).**
   - Dane: "I still think you should go. It was never mine to say." Then: last night she took it off when he'd have left it on the stone, and she held that wheel with the skin coming off, and she was right both times. He'd known the first part before last night. Last night he watched her prove it.
   - The writer keeps it to three sentences of dialogue. He says it standing, with no touch.
7. **Her condition (binding).** "Then you ask me. Every time. Even when you're sure. And you never go round me to anybody again." / "Yes."
   - **The laugh:** "What if somebody's shooting at us?" / "Then ask fast."
8. **Her accountability (binding, N4).** "Wednesday I said, 'And so do we.' For both of us. Same thing I hated you for." He doesn't let her carry all of it, and he doesn't argue it either. One look, or one short line. The dialogue doctor chooses, and it must not be a contrast construction.
9. **The fifth day (longing).** It's five days since he said he loved her on the lighthouse and she told him to ask again in five. He doesn't ask. She looks at him in his open coat with nothing under it and wants to be asked. That's one sentence with an object.
   - "What do you want, Dane?" / "I'll tell you on the other side. When nobody's paying me to stand here." (F18)

**Subtext:** He gives up the decision and keeps the belief, which is harder than doing either alone, and she hears the difference. He puts "involved" on the record because asking her every time has to start with not hiding her from his own office. She owns her half, so the repair is made between equals. He doesn't ask the fifth-day question because he has just promised to ask only when she is free to say no, and on this boat, today, she isn't yet.

**Tension source:** Situational: the boat coming closer, and the enemy's name a lie. Interpersonal: the repair is made on a speakerphone in front of a third party, and neither of them can leave the wheel. Internal: she wanted to be asked, and he didn't ask.

**Turn (the chapter's turn):** The hold is withdrawn and the relationship is on the record, in front of her. That's the point of no return. The enemy's name is false.

**Longing:** the open coat; wanting to be asked.

**Laugh:** "What if somebody's shooting at us?" / "Then ask fast."

**Texture budget (2):** the license coming back to nobody; the withdrawal on speaker, made on the record.

**Plants:** "Written" and the disclosure (→ Ch 16: taken off her protection at six; the prosecutor isn't surprised; the door held open); "on the other side" (→ Ch 16 s3); the condition (→ Scene 4's public yes, and Scene 5's "Can I switch it off?"); no license (→ Ch 17, echo only); the Coast Guard an hour off (→ Ch 15).

**Ends on (ending type: action, and it plants the empty ferry):** The wind has gone round. She brings the boat's nose toward the pier, and he goes down to take the ropes without being told. No line after "the other side."

**Word count target:** 650. **Floor 600.** It's dense, so this is the cut order: the optional lawyer clause first, then step 6 down to two sentences, then step 8's reply down to a look. Never cut the license, the one plain line, "Sunday night or not at all", "Cancel it", the withdrawal, either "Write that down", "Written", "Not all of her", "Say it to me", "It was never mine to say", the condition, the accountability line, or "the other side."

## Scene 4 (Dane)

**Setting:** The town office back room (the old clerk's kitchen) and the doorway to the front room, Sunday, about three. The daylight is going blue. One window over the sink faces the harbor. **Sensory anchor:** the smell of cloves from the oven, and Russ's flannel shirt on Dane, short in the arms, because his own is round her hands in pieces.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Russ (on the couch, leg up), Tess (at the front desk with the shotgun).

**Surface action:**
1. **Opener (amendment; Dane named first; the ham is a smell only):** "Dane Reyes had the ham in the town-office oven by three, because that was the one thing on Cutter he could still fix." Then straight on, with no ham business.
2. **One clause:** that morning Beth called the ferry board on his phone and asked for the boat for tonight, and they gave it to her (keep 2971). Her palms are in black electrical tape from the co-op.
3. **One window look, under it:** low under the point, past the harbor mouth, the one light hasn't moved since dawn yesterday. He shuts the window (N10).
4. **The public yes (keep old Ch 15 text word for word, with two changes).**
   - Beth stands in the doorway where Russ and Tess can both see her: "Russ. Tess. He's going to ask me something, and I'm going to say yes. I'm telling you myself, so nobody ever says it for me."
   - Tess's eyes come up off the desk to Beth's face and stay there.
   - Dane: "You just told them the answer." / "Ask anyway. Even when you're sure."
   - He takes the oven mitt off. "Can I kiss you? I'm asking."
   - "Yes." She doesn't come to him yet. "And what you said to me at the lighthouse. Yes to that too. I love you. I have since the rock, when you opened your hand." (**Change:** "at the lighthouse" replaces "Monday, at the generator"; flag 6.)
   - Dane doesn't move. Russ sets his glass down. "Come here."
5. **The kiss (keep; it's in his body and theirs only).**
   - She can't put her palms on him, so she hooks her wrists over his shoulders and holds her taped hands up behind his neck. He keeps his own hands at her waist, clear of her palms.
   - She kisses him slowly, after four days of not.
   - His long interior sentence runs on "and," then stops: he thinks her name and her mouth, and nothing else.
   - No family, no Ned. Russ is in the room as a watcher only.
6. **Tess goes:** "I'll be on the steps. Somebody ought to watch the harbor." She goes out past Beth without a look, and the door shuts hard. Beth watches it shut over his shoulder and doesn't let go of him.

**Subtext:** Her yes pays the condition she set at dawn, and it beats Ch 08's fifth day: she says she loves him before he has to ask. Telling Russ and Tess first is her answer to the hold. Nobody speaks for her, including him. Tess's look and Tess's door are the same thing. She heard it, and she hasn't forgiven anything.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal: a kiss in front of two people, one of whom isn't speaking to her. Situational: the light in the window, unmoved.

**Turn:** She answers his love out loud, in public. They're together again, and the island has heard it from her.

**Longing:** the whole scene; the wrists over his shoulders.

**Laugh:** Russ, the whole line: "Well."

**Texture budget (1):** the ferry board giving her the boat, by his phone.

## Scene 5 (Dane)

**Setting:** The car deck at eight, full dark, the engine already running up through the steel. **Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Ned (cuffed), the kid (tied). Tess is a voice on Russ's handheld radio, one line, optional.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open in motion (keep the old opener):** by eight it is full dark, and Dane lies on his back on the car deck with his flashlight on the underside of the bait truck. He's searching every space and both trucks for a man, or for something left behind.
2. **The phone (B7; binding).** Up behind the bait truck's back wheel he finds a cheap phone in a plastic bag, taped to the frame. Its screen lights through the plastic when he touches it. It's switched on.

## Tournament angles (three ways to build it; all three hit every binding beat above)

**Angle A: "The night is the spine."** Weight the storm. Scenes 1 and 2 run at the top of their targets, in real time and in Beth's fear register: two or three words, then silence, and the body doing the work. The sound of steel on stone carries all the way through the pier and the cut. The jam is the chapter's longest held moment, a beat-by-beat paragraph a listener can follow with their eyes shut. Scene 3 stays cold and short: the repair is spoken by two exhausted people over a wheel neither can leave, with the fewest words that carry it, and its warmth is held back for Scene 4. Scene 5 is written as a procedure that turns to dread: Dane's careful reading of ice, bag and battery is calm until "That one was his," and then the dark falls and the chapter goes to sound only. The chapter's pleasure comes from physical competence, and the romance comes from what each one's hands do for the other.

**Angle B: "Every scene is an ask."** Build each scene round one question and the answer it gets, so that the chapter visibly becomes Dane learning to ask and Beth deciding what to give:
- "What do you need?" / the flashlight;
- the shirt held up without a word / her hand off the wheel;
- "Cancel it" / "Write that down";
- "Can I kiss you? I'm asking." / "Yes… I love you";
- "Can I switch it off?" / "Off," and "Do we go?" / "Go."

Dialogue carries more of the weight and the humor runs warmer: "Then ask fast," Russ's "Well," and "Touch that and you swim." The repair is built as a negotiation between equals, with terms on both sides and her accountability line landing as the trade that makes it fair. The danger stays physical, but each danger beat also tests the new rule (he asks before the cut, he asks before the phone), so the reader sees the condition kept under pressure before anyone says it was. The risk is talkiness. Every scene still has to open on an event.

**Angle C: "Watched."** Make the big boat the chapter's constant pressure, a presence in every scene even where it can't be seen:
- **Scene 1:** a glimpse of one light through the blowing snow from the pier, then gone.
- **Scene 2:** the jam happens with the harbor mouth and what lies past it at Beth's back.
- **Scene 3:** opens on it closer in. The license coming back to nobody is the hinge: the danger is bigger than he thought, and he withdraws the hold anyway, because it was never his to say. That makes the reversal cost him something and keeps N4's order.
- **Scene 4:** the light in the window, unmoved, frames the kiss. Tess leaves "to watch the harbor."
- **Scene 5:** the payoff. They learn they have been watched from inside the ferry itself, and going dark turns the watching around. The engine finding them anyway is the chapter's last shiver.

The romance is played as two people choosing each other in plain sight of an enemy, which answers the reader note that the villain was never on the page. Dane's half (Scenes 4–5) is closer to his jury-brain interior, which keeps asking how this will look and then stops asking.
