# Judge: Skimmer, pair p1 (A vs B), Chapter 14

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** With my ears only and my hands busy, B kept telling me who was talking and kept the storm making noise ("Snow hissed on the windows," "the shriek stopped," "White water roared"). A had me working out whose mouth a line came out of right at the point I was already drifting.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 43%, on the boss's phone call: "Your Hal Mercer comes back to nobody... There's no license out of Gloucester in that name. That's a lie to a federal officer, and in this sea it still doesn't put a boarding team aboard." I stopped following there. Two lines later it got worse. "'Sunday,' Beth said. / 'And the hold stands for Monday.'" By ear, Beth says both. Then "The line went quiet. / 'Written.'" has no name on it either. I came back for "That was my good shirt" earlier, and for the kiss in the kitchen, but I drifted through the whole middle of the call. A's banter is a bit better ("I had one. You're wearing it." and "we split Wednesday"), but I was half gone by the time it arrived.

4. **Where B lost me:** The same phone call, same spot, about 43%, and B makes it a little longer: "And a text from a phone in nobody's name won't arrest that lawyer." That's more legal stuff stacked on a pile I was already skimming. What pulled me back is that B names the speaker on the lines that matter: "'And the hold stands for Monday,' his boss said," and "Then his boss said, 'Written.'" B lost me again for a second near the end at "At her word every light aboard went off. 'It's against every rule.'" I couldn't tell who said that.

5. **Confidence: low.** These are nearly the same chapter. The call comes down to speaker tags and a few sound lines, and A's extra banter almost evens it.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. It's line for line the same chapter, with small trims and added tags.
