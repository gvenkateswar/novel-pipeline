# Judge: Craft. Pair p1 (A vs B), Chapter 14

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** It's the same chapter, but in A the two of them actually bargain on the wheelhouse. He sets terms of his own ("When I think you're wrong, I say so, to your face, as loud as it takes"), and they split the blame ("Then we split Wednesday... I'll take the bigger half. I'm the one who filed it"). That makes the making-up feel adult and two-sided. B cuts all of it down to "I went first," and the scene goes thin right where it matters.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Your Hal Mercer comes back to nobody," his boss said. "There's no license out of Gloucester in that name. That's a lie to a federal officer, and in this sea it still doesn't put a boarding team aboard..." (~43%). The boss on speaker reads me the logistics: licenses, Coast Guard, when things can fly. My thumb sped up there and didn't slow until Beth said "Cancel it." Smaller stumbles came earlier. "The float, the floating dock that used to sit between steel and stone" (~7%) and "the blade under the boat that steers it" (~14%) are both glossary lines that clunk.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Yes." / "What if somebody's shooting at us?" / "Then ask fast." (~58%). With his "He thought about it, which she liked" cut, I couldn't tell who was talking. It reads as if Beth asks her own question and then answers it, and the best exchange in the chapter turns to mush. B has the same boss-call drag as A (~43%), made longer by "And a text from a phone in nobody's name won't arrest that lawyer." B also loses the shirt joke's payoff ("I had one. You're wearing it."), gives "It's against every rule" to nobody, and over-explains "It had found them." B does have better texture on the night run ("Snow hissed on the windows," "black and shining"), and that's why this is close.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The texts are about 90% identical. A wins on the reconciliation dialogue and on two voices that stay distinct. B's extra sensory lines don't make up for what it cut from the couple.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Nearly the same sentences line for line; one is a trim of the other.
