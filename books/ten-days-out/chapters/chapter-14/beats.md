## Flags before the beats (outline against the page, the log and the binding rules)

None of these blocks the chapter. Each one is settled below. Flags 1–3 add to the outline's Ch 14 or move something in it. Nobody wins, loses, ends up together or does anything different from the outline.

1. **The surprise: Tess lights the rocks [plot addition].** The orchestrator asked for a surprise inside the action that the reader won't see coming. The outline has none, because its jam is foreseeable once the steering has been set up. **Settled:** in Scene 3, just after Beth frees the jammed wheel, a white light comes on along the shore. It's the big light on the island's fire truck. Tess has driven to the harbor road above the rocks where the lobster boats went up in the first storm, and she has aimed the light at them. The light raises the danger before it helps: the rocks are far closer than Beth guessed, and she has to turn again with torn hands. Tess keeps the light on the rocks all night. She never says a word on the radio, and at first light she drives away without looking at the boat.
   - *Why it holds:* Tess is the island's emergency director, she drives the one fire truck, and she's first to every emergency (bible). A boat grinding on the pier at the storm's peak is an emergency. Nobody says anything about Beth on the island radio (World rule 11), and Tess isn't speaking to her anyway.
   - *Who has the prisoners:* Russ, on the town office couch, with his own shotgun across his knees. Dane asks in one line and Beth answers in one (Scene 3).
   - *Downstream:* nothing changes. In Ch 15, "Tess doesn't count. She's not talking." still holds, and so does what Tess hasn't forgiven in Ch 18. The light makes her silence harder to read, which is good for the Book 2 heroine.
   - **The structural misdirect** goes with it. Scene 2 builds the reader's dread on the last rope, because a rope parted like a shot in Ch 02. Dane cuts it cleanly. The danger then comes from the wheel, ten minutes after the reader has relaxed.
2. **Dane "talks her through the pain" against A15, Pacing flag 8 and the outline's "Dane says nothing" [orchestrator's binding vs. outline].** A15 saves Dane's running talk in danger for Ch 16, where it pays off ("You stopped talking"). **Settled:**
   - At the jam and through the wrapping, Dane says nothing, as the outline has it.
   - Later in the night, once the boat is steady, he says four or five short lines about her hands: what they'll do in an hour, what to keep moving, one joke. That's first aid, the way an Army MP knows it, and it's in the past tense of what has happened to her hands.
   - It is never the present-tense "here's where I am" talk Ch 16 pays. It doesn't run on, and it stops when she tells him to.
3. **Scene 4 moves from the car deck to the captain's cabin [setting change].** In the outline's s3, Beth stands on the car deck at dawn. But she's held the boat off the pier all night, and there's nobody else aboard who can take the wheel. **Settled:** Scene 4 is in the captain's cabin at first gray, with her wrapped hands still on the wheel and Dane beside her. They see the big boat through the front windows. The wind is starting to go back round, and one forward line says she'll put the boat back on the pier when it does. That keeps the calendar ("Sat night aboard at the pier"), and it means neither of them can walk away from the repair.
4. **Why the steering jams, so it's never Beth's mistake (World rule 17) [new set fact].** In the first storm (Ch 02) the boat broke loose and the steering went. **Set here:**
   - The rod that runs from the wheel's gear down to the rudder was bent. The rudder is the flat steel blade under the back of the boat that turns it.
   - There's no new rod on this side of Pruett. Over Thursday and Friday Beth straightened the old one in the vise at the co-op, and she bolts it back in Scene 1–2.
   - She says the risk out loud before they cast off: it's as straight as she can make it, and it may bind under load.
   - Leaving the boat on the pier loses it by morning for certain. Taking it off might lose it. Her call, with the risk named (outline's one-talk sentence).
   - *For the domain checker:* a mechanical rod linkage on a 1989 steel ferry of about a hundred feet. Hydraulic steering would be more usual. If the checker objects, "the bent rod" can become "the bent arm on top of the rudder" with no change to the beats.
5. **World rule 18 (from Ch 14 on, Dane asks before he acts) against the outline's wordless wrap.** **Settled:**

   - At the wheel he asks without words. He holds up his shirt, and she takes her left hand off the wheel and gives it to him.
   - The phrase "I'm asking" is not used (N11 keeps it for Ch 15).
6. **Beth's outline line "Say it to me, not to him" is a contrast construction (X, not Y).** **Reworded:** "I know what I'm choosing. I'm the one standing here. Say it to me."
7. **Ned's rope knife cuts the last rope.** Dane has had it in his right coat pocket since Ch 11, and Beth used it on Russ's trouser leg in Ch 13. If a Ch 13 fix leaves the knife with Beth, she hands it down to him in Scene 1 when she gives him the light, which is one clause.

9. **"The kid's wrists" (carry-forward).** The outline's s1 has "checks the prisoners' cuffs." On the page, Ned is in Russ's cuffs and the kid's wrists are tied with cable ties. The kid is never "in cuffs."

## Scene 1

**Setting:** The town office front room, a little after ten on Friday night, the storm at its peak. The only light is a lantern on the clerk's desk and the stove's open door. Then the harbor road and the stone pier at its west end, in snow blowing sideways. **Sensory anchor:** the sound. Steel grinding on stone, carried along the harbor road and through the town office wall, a long shriek and a thud each time the sea lifts the boat and drops it on the pier. A second anchor at the pier: the boat rolling against bare granite, with the stub of the broken float jerking on its chain.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane. Tess, with Russ's shotgun (one line, to Dane only). Russ on the couch, and Ned and the kid in the back room, are silent images only (five people on one page is this book's worst-scoring pattern).

**Point of view:** Beth.

**Want:**
- **Beth** wants her boat off that stone before it's holed. Under that, she wants to have finished the steering, because she's spent two days fixing the nearest broken thing so she wouldn't have to decide anything about him.
- **Dane** wants her alive at ten on Monday, and he wants to be useful to her in a way she'll let him be.

**Surface action:**
1. **The binding opener.** Then the sound, plainly: the boat's steel side grinding on the stone pier, a quarter mile down the harbor road. Beth is already on her feet with her coat half on, before she's decided anything.
2. **Dane checks the prisoners before they go [Dane on the job], fast, three or four sentences of action, no talk:**
   - The kid lies on the back-room floor with his face to the wall and his wrists in the cable ties. Dane runs a thumb under each tie and adds a fresh one. *Restate:* "They've got a boat," and not one word since, however they asked.
   - Ned sits cuffed to the pipe in Russ's cuffs, awake, listening to his own boat on the stone. *Optional, his one line, weather first, no name:* "Wind's gone round." Beth doesn't answer.
   - Russ is on the couch, gray, his leg up and tied. *Restate:* shot in the thigh in her doorway.
   - Tess hands Dane the shotgun. He breaks it open, checks it, closes it and hands it back. **Tess's one line, to Dane only:** "It's loaded, Marshal. I loaded it." She doesn't look at Beth. **Plant for the surprise:** Dane tells Tess where they'll be, in one clause ("off the pier, inside the harbor, till light"). Tess says nothing, and her keys are on the desk beside the fire truck's radio.
3. **The longing, set off by his hands (plain, binding):** watching his thumb go under the ties on the kid's wrists, the same careful hands as on her knee on Tuesday, she wants him. She ended it on Wednesday, and she hasn't stopped wanting him for one hour since. One or two plain sentences. Nothing of her father's.
4. **The pier, the decision, out loud.** In the wind the boat rolls, steel on bare granite, with no float between them anymore. Gloss "the float" in one clause: the floating dock that used to sit between the boat and the stone, smashed in the first storm. **The plain fact, said by Beth:** "By light it's holed. Then nothing crosses Sunday, and Sunday's the one night the sea lets anything out." Then: she's taking it off. She'll hold it in the harbor till the wind goes back round.
5. **The argument, face to face, both partly right (one-talk test):**
   - Dane: she's had the steering in pieces two days and doesn't know it works. If it fails out there, she's on the rocks in the dark. On the pier it only grinds.
   - Beth: on the pier it's holed for certain. Out there it might hold.
   - Neither is wrong. He doesn't tell her no. **He asks, which is the first sign of what Scene 4 will say:** "What do you need?"
   - Beth: "Two hands on the ropes that know what they're doing."
   - **Dane (binding laugh, tipping into flirting):** "I worked the back of my mother's cousin's lobster boat two summers. I'm good at exactly this."
   - Beth's answer is the undercut: she pulls the flashlight out of her coat and puts it in his hand. The first thing she needs is a light. (Her target is a man who's sure he's prepared. Nobody laughs at their own line.)

**Turn:** She decides, and he asks instead of deciding. Small, and it points at Scene 4.

**Subtext:** The steering has been her way of not deciding about him. Now the boat is forcing a decision on her, and he's about to be in a closet with her holding a light. He asks "What do you need?" because "She'll go." is still between them, and asking is the only thing he can do that isn't that.

**Tension source:** Situational: the boat dying on the stone, minute by minute. Interpersonal: a face-to-face disagreement with no one wrong. Internal: wanting a man she's ended it with, watching his hands do his job.

**Ends on (ending type: action):** At the hatch at the back of the car deck, Beth pushes her gloves into her coat pocket, which is her habit and the plant for the palms. She drops down into the dark of the steering space, and the flashlight comes down after her in his hand. No dialogue after the flashlight.

**Texture budget (2):** steel grinding on bare stone (the float gone); Dane checking the cable ties and the shotgun.

**Plants:** gloves in her pocket (→ Scene 3); "Sunday's the one night" (→ Scene 4, N1); Tess, her keys and the fire truck (→ Scene 3); "What do you need?" (→ Scene 4's repair).

**Word count target:** 380. **Floor 350.** If it runs long, the prisoner check drops to Dane's thumb under the ties and the shotgun, and Ned's optional line goes. Never cut the opener, "By light it's holed…", "What do you need?", the lobster-boat line, the flashlight, or the longing sentence.

## Scene 2

**Setting:** The steering space under the hatch at the back of the car deck. It's the size of a closet, cold steel, and every wave booms through the hull a foot from her head. One flashlight. Then the captain's cabin up the steel stair, and the car deck below its front windows, in the deck lights and blowing snow. **Sensory anchor:** the flashlight beam on her bare hands and the bolt, and Dane's breath on the back of her neck because there's no room for him to be anywhere else. A second anchor, at the end: the last rope groaning on the post, so tight it hums.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.

**Point of view:** Beth.

**Want:**
- **Beth** wants the rod in and the boat off the stone, and she wants not to feel him a foot behind her.
- **Dane** wants to be exactly as useful as she lets him, and nothing more.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open in motion,** with no recap. She's on her back on cold steel with a wrench, bolting the straightened rod back onto the rudder's arm. Her orders are plain: "Lower." "There." "Don't move it." He holds the light exactly where she tells him, and he doesn't move it **[Dane takes orders; holds what she tells him to hold]**.
2. **The steering, in plain words, said by Beth because he asks one easy question** ("What does that do?"): the wheel up top turns a gear, the gear pushes this rod, and the rod turns the rudder. Gloss: the flat steel blade under the back of the boat that turns it. The first storm bent the rod. There's no new one this side of Pruett. She straightened it in the vise at the co-op.
   - **The risk named, out loud (flag 4):** "It's as straight as I can get it. It might bind when the sea's on it."
   - Dane: "And if it does?"
   - Beth: "Then I'll find out at the wheel."
   - **Say it twice from Scene 1, one line, inside the work:** by light it's holed, so this is the only way.
3. **The longing and the slip (the flirt, binding).** In the cramped dark his chest is almost at her back and his breath is on her neck, and she wants him so plainly that her hands slow on the wrench. Say it in plain words. Then, without thinking, at the last bolt:
   - Beth: "Hold it still, Marshal."
   - They both hear it. "Marshal" is her flirting word, the tease (bible), and she hasn't used it since Wednesday.
   - Dane, quietly (he goes short when he wants her): "You called me Marshal."
   - Beth: "It's what you are."
   - Dane: "You only say it one way."
   - Beth answers the way she covers anything, with a task: "Hold the light." She pulls the bolt tight. He holds the light. Neither says anything else about it.
4. **Up to the captain's cabin.** She climbs the steel stair on the stiff knee. The engine comes up through her boots. She puts her hands on the wheel. This is the first time she has ever been the one in charge of it, with nobody beside her.
   - **The one father beat in the chapter (non-romantic, binding placement), one or two sentences:** her father stood at a wheel every working day until the bank took his boat, and she carried the papers out to the bank man's truck. Her hands go to this wheel as if they already know it.
   - Nothing about Dane in the same paragraph. Move straight into the ropes.
5. **The ropes and the cut [A7; Dane on the job].** She calls the ropes off one at a time from the cabin window. Dane on the car deck (gloss: the open deck where the trucks park) lifts each off its post and throws it clear.
   - She keeps the last one on, at the back, so the boat will swing on it and bring its back end away from the stone. Plainly: that one rope is taking the whole boat's weight. It goes so tight it hums.
   - **The dread, a breath long:** in the first storm a rope like that parted like a gunshot, and Ned dragged her out of its way. Her hands want to let go of the wheel and go down there.
   - **Dane calls it before she does,** shouting up: "It won't come off the post. Cut it?"
   - "Cut it." The question costs a second, and the reader feels the second.
   - He stands to the side of the rope's line, because a cut rope under that much weight comes back like a whip. He knows that from two summers on a lobster boat, said in one clause. He cuts it with Ned's rope knife at the rail.
   - The cut end cracks back onto the empty pier where he isn't standing.
   - **She watches him do it right.** One plain sentence. Admiration, with no touch.

**Turn (binding):** The last rope is cut. The boat is off the stone, and in this wind there's no putting it back until the wind goes round.

**Subtext:** The slip is the truth getting out of her mouth while her hands are busy. He takes it and doesn't push it, which is the most he's ever not pushed. Taking the wheel for the first time is the captain's job she's been too afraid to ask for, done in the dark because there's no one else to do it.

**Tension source:** Physical: the closet, the boom of the sea, the humming rope. Interpersonal: the slip. Situational: the dread of Ch 02 repeating.

**Ends on (ending type: motion):** The boat swings free of the pier into the dark and the snow, and the stone falls away from its side. Dane is still on the car deck with the knife in his hand, looking up at her window. No dialogue after "Cut it."

**Texture budget (2):** the steering explained (the bent rod); a loaded rope cut, and where to stand.

**Plants:** the rod might bind (→ Scene 3); Ned's knife used for the boat (optional, → Ch 16 or 18); the first time at the wheel (→ Ch 16, Ch 18).

**Word count target:** 480. **Floor 440.** If it runs long, step 2's explanation drops to the rod, the rudder (glossed) and the risk line, and the "Marshal" exchange loses "It's what you are." Never cut the risk line, the slip, the father beat, "Cut it?" / "Cut it." or where he stands.

## Scene 3

**Setting:** The captain's cabin, inside the harbor, all night. Snow in the deck lights. The harbor mouth is white water all the way across. Below the harbor road on the boat's right side are the rocks where the island's lobster boats went up in the first storm. **Sensory anchor:** the wheel's spokes, cold and wet, in her bare palms. Then, after the jam, the skin gone from them and the wheel sticky. A second anchor: the chains on the two trucks ringing on the car deck every time the boat rolls.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane (from step 3). Tess, seen only as the light and the shape of the red truck behind it.

**Point of view:** Beth.

**Want:**
- **Beth** wants to hold the boat's nose into the wind until light, and she wants to do it herself.
- **Dane** wants to help without taking anything from her.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open in the moment, with the fear coming (World rule 8),** never a summary of the hour before. She has the nose into the wind. Then the wheel goes heavy at one point in its turn, the way the rod might. She feels it in her palms before she understands it. Give it a breath.
2. **The jam, said plainly for a listener:**
   - She turns to bring the nose back round. The wheel stops and won't come back.
   - **Restate Scene 2:** the rod she'd straightened has bound, with the blade turned all the way to one side.
   - So the boat keeps turning, slowly and then faster, toward the rocks below the harbor road.
   - Beth's fear register: no words, or two. "Come on." At most one swear, aimed at the wheel.
   - She throws her whole weight on it. The rod comes free all at once. The sea pushing on the blade spins the wheel back through her hands, and she holds on as it spins, and it tears the skin off both palms. The white rope scar across her left palm opens with the rest.
   - She stops it. She holds it anyway. **Her choice, her cost (binding):** she could let go and she doesn't.
3. **Dane in the doorway.** He felt the boat turn wrong through his feet on the car deck, where he was checking the trucks' chains, and he came up the stair. He sees her hands. **He says nothing (outline).**
4. **The surprise (flag 1).** A white light comes on along the shore, very bright, and swings, and stops on the rocks. Behind it is the shape of the island's red fire truck on the harbor road.
   - **The light shows danger before it helps:** the rocks are close, about thirty feet, where she'd thought a hundred. The boat is still swinging toward them.
   - **What Beth does, plainly:** turning all the way could bind the rod again, so she turns the wheel most of the way with her torn hands and holds it there. The boat comes round slowly, slowly. The rocks go past in the white light a boat's length off.
   - One line of her knowing who it is: Tess. Tess has put the light on the rocks for her and hasn't said one word on the radio.
5. **The shirt (binding; the wordless ask, flag 5).**
   - *Plain, one clause:* the ferry's first-aid box is empty in the town office since Russ's leg.
   - Dane takes off his coat, then his shirt, and puts the coat back on over his bare chest. He holds the shirt up: a question with no words.
   - Beth gives him her left hand off the wheel. "Hold it here. Don't turn it." He holds the wheel exactly where she put it, no more.
   - He tears the shirt and wraps her left palm, then her right, while she steers. He's quick and sure, the way he was with her knee, without saying so.
6. **Who has the prisoners (one line each):**
   - Dane, at the light: "Who's on the prisoners?"
   - Beth: "Russ. She'll have given him the shotgun."
7. **The long night, and his few lines about her hands (flag 2).** At most four or five short lines from him in the whole stretch, about her hands only: in an hour they'll stiffen, keep the fingers moving, the cold is helping. One is the laugh:
   - Dane: "That was my good shirt."
   - Beth: "You don't have a good shirt."
   - She tells him to stop, and he stops. No present-tense "where I am."
8. **The longing (binding, plain).** His coat is open over bare skin in a freezing cabin, and his hands are steady and careful on hers. She wants him. She's angry with him. Both are true. Say all three in plain words. Nothing of her father's, and nothing of Ned, in this beat.
9. **The near-bet (binding, the outline's line).** Hours on, with the white light still on the rocks:
   - Dane: "Dollar says it holds till light."
   - Beth: "I don't bet on—"

**Turn:** Her palms are torn by her own choice. Tess lights the rocks, and the reader didn't see her coming.

**Subtext:** She has never let anyone help her with a job, and tonight two people do: one she won't forgive yet, and one who won't speak at all. She lets them, because the boat matters more. Dane wraps her hands with nothing to say about it, because the last thing he said about her life was two words to his boss, and he knows it.

**Tension source:** Situational and physical: the jam, the rocks, the pain. Surprise: the light. Internal: wanting him and being angry, both at once.

**Ends on (ending type: a line cut off):** "I don't bet on—" She stops, because she can't see it: not the rod, not the wind, not the morning. One plain sentence. No reaction from Dane.

**Texture budget (2):** the jam and the torn palms; the fire truck's light on the rocks.

**Plants:** the palms (→ Ch 15–17); Tess's light (→ Scene 4, Ch 15, Ch 18); the rod bound once (→ Ch 15–16, flag 11); the near-bet (→ Ch 15).

**Word count target:** 560. **Floor 520.** This is the chapter's danger scene. Never compress the jam (steps 1–2), the light and her turn (step 4), or the wordless ask. If it runs long, step 7 drops to the shirt joke alone, and step 6 drops to Beth's answer folded into narration.

## Scene 4

**Setting:** The captain's cabin at first gray, Saturday, about six. The snow has thinned and the wind is starting to go back round. Past the white water at the harbor mouth, off the point, out of the wind, where no boat should be lying in November, the reader sees what Beth sees. Dane's phone is propped on the shelf under the front windows. The harbor has one bar from the tower on the ridge. **Sensory anchor:** her hands in his torn shirt on the wheel, the cloth stiff and brown. A second anchor: down on the harbor road, the fire truck's light going off and the truck pulling away.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane. Dane's boss, a voice on speaker (call two of two in the back half [A8]).

**Point of view:** Beth.

**Want:**
- **Beth** wants to be spoken to, and asked, and not decided about.
- **Dane** wants her alive, and he has learned what wanting that has cost him.
- **The boss** wants the case and her people safe, and she says what she can't do.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open on the sight,** never on the night before. In the first gray past the harbor mouth, off the point, a big fishing boat lies out of the wind, showing one light.
   - **Beth reads it (her skill, plain):** "That's not from here." Nobody from here lies off the point in November. She knows every boat on this coast by its shape.
   - **Link the two said-twice lines, for the listener:** the kid's "They've got a boat," and Ned's "It comes in from offshore." Here it is.
   - Never "no lights" [A20]. It's "the big boat" or "the boat showing one light."
   - **Restate Scene 3, in one clause each:** her wrapped hands on the wheel; on the harbor road the fire truck's light goes off, and it drives away without Tess once looking at the boat.
2. **Dane calls it in the minute they see it, on speaker, in front of her [A16; Dane on the job].** He reports what it is and where, plainly: a big fishing boat lying off the point with one light, watching the harbor mouth.
3. **The boss's facts, in two lines (N10, N1, A4, A14):**
   - Line one: "The Coast Guard is out on the storm's distress calls, and it won't come for a boat that hasn't done anything. If that boat comes at you, call them, and they'll come from an hour off."
   - Line two: "Marine Patrol's boat on your stretch is the one with the hole in it, and the state won't send its next one into a shooting. A text from a phone in nobody's name and Ned's word won't arrest that lawyer, and the kid isn't talking. If she isn't in the room on Monday, Cole goes free at two."
   - Gloss "Marine Patrol" in a clause: the state police on the water.
   - **Dane (N1, binding):** "The harbor mouth is white water till Sunday evening, and the next storm's in before light Monday. Sunday night or not at all." Beth gives one word of agreement. It restates her Scene 1 line.
4. **The repair (binding, A9). Dane turns from the phone to Beth and says it to her face, with his boss listening:**
   - "I decided your life for you because I was scared, and I called it my job. I still think you should go. It was never mine to say."
   - To the phone: "She doesn't go into the program unless she says so. Write that down." One clause for the listener: the program is the new name in a new state.
   - Boss: "Then she's on her own after Monday."
   - Dane: "Not all of her." Then, plainly: he has put in for the Maine office.
5. **He makes the boss speak to her.** Dane: "Tell her yourself. She's right here."
   - The boss, to Beth, the first time she has spoken to her directly in the book: after Monday nobody from her office stands between Beth and those people. Does she understand what she's choosing?
   - **Beth (flag 6 rewording):** "I know what I'm choosing. I'm the one standing here. Say it to me."
   - The boss ends the call.
   - *Optional laugh, one exchange at most.* Dane: "Nobody's ever put my boss on speaker and told her how to talk." / Beth: "Captain." (She's had the wheel all night.)
6. **The fifth day (binding).** In her head, plainly: on Monday, on the lighthouse, she told him to ask her again in five days, and this is the fifth day. He doesn't ask. She notices, and she notices she wanted him to. The longing is plain: she wants him and she wants to be asked. No father, no Ned.
   - Beth: "What do you want, Dane?"
   - Dane: "I'll tell you on the other side."

**Turn (binding, the chapter's turn):** The big boat is real, and nobody is coming to escort them. The program is off the table, on the record. They're two equals now, each keeping their own view: the relationship's point of no return (~79%).

**Subtext:** He gives up the decision without giving up the belief, which is harder than either alone, and she hears the difference. She answers with the risk priced in. "I'll tell you on the other side" is him keeping her condition before she has said it (Ch 15): he won't ask her for anything until she's safe enough to say no.

**Tension source:** Situational: the boat sitting there, watching. Interpersonal: the repair is made on a speakerphone in front of a third party, and neither of them can walk away from the wheel.

**Ends on (ending type: a line, then an image):** "I'll tell you on the other side." Then one short sentence: past the white water, off the point, the one light hasn't moved. No question in narration and no reaction beat after the image. Ch 15 opens on the ham.

**Texture budget (2):** the boss's no-escort facts in two lines; Beth reading a boat's shape.

**Plants:** the big boat (→ Ch 15, 16); no escort (→ Ch 15 s2, Ch 16, N9); Sunday night or not at all (→ Ch 15); the program off; the Maine office (→ Ch 15, 17); "on the other side" (→ Ch 17); the unasked fifth day (→ Ch 17).

**Word count target:** 530. **Floor 490.** If it runs long, cut the optional "Captain" exchange, then trim the boss's line two to the Marine Patrol and Monday sentences (the lawyer line can move to Ch 15 s2 as a restate). Never cut "That's not from here," N1's "Sunday night or not at all," the repair lines, "Write that down," "Not all of her," Beth's "Say it to me," the fifth day, or the last line and image.
