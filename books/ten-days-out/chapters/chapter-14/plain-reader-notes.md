# Plain-reader notes: Chapter 14, "The Wheel"

Draft read: `chapters/chapter-14/final.md` (Nov 6, 2026). I read chapters 01 to 14 `final.md`, in order, and nothing else.

## 1. Retelling

It's Friday night and a storm is smashing Beth's ferry against the stone pier, so she decides to take it off the pier and hold it out in the harbor until morning. If she leaves it, it gets a hole in it by morning, and then it can't make the crossing on Sunday. Dane doesn't tell her no. He holds the flashlight while she lies in a tiny space under the deck and bolts the steering back together. The storm had bent the steering rod and she had only straightened it as well as she could. He takes the ropes off and cuts the last one with Ned's knife, standing out of the way the right way, and she takes the wheel by herself for the first time ever. Out in the harbor the steering sticks and the boat swings toward the rocks. She throws her weight on the wheel, it spins back through her hands and tears the skin off both palms, but she holds it. Tess shines the fire truck's light on the rocks so Beth can see them and steer past. Dane takes off his shirt and wraps her hands with it. At first light a big strange fishing boat is waiting off the point: this is "the boat" the kid warned about. On the phone the boss says nobody can come and help until Tuesday, and if Beth isn't in the room Monday, Cole goes free. So they have to cross on the ferry Sunday night. Dane takes back what he told his boss about Beth going into hiding, says it was never his choice to make, and tells his boss he has asked to be moved to the Maine office. Beth wants him to ask her about love again. He says he'll tell her "on the other side."

## 2. What's at stake

Beth has to get to Portland and speak to the grand jury on Monday morning, or Cole goes free at two. The men on the strange boat are waiting to stop her, nobody official can come and help, and her damaged ferry is the only way off the island, on Sunday night.

## 3. The romance

Yes, I felt it. They are angry with each other and still want each other, and this chapter moves them back toward each other.

- "She thought about turning over in the dark and dragging him down by his coat." Clear.
- "She wanted him. She was angry with him. Both were true." Clear, and it's said straight out.
- The big moment: "I decided your life for you because I was scared, and I called it my job. I still think you should go. It was never mine to say." Then "I've put in for the Maine office." That landed. He is giving something up for her.
- "This was the fifth day, and he didn't ask... and wanted to be asked." Then "I'll tell you on the other side." It works because the book reminds me of the five days. I could follow it without remembering Chapter 8.

What I didn't get: the "Hold it still, Marshal" exchange ("It's what you are." / "You only say it one way."). I could tell it was meant to be sweet, but I didn't understand what they meant. Something about how she says "Marshal"? I missed it.

## 5. Where I lost the thread

1. "She had ended it on Wednesday." (line 11) Ended what? I guessed she ended things with Dane, the "Then they stop. And so do we" scene. But I wasn't tracking days of the week, and "it" is vague. Saying "She had ended things with him on Wednesday" would fix it.
2. "Beth watched Dane's thumb go under the ties... and found her own hand closed round her wrist." (line 11) I didn't know what this gesture meant. Is she remembering being touched? Is she nervous? It slid past me.
3. "Tess. She had put the light on the rocks, and the radio on the shelf stayed silent." (line 117) Then at dawn: "The light never once swung toward the boat." (line 165) Tess keeps not looking at Beth too. Am I supposed to suspect Tess? Or is she just being Tess, angry but helping? I couldn't tell whether the book is warning me or not. If it's a clue, it's too quiet for me. If it isn't, it made me worry for no reason.
4. "Dollar says it holds till light." / "I don't bet on—" (lines 145 to 149) She gets cut off mid-sentence, and that usually means something interrupts her. But nothing happens. The next line is just the rod, the wind and the hours. I waited for a bang that never came and went back over it to see if I'd missed an event.
5. The boss's long speech (line 169) starts with no "his boss said," so for a second I didn't know who was talking on the phone. It's sorted out a few lines later.
6. Line 25, "Then nothing crosses Sunday, and Sunday's the one night the sea lets anything out." This came before I knew Sunday night was the plan, so it went over my head. It made sense later at line 177.

## 6. Verdict: PASS

I could retell the main event: she takes the ferry off the pier, the steering fails, she holds it through the night with torn hands, and the strange boat turns up at dawn. I could say what's at stake. Nothing important reached me only through a word I didn't know, because the steering and the rudder were explained in plain words. The romance moved, and I felt it in Dane's apology and his move to the Maine office.

Things to fix even though it passes: the "I don't bet on—" cut-off that leads nowhere, the unclear Tess signals, and the vague "ended it on Wednesday."
