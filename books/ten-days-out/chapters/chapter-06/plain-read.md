## 1. Retelling

Beth is cooking onions in the summer house where they're hiding, and Dane has finally fallen asleep in his chair after sitting up all night. She plans to take him up to the bed tonight. Then she smells smoke that isn't from her stove. Someone has set the back of the house on fire, and a flashlight is waiting at the front steps to catch them when they run out. She takes them out the pantry window on the side, carrying both pairs of boots. Dane's laces have frozen stiff, so she ties his boots for him in the snow, and he tells her he's going to kiss her for an hour once they're off this hill. In the trees they find the spot where the man with the dragging foot stood all day looking down into the kitchen, waiting for Dane to fall asleep. He shoots at them. They run through the woods and hide behind a boulder, and a shot chips the rock by her face. As they run, they work out that someone told the man where they were. Only four people knew: Beth's three, Tess, Russ and Ned, and Dane's boss. Beth can't finish a sentence saying any of her three is innocent. They reach the iced rocks above the sea, a big wave is coming, and she takes his hand and tells him to do exactly what she does.

## 2. What's at stake

The man with the dragging foot is trying to kill Beth, the only person who saw the drugs, before she can talk to the grand jury. If she dies, Cole Varney goes free. Now it's worse, because one of the four people they trusted with the hiding place told the killer where they were.

## 3. The romance

- "Tonight she was going to wake him on purpose and take him up to the big bed... He wasn't getting the chair again." (Clear. She wants him.)
- "When we're off this hill, I'm going to kiss you for an hour. It goes on the list ahead of sleep." / "An hour's not long, Marshal." (The best moment. Funny and hot, and it happens while she's tying his boots under fire.)
- "She had known this man three days, and she trusted him more than anyone she had known her whole life. That was the worst news of the day. She held on harder." (I felt this one most. It's love turning up on the same night her old trust breaks.)
- "She was not losing him on her own shore." (Clear.)

There's less wanting each other in the body than in Ch 3–5, but the chapter says out loud how she feels about him, so a half-listener can't miss it.
