**Pull score for Chapter 6: 3 of 5.** The opening and the last page pulled me. The middle felt like a chapter I had already read twice.

**I'd keep reading, but...** this is the third night in a row with the same shape: they shelter, the man with the dragging foot finds them, shots, they run along the icy shore, and he won't follow "on that foot." I can feel the book going in a loop.

---

## Where I got bored

- **"One clean print. Then the drag."** By this point I've seen the dragging-heel print in Chapters 1, 3, 4, 5 and now twice here (the log, then the clearing: "one print, then a long line plowed by the bad foot"). In Chapter 1 it was a great detail. Now my eyes skip it. It's the only thing the villain is, and it keeps getting described instead of him doing anything new.
- **"He won't come down iced rock on that foot. Not toward a man with a gun."** That's nearly word for word the reasoning at the boathouse in Chapter 4 ("the man wasn't coming down it with that foot, in the dark, toward a marshal with a gun"). Same move, same reasoning, same shore. I knew how the escape would go before it started.
- **"Dane raised his gun. Past the glare there was nothing to aim at, and he didn't fire. Beth saw that."** He hasn't fired in Chapter 3, twice in Chapter 4, and now twice here. The first time it told me something about him. By the fourth time it's a running gag nobody's laughing at, and I've started wondering what he's there for.
- The chase in the spruces from "The flashlight swung through the trunks above them" to "They ran." Dane's whispered commentary ("Light's on the birches. Now it's past us. Now he's turning...") is the same device as Chapter 3's "I'm still here" and Chapter 4's "Still here." The book has told me twice that he talks through danger. Here I got the trait instead of a scene.

## Where I got confused

- **The flashlight at the front steps.** Dane sees it "low and steady, at the door" and decides the man is waiting at the bottom of the steps. Then the first shot comes "from the corner of the house below them." Was the flashlight a decoy he set down? Did he move? I couldn't tell where the man was, and that matters in a chase.
- **"Nobody could see this house," Dane said. "Somebody told him."** But I'd just been shown that the man could see the kitchen perfectly from a log fifty feet up the hill, and had been there "since before first light, maybe all night." So he found the house the night before. Fine, but did he follow them there? Overhear Dane's speakerphone call on the store steps in Chapter 5 (said out loud, on speaker, in public)? Or was he told? The book tells me "somebody told him" as if that's settled, and I wasn't convinced. Dane's own phone call on the store steps is the leak I'd suspect first, and nobody mentions it.
- **"She tried her old habit, one plain sentence for each of them."** What habit? I've never seen it before. It showed up just in time to be broken.
- I lost track of what day it was. I had to work it out: Thursday the truck, Friday the storm and Russ's house, Saturday the loft and the store, a night in the north-side house that happened offstage, now Sunday dusk. The night skip is covered in one sentence ("where she had slept alone last night with the door open"), and I had to reread to be sure a whole night and day had passed.

## Where I stopped believing

- **The shooter waited all day.** He sat on that log and watched her stand at the kitchen window "all day." In Chapter 3 this same man walked up to a lit window and opened fire. Here he has a clear view of her for hours and does nothing until Dane dozes for twenty minutes, then sets a fire instead. Why? Nobody on the page asks, so I did.
- **Lacing boots under fire.** A gunman is at the front of the house, the house is on fire, and Beth kneels in the snow tying double knots in Dane's laces while he tells her he'll kiss her for an hour and has "thought hard about where on the list." I like both lines, but I didn't believe anyone would say them there. Also, boots drying all day on a mat inside a heated house don't end up with laces "stiff as wire." That reads like the laces exist so she can tie them.
- **Stopping at the log.** They're being hunted and they stop fifty feet into the trees to study packed snow, count prints, and work out sightlines to the stove. It's a good discovery, but nobody running for their life does a crime-scene read.
- **The marshal is useless again.** He slept, he slipped on rocks in Chapter 4, he fell onto the ferry in Chapter 2, he never fires, and here he jokes, "I'm terrible at this... and there were witnesses." I like that Beth is the competent one. But I've stopped believing he's "very good at his job," which he promised me in Chapter 1. The book hasn't shown me one thing he does better than she does.
- **"She had known this man three days, and she trusted him more than anyone she had known her whole life."** I believe she wants him. I don't believe the trust yet, and especially not this fast after one sentence about Russ teaching her to bait a trap at six.

## Where it sounded like AI

- "Fear came up her arms and into her hands." Feeling stated as a stage direction.
- "So she had to be the one to talk, and it cost her." I'm told the cost instead of seeing it.
- "Now it sounded like what a woman says right before she finds out she's wrong." Neat and over-explained.
- "That was the worst news of the day. She held on harder." A tidy button line.
- "She had lost a house tonight, and her bag, and her trust in her three people, all in one hour. She was not losing him on her own shore." A recap of the chapter's emotions right before the climax. It read like a summary, not a thought.
- "Nobody in her whole life had ever stopped for her that fast." Another "never in her life" superlative. The book does these a lot ("more afraid than she had ever been in her life," "the first time in her life she couldn't finish one," "for once in her life nobody had the wheel").
- Tics I'm noticing across the book now: pulling gloves off with her teeth (Chapters 2, 4, 6), "her father's sweater," Dane's father anecdotes as banter filler (the Lowell bus in Chapter 3, "resting my eyes" here), "Cole's lawyer in a good coat," "She said nothing. She listened."
- Every chapter ends on a sharp last line or a cliffhanger. This one ends on a line too ("Hold my hand and do exactly what I do."). It works, but I can see the pattern.

## What I want to know next

- **The September photo.** Chapter 5 ended on the best hook in the book: her picture left Tess's board in the first week of September, before she'd seen anything. Chapter 6 drops it completely. Beth runs through her three suspects and never once thinks about September. That was the question I turned the page for, and it's still the one I most want answered. Who wanted her face on a wall two months early, and why?
- The wave. Do they get through it, and does Dane go in? (Ned can't swim and won't wear a life jacket, which Chapter 2 made a point of, so I'm watching for that.)
- Tess's phone buzzing exactly when Russ had his phone to his ear. Russ answering "Late" and "Nobody" too fast. Ned's fear on the ferry in Chapter 1. Which of the three is it? Or is it Dane's boss, who knew too?
- Where is Cole Varney? The man the whole case is about hasn't been on the page since Chapter 1.
- Who is "the kid"? He has no name, washes his dishes, and pins up her photo. Is he a stalker or a hired gun? That's more interesting than him hunting her again.
- Lower down: whether the condoms ever make it out of that coat pocket. The bed has been promised and put off twice now, and I'm starting to feel teased by the book rather than by the characters.

## Best moment

The opening, before the smoke:

> "She had found butter in the cold pantry and two onions in the grocery box. She had cut them thick and let them go slow in an iron pan. The smell had filled every shut room of the house before anything else was ready, the window over the sink had gone blue, and her feet were dry for the first time in three days."

It's the first time in six chapters the book slows down and lets someone live, and then "smoke that wasn't hers" comes in under the onions. It's also the only time the threat arrives in a new way (a smell, the kettle sound of fire) instead of through a dragging footprint. Close second: Beth shutting the stove vent on her way out, "It was habit, and it was crazy." That is a real person.

Runner-up for dialogue: "I don't bet on what I can see. Pantry." and "This is my quiet."

## Listener pass (if heard aloud)

The short declarative sentences play well in audio, and the fire opening would hold a listener. The spruce chase is hard to follow by ear: positions change ("the corner of the house below them," "the light came up the side," "on the birches") with no fixed landmark, and Dane's whispered running commentary sounds the same as the narration around it. The Tess / Russ / Ned "sentence that stopped" passage sounds like a stutter when read aloud ("Tess was. ... Russ was. ... Ned was.") and only works on the page.
