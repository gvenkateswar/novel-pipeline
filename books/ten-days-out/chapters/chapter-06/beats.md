## Flags before the beats

1. **The bet plant has to move inside s1.** The outline puts "Dollar says…" / "I don't bet on what I can see" in s1 "while she cooks," but the binding first line has Dane asleep while she cooks. **Fix used:** the bet comes in s1 after he wakes, with the pan still spitting on the stove, about the flashlight at the front steps. She *can* see it, which makes her line literally true, and that makes the plant land as a joke and a threat together.
2. **Ch 05 set the condoms up "for tonight" (Saturday), and World rule 14 allows no sex outside the four heat scenes (heat #2 is Ch 07).** If the page doesn't say what happened Saturday night, a reader will think a love scene was skipped. **Fix used:** one line inside s1: Dane sat up all Saturday night in a chair facing the door with the gun on his knee, and the box from Tess's counter is still zipped in her coat. It's new information. It also explains why he's going under now (three nights without real sleep) and gives Dane a cost for losing the Ch 05 "nobody hears" argument, which the Ch 05 reader said he gave up too easily. It plants the condoms for Ch 07.
3. **Calls to the boss.** Ch 05 ends on "Call me at six" (Saturday). The north-side house has no signal (bible, Phones). Nothing in this chapter needs a call, so **there is no boss call in Ch 06**, which also breaks the boss-call shape the readers flagged. For the continuity log: the Saturday six o'clock call is off the page (from the store steps before they drove north is the safe reading), and the boss has heard nothing since. Dane's next report is from the tower in Ch 08.
4. **For later beat sheets, not this one:** in Ch 05 the boss agreed to get the records "in two" days (Monday, Day 4). The outline and bible have them come back clean on Day 5, said in Ch 10 s1. Either Ch 08's tower call says they aren't in yet, or Ch 10 says "they came back yesterday." Flagged for the Ch 08 and Ch 10 beats.
5. **Format note.** The agent format asks for 4–6 scenes of 500–700 words. With the bible's 1,840 target, four scenes run 400–520 each. They're short on purpose: this is the one fast chapter between Ch 05's talk and Ch 07's sea.

No conflict with the outline's plot. Who wins, who's hurt and what burns all stand.

## What the reader expects, and how this chapter beats it

The Ch 05 cold reader predicted: *"the north-side house gets attacked tonight, probably during or right after the scene the condoms set up."* The brief says to surprise the reader in **how and when**, not whether.

- **When:** the night the reader braced for passes with nothing happening. So does a whole gray Sunday. The man waits for the *last* light of the *second* day, and he waits for the one thing Dane swore wouldn't happen: Dane asleep.
- **How:** no shot through a window and no man on the stairs. He sets the back wall burning while Beth's own stove is going, so the smell of smoke in the kitchen is ordinary for a minute, and he waits in front of the **front door** for the fire to push them out to him. Beth beats the plan with a window and her knowledge of the house.
- **The new fact on the page (the plot moves):** the trampled place under the spruces where he stood and watched the kitchen window all day. He knew this house by Sunday morning at the latest, and he waited for the marshal to sleep. It's new, it's about a person, and it doesn't point at anyone.

**World rule 12 / the decoys:** no new clue on Tess, Russ or Ned. Beth's suspicion stays on the four as a group, and Dane puts his own boss into the count in the same breath ("And one of them's mine"). The lean on Tess and Russ stays exactly as heavy as Ch 05 left it.

## Recycled gestures and patterns to keep off this chapter's page

From the Ch 02–05 reader notes. The writer, the dialogue doctor and the voice editor all check against this list.

- No Russ cap (Russ isn't here). No snow "flat," "came flat" or "blowing flat." Snow in this chapter is wet, heavy, falling straight, or knee-deep in a gully.
- No "polite and slow" and no "Ms. Calloway." Dane isn't angry at her in this chapter. He's angry at himself, and that shows as silence (s2).
- No "good coat," no lawyer and no jury in Dane's mouth. No rope scar. No "Still here," "I'm right here," "Here's how this goes," "Can I?", "I'm still talking." No "she let herself [feel X]." No "X frightened her more than anything."
- **The wanting lines change shape in every scene.** No "She wanted him" or "He wanted X" sentence anywhere in the chapter. Longing in this chapter is a plan (s1), a spoken line (s2), a hand (s3) and a refusal to lose him (s4). Each is listed per scene.
- **The scene endings change shape:** s1 cuts mid-motion, s2 ends on a sound, s3 ends on an image, s4 ends on the outline's line of dialogue. No scene ends on a neat verdict line that explains the beat.
- **Rhythm (the "metronome" note):** the writer must put at least two long sentences (35+ words, running on "and") in Beth's interior: one at the onions in s1 and one at the deduction in s3. The chase runs short. The two places where she thinks run long.
- The audit's requirement for three interior passages longer than the dialogue around them is met at: the onions and the box (s1), the trampled snow (s2), and the deduction (s3).

### Scene 1
**Setting:** The north-side summer house's kitchen, dusk, about four o'clock on Sunday. A stranger's iron pan, onions in butter, the window over the sink going blue. Sensory anchors (choose two): the onions (smell first, then the sound of them going soft); then smoke with a different, wrong smell, a soft roar behind the back wall.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane. (The kid is seen only as a light.)
**Surface action:**
- Opens on the binding line. Then give the ordinary pleasure three or four sentences of room (bible, Beth pleasure 1): onions going gold, the smell filling a shut house before anything else is ready, her first warm kitchen since Thursday.
- One line, inside the cooking, for Saturday night (Flag 2): he sat all last night in that chair with the gun on his knee and his face to the door, and the box from Tess's counter is still zipped in her coat. She heard him go under twenty minutes ago and didn't wake him, because she can hear this whole hillside from this stove.
- She smells it, and it isn't hers: wet wood, paint, something under the onions. Her own stove smoke is going up the chimney, which is why it took her a minute. Give the fear a beat to arrive (World rule 8): she stops the pan, listens, and hears a low roar where the back wall meets the woodpile.
- She wakes him with her hand flat on his chest, no words. He's up with the gun before his eyes are open.
- Dane's first line, self-mocking, at length (his register): something like *"I was resting my eyes, which is what my father says, and he's lying too."* She doesn't laugh.
- The back hall is orange. Dane goes to the edge of the front window and sees a flashlight, steady and low, at the bottom of the front steps. Waiting.
- **The plant, played under the threat:** Dane: "Dollar says he's at the front door." Beth, who can see the light from where she stands: "I don't bet on what I can see."
- Beth picks the side window (the pantry or small bedroom, facing the woods and away from the steps) because she has been in this house all day and knows which window opens. She takes her coat off the hook first and puts it on over her father's sweater (the box is in it), then grabs both pairs of boots. Her bag and the groceries stay. She shuts the stove off on the way past. It's a habit and it's absurd, and she knows it.
- The window sticks. She hits the frame with the heel of her hand the way you do a swollen door, and it goes.
**Subtext:** She let him sleep, as a gift, and the gift is what the man was waiting for. She doesn't think that yet. He knows exactly what he did, and for once he talks less.
**Tension source:** Situational. Fire behind them, a gun at the front. And a quiet one: her ears, which she trusts more than anything, missed it, because fire makes almost no sound until it's too late.
**Turn:** Ordinary kitchen to burning house. Then a second turn: the front door, the obvious way out, is the trap.
**Longing (plain, shaped as a plan):** while she cooks, before the smoke, one plain line about tonight: she's going to wake him up on purpose tonight, in the bed upstairs nobody has used, and he's not getting the chair. No "she wanted."
**Laugh:** Dane's waking line, then the bet and her answer.
**Plants:** the bet; the condoms in her coat; Dane asleep on watch.
**Ends on (cut mid-motion):** she goes out the side window first with both pairs of boots, drops into the snow in her socks, and turns with her arms up for him, smoke coming out over the sill above her into the last of the light. Cut there, before he lands.
**Word count target:** 520.

### Scene 2
**Setting:** Outside the side wall, then the spruces uphill of the house. The last gray light. Wet snow over their ankles, then knee-deep under the trees. The fire now loud on the far side of the house, with a glow coming round the corner.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane; the kid (unseen, then a flashlight).
**Surface action:**
- Socks in snow, for the second time in the book, and this time Dane's too. One line on the reversal, from her side: the man who put his coat around her in the snow is standing in his socks in it.
- Boots on, crouched against the side wall out of sight of the front steps. Her boots go on in two seconds. His new ones won't: the laces are frozen stiff. She pulls her gloves off with her teeth (the one gloves beat in the chapter; the bible's gloves-off motif) and laces his boots for him, fast, bare-handed, double-knotted, while he keeps the gun on the corner.
- **Laugh:** Dane: "My mother used to do that." Beth: "Your mother had more time."
- They go up into the spruces, the opposite way from the steps. Fifty feet in, Beth stops. A trampled place under the biggest tree: snow packed flat and yellow-gray, a log brushed clear to sit on, and the boot prints going back and forth, back and forth, one clean and one dragged, more of them than anyone could count. From here you see straight into the kitchen window, where her pan is.
- **The discovery, said plainly in her head (World rule 6):** he stood here and watched her cook. He was here before the light went this morning, maybe all night, and he watched the marshal sit up in that chair, and he waited.
- Dane looks at it and **stops talking.** That's the turn. The man who talks through everything says nothing, and she sees his face in the fire's light and knows exactly what he's thinking about himself. (He doesn't say "I fell asleep." Ch 07 gets that line.)
- Because he's silent, she has to be the one who talks, and it costs her: "Dane. Move." (Two words. That's her limit in fear.)
**Subtext:** He failed at the one thing he's sure he's good at, in front of her. She won't let him stand there and have that out with himself where a man with a gun can see him.
**Tension source:** Internal for Dane (shame, read from outside), situational for both (they're exposed in the glow, and the man is somewhere between the front steps and here).
**Turn:** They were hiding from a man who'd find them. Now they know he found them and then waited, patiently, for the marshal to sleep. Their roles flip for one beat: she talks, he's silent.
**Longing (plain, spoken, his):** while she's on her knees doing his laces, low, at the length of a Dane sentence: when they're off this hill he's going to kiss her for an hour, and he wants it on the list ahead of sleep. Beth: "Boots first." (The laugh stays the mother line. This one is wanting.)
**Plants:** the dragging heel in the snow; he waits and watches windows (→ Ch 08 "open sand"); Dane's silence (→ Ch 07 confession, Ch 16).
**Ends on (a sound):** the first shot, from the corner of the house below them, into the spruce over their heads. Snow comes down off the branches onto them. Cut.
**Word count target:** 420.

### Scene 3
**Setting:** The woods downhill toward the north shore, nightfall. Full dark under the trees by the end. Sensory anchors (choose two): the flashlight swinging through the trunks; the crack of the gun flat and close among trees; the fire's orange behind and above them, getting smaller.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane; the kid (a light, two shots, a heel in the snow).
**Surface action:**
- **The chase.** Beth leads, downhill, with Dane's left hand in her right hand below the tape on his forearm. His gun hand is free. She holds his hand the whole way (outline romance beat).
- Dane talks, low, present tense, specific, and it's new content, never "still here": where the light is, how far, how fast. "Light's left. Behind the house. He's found the window." "He's coming the long way, round the woodpile, because of the foot." Beth says nothing.
- **A new signal between them (talk against silence):** she presses his hand once, hard, and he stops mid-word. He obeys at once. She notices, and it's the first time anyone has ever stopped for her that fast.
- **Laugh, in the pause:** crouched behind a boulder while the light passes above them, he keeps a running commentary in a whisper. Beth: "Quieter." Dane: "This is my quiet."
- Two shots, at sound. One hits the boulder. They run again.
- **The outline lines, while they run (exact):** Dane, between breaths: "Nobody could see this house. Somebody told him." Beth: "I know."
- **The deduction (long interior; one of the two long-sentence places).** This is her own reasoning, not a recap. The loft, which nobody knew, nobody came to but Russ, and he knocked. This house, which nobody could see from any road or window, four people knew by Saturday afternoon, and it burned. Three of the four were hers. She tries her habit, a one-sentence verdict on each of the three, and for the first time in her life she can't finish one. Give the three equal weight. No new fact about any of them, and no extra lean on Tess or Russ (World rule 12).
- Dane, fairly, while they run: "And one of them's mine." He keeps his office in the count himself. One line, no more.
- The cost, one line: *Three of them would die for me.* She thinks it again, and this time it sounds like something a person says before she's wrong.
**Subtext:** She has just lost the thing she trusted most, her own verdicts on her own people. She holds onto his hand harder, because it's the one verdict she's still sure of.
**Tension source:** Situational (shots, the light) and internal (the end of "three of them would die for me").
**Turn:** "I know." Beth concedes that someone she loves may be selling her. It's the chapter's key event, and it can't be luck anymore.
**Longing (plain, as action, and one plain thought):** her hand in his the whole way. One plain line in her head: she trusts this man she met three days ago more than anyone she's known her whole life, and that is the worst news of the day. (No "she wanted.")
**Plants:** talk against silence (the hand-press); the dragging heel (a long line ploughed through the snow behind the light, seen once when it crosses a clearing); the say-it-twice facts for Ch 07.
**Ends on (an image):** they break out of the trees at the top of the shore. Below them is gray granite running down into black water, white where the sea comes up. Behind them, up the hill, the summer house is a whole lit window of fire, and they're standing in its light.
**Word count target:** 500.

### Scene 4
**Setting:** The north shore rocks, full dark. Granite with ice on it running down into the sea between the summer house and the lighthouse a mile on. Sensory anchors (choose two): the ice on the rock under her palms; a wave coming in, a roar and then nothing; the fire's glow on their backs.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane; the kid (a flashlight at the top of the bank).
**Surface action:**
- They're lit from behind by the fire. The flashlight comes out of the trees at the top of the bank and stops there.
- **Beth's choice, said aloud in her short register, with the reason (World rule 17: her danger calls are the smart ones):** down onto the rocks at the waterline. He won't come down iced rock on that foot, not toward a marshal (she saw it under the boathouse in Ch 04; she uses it here without restating it). From the top he can shoot, so they can't stay in the glow, and the only way north out of his light is along the low rocks where the sea comes up. It's said to Dane's face, two lines.
- Dane looks at the rocks, the ice and the black water, in his boots. His line, self-mocking: he needs her to know he's terrible at this. Beth: "I know. I watched you jump." (A callback to Ch 02's jump, said once, and her verdict.)
- A shot from the top goes wide, because the man can't see well past his own light. Dane raises the gun, can't see past the light either, and doesn't fire. Beth notices that he doesn't.
- They go down onto the rocks. Beth's hands are bare on the ice again (gloves still in her pocket from s2). She puts her feet only where she knows the rock.
- Beth can hear the next wave building out in the dark before Dane can see it: a long gathering sound.
- **Longing (plain, a refusal):** one line in her head as she turns to him: she has lost a house, a bag and her three people's names in one hour, and she is not losing him on her own shore. (No "she wanted.")
- She turns back, takes his hand, and looks at him in the last of the fire's light.
**Subtext:** She's asking him to give up the only thing he still has, his judgment, and put his life in her hands on her ground. After s2 he has no argument left. He takes the hand.
**Tension source:** Situational. Ice, the sea at fifty degrees, a gun above them, and him in the wrong boots.
**Turn:** The protector becomes the protected. He does exactly what she says, from here into Ch 07.
**Laugh:** "I know. I watched you jump."
**Plants:** the light holding at the top of the bank (→ Ch 07, where it holds on the water where he went in); he won't come down iced rock (← Ch 04).
**Ends on (binding line of dialogue):** with the wave coming up white out of the dark: "Hold my hand and do exactly what I do."
**Word count target:** 400.
