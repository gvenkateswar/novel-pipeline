**I'd keep reading, but.** Chapter 2 ended with a man in her mudroom and "Who knew you were here?" / "You." Chapter 3 swaps that threat for a long, low-pressure day. Nobody chases anything. The limper never shows. The marshal asks one question in a store and lets the answer go. The chapter lives on the romance and on the phone, and the danger plot sits still for most of it. If Ch 4 is also a "quiet day on the island" chapter, that is where I would put the book down.

## Where I got bored

- **The north road walk.** *"Any boat down there could be theirs," he said... "I've been carrying that around since noon."* I don't know what "that" is. Is it the boat problem, the noon appointment, or the phone? The paragraph exists to get them to Ashby, and it reads that way. I skimmed it.
- **The middle of Marr's.** *"So far today you're the post office, the hardware store and air traffic control. What is your job, exactly?" / "Emergency management director. Since town meeting."* That's cute, but it's the third or fourth time the store slows down to show me how the island works: the urn, the soft sills, the propane price, Ida's heart, LifeFlight, the phone shelf, the tags. Each item may matter later. Piled together, they read like a list of clues I'm being asked to remember.
- **The opening footprint.** *"The man from the reefer had stood on her steps."* Ch 2 already made me sure someone was in the mudroom, so this confirms something I already believed. It's a fine image but a slow first beat.

## Where I got confused

- **"Pike: Q's request approved."** I took Pike to be his boss. Who is Q? The book has never mentioned a Q. I didn't know if that was the point or if I'd missed someone. Hearing it read aloud would make it worse.
- **"Firm at thirty-one. Saturday noon."** After a beat I worked out that this is the Webbers Cove boat. Then at noon in the store, *"Dane looked at it, then at Arsenault, and stayed where he was. Beth had read the message that morning, and she watched him let it go."* Why look at Arsenault? Is Arsenault the seller? Is noon a meeting with Arsenault? Or is he just letting the boat go while watching the suspicious man? I read it three times and still don't know which.
- **How does Tess know 22:40?** *"Your back steps had you home, twenty-two forty."* Did she see them from the road with the flashlight in Ch 2? Did someone tell her? It is the most important line in the store scene, and Beth doesn't react on the page to the fact that Tess knew. She protects her instead. I couldn't tell whether Beth hears it as a kindness or a threat, so I couldn't either.
- **Beth hides the log from Dane.** Her palm goes flat over 22:40 *"before she'd decided anything."* That's a big choice: she's keeping evidence from the marshal in front of a roomful of people. I liked it, but I wanted to know why, and the chapter doesn't tell me. Is it loyalty, or does she not trust him now?
- **"Then don't sit with your back to me."** I had to reread to see that Beth says this and then takes the door-facing chair herself, making him sit with his back to the door. It's a good trust test once I got it. The first time through, I couldn't tell who was speaking.
- **Ashby.** The paragraph moves from her father's fish house, flooded till Monday, to "Ashby had water on three sides." I didn't know what Ashby was (a house, a family, summer people?) until the Connecticut sheet on the window much later.
- **"the hard ridge across his palms."** A ridge from what? He has courtroom boots and fudge-shop shoes. If that's a callus from boats or guns, I want a word on it. Right now it reads like a stock romance hand.
- **Why they stayed in the house.** Someone tried her kitchen door at 11:40 and ran. The next chapter opens with the stove lit in the same kitchen. Did they stay there till morning? Nobody mentions it.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Dane calls the new location in again.** Last chapter, the glow under his chin came minutes before someone stood in her mudroom, and he himself asked "Who knew you were here?" Now he reports *"The move, and the house"* the same way, over the same phone, from an open porch. A marshal who suspects a leak would at least hesitate, use a different channel, or report a vaguer location. He doesn't, and Beth, who has just pulled out of a kiss over that phone, holds the curtain and watches. The dread works, but it rests on both of them being careless at the exact moment the book has trained me to be careful.
- **"I won't lie to you about whether there's something."** He says almost exactly this twice in one chapter, at the stove and in the shed. The second time it sounds like a rehearsed line rather than a man improvising under pressure.
- **"I'd rather be kissed by a man I can check."** I loved it as a line. I don't quite believe a person says it mid-kiss with her coat open. It sounds written.
- **Russ, cap off, cap on.** He does it twice in this chapter. It reads as a stage direction for the character, not something a man does.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **The "the way she'd" simile pattern.** It runs all through the book and is thick in this chapter: *"held it the way she'd hold anything she meant to check," "the way you'd ask about a lost dog," "knew what was in there the way she knew boats," "the voice she used on deck when a line was about to part."* Earlier: *"the way she'd tell Ned a binder had slipped," "the way she'd go along a chain with her hand," "the way he looked at weather"* (twice across chapters). Each one is fine. Together they are a machine.
- **Feelings explained after the image already did the work.** *"He shaved slowly, like a man handling a dead man's things in front of his daughter."* The scene already shows this. *"It came up out of her from a long way down, older than him."* "Don't write me down" lands on its own, because Ch 1 gave me the father in blue pen. *"a man who hadn't slept."* *"she heard herself make a sound she never made"* is stock romance.
- **Every chapter ends on a crafted button.** Ch 1: hand to the hip. Ch 2: "You." plus the glow. Ch 3: *"the sheet dropped back over the glass between them, out of her opening hand."* All three are good, and all three are the same move: a final concrete image weighted with meaning. By the third it's a pattern I can predict.
- **Details reused until they turn into tics.** The phone "needs sky." Dane talking low "and he didn't stop" (twice in Ch 2). Dane's *"I'm told"* (*"I'm told it isn't my best one," "I'm told it's what I'm for"*). Watching her hands. Ned turning at the top of the ladder *"the way he always did"* (Ch 1 and Ch 2). "Nobody but me and God" (Ch 2, finished by Beth in Ch 3). Some of these are surely deliberate plants, but there are so many repeats that I can't tell which ones the book wants me to notice.
- **The long sentence in the shed.** The "and... and... and... and she let it" run-on (*"Then they warmed, and moved, up her spine..."*) is a recognizable heat device. It worked for me once. It's the same breathless run the wrist paragraph used in Ch 1.

## Prediction: who's the leak?

The question the book has planted: someone told the man with the dragged heel that Beth was home on the night of the storm, when the net said she was aboard. Who knew she was home? Dane, Ned (told directly), and somehow Tess (22:40).

- **Ned: 35%.**
  - Ch 1: *"Eleven years, and he had never once sent her home."* He sent her home the night the dirty reefer crossed. He knew.
  - Ch 1: *"That crate of his come open, did it?"* He was in the wheelhouse. How did he know it opened?
  - Ch 1: he takes the store's bag with Tess's tag and says "I'll lock up." He's handling the money.
  - Ch 2: Beth tells him "Home. Don't tell anybody." He's one of the only people who knew.
  - Ch 3: he hands over the Ashby key, and *"a generator running out there would only look like Ned."* He now knows the second location too, and the cover story is literally him.
  - Against: the book loves him (the warm gloves, "Bethie," the secret that he can't swim). That love is exactly why I suspect him. The book seems set up to break her heart this way, and I'm braced for it, which also makes it the obvious answer.
- **Tess: 30%.**
  - Ch 2: *"a fold of bills came up with it, hundreds on the outside."* Both Beth and Dane see it.
  - Ch 3: *"Your back steps had you home, twenty-two forty."* She knew, and she wrote it down twice.
  - Ch 3: *"Put that away, Lyle. It's on your account."* She runs a tab for the Gloucester dragger, then laughs *"too loud."*
  - Ch 3: she pushes hard for Beth to sleep at her house ("Then tomorrow"), and she has everyone's phones on a shelf in her office.
  - Against: *"Didn't put you on the air, Beth."* She's either protecting Beth or covering herself. She's almost too visibly suspicious for a three-chapter book.
- **Dane, or his office (Pike/Portland/"Q"): 20%.**
  - Ch 2: the glow under his chin minutes before the mudroom door went quiet.
  - Ch 3: *"Log daily. Do not disclose to witness."* *"Q's request approved."* Someone upstream is asking for her whereabouts every day.
  - Against: he's the romantic lead, and the book wants me to suspect him so it can clear him. My guess is the leak runs through his reports, but it isn't him personally.
- **Lyle Arsenault (the dragger): 10%.** Lit through the blackout, a man on the afterdeck facing her hill, *"kind as a deacon,"* knew her father, has a tab with Tess. I see him more as the buyer or muscle than the leak.
- **Russ Whitcomb: 5%.** He waves off the cable cut too quickly (*"Somebody'd have to beat the rust to it"*) and asks "Where you sleeping tonight?" in public. That might be a warning, though, not a threat.

My best guess: Tess talks and Ned knows, and the marshal's log is how it gets confirmed.

## Listener pass (heard, not read)

- **"Q's request approved"** won't survive audio. One letter, no context, gone before it registers.
- **Lyle / Arsenault / the Margaret Ann.** One man, three names, introduced in one paragraph. By ear I'd lose that "Lyle" is the dragger captain.
- **Untagged exchanges.** The kitchen ("Then don't sit with your back to me.") and parts of the store depend on paragraph breaks that a narrator can't show. The shed dialogue is fine because there are only two people.
- **The text message beats** (italic texts, *22:40* on a hand) need the narrator to change voice, or a listener won't know what is quoted.
- **The run-on in the shed** will either be gorgeous or breathless mush depending on the narrator. It's long for one breath.
- **The scene break inside the shed** (between "took hold of the pull cord" and "She primed it by feel") would sound like a stumble in audio. It's the same place and the same minute.
- **What plays well aloud:** "Third one." / "Is that a rule?" / "It is for this one." "Don't write me down." Tess's patter.

## Romance check

- **Build:** it's earned. The wrist on the ramp (Ch 1), lying flat under the bulwark and the knee under the blanket (Ch 2), then the razor (Ch 3). Each step is a little closer, and each rises out of danger or work, not out of nothing. When she watches his throat under the lather and *"wanted it to be her thumb,"* the slow burn finally tips.
- **The kiss:** she initiates it, at the moment he's unguarded (the grin when the engine catches). That's right for her. "Under. Not over." is specific and hers.
- **The stop:** this is the best structural choice in the chapter. She stops because of the phone pressing into her hip, which ties the romance to the suspense. "Don't write me down" reaches back to the father in blue pen in Ch 1. That's real character, not a convenient brake.
- **Two voices:** mostly distinct. Beth speaks in clipped, nautical, corrective lines ("Line." "She." "Other way."). Dane is polite, wry, and self-deprecating, and his long sentences run out of control. The risk is that Dane's charm is one note: every line is a polite joke at his own expense. In the shed, where he goes silent, he's more attractive than anywhere he talks.
- **What I'm missing:** what Dane wants from her beyond safety and decency. The boat ("a boat I can't be called off of") is the only private want he has, and it's the best thing about him. The chapter uses it as a phone message and then drops it.
- **Heat level so far:** warm and on the page, clothed, hands under wool. It promises more.

## What I want to know next

1. Will someone come to Ashby tonight? That's the pull. If the call he just made leaks, I'll know it's his channel. If no one comes, I'll feel cheated.
2. How did Tess know 22:40, and why did Beth hide it?
3. Who is Q, and what did Q request?
4. What did Ned know the night of the reefer, and how did he know the crate opened?
5. What is Arsenault doing in the bay, and why did Dane look at him at noon?
6. Who is the limper? He rows *"like somebody who had learned before he could walk,"* so he's local. Whose son is he?
7. Was the bridge cable cut?

The questions are real. My worry is that six of the seven are about clues, and only the first is about something happening.
