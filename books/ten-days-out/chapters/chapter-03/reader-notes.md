## Where I got bored

- **"Cole's truck had been clean. Everything his lawyer needed to make go away was standing in this kitchen, putting socks in a bag. Her eyes were the whole case."** and the paragraph after it, **"He had been wrong at the shack about his own office. The man had left Pruett at half past ten, before she ever called..."** Chapter 2 already did this whole reasoning out loud, on the pier, nearly line for line ("Her eyes are the case", "I was wrong about that", "Somebody still sent him before you called"). Here I'm reading it a second time inside Dane's head, with nothing new added. My attention went right there, three pages in.
- **"The grand jury has to hear her by Monday morning, or the prosecutor says Cole walks."** That's the third time I've been told this. Beth's comeback, "She's heard," says what I was thinking.
- "Cole's truck was clean" comes up twice in this chapter (once in the kitchen, once in the speech after the kiss), after appearing in Chapter 2 as well. By the speech I was skimming it.
- The whole Russ's-house setup, from the lights going out to the coffee: everyone is waiting, and the only tension is Dane telling me he wants her. The time jump from about 4:30 to "By ten" skips the six o'clock call, so the evening felt like filler between two scenes.

## Where I got confused

- **"Call me at six," she said.** This comes right after Dane speaks, with Beth in the room. For a moment I thought Beth said it. It's the first time I learn the boss is a woman, and Chapter 2's "She wants you in the room" was about the prosecutor, so the "she" landed on the wrong person.
- **"Choke, Russ." / "Is the choke off?" Russ said. "It is." Another pull.** Did he fix it, or was she wrong? I couldn't tell who had been right about the choke, and the generator just starts.
- **"The Dyers have a nephew in Augusta."** I don't know if Beth is saying the call is fake (the nephew lives in Augusta, so why is he on the island?) or that it's real (yes, there is a nephew). Nobody answers her, Russ goes anyway, and the line just hangs there. If it's a warning, it's odd that the marshal ignores it. If it isn't, why include it?
- Russ explains Tess in the middle of an emergency ("That's Tess. Who's Tess? The store. And in a storm she's the emergency director..."). I lost track of whether he's telling Dane or the reader.
- Where does Russ end up? He leaves for "twenty minutes", the house gets shot up, and he never comes back into the chapter. I don't know if he's walking into his own trap or if he's part of it.

## Where I stopped believing

- **The decoy call.** The second the radio crackled with a hurt man at an empty house, "out in his own boat Thursday," I knew it was the setup. The man in the hoodie came over in a boat Thursday night. Dane spent all of Chapter 2 saying someone on the island is talking. Then the island's only armed guard leaves the porch, and a marshal with forty-one witnesses behind him thinks "the likeliest thing in the world" and lets him go. I was ahead of the professional by a full page, and that made him look slow. "He hated it anyway" doesn't fix that. It just tells me the author knows too.
- **Dane's speech during the shooting.** "Here's how this goes... I'm very good at it, ask anybody in Massachusetts." That's a lot of polished, charming talk with glass flying and shots still coming. The short line, "I'm still here," is the one I believed.
- The jury speech after the kiss ("if Cole's lawyer can stand up and tell twelve strangers that the marshal was sleeping with the only witness...") is a lawyer's paragraph coming out of a man who's just been bitten on the lip. It's too whole and too tidy for that moment.
- Russ sits outside on the porch with a shotgun from 4:15 until 1 a.m., in a snowstorm, in full view. Maybe that's who he is, but nobody notices it's cold.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **The same desire-plus-verdict sentence, five times in one chapter:**
  - "He wanted her... and it was the least useful thing he could think in a house a man had broken into."
  - "He didn't have to look down to fight with her, and he liked that more than he could afford to."
  - "He wanted her more than he had ever wanted anything, and that was going to be a problem."
  - "He would do anything to keep this woman breathing where he could hear it, and that reached well past the job."
  - "he'd follow her anywhere on this island, and that was a terrible thing for a marshal to know about himself."
  
  Add "He wanted that again, and more, and he was supposed to be watching the road" and "He wanted her so badly he'd stopped watching the window." He has the same thought every scene. By the fourth one I was rolling my eyes. The kiss shows me all of this better than any of those sentences.
- **"The whole harbor road could see where she was, and Dane had made sure of it. The call had come from the Dyer house, and Russ had gone."** The narration explains the irony to me, in case I missed it. I didn't.
- **"he already knew that quiet was how she got scared."** It tells me what the scene could have shown.
- **Scene-ending lines that click shut:** "Dane stood at the window and saw what the road saw." "Then tomorrow." / "Probably sooner." "I know a place." Every section ends on a button. The last one works. Together they feel like a pattern.
- **"Here's how this goes"** again. Dane said it in Chapter 1 too. It's turning into his catchphrase, but it reads like the writer reaching for the same line.
- **Russ asking and answering his own questions** three times ("Who taught you that? Your father. Who taught your father? Mine...", "Who's coming up these steps past me? Not him.", "That's Tess. Who's Tess? The store."). It's a voice, which I appreciate, but three in one chapter is a tic, and it's the only thing Russ gets to be.
- "for once his head went quiet" and "a sound he had never once made in front of a witness" are fine on their own. With the five verdict sentences around them, the whole chapter feels like Dane narrating his own feelings to a therapist.

## What I want to know next

- **Who sent the fake radio call?** Someone phoned the store "from the Dyer house." Is it the hoodie man, or is Tess in on it?
- **Is Russ okay, or is Russ the leak?** He picked the house, sort of, and he left on cue.
- **Dane's boss.** Dane said he'd report to her and nobody else. In this chapter he gives her the house, the porch, the shotgun and "one door at the back" on speaker, and a few hours later a man walks up to the window. Am I meant to suspect her? I do now.
- **Ned.** He was frightened in Chapter 1 and has been off the page since he limped home. I'm still waiting on that.
- **Where is "a place" Beth knows**, and how does she get a bleeding marshal there in a storm with a gunman behind the woodpile?
- Whether the kiss leads anywhere before the courtroom problem catches up with them. "Probably sooner" is a promise, and I'm holding them to it.

These questions are real, and the last line works. I'd turn the page.

## Best moment

> "I'm going to kiss you," she said. "If you've got a reason why not, you've got about three seconds, and it had better be better than a jury."
>
> He counted. He counted everything. He got to two.
>
> She took a fistful of his shirt and kissed him.

And its payoff afterward: "Three seconds," Beth said, "and you came back with a jury." / "I got to two. The jury took the third." Also "A jury doesn't get a vote on who kisses me. I do. I voted." Beth takes charge here and is funny doing it, which is the most alive she's been. "He counted everything" tells me more about Dane than all five of the wanting sentences put together.

Runner-up: "Her cheek was on the glass, eyes open." One plain image in the middle of the shooting, and it scared me more than anything else on the page.
