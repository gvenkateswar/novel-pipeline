# Reader Notes: Chapter 03, "The Generator Shed"

**Draft read:** Ch 03 `final.md` after the tournament rewrite, Oct 29. Read cold, after Ch 01 and Ch 02 `final.md`. Nothing else opened.

**Pull score:** 7/10 for the chapter. By scene: kitchen/razor 4 of 5, store 3 of 5, Ashby generator shed 4 of 5.
**Would I keep going?** Yes, straight into Ch 04. Warning anyway: I'd keep reading, but the store scene is where I started skimming, and I could see the clues being set out for me.

## Where I got bored

- **The store scene, from the Arsenault exit to the Ashby exchange.** "Tess snapped a yellow tag through the zipper of the deposit bag that would sit in the safe till a boat ran. 'Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's. I won't have my money in Ned's color.'" My attention went here. Nobody says that out loud to a person who already knows it. It's there for me, and I could feel it being handed over.
- The LifeFlight / Ida Dyer beat. "LifeFlight won't lift Ida Dyer. If they won't fly Ida, nobody's flying." A new name, a new agency, and a fact I'd already got (nobody's leaving). The "slow putting my hand down" / "She never put it down" banter saves it, just about.
- "She saw the hook with the two keys for Tess's Ford on their MARR'S tag, and the shelf of phones charging under it with Ned's among them in its rubber band." This is a camera pan over evidence. The action has Dane running out the back door after the man who was in her mudroom, and the sentence stops to take inventory. I noticed the keys and the phones, and I noticed being made to notice them, and that's two different things.

## Where I got confused

- **"Pike: Q's request approved. Log daily. Do not disclose to witness."** Who is Pike? I'm guessing his boss. Q is "Homeland Security. Portland." and that's all I get. The chapter leans hard on this message (Beth's whole reason for stopping in the shed), and I'm not clear what the "request" is. Is Q asking to use her? Watch her? Investigate someone on the island? I can tell the vagueness is on purpose, but I couldn't tell whether I was meant to have a theory yet.
- **"Firm at thirty-one. Saturday noon."** It took me a beat to tie this to the Webbers Cove in Ch 01. I got there ("The thirty-one's mine"), and the noon clock payoff in the store is good once I did. It's still a hurdle.
- **"Then don't sit with your back to me."** I read the chair business twice. I think it's a trust test: she takes the chair facing the door and makes him sit with the door behind him. It's a good idea, but the line doesn't follow cleanly from "You're out of milk." I didn't see the logic until the next paragraph.
- **"Her father's fish house had the cove over its sill till Monday."** I don't know what this means for sure. Flooded? Tide over the sill, so it can't be used? The Ashby setup comes in two packed sentences while Ned is standing at the urn, and I had to rebuild it.
- **"Don't write me down." / "Ms. Calloway. No."** No to which question? To telling her what's in the log, or to not writing her down? I think both, but I wasn't sure.
- Who knew she was home last night? Ch 02 ended "Who knew you were here?" "You." Now Ch 03 shows Ned knew (she told him) and Tess knew (22:40 in the log). I like that Beth covers the page. But it makes the Ch 02 ending feel like it partly lied to me, and the narration has to step in and explain ("She hadn't given him Ned's name last night...") so I'd catch it.

## Where I stopped believing

- **The phone left face up on the table, screen lit, showing "Do not disclose to witness."** In Ch 01 this man turns his screen away from her with his thumb "as if he did it in his sleep." Now he puts it down lit in front of her and walks to the stove. If it's deliberate (he wants her to see without him having told her), the page doesn't sell it: "he shut his eyes for a second" reads like a man caught out. If it's an accident, I don't buy it from this character. Either way it's the most convenient thing in the three chapters, and the rest of the chapter hangs on it.
- **Dane leaves her in the store with Arsenault.** Two paragraphs earlier he lets his boat go at noon because "he wasn't leaving her in a room with the dragger's skipper." Then he goes straight through the curtain after the hooded man and leaves her in a room with the dragger's skipper. "Russ, the front" doesn't cover it. The page contradicts itself here.
- **Arsenault's exit line.** "Tess, this store's the heart of the island. I'd hate to see it have a hard winter." That's a villain line. Add the hundreds in Tess's vest in Ch 02, "It's on your account," and the laugh "too loud, the laugh you could hear from the fuel float," and Tess-is-compromised is no longer a suspicion. It's being announced. I'd believe it more if I'd had to work for it.
- "the only clean-shaven man on a dragger in the bay." How would Beth know that about every man on every dragger? Small, but it stuck.
- The consent exchange in the shed ("Do you want—" / "Yes." / "Do you?" / "Yes.") comes close to sounding like a script. "Under. Not over." is great because it's specific and hers. The yes/yes/do you/yes part sounds like a template.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Every scene ends on a crafted line.** "she watched him decide to sit in it." / "Asking because somebody will." / "while he told Portland, in words she couldn't hear, where she was." Ch 01 and Ch 02 did the same. They're good lines, but by now I can feel each scene lining itself up for one.
- **Small aphoristic button lines in the narration:** "The store looked up at the back of her house. It always had." / "Up the slope the yellow windows stood lit for nobody." Pretty, and too finished.
- **Tics carried over from Ch 01–02:**
  - The dragged left heel. In Ch 03 alone: the print at the steps, Dane's question in the store, the man at the propane cage. Across three chapters I've lost count.
  - Phone calls "under the open sky." Ch 03 has the yard, the step, "outside under the sky" in the store, and the porch "under the open sky."
  - Her fist in his jacket or collar: Ch 01 on the ramp, Ch 02 under the cable, and now "She took his jacket in her fist and brought him down to her." It might be a motif. It reads like a habit.
  - "Her father's": razor, mirror, kitchen, sink, chair, clock, blanket, fish house. The razor works. The pile-up doesn't.
- **Doubled "slow" in the kiss:** "he kissed her back slowly, and kept it slow... the breath went out of him through his nose, long and slow."
- **Telling after showing:** "she wanted it to be her thumb" is fine. But "He tapped it twice on the edge of the basin, which was what her father had done and nobody had shown him" is followed right away by her saying "He tapped it twice too... On the basin." I got it the first time.
- **Everyone quips the same way:** Dane and Tess share one witty register. "I went in like a man looking for the restroom" / "Can't I don't stock" / "Wrong's free, Marshal." Tess's shopkeeper-pricing jokes are hers, and Russ ("Can't, can I? No.") sounds like a person. But Dane's long, polite, self-mocking sentences are starting to feel built for effect rather than said.

## Predictions

2. The hooded man with the heel is Arsenault's, rowing out of the *Margaret Ann*. He's island-born ("like he'd built them," "learned before he could walk"), possibly someone Beth should know by sight.
3. The leak is not Dane himself. It's either Q's channel or Tess's log and net. Dane's calls will look guilty for at least one more chapter.
4. Someone comes to Ashby tonight or tomorrow night. The lit windows and the generator noise will matter.
5. The log ("Log daily") turns out to be about the case against someone on the island, and Dane is told not to tell her because she'd warn them.
6. Dane loses the Webbers Cove because of her and doesn't mention it.
7. They sleep together within two chapters, after the log comes out in the open.

## Listener pass (how this would land in audio)

- **The text messages won't survive text-to-speech.** "Pike: Q's request approved." aloud becomes "Pike Q's request approved," which is mush. "Firm at thirty-one. Saturday noon." sounds like nonsense without the page.
- **"over 22:40"** is written in numerals once, after "twenty-two forty" in dialogue. A TTS engine may say "twenty-two colon forty" or "ten forty." Either way it trips.
- **The store scene is a name storm for the ear:** Russ Whitcomb, *Second Wind*, Lyle Arsenault, *Margaret Ann*, Musto, Gloucester, Tess, Marr's, Ida Dyer, LifeFlight, Ashby, the Connecticut people, Ned. In audio I'd lose who is who around Arsenault's entrance.
- **The Ned/Ashby hand-off** jumps location ("Ned was round the side wall") without a clear shift. Heard aloud, "Ashby" lands as a word with no meaning attached for a sentence or two.
- **The consent exchange** has no tags on the first line. "Do you want—" "Yes." He said it into her hair. "Do you?" "Yes." On the page I can work it out. In the ear I lost who asked first.
- **What works aloud:** the long run-on paragraph in the shed ("Then they warmed, and moved...") reads well spoken, because the rush of "and" is the point. "Third one." / "Is that a rule?" / "It is for this one." is clean, short and easy to follow by ear. The razor scene is quiet and holds up read aloud.

## Best moment

The generator catching:

> "On the third pull it caught. It coughed and coughed and ran, ragged and then even, and the shed filled with the hammer of it, and she was watching his face when it happened. He grinned like a boy with a fish on. He stood with the cord handle still in his fist and his other hand pressed flat to his chest, as if he could feel the engine in there."

Then "Is that what you look like when something works?", and the man who has talked all chapter says nothing. That's where I believed she'd kiss him, and she did. Close second: "You're out of milk," he said, still pouring. "That much I can tell you." A refusal, an apology and a joke in one line, and it's the most Dane thing in the book.
