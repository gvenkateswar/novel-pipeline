## Flags (read before drafting)

1. **Suspect balance is this chapter's structural job.** After Ch 02 the cold reader puts Ned at 55–60%, Tess at 25% and Dane at 15%. Ch 03 swings the weight to Tess and keeps Dane live.
   - **Tess gets six beats from the bible's "Why the reader believes it's her" list.** Every one is true and innocent of the leak (CASE FACTS):
     1. She writes everything down. Her left hand is blue with entries, and the storm log is open on the counter.
     2. **She saw the flashlight, and she knew the net was a lie.** "Net had you aboard. Your back steps had you home." "22:40" is under her hand and in the log, and she kept it off the air.
     3. The keys for her Ford hang on the hook in her office, where Beth can see them from the counter.
     4. She calls Arsenault "Lyle" and laughs too loud at his joke.
     5. She puts his order on an account.
     6. She gets sharp with Beth the one time Beth looks at the log too long: "Don't you look at my book like the bank does."
   - **Dane gets the screen** (the outline's concrete reason to doubt him). "Do not disclose to witness," and a thirty-one he won't account for. Then the porch call after the kiss, seen from inside.
   - **Ned gets nothing new that reads as guilty, and no sympathy plants.** No hip, no swimming, no gloves, no coffee-as-apology, no "Bethie" lingering. He appears once, outside, with the Ashby key, and that's all.
2. **Staging change: Ned's phone (outline Ch 03 s2).** The outline has *Ned* hanging his phone on the charger under the key hook. Read after Ch 02, that is one more Ned plant. **New staging, same facts (CASE FACTS: Ned's phone charges behind the counter):**
   - The charging shelf in Tess's office is communal. Under the hook with the Ford's two keys, three or four island phones charge, each with its owner's mark (a rubber band, a cracked case, a Red Sox sticker). Beth knows each one the way she knows boats.
   - **Tess** says the line: "Only place on the island it's a phone." It's hers, and it's true for her as well.
   - Ned is not at the counter.
   - The plant stays fair: Ned's phone is among them, and the means still holds for the Ch 9/10 payoff. It's also equally true for Tess, whose own phone is in her vest.
   - Flagged for the orchestrator as a staging change with no plot change.
3. **Beth's reason for leaving Ned out of "You."** In s2, at the palm-flat beat (the book's one "palm flat"), she covers Tess's handwriting from Dane. One interior sentence ties that to last night's answer. She didn't say Ned's name, and she won't show him Tess's hand, for the same reason: they're her people, and you don't put your own people into a marshal's book.
   - It reads as loyalty to "ours," not as a clue about Ned, and it binds Ned and Tess into the same protected pair.
   - It also plants Ch 6's blind spot without naming it. One sentence; don't explain it further.
4. **Questions the cold reader says nobody asks. One line each, said by a person:**
   - **Was the bridge sabotaged?** Russ, at the store, when Dane asks about the cable. He asks and answers himself, in his own voice: "Somebody cut that cable? Somebody'd have to beat the rust to it. That tower's been on the district's list since Labor Day."
   - **Other ways off the island:**
     - *Aircraft.* Tess, as emergency management director: LifeFlight won't fly for Ida Dyer. It's the pilot's call, for icing and ceiling, and the island's EMT is staying with her. If they won't lift an eighty-eight-year-old with a bad heart, they won't lift anybody.
     - *Private boats.* Dane in s3, said once (bible note 34): any island boat could be theirs; he can't read these seas; and whoever takes her leaves a wake everyone can follow.
5. **The store scene is about Tess and Beth, with the clues incidental.** The reader felt the Ch 2 stop "delivering clues." The spine of s2 is the two of them:

### Scene 1 — The last shave
**Setting:** The cottage, Saturday morning (Day 2), first light gray, the storm still on. The back steps, then the kitchen with the stove lit at last. Sensory anchor: kettle steam on the cold window over the sink.
**Characters:** Beth, Dane.
**Want:**
- Beth wants to know what he did out front last night, and won't ask. In the way: she wants him, and she's angry at him for the glow under his chin.
- Dane wants to sleep and can't, and wants to be trusted and won't ask for it.
- The reader learns Beth's want when she reads the screen to his face instead of putting it down.
**Tension source:** interpersonal, live from the end of Ch 02 ("You."), under a warm domestic surface.
**Surface action:**
1. **Open (outline):** her boot set down beside the print in the slush at the foot of the back steps. The left heel drags. In daylight it's plain, and it's the man from the reefer. One beat, no comment.
2. **The shave (the longing, at length).**
   - In the kitchen he runs a thumb along his jaw. It was a one-day job, and he has no razor.
   - Without a word she takes her father's safety razor from the cabinet over the sink and puts it on the drainboard.
   - He shaves at her sink with kettle water and her father's mirror propped on the window frame, sleeves shoved up. He's careful with the razor because of whose it is. He says one dry, specific thing about it, about the razor or about her father's taste in mirrors, never "I'm told."
   - **Her interior runs long:** the line of his throat as he tips his head; his forearms; a man in her father's kitchen being careful with her father's things; that she is standing here watching it with the coffee going cold. It's a thought about him as a man, plainly. One small lie she tells herself to stop it, and it doesn't hold.
3. He goes out to the yard to call (under sky; she doesn't watch this one). He comes back in, sets the phone down on the table to pour coffee, and the screen is still lit.
4. **The screen.** She picks it up. Pike's message is open: *"Pike: Q's request approved. Log daily. Do not disclose to witness."* She presses the arrow once. The broker's line: *"Firm at thirty-one. Saturday noon."*
5. He turns with the pot and sees the phone in her hand, and he shuts his eyes a second, a man who hasn't slept. "Who's paying you thirty-one, and what don't I get told?"
6. "Nobody pays me but the government. The thirty-one's mine. The other one I can't tell you, and I won't lie to you about whether there's something."
7. **In her head (R3-5):** a protection log; case secrets; the things they never tell witnesses. It's a log of where she's kept and what they won't let her know. She doesn't read it as a log *about* her. One or two sentences.

**Subtext:** He looked after himself with her father's razor ten minutes ago, and now he has a list of things he won't tell her. She lets the razor and the screen sit side by side on the drainboard and doesn't move either one.
**Texture budget:** (1) the razor and the shave; (2) the screen. Nothing else.
**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** **Dialogue that opens the next fight, not a button.** "Then don't sit with your back to me." Follow it with **one action, not an image**: she takes the chair facing the door, the one he'd have taken. He sits with his back to her, which he never does, and she watches him decide to.
**Word target:** 600.

### Scene 2 — The store
**Setting:** Marr's General Store, Saturday, late morning to just after noon. The storm is on, the store is full because everyone's at the store in a storm, the urn coffee is free to the storm, and the store generator roars out back. Sensory anchor: the bell over the door, and the floor's soft give by the counter where the old sills are going.
**Characters:** Beth, Tess, Dane, Russ Whitcomb, Lyle Arsenault, Ned (outside only, at the end), and islanders in the background.
**Want:**
- **Tess** wants Beth at her house tonight, under her roof, where she can watch her. In the way: Beth won't, and Tess knows more than Beth wants her to.
- **Beth** wants to get supplies and a place to sleep without anybody on this island knowing where. In the way: Tess, who knows everything.
- The reader learns Tess's want when she says it straight out: "You'll sleep at mine."
**Tension source:** interpersonal, warm and sharp at once (Tess and Beth). Situational under it: Arsenault walks in.
**Surface action (in order; the spine is Tess and Beth, and the clues fall out of it):**
1. Dane asks the room who drags a left heel. **Russ, leaning on the counter, cap off and back on:** "That narrows it to about forty of us." Dane asks about the bridge cable, and Russ asks and answers himself (Flag 4): somebody'd have to beat the rust to it, and the tower's been on the district's list since Labor Day. Dane and Russ go out together to look at the road.
2. **While the bell is still swinging behind Dane**, Tess is sealing the morning's bag at the counter and talking the whole time: "Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's. I won't have my money in Ned's color." Said as housekeeping, mid-patter, and gone at once into the next thing. Dane is outside and doesn't hear it.
3. **The spine.** Tess puts both hands on the counter.
   - "Net had you aboard. Your back steps had you home, twenty-two forty." It's on her hand, and it's in the log, open between them in her writing. "Didn't put you on the air." This is the one time she says "Beth."
   - Then, fast and bright: she'll sleep at Tess's tonight, there's the cot, she'll feed the marshal on the porch.
   - Beth, four words: "Can't. Not tonight, Tess."
   - Beth's eyes stay on the log a second too long, and Tess goes sharp: "Don't you look at my book like the bank does."
4. **The palm (the book's one "palm flat").** The bell goes, and Dane comes back in. Beth's palm goes flat over the page, over 22:40, before she has decided to. **The loyalty line (Flag 3), one interior sentence:** she didn't give him Ned's name last night, and she won't give him Tess's hand now, and it's the same reason. They're hers, and you don't put your own people into a marshal's book.
5. **Through the curtain behind the counter, glimpsed (Flag 2):** Tess's office; the hook with the two keys for Tess's Ford on a MARR'S tag; under it the shelf where three or four island phones charge (she knows whose by their cases). Tess, to Dane, who's looking: "Only place on the island it's a phone." One sentence of seeing. Don't linger.
6. **LifeFlight (Flag 4).** Tess is on with the mainland about Ida Dyer, and it's no: the pilot's call, icing and ceiling, and the EMT is staying with her. Dane hears it, and so does the reader: nothing is flying anybody off this island. **The laugh.** Dane asks what her job is, exactly, and Tess: "Emergency management director. Since town meeting. I was slow putting my hand down."
7. **Arsenault.**
   - The bell. A tall, clean-shaven man in a good Musto jacket, the only clean-shaven man on a dragger in the bay. Beth places him by his boat (the habit, once this chapter): he is the *Margaret Ann*, the Gloucester dragger lit through the blackout.
   - "Pack of Golds, and whatever the gentleman's having." He counts out exact change before Tess says the price.
   - Tess laughs too loud at whatever he says next, calls him "Lyle," and puts his order on his account.
   - He gives Beth one line, kind, which is worse: he bought fuel off her father when she was about this high.
8. **Noon (the boat, no words).** The clock over the door gets to noon. Dane looks at it, then at Arsenault, and doesn't move. Beth, who read the screen, watches him let it go. One sentence in her head at most. No words between them.
9. **Outside, by the store's side wall, out of the store's hearing (R2-7):**
   - Beth asks Ned for Ashby. Ned is up from the boat for the urn, and this is his one appearance.
   - Her reasoning stays in her head: the cove's over the fish house sill till Monday. Ashby has water on three sides and one road. Ned starts its generator every storm, so it running won't look like anything.
   - Ned gives her the key off his ring. "Nobody but—" / Beth: "Me and God. I know." She says it, finishing his promise for him. No sympathy detail for Ned: no hip, no gloves, no weather.
10. Russ comes round the corner of the store, leans on the wall, and says it to the both of them.

**Subtext:** Tess is frightened for her, and fright comes out of Tess as orders and prices. Beth loves her and is hiding her handwriting from the man she wants. Every guilty-looking thing Tess does in this scene is love or money, and the reader can't tell which.
**Texture budget:** (1) the seal tag line, said in passing; (2) the log and 22:40. Everything else (the hook, the phones, LifeFlight, Arsenault's change) is a glimpse inside a beat, never its own paragraph.
**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** **Question, asked by someone else and left unanswered.** Russ: "Where you sleeping tonight? Not asking for me. Asking because somebody will." Beth doesn't answer him. Neither does Dane. **No closing image after it.**
**Word target:** 950. If it runs long, cut the Arsenault exchange (item 7) to two lines of his. Keep items 2, 3 and 4 whole.

### Scene 3 — The north road
**Setting:** The north road over the island's spine after dark, in the snow. The house lights along the shore are out except for the generator windows. Sensory anchor: snow squeaking under their boots, and the Ashby key cold in her glove.
**Characters:** Beth, Dane.
**Want:** Dane wants her to know why he won't put her on a lobster boat, even Russ's, so she stops looking at the harbor like it's a door. Beth wants to get there.
**Tension source:** the walk in the open, the two of them since the morning's fight, and nothing said about it.
**Surface action:**
1. She's walking fast. He says it once, because she's been looking at the boats all day (Flag 4, bible note 34): any boat on this island could belong to them; he can't read these seas; and whoever takes her off leaves a wake the whole bay can follow. That's three reasons in one sentence of his.
