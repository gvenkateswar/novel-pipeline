### Scene 1
**Setting:** Beth's house (her father's), on the rise above Calloway Cove, about ten in the morning, Day 1. The storm is up, with snow blowing flat and the light gray. Sensory anchors: snow drifted across the kitchen floor under the window she went out of last night, and from that window the harbor below (the ferry tied along the pier with its ramp hanging over the water, the lobster boats lying on the rocks).
**Characters present:** Dane, Beth. Russ waits at the foot of the hill in his truck (seen, one line, no dialogue).
**Surface action:**
1. **Open on the new line exactly** (flag 1). Then the minute he loses. At the bottom of her steps Dane, gun out, tells her to wait in Russ's truck while he clears the house and brings out what she needs. She's already on the steps. The exchange that loses it for him: "If he's in there and you walk in first, how's that going to sound in a courtroom?" / "It's going to sound like I did it." She goes in. He goes in ahead of her by one step, which is the most he gets.
2. **Fear before danger (the 15% slowdown).** He clears the rooms, talking the whole time in a low, plain run ("Kitchen's clear. Hall. I'm in the back room now."), and she stands in her own kitchen without a word. In his head: she's gone quiet, and he has learned in one night that quiet is how she's scared. The snow on the floor. The board he nailed this morning. The house is empty and stays empty. The danger is that he expected it not to be.
3. **She packs, fast, with her hands** (bible: what she notices first is hands, and she works). A canvas duffel, clothes, the phone in its zip bag. She takes her father's sweater off the hook by the stove and pulls it on over her own, and Dane has to look at the window. **Optional, if the words allow** (bible friction 3, owing): she gives him back his coat the moment she has her own on. "You're welcome." / "I didn't say anything." / "I noticed."
4. **Say it twice, inside the action, each line with a reason:**
   - *Dane's interior, watching her pack (the case, at its true size):* the truck was clean, the cold storage was hosed down, and everything Cole Varney's lawyer needed to lose was standing in this kitchen putting socks in a bag. Her eyes were the whole case. (Use "the whole case," not the capped phrase.)
   - *Dane's interior, at the window (the kid left Pruett before she called):* he'd been wrong at her shack last night. Nobody had given the man her name. He'd simply known the house, and the hill, and the window, the way everybody on this island did, and that was why she couldn't sleep in it.
   - *What he sees from the window, one sentence:* the ferry that couldn't run, the dock that was gone, and the island's own boats on the rocks. No way off.
5. **The argument he wins, under 100 words, at her front door with the duffel on her shoulder.** Beth: she'll sleep aboard the ferry, in her old bunk in the crew room, and keep an eye on the boat. Dane's three reasons, plain: a boat tied to a pier has one way off it; the man came by water last night; and the ferry is the first place anyone on this island would look for her. Then Russ's: the island's one policeman with a shotgun on the porch, a woodstove, and the only generator on the harbor road that works. And every house on the road can see it. Dane says that last part out loud as the point: "Let them look. Let them see Russ on the porch." (N2: this is a deliberate choice, made to her face. Ch 05 depends on it.) Beth's verdict, conclusion first: "Fine." She takes the duffel down the steps herself before he can offer.
**Subtext:** He has to protect a woman who won't be protected in the way he's trained, and he'd lose the argument again to watch her win it. She's walking back into the place a man came for her, and she does it first so it stays hers.
**Tension source:** Situational (the broken house, the gun out, the empty rooms) and interpersonal (two arguments, one lost and one won, both face to face).
**Turn:** He loses her house to her and wins her bed for tonight, and the bed he wins is the most visible house on the island.
**Longing (can't miss it):** Plain words in his head when she pulls the sweater on: he wanted her. He'd wanted her since she swung an oar at his head, and it was the least useful thing he could be thinking in a house a man had broken into. Then the window.
**Laugh:** "How's that going to sound in a courtroom?" / "It's going to sound like I did it." Her verdict on his habit, which also plants his habit for s3.
**Plants:** His jury habit, out loud (→ s3, Ch 13, Ch 17). Russ's house seen by the whole road, chosen on purpose (→ s2, Ch 05 "The whole harbor road knew where I was"). Sleeping aboard is ruled out for now (→ Ch 13–14, when it becomes the bait).
**Ending type:** Action button. She carries her own duffel down to Russ's truck. Dane follows with nothing in his hands and knows it.
**Ends on:** Beth going down the steps with the duffel, and Dane behind her, empty-handed.
**Word count target:** **400** (floor 370).

### Scene 2
**Setting:** Russ's house on the harbor road, dusk, about a quarter past four. A plain house with a porch facing the road, a woodstove ticking, and a kitchen window that looks out on the road and the harbor. Sensory anchor: the island's lights going out house by house along the harbor road, toward them. Second: the generator's flat roar when it catches.
**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Russ, Dane's boss (voice on Dane's phone, on speaker).
**Surface action:**
1. **Open in motion. No summary of the afternoon.** Dane is at Russ's kitchen window when the power goes. The store's lights first, then the church, then house after house along the harbor road coming toward them, until the window is black and so is the kitchen.
2. **The generator, and a laugh in Russ's voice.** Russ hauls on the generator cord in the shed off the kitchen, three times, four. Beth, from the doorway, without moving: "Choke, Russ." Russ: "Is the choke off? It is. Who taught you that? Your father. Who taught your father? Mine, and he never got it right either." It catches. (Beth tells him and doesn't touch it. Her own machine starts under her hands in Ch 08. Don't take that from her here.)
3. **The house lights up, and it's the only one** (N2 on the page). In Dane's head, plainly: when the bulbs came back, Russ's was the only lit house on the whole harbor road, and every dark window on the road could see it. That was what he'd asked for. He watches it work and doesn't like it.
4. **Russ takes the shotgun out to the porch**, cap off and back on. Russ: "Who's coming up these steps past me? Not him." He sits in the snow light with the gun across his knees, where the whole road can see him. (Don't let Russ say "Nobody." That's his Ch 05 lie.)
5. **Dane reports to his boss in front of Beth** (World rule 9). It's the night call she asked for in Ch 02. He puts it on speaker at the kitchen table. "Russ Whitcomb's house, on the harbor road. He's on the porch with a shotgun. Generator, woodstove, one door at the back." The boss, a short condition: "Call me at six." *Say it twice:* the boss or Dane, one line: the grand jury has to hear her by Monday morning, or the prosecutor says Cole walks. (If the boss says it, Beth answers with one flat verdict. If Dane thinks it, he thinks it looking at her.)
6. **The ordinary pleasure:** Beth at the stove with Russ's coffee, the first hot mug she's had since this started, held under her chin in both hands until it's cool enough. Give it three sentences, from Dane's side. He can't stop watching her do it.
**Subtext:** He has made her the brightest window on the island on purpose, and the purpose is sound. He's still the only one in the room who knows how much a lit window can cost. She's in a friend's kitchen, among people she's known all her life, and for the first time she lets someone else watch the door.
**Tension source:** Situational. The blackout is the storm closing in, and the lit house is a target and a fortress at once.
**Turn:** The island goes dark, and she becomes the one lit window on it.
**Longing (can't miss it):** In his head, plainly, watching her with the mug: he could still feel her on the pier this morning, both arms, and the breath he hadn't let go. He wanted that again, and more than that, and he was supposed to be watching the road.
**Laugh:** Russ answering his own questions about the choke and the fathers.
**Plants:** The kitchen window looks onto the road (→ s4). Russ on the porch, seen by every house (→ s4 when he leaves; Ch 05). Dane reports morning and night in front of her (→ Ch 05, 08, 10). Who knows where she is tonight: the whole road and the boss (the channel for s4, World rule 11).
**Ending type:** Image. Russ on the lit porch with the shotgun across his knees, the only light on the road, and the dark houses all around facing him.
**Ends on:** Dane at the window, seeing what the road sees.
**Word count target:** **330** (floor 300).

### Scene 3
**Setting:** Russ's kitchen, about ten at night. Dane has turned the kitchen light off himself so nobody on the road can see in, so the room is lit only by the stove's glow through its door. The generator roars through the wall. Russ is on the porch on the far side of it. Sensory anchors: the dark kitchen and the stove's orange glow; Russ's coffee left on the stove. Second: the generator, so loud that whatever they say stays in the room.
**Characters present:** Dane, Beth.
**Surface action (the first kiss, his reason, her answer, both knowing):**
1. **The approach. Give it room: the 15% slowdown goes here.** Dane stands at the edge of the dark window watching the road. Beth comes and stands beside him, close, and her eyes are level with his. He doesn't have to look down to fight with her, which is one of the things he likes. They talk low about nothing that's a plan: Russ's terrible coffee, or her father's oar, or how many nights he's slept this week. Every exchange has a line the listener can't miss. Dane's interior (bible interior 4, usable as written): he wanted her so badly he'd stopped watching the window, and the window was his whole job.
2. **She says it out loud** (bible flirting line 2): "I'm going to kiss you. If you've got a reason why not, you've got about three seconds, and it had better be better than a jury." Dane counts in his head, because he counts everything. He gets to two.
3. **The kiss.** She kisses him, and he kisses her back, hard. His hand at her jaw, hers fisted in his shirt, her back against Russ's counter. Real time, close in his body: she tastes of Russ's coffee, her braid is cold and her mouth isn't, and for once his head is quiet. Keep it to the kiss: hands on faces, waists and hair. It's a kiss with weight, and Ch 04 is the heat scene, so don't spend it here. **Consent is plain** (she initiates; he answers).
4. **He stops it, and stays close.** His forehead against hers, his hand still on her face. He says why, in his long sentences, which build to the point: the truck was clean, so she's the whole case, and if Cole's lawyer can stand up and tell twelve strangers the marshal was sleeping with the only witness, those twelve people start wondering what else the marshal told her to say. Dane's interior (bible interior 1): he could already hear the man saying it, slowly, in a good coat, to twelve people from Maine. The joke she set up lands: "It's a jury." (No "we can't," no contrast construction, and no Marisol. The reason belongs to this case.)
5. **Beth argues back, and it's flirting.** Use the lines as they now stand: "Then don't lie about it. If they ask, I say yes, and then I tell them what I saw. You think twelve people from Maine never heard of two grown adults in a snowstorm?" And: "A jury doesn't get a vote on who kisses me. I do. I voted." Whole sentences, conclusion first, no "please."
6. **Both partly right, said out loud (World rule 9, the one-talk sentence).** Dane: "He'll still say it." Beth: "Let him. I'll still say yes." Then Dane: "I don't have an answer to that." He tells her so plainly, without sulking, which is on her list. His interior, plainly: she was right about most of it, and the part she wasn't right about would come due in a courtroom some day, and he wanted her more than he had wanted anything in his life. That last clause is a short, undercutting one, in his shape.
7. **The laugh (outline):** Beth: "How many witnesses have kissed you?" Dane: "As of tonight? One. I'm keeping count."
8. **Both knowing (outline, and the task):** Dane: "Tonight's a bad night." He gives the reason plainly, because he never withholds from her: he's on watch, there's a man out there, and Russ is ten feet away through a wall with a shotgun. Beth: "Then tomorrow." Dane: "Probably sooner." Neither of them pretends otherwise.
**Subtext:** He holds back for a reason he believes, and he wants to lose the argument, which is why he can admit it. She has never asked anyone for anything, and she just took something, out loud, in front of the one person who'd argue.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Want against his job, argued face to face, both partly right. Underneath is the lit house and the man outside, which neither forgets.
**Turn:** He stops the kiss for the jury, she argues him past it, and the question stops being whether and becomes when.
**Longing (can't miss it):** The whole scene. Interior 4 before the kiss, the kiss itself in his body, and "more than he had wanted anything in his life" after.
**Laugh:** "It's a jury." (the setup paying off), then "As of tonight? One. I'm keeping count."
**Plants:** The cost of sleeping with the witness (→ Ch 13, the boss; Ch 17, the lawyer and the juror). "Sooner" (→ Ch 04, "You said sooner."). The lawyer in a good coat as Dane's private picture (→ Ch 17). Talk against silence is already present here: in the quiet, he's the one who talks.
**Ending type:** Dialogue button. "Probably sooner." Cut.
**Ends on:** "Then tomorrow." / "Probably sooner." Both of them know.
**Word count target:** **640** (floor 590). The chapter's heart. If anything runs long, cut from s2, never from here.

### Scene 4
**Setting:** Russ's kitchen and porch, one in the morning. Beth is asleep on Russ's couch by the stove under a blanket. Dane is awake at the table with his back to the wall where he can see the door and the window. Sensory anchor: Russ's radio crackling on the porch rail. Second: glass on the floor under her cheek.
**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Russ; Tess (voice on the island radio).
**Surface action:**
1. **The call, on the island radio.** Tess's voice, loud and in headlines. Dane gets the gloss from Russ, who answers his own question: "That's Tess. Who's Tess? The store. And in a storm she's the emergency director, which means she drives the one fire truck and runs this radio." (The first use of the term in the book, glossed in the same breath.) Tess: the Dyer house has just phoned the store. The caller says he's the Dyers' nephew, out in his own boat yesterday to check the house before the storm. A tree has come through the roof, and he's hurt and can't get his leg out (flag 4). Tess says nothing about Beth on the radio (rule: nothing private on the island radio). Russ answers formally, the way he only talks on the radio.
2. **Beth wakes at the voices** and sits up. One plain line from her that makes it believable to the listener, e.g. "The Dyers have a nephew in Augusta." Dane's interior laugh-adjacent beat: on an island of two hundred and forty people, everybody seemed to have a nephew somewhere.
3. **Why Russ goes, and why Dane lets him (safer option closed, one line):** a man may be under a tree on a night like this, Russ is the only one who can get there, and Dane won't leave Beth to go himself. Dane, in his head: a tree through a roof in this wind was the likeliest thing in the world, and he hated it anyway. (General unease only. Rule 12: no suspicion pointed at anyone.) Russ takes the shotgun with him. "Twenty minutes," Russ says. The porch is empty.
4. **The twenty minutes. This is the fear before the danger: give it room.** Dane talks low and steady, because the house is too quiet without Russ, and she lets him. Beth goes silent and listens, sitting on the couch in her father's sweater (talk against silence, bible friction 1; this is the chapter's plant for Ch 16). He realizes, plainly, that the generator roaring through the wall is the only working generator on the road, and it's covering every other sound on the island. That's the cost of the lit house, arriving.
5. **Beth: "Stop."** He stops talking. She has her head up, listening for something under the roar, and he can't hear it.
6. **The kitchen window blows in.** Real time, beat by beat, no cutaway, cause before effect (bible high-point register): a shot, and the glass comes in across the table. Dane is off the chair and has her on the floor under him before the second shot, between her and the window. Glass in his forearm, which he notices late. **Dane talks, low and steady, in the present tense** (bible afraid line 7, usable nearly as written): "Here's how this goes. You stay on the floor, I stay between you and the window, and you keep breathing where I can hear it. You don't have to talk. I'll talk. I'm very good at it, ask anybody in Massachusetts." Beth says nothing. Her cheek is on the glass on Russ's floor, and her eyes are open. More shots into the kitchen. The stove's glow is the only light.
7. **Dane draws and doesn't fire (flag 6), one line:** he can't see the man, and behind the man is a road full of houses with people asleep in them. He's going to get her out the back.
