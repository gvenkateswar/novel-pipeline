**Issue 1**
- Problem: The noon beat no longer says what noon is. "The call" has no antecedent: S1 never says Dane has a call to make at noon, and S2 drops "Saturday noon" and any mention of the boat, so a reader will take "the call" as a call to Portland. The clause also states Dane's motive as plain narrated fact in Beth-only POV, instead of as her reading of him. Ch 04's "Sold. Sorry." then has nothing to pay off.
- Location: "The clock over the door came up on noon. Dane looked at it, then at Arsenault, and stayed at the end of the counter, because the call meant going outside and he wasn't leaving her at a counter with the dragger's skipper."
- Suggested fix: Make it Beth's inference and tie it to the screen without using "thirty-one", e.g. "Saturday noon, the screen had said, and Beth knew the call that kept the boat meant going outside. He looked at the clock, then at Arsenault, and stayed at the end of the counter."

**Issue 2**
- Problem: Ned's "Nobody but—" is now fused to the sentence about the light, so it no longer finishes as a promise. In Ch 02 it was Ned's promise of silence ("Nobody but me and God"). Here it reads as "nobody will look twice at a light, nobody but..." and Beth's "Me and God. I know." answers a different sentence. The beat sheet and the pre-S2 text had the key handed over first and "Nobody but—" standing alone as the promise. This is plant 2 of 3.
- Location: "I run her every storm anyhow. Nobody'll look twice at a light. Nobody but—" / "Me and God. I know."
- Suggested fix: Put the light sentence before the key, then give "Nobody but—" its own line after the key is in her glove: "I run her every storm anyhow. Nobody'll look twice at a light." / He worked a key off his ring and put it in her glove. "Nobody but—" / "Me and God. I know."

**Issue 3**
- Problem: Ashby is never identified in S2, and the generator is never named. "I need the Ashby key" and "I run her every storm anyhow" leave the reader unsure whether Ashby is a house, a boat or a place. "Her" is ambiguous in a book that already flagged boat/Beth "she" confusion. S3 only explains Ashby on arrival, after the key has been given and the reader has already been asked to hold it. The beat sheet required a clause saying what Ashby is, and the generator.
- Location: "I need the Ashby key, Ned." / "Bethie." He worked a key off his ring with his thumb and put it in her glove. "I run her every storm anyhow."
- Suggested fix: Add one clause, either in Beth's line or in Ned's, naming the place and the engine, e.g. "I need the Ashby key, Ned. The north point." and "I run the Ashby generator every storm anyhow."

**Issue 4**
- Problem: Arsenault calls Beth by her surname. The bible gives him first names for everyone ("First names for everyone. Generous."), and surname address is Tess's tag. The line also has no clear addressee: it could be Beth or Dane, and Dane is "Marshal" or "gentleman" to him. Beth's "Calloway" is Tess's and the net's name for her.
- Location: "Arsenault took the cigarettes, said "Calloway," and went out into the snow."
- Suggested fix: Change to "Beth" and add a plain addressee, e.g. nod to Beth and say "Beth." Or cut the name and leave him leaving on a nod.

**Issue 5**
- Problem: Dane has nothing like a store scene's talk. His only lines are "Coffee," to Arsenault and the restroom line to Russ. He never speaks to Tess. S3 says he "had talked all day to Russ and Tess and the snow," which S2 no longer supports. The beat sheet also asked for Dane to be "talky in the store (he and Russ), with specific lines". The join with S3 is false as written.
- Location: S3: "He looked at her, a man who had talked all day to Russ and Tess and the snow, and now he didn't say a word." S2: "Coffee," Dane said.
- Suggested fix: Either give Dane one more spoken exchange with Tess and one with Russ in S2 (for example a line to Tess about the heel track on the dock, or to Russ before the bell), or change S3's clause to match S2, e.g. "a man who had run, talked and shivered all day."

**Issue 6**
- Problem: Tess's new line about Beth's mother is a new fact on the page that the bible does not record. The bible gives only "Her mother, Ruth, left for Bangor when Beth was nine." The timing is tight. Tess "came to Cutter at 22 to sternman a summer and stayed" and has run the store nineteen years. Tess is 46 and Beth 33, so Tess was 22 in the very year Beth was nine. "I put you to bed the winter your mother got on the Bangor bus" therefore requires Tess to have arrived that same year and to have been new enough to put a child to bed. It also moves Tess from "near-sisters" toward a mother figure. Both women already know the fact, so the line tells Beth her own history. The bible's rule is that facts change only with author approval, and the continuity log has no entry for this one.
- Location: "I put you to bed the winter your mother got on the Bangor bus."
- Suggested fix: Either cut the line, or keep it and log it for author approval as a new fact (Tess's first winter on the island; Alton at sea or ashore) with the ages checked against the bible. A safer rewording is a line that does not date Tess's arrival, such as "I fed you supper for a month after your mother got on the Bangor bus."

**Issue 7**
- Problem: The cot is placed in two places. Tess says the cot is "in the back room" (the store's) and in the same speech says "You'll sleep at mine." At the end, the two cot blankets she puts back "under" the counter are in the store. The reader cannot tell where Beth would sleep. This also muddles the beat that Tess's refusal lands on her.
- Location: "Cot's in the back room. Marshal can eat on the porch. You'll sleep at mine."
- Suggested fix: Make it one place: "Cot's made up at mine. Marshal can eat on the porch. You'll sleep at mine." Or say the blankets are for the cot at her house ("blankets for the cot") so the end image matches.

**Issue 8**
- Problem: The scene's space does not hold together. Tess "was talking before the curtain fell shut," which puts Tess and Beth in or at the office. Then Tess "threw the bag in the safe" (the safe is in the office), but Beth's eyes then go down "the open log" and her palm goes down on "the log", which is on the counter in the shop. Dane then "came through the curtain." The reader cannot place Tess, Beth, the safe, the log or the curtain at any moment. This is the beat that carries the palm flat.
- Location: "Tess threw the bag in the safe and slammed it. Beth's eyes went down the open log a second longer than they should have." and "Beth's palm went flat on the log, over the night's column, before she'd decided anything."
- Suggested fix: Add one movement beat so the positions are clear, for example "Tess threw the bag in the safe, slammed it and came back out through the curtain to the counter," and have Dane come in from the back as "Dane came through the curtain" only after they are in the shop.

**Issue 9**
- Problem: "Ned's among them in its rubber band" follows "Tess's truck keys" in the same sentence. A reader reads Ned's keys on a rubber band, not Ned's phone among the charging phones. The sentence also uses "Tess's truck keys", and the bible's MOTIF LIST caps "Tess's truck" at 1 book-wide. The bible says to name the pickup differently each time and the beats said "the keys for Tess's Ford".
- Location: "Going back in, Beth hung Tess's truck keys back on their hook above the charging phones, Ned's among them in its rubber band."
- Suggested fix: "Going back in, Beth hung the keys for Tess's Ford back on their hook, her knuckles among the charging cords and Ned's phone in its rubber band."

**Issue 10**
- Problem: The beat sheet bars any Ned sympathy detail in this chapter and specifically "no gloves" (the gloves are a Ch 02 / Ch 10 plant and read as a set-up for betrayal). "In his glove" gives Ned a glove again in his one appearance. Beth also took Ned's gloves last night in Ch 02 and nothing says they were returned.
- Location: "Ned was at the side wall out of the wind, a paper cup from the urn steaming in his glove."
- Suggested fix: "Ned was at the side wall out of the wind, a paper cup from the urn steaming in his hand."

**Issue 11**
- Problem: Arsenault's account is entered by hand. This puts a name on paper again, a retired motif for Ch 03 (the continuity log lists the name-on-paper motif as retired). S2 already has Tess's hand and the log doing the paper work, so this adds an extra instance.
- Location: "He left it where it lay while she wrote him in."
- Suggested fix: "He left it where it lay while she rang him up on the account." Or cut the sentence.

**Issue 12**
- Problem: A fact outside Beth's knowledge is narrated as hers: what Tess did before Beth walked in. The scene opens in Beth's POV on Tess's prior action, not on anything Beth could see or infer in that moment.
- Location: "Two cot blankets sat folded by the till. Tess had done that before Beth walked in."
- Suggested fix: Cut the second sentence, or make it Beth's reading: "Two cot blankets sat folded by the till, and Beth knew they'd been there since before she came in."

**Issue 13**
- Problem: Tess explains the seal-tag habit and the pay route to the one person who already knows both. "Your pay rides to Pruett in my bag every other week" is information both characters have, delivered for the reader. The line is mandated as the hook, but the explanation around it makes it read as exposition. The same pattern was the reason the earlier store scene read as bookkeeping.
- Location: "Your pay rides to Pruett in my bag every other week. Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's. I won't have my money in Ned's color, and I won't have you in a house I can't see."
- Suggested fix: Cut "Your pay rides to Pruett in my bag every other week." and let the action carry it: Tess holds the sealed bag up and says only "Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's. I won't have my money in Ned's color, and I won't have you in a house I can't see."
