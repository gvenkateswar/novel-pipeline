1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B the hood turns up at the propane cage in the middle of Tess's fight with Beth, so the scare cuts into the argument and both get sharper; in A the chase happens first and then the scene slows into tag colours and the storm log with nothing left to push it.

3. **Where A lost me:** ~39%, "You went down my back slope after him on top of the tide," Tess said. She hadn't come into the scene yet, Dane isn't in the room, and two lines later the same speech says "I can see your steps from right here, Beth." The next paragraph is Tess again ("Tess slapped the hand down"). For about three paragraphs I couldn't tell who was talking or who she was talking to, and Tess's voice, the best one in the chapter, came to me garbled. The geography is muddy too: "her back steps" and "Marr's back step" sit on the same slope. A's one gain is the thumb on the cold skin of his neck with Tess "look[ing] from one hand to the other". That's a better beat than B's turned collar, but by then the damage was done.

4. **Where B lost me:** ~30%, Tess's speech in one block: "Net had you aboard. Your back steps had you home, and a light going on them. Twenty-two forty... Cot's made up. You'll sleep at mine." That's five beats in one paragraph, and it reads as a briefing, not a woman getting angry. After that comes the dock-rail cutaway for the second and third time ("Through the window, Dane was back at the rail."), and by then it had become a tic. The voice came back at "No. My money I can count."

5. **Confidence: medium.** Both middles are mostly the same lines in a different order. B wins on order and because you can follow it. A loses on a muddle a reader can't get past, not on the writing.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The first and last scenes are word for word the same, and Tess, Russ and Ned talk the same way in both.
