Winner: B

1. Why: In B, Dane is in the store for the coffee, the chase and the restroom joke, so the couple shares the room. In A he is out at the dock rail, and I got a run of "Through the window, Dane was..." lines that read like a sentry log.

2. (Where A lost me) "Through the front window, Dane stood at the dock rail with his back to the harbor." at about 38%. It comes back as "Through the window, Dane was back at the rail." and "Through the gap, past Tess at the safe..." The scene turns into someone watching windows, and Dane and Beth aren't together. The rapid stage business, like "Arsenault took his cigarettes, nodded to her and went out, and Dane went after him to see which way he walked," also made my thumb speed up.

3. (Where B lost me) "Noon came up on the clock over the door. *Saturday noon*, the screen had said, for the Webbers Cove. The call needed open air, and open air meant leaving her at the counter beside the man who'd bought his coffee." at about 40%. It's a logistics puzzle I had to work out. B is smoother elsewhere, but this is where I slowed down. Nothing in B made me want to quit.

4. Confidence: medium. The scenes are about 90% the same text, and the generator-shed kiss at the end carries both. B just reads cleaner and keeps the pair together.

5. Same-author check: Yes. They're the same chapter with the same voice, and the middle scene is rearranged.
