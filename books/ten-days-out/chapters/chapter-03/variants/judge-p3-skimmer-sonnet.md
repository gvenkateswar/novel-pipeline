Persona: Skimmer. Judge: sonnet.

1. Winner: A

2. Why: A keeps something physical happening in every stretch (a chase out the back, a dark skiff off the point, then the shed) and ends with her standing against him while he tells Portland where she is, which made me want chapter 4 more than B's dropped sheet.

3. Where A lost me: My thumb sped up in the store, at "Dane had his coffee and his back to the hardware shelf" (~32%). It sped up for good at the ledger beat, "Net had you aboard... Your back steps had you home, twenty-two forty" (~48%), then through the Ashby geography paragraph, "The Ashby place was a big summer cottage on the north point that belonged to people from Connecticut..." (~58%). Dane's one-sentence refusal of Russ ("Thank you. I can't, because any boat on this island could be theirs, and I can't read this sea well enough...") is a wall of clauses and I skipped most of it.

4. Where B lost me: The same ledger stretch, at "Net had you aboard," Tess said, low (~45%). B's version of the heel-dragger is flatter. The man just stands in the doorway, then walks out, and Dane "didn't go down onto it." After that the chase turns into Dane explaining why he didn't chase ("One of me against a dragger crew on the water is a body count..." ~52%), which read like a briefing and made my thumb move. The Ashby paragraph ("a Connecticut family's summer cottage...", ~60%) did the same.

5. Confidence: low to medium. They are about 85% the same text. A's edge is the chase, the skiff, the stronger phone-in-the-pocket beat and the ending. B is tighter line by line and its lights-out dragger is a good hook, so a different tired reader could pick B.

6. Same-author check: Yes. They are the same chapter with different middles, with the same razor, ledger and shed scenes and the same sentences throughout.
