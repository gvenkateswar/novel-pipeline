Winner: B

1. Why: B puts Beth and Tess in a room with something at stake (the cot blankets, "I put you to bed the winter your mother got on the Bangor bus") and I cared about them, where A gives me a chase through fish houses and a key handoff that read like logistics.

2. Where A lost me: "The tide was at the top. The sea had come up among the fish houses between the store dock and the co-op pier, thick with slush, and the lanes between the sheds were half water and half ice." (about 30%). I was reading a map. It got worse when Ned, the key, and the cup on the woodpile came in as a quick errand before the Tess scene. Tess's speech then stacks five things at once: "Net had you aboard. Your back steps had you home. Twenty-two forty." I skimmed to the bell.

3. Where B lost me: "Below the step the shore fell away to the fish houses on their tarred posts, with the top of the tide in among them." (about 42%). It's the same geography lesson in a smaller dose. I also lost a beat when Dane is gone for the whole first half of the scene, and the Ned key exchange is tacked on after the milk. But the blankets going back under the counter at the end got me through.

4. Confidence: medium. The two share about 80% of their words, and the shed scene is identical. It comes down to the middle, and I'd bet B by a small margin.

5. Same-author check: Yes. The opening, the shed scene and the voice are word for word the same, and only the middle was re-staged.
