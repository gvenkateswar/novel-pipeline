# Chapter 03 Issues (draft.md, 2,047 words, in the 1,800–2,110 range)

**Issue 1**
- Problem: Dane flatly concludes "Nobody had given the man her name." Ch 02 final (just revised) leaves the leak question open: Dane says "Somebody still sent him before you called. So who told Cole you'd seen?" and answers Beth's "He'd have told Cole himself" with "Maybe." He only drops the office theory ("I was wrong about that"). Ch 03 closes the question in Dane's head one chapter early, and it also borders on the Ch 06 "somebody told" reserve.
- Location: "And he'd been wrong at the shack. Nobody had given the man her name. The man had known the house and the hill and the window, like everybody on this island, and that was why she couldn't sleep here."
- Suggested fix: Narrow it to what Ch 02 settled: "And he'd been wrong at the shack about his own office. The skiff explained how the man knew the house, the hill and the window, like everybody on this island. It didn't explain who had told Cole she'd seen, and that was why she couldn't sleep here."

**Issue 2**
- Problem: The beat sheet's say-it-twice item for this scene (the kid left Pruett before she called) is not on the page. The draft restates the clean truck and the stranded island, but not the plain fact Ch 02 established through Russ. World rule 6 requires plot facts to be restated in plain words in the next scene.
- Location: "The truck was clean. Everything Cole Varney's lawyer needed to make go away was standing in this kitchen, putting socks in a bag. Her eyes were the whole case."
- Suggested fix: In the Issue 1 rewrite, add one plain clause: the man had left Pruett at half past ten, before she ever called.

**Issue 3**
- Problem: The "Dyers' nephew" cover story says he came "yesterday." The call is at one in the morning on Saturday, so "yesterday" is Friday. On Friday the bay was already closed (the Marine Patrol boat left at first light, the ferry broke loose, the lobster boats went onto the rocks), and Beth and Dane stood on the pier all morning. Nobody could have come out in his own boat before that storm on Friday, and Beth works the ferry and would know it. The intended meaning, "before the storm," is Thursday.
- Location: "Says he's the Dyers' nephew, out in his own boat yesterday to check the house before the storm."
- Suggested fix: Change "yesterday" to "Thursday". Ch 04 and Ch 05 beats should carry the same word.

**Issue 4**
- Problem: Tess addresses Russ by his first name. The bible gives her surnames for everyone ("Calloway," "Whitcomb," "Haskell," "Marshal") and reserves first names for when it is serious, so the reader hears it. This is her first appearance, in her voice alone, and it sounds like nobody in particular.
- Location: "Russ! The Dyer house just phoned the store."
- Suggested fix: "Whitcomb! The Dyer house just phoned the store."

**Issue 5**
- Problem: The line that loses the argument has no speaker tag and follows a paragraph about Beth's action ("She was already on the steps."). A listener will hear it as Beth speaking, and then the reply, "It's going to sound like I did it," makes no sense. The beat sheet gives the first line to Dane.
- Location: "She was already on the steps.\n\n\"If you walk in first and he's in there, how's that going to sound in a courtroom?\""
- Suggested fix: Tag it: "Dane said, 'If you walk in first and he's in there, how's that going to sound in a courtroom?'" Alternatively, put the tag on "Wait in Russ's truck" so he is clearly the speaker of the exchange.

**Issue 6**
- Problem: Dane remembers "her arms around him on the pier." In Ch 02 final, Dane "took hold of her. Both arms, in the snow. She let him." Beth's arms are never around him, and she says "You can let go" before he does. His memory contradicts the Ch 02 image.
- Location: "He could still feel her arms around him on the pier this morning, and how he hadn't let go when she told him to."
- Suggested fix: "He could still feel her against his chest on the pier this morning, and how he hadn't let go when she told him to."

**Issue 7**
- Problem: The first kiss runs as a single paragraph of about ten consecutive sentences saying who is touching what. The HEAT RULES choreography limit is never more than two sentences in a row about who is touching what, then a thought, a sound or a line. The paragraph never breaks for a thought or a line, which flattens the beat the beat sheet calls "a kiss with weight."
- Location: "He kissed her back hard. His hand went to her jaw, and her back hit Russ's counter, and she didn't care, and neither did he. She tasted of Russ's terrible coffee. Her braid was cold against his wrist and her mouth was hot, and she opened it under his and made a low sound in her throat that he felt down his spine. He slid his other hand under the hem of her father's sweater to her waist. Her skin was warm. She pressed into him, hips and stomach and all of her, and pulled him in by the shirt like a rope she was hauling."
- Suggested fix: Split into three short paragraphs, each with a non-touching beat between them: a line from Beth ("Come here" is hers), a joke of his about the coffee, and the Russ-through-the-wall thought, all inside the kiss.

**Issue 8**
- Problem: Snow is drifted across Beth's kitchen floor under the window over the sink, which implies the window has been open since she went out of it in Ch 01. Ch 02 shows Beth sleeping on the couch by the stove in that same kitchen, and Dane at the table from four o'clock until dawn, boarding the back door. Nothing in Ch 02 mentions an open window, and it would have been the first thing he closed.
- Location: "Snow had drifted across the floor under the window over the sink."
- Suggested fix: Make it consistent with a closed window: "Snow had sifted in under the sash of the window over the sink and lay in a thin line on the floor." Or add the glass broken by her exit, with Dane's board across it.

**Issue 9**
- Problem: The boss says "Call me at six" at about a quarter past four in the afternoon. A listener cannot tell if this is six that evening or six tomorrow morning. Ch 02 sets the routine as "every morning and every night," so the time matters to the habit. The bible also requires times a text-to-speech voice reads cleanly.
- Location: "Call me at six. The grand jury has to hear her by Monday morning, or the prosecutor says Cole walks."
- Suggested fix: "Call me at six tonight." or "Call me at six tomorrow morning."
