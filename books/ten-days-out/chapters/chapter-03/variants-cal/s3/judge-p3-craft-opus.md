Winner: B

**Why:** B gives him a voice by taking it away. She waits for "the rest of it... a clause and a turn at the end," it never comes, and after that every small thing he does (the armpit hand-warming, the shrug, the hip turned away from her, the near-laugh) tells me who he is. A has the better idea, the handed-over watch, but it gets there through a briefing and a run of permission questions.

**Where A lost me:** "Somewhere below the hill the dragger sat dark on her mooring. A skiff could be off her by now... That was all the cover they had." (~17%). That's logistics where the kiss should be. I skimmed it again at "Beth?" / "Yes. Go on." (~55%), the fourth yes in the scene.

**Where B lost me:** "The set hammered at her back, stroke on stroke, and his hand kept time with it, steady as the injector." (~66%). The engine metaphor has been stretched too far, and "injector" is a manual word. It's one sentence, though, and the "Don't you laugh" exchange just before it carried me past.

**Confidence:** medium. A's "Nobody had the door now. She knew it, and she let it be." is a real turn, and a reader who wants the suspense in the bed would pick A.

**Same-author check:** Yes. The opening and closing pages are identical and the cadence is the same; one version adds the threat, the other adds his personality.
