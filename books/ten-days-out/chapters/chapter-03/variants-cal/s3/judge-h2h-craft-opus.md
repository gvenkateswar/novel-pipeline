Winner: B

**Why:** B lets the two of them sound and act like particular people in the shed (he goes quiet, warms his hands in his armpits like a man in the ferry line, turns his gun hip away without a word), while A runs a consent call-and-response that any couple in any book could say.

**Where A lost me:** "Do you want—" she said. / "Yes." He said it against her temple. Then, quieter: "Do you?" / "Yes," she said. (~52%). After "Where?" / "Under the wool." just before it, the scene turns into a checklist of yeses. Both of them lose their voices there, and my eye ran ahead to the phone in his pocket, which I'd guessed was coming.

**Where B lost me:** "She made a sound she had never heard out of herself, and the engine covered it." (~55%). It's the one stock romance line in a scene that had been making its own similes ("rough as new line", "a man working a knot he didn't mean to cut"). I slid over it, and the simile count after it ran a little rich. It didn't lose me, just made me blink.

**Confidence:** medium-high. A's interruption cuts harder because the phone stops them mid-want. In B it lands after she's come, which costs her less. But B's middle has the best writing in either file. "With him there was always a rest of it, a clause and a turn at the end. None came." is voice work A never tries.

**Same-author check:** Yes. The frame is identical word for word, and the shed rhythm (engine strokes, yellow windows, cold at the ankles) runs through both.
