## Flags before the beats

None of these blocks the chapter. Each is settled below. Nobody wins, loses, ends up together or does anything different from the outline.

1. **The public yes is built so the reader won't see it coming [plot addition].** The outline has Dane ask "Can I kiss you? I'm asking," Beth say yes in front of Russ and Tess, Tess leave, and Russ say "Well." The cold readers now predict that. **Settled:** the outcome stays the same, but Beth does the public part herself and does it first. Once the ham is in, she turns to Russ and Tess and tells them what is about to happen: he's going to ask her something and she's going to say yes, and she's telling them herself so nobody ever says it for her. Dane has no plan to ask anything right then, so he's caught out along with the reader. She makes him ask anyway ("Even when you're sure"). Then he asks, she says yes, and they kiss.
   - *Why it holds:* it's her Ch 05 move. She buys the condoms in front of Tess and two lobstermen because she won't be embarrassed (bible: "she says the embarrassing thing out loud on purpose"). It answers "She'll go." exactly: he once told someone her answer without asking her, and now she tells people her own answer, to their faces. It's also the end of her arc in the book's terms, a woman who has never asked anyone for anything saying out loud what she wants.
   - *What it costs her:* she says it to Tess's face while Tess isn't speaking to her. Tess doesn't answer. She gets up and goes out. The yes costs Beth something in public.
   - *Downstream:* nothing changes. Tess still isn't speaking to Beth (Ch 18 holds). The condition is in force for Ch 16 ("Say go") and Ch 17.
2. **"I'm asking": one use here [N11 against the outline].** N11 allows at most two in the back half. Ch 17 needs Beth's "Will you stay? I'm asking. I don't, mostly." (binding declaration), and the outline gives Ch 15 two more. **Settled:** Ch 15 uses it once, in the kiss line. At the cabin door, "There may be a boat out there. I'm asking." becomes **"There may be a boat out there. Do we go?"** He's still asking, and the phrase isn't spent.
3. **"Dollar says": the last use is spent here, by Beth [cap].** The outline gives Ch 15 her first bet. **Settled:** Dane makes a claim with no bet in it ("That is not going in that oven."). Beth takes his phrase and uses it against him: "A dollar says it does." It's her first bet in the book and it's the turn in Scene 1. **Downstream for Ch 18:** "Dollar says" is used up. Ch 18's bet has to be worded another way ("A dollar it floats till noon"). "I don't bet" is **not** used here, so its one remaining use is free for Ch 18.
4. **"Bethie" budget, a flag for the Ch 16 beat sheet.** Ch 15 uses none. But the outline's Ch 16 has two ("Alton's way, Bethie." and "I've got it, Bethie. Go."). With about nine used and a cap of ten, only one fits. **Recommendation for Ch 16:** keep "I've got it, Bethie. Go." and make the other "Alton's way. You've seen it on the door."
5. **Two outline scenes become four.** The outline's s1 (900) splits into Scene 1 (the condition, the two of them alone) and Scene 2 (the bet paid, the public yes). The outline's s2 (800) splits into Scene 3 (the search and where everyone is) and Scene 4 (the cabin, Ned, the cast-off, the light). No plot change. Scene 1 stays private so the rupture is settled face to face before anyone else is in the room.
6. **Beth spent Saturday on the rod (Ch 14 carry-forward, binding).** It's said plainly in Scene 1, when her taped hands can't lift the ham, and restated in Scene 3, when she goes down the hatch and puts her hand on it. That answers the listener's memory of the jam. Ch 16 may use the fear of it inside the ledges, with no second jam.
7. **The boss's line restated (Ch 14 carry-forward, binding).** The lawyer line was cut from the Ch 14 draft, so Ch 15 is its first full statement on the page. Dane says it in Scene 3 (= outline s2), in plain words, in front of the kid. Scene 4 restates it in one clause. No call with the boss in this chapter [A8].
8. **The no-escort line (A4), restated once.** In Scene 4, in Dane's head while he looks at the light, in one line.
9. **Life jackets over tied and cuffed wrists.** Getting a life jacket onto a man with his wrists tied takes a step, so Dane does it on the page as competence. He cuts the kid's cable tie, puts the jacket on him and ties his wrists again with a fresh one. He opens one of Ned's cuffs, puts Ned's arm through and closes it again. The kid is never "in cuffs."
10. **The cuff key (N8 plant).** When Dane cuffs Ned to the rail behind Beth in Scene 4, he puts the cuff key in her coat pocket. Ch 16 needs her to hold it.
11. **The handheld radio (plant for Ch 16).** Dane runs the big boat on the handheld radio in Ch 16, so in Scene 3 Russ hands him the constable's handheld from the stretcher. Beth has the ferry's own radio in the cabin for the Coast Guard.
12. **Stale bible items, overridden by the outline:** a crossing of "about six hours" (now three hours at best the short way [A17]); "the kid waits at the harbor mouth" and "the hand on the rail" (retired; it's the big boat); "Ned holds the wheel while she handles the lines at Pruett" (Ch 16 settles this); "Russ guarding Ned" (Tess has the shotgun). The bible's "Can I kiss you? I'm asking. You told me to ask every time…" is trimmed to the first two sentences, because the condition was set two pages earlier and the listener doesn't need it said back.
13. **Small fixes for Ch 14, for the orchestrator (not this sheet's to make).** The Ch 14 draft has "he tore the shirt with his teeth," which is close to the banned gloves-with-teeth tic, and "Russ lay gray on the couch," which is a gray face. Ch 15 uses neither.

## Scene 1

**Setting:** The town office kitchen, Sunday at about three in the afternoon. It's a narrow room with a woodstove for heat, an electric stove and oven on the generator, and one window that looks down the harbor to the white water at the mouth and, past it, the point. **Sensory anchor:** the cold, slick weight of the ham in Dane's hands against the oven's too-small mouth, the rack in the way, and the clove-and-sugar smell of the glaze he has already put on it. A second anchor: Beth's hands, taped across both palms in white, held a little away from her body because they hurt.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth. Russ is asleep on the couch in the front room through the open doorway; he's heard once, snoring, and says nothing. Tess is out at her store. The back room door is shut, and nobody mentions it.

**Point of view:** Dane.

**Want:**
- **Beth** wants to start again on terms that will hold, said by her, before anyone else is in the room.
- **Dane** wants her, and wants the ham in the oven, and has promised himself to ask her for nothing large until she's safe enough to say no.

**Surface action:**
1. **The binding opener.** Then, at once, the condition, in her words (binding):
   - "You ask me. Every time. Even when you're sure. And you never tell anybody I said yes when I didn't."
2. **Dane understands it, in one sentence of his long interior** that runs on "and," with the ham still in his hands. It's the answer to the town office on Wednesday, when he told his boss "She'll go" with her three feet away. It's the condition for something, and she hasn't said what. The short undercutting clause: he's holding eleven pounds of pig and can't put it down.
3. **Where she's just been, brought in at the moment it matters (one clause each, no past-perfect block):**
   - She has come in from the phone on the front-room desk. The ferry board has given her the boat for tonight [DC]. She asked them for it herself. It's the first time she has asked anybody for the job out loud since the beach, and he can see it on her.
   - **Saturday on the rod (binding, plain):** she tries to take the ham from him to show him how, and she can't grip it. She spent all of Saturday at the co-op vise with the steering rod, straightening it again with her palms taped, and she's paying for it today. Dane held the light for most of it. Say it in two plain sentences: what she did, and what it cost her hands.
4. **The argument that tips into flirting (binding).** He takes the condition clause by clause, seriously, the way he'd take terms from a judge. That is his respect, and she knows it.
   - Dane: "Even when I'm sure?"
   - Beth: "Mostly then."
   - Dane: "What if I'm sure and somebody's shooting at us?"
   - Beth: "Then ask fast."
   - He says yes to all of it. He doesn't make a speech. (Long sentences are his habit, and here he keeps them in his head.)
5. **The rupture resolved, face to face, both partly right (binding).** Beth gives her own accountability, which nobody has asked her for. That makes it the surprise of the scene:
   - "On Wednesday I said so do we. I decided that for both of us, in that room. Same thing I hated you for."
   - Dane doesn't let her take all of it. He keeps his view: "I was right about what's out there." His eyes go to the window. **The one light isn't named yet.** He just looks.
   - Beth: "You were. It's still mine to say." Then, plainly, the take-back: "So that part's off too."
   - Neither gives up a view. He still thinks the program would keep her breathing. She still won't go. Both say it, in one line each at most. Nothing is misunderstood.
   - **He keeps Ch 14's promise.** He doesn't tell her what he wants. In his head, in one clause: on the other side, he said, and they're still on this one.
6. **The longing (plain, Dane's).** He looks at her mouth while she gives him terms, and he thinks about kissing her in a kitchen that smells of cloves, in plain words, without "he wanted her." He doesn't ask, because under her rule asking means meaning it, and Russ is ten feet away.
7. **The bet (binding turn).** Dane tries the ham again and it won't go past the rack. He says, with total confidence: "That is not going in that oven."
   - Beth, who has never taken a bet in her life: "A dollar says it does."
   - He stares at her. She's never bet. She's taken his line and used it on him. One sentence in his head: in eleven days she has never once taken his bet, and now she's making her own.

**Turn:** Beth bets. It's small and irreversible: she has stepped back into the game they had, of her own will, and she has said why first.

**Subtext:** She comes to him straight from the first thing she has ever asked for and been given, and she asks for the next thing before her nerve goes. Her condition is about Wednesday, and it's also the rule she couldn't keep herself: she decided "so do we" for him. He hears that she's owning it, and he loves her for doing it first. He can't say so without breaking his own word.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal: the terms of a second start, argued with real stakes (one of them says yes for the other again and it's over). Internal: Dane holding back what he wants to say. Situational, at the edge of the frame: the window, and what he looks at through it.

**Ends on (ending type: action):** Beth takes a dollar out of her coat pocket with two taped fingers and puts it on the counter beside the oven. No line after it.

**Texture budget (2):** the ham against the oven (the rack, the glaze); her taped palms and what Saturday at the vise cost them.

**Plants:** the condition (→ Scene 2, Ch 16, Ch 17); the bet (→ Scene 2); "So that part's off too" (→ Ch 17); the window (→ Scene 2).

**Word count target:** 500. **Floor 460.** If it runs long, step 3's board clause shrinks to "the board had given her the boat," and step 4 loses the "shooting" exchange. Never cut the opener, the condition, "Mostly then," Saturday on the rod, Beth's "Same thing I hated you for," "It's still mine to say," or the bet.

## Scene 2

**Setting:** The same kitchen and the front room, minutes later. The light outside is going blue. Tess is back from the store with a roasting pan under her arm and the shotgun in her other hand, and Russ is awake on the couch with his leg up on two cushions. **Sensory anchor:** the oven's heat on Dane's face with the door open, and the rack sliding out in his hands. A second anchor, under the comedy: through the kitchen window in the last light, past the white water at the harbor mouth, off the point, one light.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Russ, Tess. (Four people with lines in one scene, which this book handles badly, so Tess has none. She acts and leaves.)

**Point of view:** Dane.

**Want:**
- **Beth** wants to win, and then she wants something much larger, out loud, in front of the people she's known all her life.
- **Dane** wants to lose the bet well.
- **Russ** wants to be entertained, and he is.
- **Tess** wants Russ fed and nothing from Beth.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open in motion.** Tess comes in with the roasting pan from her store, walks past Beth and hands it to Dane. Show the not-speaking in that one action. She sits at the front-room desk with the shotgun across her knees.
2. **The ham, by Beth's orders and Dane's hands (binding laugh; the argument tipping into flirting).** Her hands are taped, so she makes it fit with his hands. Her orders, short, the way she ran the closet in Ch 14:
   - "Take the rack out." "Bone end first." "Lower." "Turn it." "There."
   - He does exactly what she says, and doesn't move anything she hasn't told him to.
   - **Russ, from the couch (his habit: he asks a question and answers it himself):** "Is it going in? It's not going in." Then, as it slides in: "It's going in."
   - The oven door shuts on the ham with a quarter inch to spare.
   - Dane pays the dollar into her taped hand. She folds it into her coat pocket like it's worth more than a dollar. (Her first winnings. No narrated meaning beyond that.)
3. **The count (binding outline exchange).**
   - Dane: "The ham is eleven pounds, and there are three of us."
   - Russ: "Four."
   - Beth: "Tess doesn't count. She's not talking."
   - Tess doesn't look up. The two in the back room are never counted, and the page doesn't say so.
4. **The one light, under the comedy [A16] (binding).** Dane goes to the window to crack it against the oven heat, and there it is in the last of the daylight: the big boat off the point, out of the wind, showing one light, where it's been since dawn yesterday. One sentence. He closes the window. He doesn't say anything about it in this room. The listener has it.
5. **The public yes (flag 1, the surprise; binding outcome).**
   - Beth walks to the doorway between the kitchen and the front room, where both Russ and Tess can see her, and says it to them, plainly:
     - "Russ. Tess. He's going to ask me something, and I'm going to say yes. I'm telling you myself, so nobody ever says it for me."
   - Dane is caught completely. He hasn't planned to ask anything this afternoon. In his interior, in one clause: she has done in a doorway what he did on a speakerphone, the right way round.
   - Dane: "You just told them the answer."
   - Beth: "Ask anyway. Even when you're sure." (The Scene 1 condition, restated, used on him.)
   - **Dane (the chapter's one "I'm asking"):** "Can I kiss you? I'm asking."
   - Beth: "Yes. Come here."
6. **The kiss (romance obvious to a listener; give it room, 120–160 words).** Bodies, theirs only:
   - She can't put her palms on him, so she hooks her wrists over his shoulders and keeps her taped hands in the air behind his neck.
   - He's careful of her hands. Russ's shirt is too short in the arms, and the oven is hot at his back.
   - It's a kiss after five days of not, and it's in front of the people she grew up with, and she doesn't hurry it.
   - Dane's interior goes quiet as it does when he wants her (bible: wanting, he goes quiet). One short line in his head that is about her mouth and nothing else.
   - **No family, no Ned, no father, nothing of the back room in this beat or in the lines leading into it.**
7. **Tess leaves the room (binding).** She stands, puts the shotgun under her arm, and goes out the front door onto the steps without a word to anyone. The door shuts. It is Beth's cost, plainly: she said it to Tess's face, and Tess walked out on it. Beth sees her go. One clause in Dane's interior: Beth watched the door shut and didn't let go of him.
8. **Russ, from the couch, the whole line:** "Well."

**Turn (binding, the chapter's romantic turn):** The public yes. It's said by Beth, to Russ and Tess, before he asks, and then he asks anyway. The rupture is over in front of witnesses. Neither of them gave up their view to get here.

**Subtext:** Beth is doing in public what Dane did wrong on Wednesday, and doing it right: speaking her own answer, to people's faces, with the man in the room. It also costs her. ## Scene 3

**Setting:** The ferry at the stone pier, about eight at night. Full dark, the snow stopped, the wind dropping. The deck lights are on. Gloss **the car deck** once: the open deck where the trucks park. The lumber truck and the co-op's bait truck are still chained there. The passenger cabin is lit and warm, with benches. Gloss **the hatch** once: the square lid in the deck at the back, over the steering. **Sensory anchor:** Dane's flashlight on the underside of the bait truck, and the cold smell of old herring that comes off it. **Characters present:** Dane, Beth. Russ on a stretcher on the passenger cabin floor, and Tess beside him with the shotgun (Tess has no lines to Beth; at most one to Dane). Ned in Russ's cuffs. The kid with his wrists tied. Ned and the kid are seen and handled, and Ned says nothing in this scene.

2. **Where everyone is, said once (binding, the outline's list), each set off by Dane seeing it, never a list in a paragraph:**

## Scene 4

**Setting:** The captain's cabin, about ten at night, with the wheel, the ferry's radio, the front windows black, and the deck lights on the car deck below. Gloss **the rail** once: the steel bar along the back wall of the cabin. Then the car deck, and the pier, and the harbor mouth. Gloss **the ledges** once: the rocks just under the water on both sides of the harbor mouth, where a boat has to slow. **Sensory anchor:** the cold steel of the rail under Dane's hand as he closes the cuff on it. A second anchor: at the end, through the back window, one light low on the black water, and a big engine under it, heard over their own.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Ned.

**Point of view:** Dane.

**Want:**
- **Beth** wants Ned at her shoulder at the ledges, and she wants it on her say.
- **Dane** wants Ned anywhere but behind her, and he wants to keep the promise he made in the kitchen.
- **Ned** wants to be of use to her once.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open on Beth's ask, face to face [A23] (binding one-talk), at the wheel, no recap:**
   - Beth: if anything happens at the ledges, she wants Ned in this cabin at her shoulder. He's the only other person aboard who knows them.
   - Dane says he doesn't like it, and says why, to her face, in two lines at most: he's the man who sold her to them, and in here he's an arm's length from her wheel.
   - Beth answers it: he can't swim, and everyone he has ever loved on this island is on this boat.
   - **Dane agrees to her face.** It's her call, and he says so in a few words.
   - Neither is wrong. Dane keeps his view, and she keeps her decision. It's the kitchen's rule working in the first hour.
2. **Ned to the rail.** Dane brings Ned up the stair. He opens one of Russ's cuffs and closes it on the rail behind her. Ned stands where he has stood for thirty years, with someone else at the wheel. Ned says nothing, or at most one line of weather with no name in it ("Wind's going down."). **Dane puts the cuff key in Beth's coat pocket (flag 10, N8),** with her knowledge, said in one action.
3. **The crossing, plainly [A17] (binding one line), said by Beth:** in what's left of the sea the boat can only crawl, so it's three hours at best the short way.
4. **Dane on the car deck; the longing (plain, Dane's; kept apart from Ned).** Dane goes down to take the ropes off the posts at her call, as he did on Friday night. From the car deck he looks up at her window: her taped hands on the wheel, her face lit from below. This is the one place in the chapter where **"He wanted her"** may be used, or say it another way. Then the plain fact that undercuts him: he can't do one thing about it until Portland, and he's glad of the rule.
   - **Restate the lawyer line in one clause (binding, next-scene restate),** in Dane's head as he passes the lit passenger cabin window: the boy on the far bench is the only one who could put that lawyer in a cell.
5. **The ask at the cabin door (flag 2, binding).** He comes back up. At the door, before they go:
   - Dane: "There may be a boat out there. Do we go?"
   - Beth: "Go."
   - **"Sunday night or not at all,"** restated in one clause in his head: it's tonight, or Cole walks out at two tomorrow.
6. **The no-escort line [A4] (binding, restated in one line),** in Dane's interior as she takes it off the pier: nobody is coming out with them. The Coast Guard comes only if that boat comes at them, and then from an hour off. The Marine Patrol boat on this stretch has a hole in it, and the state won't send another into a shooting.
7. **Ten o'clock.** She takes it off the pier and across the harbor, slowly. She slows for the ledges at the harbor mouth. White water shows on both sides in the deck lights. The radio stays quiet. Dane stands at her shoulder on the other side from Ned, with the rifle.
8. **The image (binding).** On the far side of the mouth, Dane looks back through the cabin's rear window. Off the point, the one light is moving now. It swings in behind them, and under their own engine he hears a bigger one, keeping pace.

**Turn (binding, the chapter's plot turn):** The big boat is following them out.

**Subtext:** Dane gives way on Ned because he made a rule in the kitchen and he means it, though it costs him everything he knows about keeping a witness alive. Beth puts the man who sold her at her shoulder because she needs what he knows. Each of them is trusting the other's judgment against their own fear, and the light behind them is the test.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal: the Ned argument, short and real. Situational: the ledges, the dark, a boat that has watched them for a day and a half. Physical: three hours of crawl ahead.

**Ends on (ending type: image):** One light and a big engine, keeping pace behind them. No line after it, no question in the narration, no reaction beat. Ch 16 opens: "The boat showing one light came up on them fast…"

**Texture budget (2):** Ned cuffed to the rail, and the cuff key in her pocket; the ledges at the harbor mouth.

**Plants:** Ned at the rail and the key (→ Ch 16); the radio and the rifle in Dane's hands (→ Ch 16); the big boat behind them (→ Ch 16 opener).

**Word count target:** 440. **Floor 400.** If it runs long, step 2's weather line goes and step 4's restate drops to five words. Never cut Beth's ask and Dane's agreement, the key, three hours at best, "Do we go?" / "Go.", the no-escort line, or the last image.
