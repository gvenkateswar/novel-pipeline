**Issue 1**
- Problem: The upper deck is placed three ways. It is glossed as behind her and above her. Dane's radio line says "behind you." Narration twice puts it "over her head." The kid is also brought UP the stair from the passenger cabin to the captain's cabin in s5, but the upper deck is the roof of that same passenger cabin and is reached by a ladder going UP from the captain's cabin. A listener cannot hold levels that contradict.
- Location: "The upper deck was the open roof over the passenger cabin, behind her and above her, up a ladder, with a rail round it." / "The rifle cracked over her head, three times." / "Then came shots, a long burst of them, at the upper deck over her head." / "Dane was already talking down the steel stair to the kid, telling him to come up slowly."
- Suggested fix: Pick one picture and make every line obey it. For example, the upper deck is the roof behind and above her cabin. Change both "over her head" lines to "behind her". In s5, have Dane call the kid up from the passenger cabin only if the levels allow it. Otherwise change "come up" to "come forward".

**Issue 2**
- Problem: The big boat is put on both sides of the ferry's front-to-back line within a few lines: behind her, between her and the harbor mouth, and ahead, between her and Pruett. The listener loses where it is at the key moment.
- Location: "The big boat was between her and the harbor mouth." / "The light came up on her left again, between her and Pruett."
- Suggested fix: Keep one position. For example: "The big boat was behind her on the left, between her and the harbor mouth." Cut "between her and Pruett", or say that Pruett is ahead and to the left and the big boat is pressing her off that line to the right.

**Issue 3**
- Problem: The man's second line, "You talk a lot, Marshal.", has no speaker. Beth uses "Marshal" as her tease, so a listener hears Beth say it. The next paragraph then contradicts the scene. "Nobody on that boat had spoken before" is false, because the man spoke two paragraphs earlier. "Now they knew what he was" is false, because the man has just said "Marshal" and has already asked for "the girl and the boy". The beat is also narration explaining a line of dialogue.
- Location: "You talk a lot, Marshal." / "Nobody on that boat had spoken before. Now they knew what he was."
- Suggested fix: Tag the line: `The man's voice said, "You talk a lot, Marshal."` Cut the "Nobody on that boat had spoken before" paragraph. If a beat is wanted after "Everybody says so.", use Beth noticing that the man used his rank without being told, in one short sentence.

**Issue 4**
- Problem: The bluff does not hold up logically on the page. (a) The big boat has just watched her steer for several minutes: bend right, hold along the white water, close the point. A sudden "steering's gone" is not credible without a visible cause. (b) The stated reason they will believe it is weak and points the wrong way: "watched the ferry sit at the pier all Saturday with nobody aboard". An empty ferry says nothing about steering at sea. What they actually saw is Friday night's jam and swing toward the rocks (Ch 14). (c) The ask itself goes out on the open air ("Can I tell them about the steering?" / "Tell them."), and the page never says why that does not read to the enemy as a setup.
- Location: "The men behind her had watched the ferry sit at the pier all Saturday with nobody aboard. They would believe it. A boat that couldn't steer couldn't run for the rocks on purpose." / "Her steering's gone."
- Suggested fix: Anchor belief in something the men saw: the ferry swung toward the rocks on Friday night under the fire truck's light. Have Dane add "It just went" to the lie. Make the drift begin before the man answers, so the visible stagger is the proof. Add one clause on how the open-air ask sounds to them: a marshal reluctantly admitting a weakness.

**Issue 5**
- Problem: The reason the big boat follows her into breaking water, and does not simply stay outside or see that she is turning in on purpose, is never given. The big boat's aim has also turned murky. Beth's belief is that the storm will be blamed for a wreck. The man offers to "take you off before you hit". The only reason it hits is Dane's flat "It's very big", which is a quip and not a cause. Why a boat following right in her wake hits a rock is unexplained. The "second rock" language is also ambiguous (see Issue 17).
- Location: "Behind her the one light swung in, fast, to be aboard before the ferry hit." / "The big boat had come in close behind her, blind, and found the second rock."
- Suggested fix: Add one plain sentence in s2 or s3 giving the men's aim (they want the girl and the boy off before the ferry hits, so the wreck leaves no witnesses) and one that shows why it hits: its helmsman is watching the ferry's stern, and it is too wide to make the tight turn she just made. Dane's "very big" can carry that.

**Issue 6**
- Problem: The lie calls the ferry "her." Every boat is "it." The line also sets Beth and the ferry side by side, so a listener could hear it as "Beth's steering." The line is the hinge of the chapter, so the referent must be clean.
- Location: "Her steering's gone."
- Suggested fix: "The steering's gone." Dane's three-word shortness survives.

**Issue 7**
- Problem: Beth's question to Ned is purposeless as written. She already knows "the one gap" (she says she has to turn in at it), and Ned's answer gives her nothing. "You've taken it." has no question mark in a chapter meant for the ear, so it can be heard as a statement. The question's real function, finding out that Ned has never taken it, is lost.
- Location: "Ned. Where do I go in?" / "You've taken it."
- Suggested fix: "Ned. Is this the gap?" or "Ned. Have you taken it?" Punctuate "You've taken it?" as a question.

**Issue 8**
- Problem: Beth's plan statement contradicts Ch 15 and itself. Ch 15 has her say aloud "A bigger boat can't follow me there" (a safe harbor). Here the line "couldn't follow her in there" is followed at once by a plan that needs it to follow. The turn from "it can't follow" to "I need it to follow" is not stated.
- Location: "The big boat couldn't follow her in there. If it saw her go in on purpose, it would wait at the far end and finish her there. She needed it close behind her, and not looking, when she turned."
- Suggested fix: "The big boat couldn't live in there." Then: "But if it saw her go in on purpose, it would sit at the far end and wait. She needed it behind her, close, and not looking."

**Issue 9**
- Problem: Several narration lines catch up or explain what the page just showed or is about to show, mostly in past perfect.
  - "had watched" (see Issue 4).
  - "He had spent thirty years ... He had never once been inside them." repeats Ned's own "Never. He never took anybody." from 20 lines earlier.
  - "The big boat had come in close behind her, blind, and found the second rock." explains the crash that was just heard.
  - "everyone he had sold was aboard with him: her, and the boy his texts had sent after her" re-states Ch 12 and the carry-forward.
  - "His own people had shot out the windows where he sat." reports a shooting the page never showed.
- Location: "He had spent thirty years outside these rocks, listening to where they broke. He had never once been inside them." / "The big boat had come in close behind her, blind, and found the second rock." / "He couldn't swim, and everyone he had sold was aboard with him: her, and the boy his texts had sent after her." / "His own people had shot out the windows where he sat."
- Suggested fix: Cut the "He had spent thirty years" sentence pair and the "had come in close behind her" sentence. Keep the reason for freeing Ned to one present-tense sentence (the only other hand who can hold the boat). Show one shot hitting the passenger cabin in s1 so the kid's glass is paid on the page.

**Issue 10**
- Problem: The danger of the narrow way is explained twice in the same scene, and the "know it to the yard" idea is repeated.
- Location: "A boat that didn't know it would be on the rocks in a boat's length." / "It was a narrow way between the rocks, and you had to know it to the yard."
- Suggested fix: Cut the s3 sentence "It was a narrow way between the rocks, and you had to know it to the yard." The s2 sentence already carries it.

**Issue 11**
- Problem: Ned is not on the page in s1. He is cuffed at the rail by her left hand. The first bullets come through the window beside her, which is that same side. No reaction or presence is given, and the listener forgets he is there until s2.
- Location: "The side window beside her broke."
- Suggested fix: One clause in s1: Ned drops to a crouch at the rail with his cuffed arm up, or says nothing and stays standing. Name him once.

**Issue 12**
- Problem: Channel and audience. In Ch 15, Dane's handheld (Russ's) reaches Tess in the town office ("Here." / "He's asleep. Don't call unless it's bad, Marshal."). In Ch 16 the same handheld is Dane's link to the ferry's speaker for about an hour, and later the Coast Guard speaks on it. Tess would hear every shot, the lie and the wound on the same air and has no reaction. The listener also never learns why Tess is silent.
- Location: "The ferry's radio speaker crackled over the engine, and his voice came out of it, thin and close." / "The radio in Dane's hand spoke. 'Ferry, this is the Coast Guard.'"
- Suggested fix: One line saying the handheld is switched to the ferry's channel, or have Tess break in with a single word at the shots. A short Tess line on the air also gives a link back to Ch 15.

**Issue 13**
- Problem: (a) "Four aboard." can be heard as four on the fishing boat. (b) Beth never tells the Coast Guard a man has been shot, and the Coast Guard, arriving to a ferry with a bleeding marshal, leaves for the chase without asking. A listener would expect the injury to be reported.
- Location: "A fishing boat is shooting at us. Four aboard." / "We're going after the fishing boat. We'll come back and see you in."
- Suggested fix: "Four of us aboard." Add either a line from Beth that one of them is hurt, or a Coast Guard question about injuries that Dane answers ("I'll live").

**Issue 14**
- Problem: Custody at the pier is incomplete.
  - Dane hands over "his gun" and "the kid's rifle", but the rifle was last seen lying beside him on the upper deck and nobody carries it down.
  - The kid's pistol and the switched-off planted phone (Ch 15, Dane's coat pocket) and Ned's rope knife are never accounted for.
  - The kid's tie "through to the bench leg" (Ch 15) is never cut, but the kid "came up the stair" tied in front.
- Location: "The first thing a deputy took was Dane's gun. Then the kid's rifle. Dane handed them over without a word, holding his side." / "The kid came up the stair with his wrists tied in front of him over his life jacket."
- Suggested fix: Have Beth or Dane bring the rifle down (one clause in s4's descent, or Beth carries it). At the pier: "Then the kid's rifle and pistol, and a phone in a bag." Add that Dane cut the bench tie when he fetched the kid.

**Issue 15**
- Problem: "The first-aid box was empty." The ferry's first-aid box was left empty in the town office (Ch 14: "The ferry's first-aid box was in the town office, empty since Russ's leg"). The page does not say whether this is that box or a second one on the upper deck.
- Location: "The first-aid box was empty."
- Suggested fix: "The box on the rail was empty." Or "The wall box on the upper deck was empty."

**Issue 16**
- Problem: The kid's statement adds almost nothing new, and the stakes for the reader are missing. Ned already gave the September photo and its reason in Ch 12 ("who'd have the boat after me ... I told him you"). The interior line after it ("Cole had known she'd have this boat before she did...") explains what the kid just said. "Ned heard the lawyer named" is wrong, since the kid says only "Cole's lawyer" and Ned named him himself in Ch 12. The cut Ch 15 line ("the kid is the only one who could make the lawyer charge stick") leaves no stake for "I want that written down." That line also has no speaker tag. It follows Dane's line and could be read as his.
- Location: "Cole had known she'd have this boat before she did, and he had wanted her face for it. Beth kept her hands on the wheel." / "At the rail, Ned heard the lawyer named. He shut his eyes." / "I want that written down."
- Suggested fix: Cut the Cole interior line. Tag the kid ("The kid said, 'I want that written down.'"). Give the kid one new fact the book has not delivered, such as who sent the big boat or what comes next, or have Dane say aloud why this matters in one line: "Say that again for the record, to a judge." Change Ned's beat to "Ned heard Cole's lawyer mentioned" or cut it.

**Issue 17**
- Problem: "Second rock" collides with the "second" turn of the pencil line ("Out again at the second") a few lines earlier. A listener hears one object ("the second") and cannot tell whether the big boat struck the thing Beth just passed. It also lands after the third turn, so the order is muddled. Ned's line "Nobody's in," then "Nobody in the water," says the same thing twice in one breath. It does not say how a man listening could know. "It stayed up" is an idiom a listener may not parse as "stayed afloat."
- Location: "found the second rock." / "Nobody's in," he said. "Nobody in the water." / "It stayed up."
- Suggested fix: "found the rock at the second bend." Ned: "Nobody's in the water. I'd hear them." Replace "It stayed up." with "It floated."

**Issue 18**
- Problem: A pronoun fails at the cost line. After Dane's voice cuts out, "he" can be read as Ned, the nearest man, and not Dane, who is silent.
- Location: "If she let go now, everyone aboard went on the rocks, and he went with them."
- Suggested fix: "...and Dane with them."

**Issue 19**
- Problem: "Keep it straight" is used three times in nine lines, once in narration ("She kept it straight."). It has become an echo tic inside one stretch, and it competes with the one phrase that has to land, "I'm still talking, so I'm still fine."
- Location: "I'm still talking, so I'm still fine. Keep it straight." / "She kept it straight." / "I'm going to shoot at it. Keep it straight."
- Suggested fix: Keep the first, change the third to "I'm going to shoot at it. Hold your line." or just "I'm going to shoot at it." Cut "She kept it straight." or replace it with a physical beat.

**Issue 20**
- Problem: The Ch 15 to Ch 16 hand-off and the clock. Ch 15 ends at the harbor mouth with white water on both sides and the big engine heard off the point. Ch 16 opens in "open water" with the big boat already alongside, and no line gets her through the mouth. No clock cue exists between ten p.m. and "a little before four" except "about an hour after her call". A listener cannot tell how long the ledges took or how late it is when the Coast Guard arrives.
- Location: "The boat showing one light came up on them fast, and Beth Calloway knew in the first minute that she couldn't outrun it."
- Suggested fix: One clause in the first paragraph putting them past the harbor mouth. One clock cue in s2 or s4 (for example "close to eleven").

**Issue 21**
- Problem: Beth goes up an iced ladder and comes down it backward with a wounded man's weight on her shoulder. She has skinned, taped palms and a stiff knee (Ch 14, Ch 15). Neither is mentioned during this. Her palms are said to hurt only while pressing the hat.
- Location: "The ladder was iced, and the wind on the upper deck hit her full in the face." / "She went down the ladder first, backward, with his weight on her shoulder and his hand in her coat collar, toward the cabin where Ned had her boat."
- Suggested fix: One cost line on the way up or down (her taped hands on the iced rungs, or the knee) so the injury ledger stays alive.
