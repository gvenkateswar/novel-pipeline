# Plain read: Chapter 4, "A Disappointing Kit"

Draft read: `chapters/chapter-04/final.md` (with chapters 01 to 03 `final.md` read first for context). No other file opened.

## 1. Retelling

Beth and Dane run out of Russ's house in the dark while the man with the dragging foot shoots at them. She leads Dane along the icy rocks below the road. Dane slips, a shot hits a rock just ahead of them, and they hide under a closed-up boathouse on posts over the water. Lying there, they hear the man's steps on the road above: one firm step, then one that scrapes. That's his bad foot, and he's walking away toward Russ's house. They cross an open stretch of snowy shore and get into the net loft over the fishermen's co-op, a room where the men mend nets, which has no window facing the road and a door that bolts from inside. Beth hangs a tarp over the window, lights the heater, and tapes up the cut on Dane's arm. They argue a little about whose fault it was that they stayed in the lit house, and then she kisses him and they have sex on a pile of old net by the heater. There are no condoms, so it's hands only, and she falls asleep while he sits up watching the door with his gun. In the morning Russ finds them and brings her bag. He tells them the call about the hurt man at the Dyer house was fake: Mrs. Dyer has been away since October, there was no boat and no tree, her back door was open, and someone used the phone on her kitchen wall to make the call.

## 2. What's at stake

Beth is the only witness who saw the drugs, and the man with the dragging foot is trying to kill her before she can tell the grand jury on Monday the 23rd. If she doesn't testify, Cole Varney walks free. (I know about the Monday deadline from Chapter 2. This chapter doesn't repeat it, but tonight's danger is clear on its own: someone is shooting at them.)

## 3. The romance

Yes, I felt it strongly. This chapter is all about them wanting each other.

- "She wanted him. She had decided back on the ice, with his voice behind her in the dark." / "You said sooner." / He had. / She kissed him.
- "It's a very disappointing kit," he said. She laughed out loud, the real thing. That made me like them together, not just want them together.
- "for once in her life nobody had the wheel, and she let it go where it went."
- "His eyes hadn't gone to the bench in a long time... he had stopped looking at it at all." This one I only half-caught at first, but the page says it plainly enough: he forgot about the gun because of her.

What did **not** reach me was the scar on her palm ("Rope," she said / he kissed the middle of her palm). It's a small gesture and I couldn't tell what it meant. It's not needed, though; the rest carries it.

## 5. Where I lost the thread

1. **The ending.** "Somebody walked in and used her phone." I understood that the call was fake and came from Mrs. Dyer's empty house. I don't know what I'm supposed to take from it. Is it the same man who shot at us, or someone else on the island helping him? The chapter stops on it as if it's a big reveal, but I couldn't say what it reveals. The listener gets "someone did it" and nothing more, so the last line went by flat for me.
2. **Russ dodging.** "When did you get home?" / Russ took his cap off and put it back on. "Late." I noticed he didn't answer, and it felt suspicious. Is Russ hiding something? Also "nobody said anything about it" when he hands over the bag. I didn't know what nobody was saying. I don't know if the book wants me to suspect Russ. If it does, it's too quiet to catch while listening. If it doesn't, it's a stray worry.
3. **Line 182, the long single-sentence paragraph** ("The rope of the net pressed its squares into her shoulders... and she let it go where it went."). Read aloud, it runs on so long that I lost who was doing what partway through. I got the feeling but not the details.
4. **Directions on the shore** (line 40): "They were going east, toward Russ's lit house." For a second I thought the man was coming toward them, since they had just come from Russ's house. Then "A second shot went off farther back" put me right. That was a small wobble.
5. "First time since the boathouse you've shut up" (line 126). Earlier the page says he went quiet at the boathouse, so it should maybe be "since the woodpile," the line used before. That's a tiny point that only caught my ear.

## 6. Verdict: PASS

I can retell the main event (they escape the shooter, hide in the net loft, and sleep together), I can say what's at stake, no plot fact reached me only through a word I didn't know, and I felt the romance clearly. The one weak spot is the closing reveal about the Dyer phone. I got the fact but not why it matters, and Russ's "Late" may be planting a suspicion that a listener won't reliably catch.
