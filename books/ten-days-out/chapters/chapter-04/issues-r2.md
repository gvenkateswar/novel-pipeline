## Ch 04

**Issue 1**
- Problem: The bag routine and the envelope's description were dropped, and the plant ledger, the beats-r2 plant table and Ch 07 s3 / Ch 09 s4 still depend on them. Nothing in the chapter says "flap tucked" or "never sealed". That an envelope sits unsealed and open to whoever is alone with the pouch is the mechanism for the $9,000. "The slip" and "it" have no antecedent, and "the teller" has no credit union (the old text had "Credit union in Pruett" and "Tess bags them Thursday night, the pouch rides over zipped, the courier at Pruett"). The word "envelope" appears once, in Beth's interior, as if the reader already knew it.
- Location: "Who sees the slip?" / "Tess. Me. The teller." / "Who's alone with it?" / "The boat." and "Minutes ago a man's hand had been a foot from her head, and Dane wanted her envelope."
- Suggested fix: Add one line of Beth's before "Who sees the slip?" that gives the envelope, the slip and the open flap (for example, "Envelope from the store, slip inside, flap tucked, never sealed."), and name the credit union once.

**Issue 2**
- Problem: The stakes behind "Put my name in" are gone. The old text said the boat would keep "till June, and then Ned would give up her wheel and the district would hire a man from away." The ledger places "Ned gives up the wheel in June" only in Ch 04 S4, and Ch 10 s1 and s3 pay it off. As written, "Put my name in" has no object (for what?) and the reader never learns that Ned is retiring.
- Location: "An hour ago a man had laid his hand on that steel, and she still couldn't say *Put my name in* to him, with the hundred-ton license pressing her ribs from her wallet."
- Suggested fix: Add one clause that says Ned leaves the wheel in June and the district will hire from away, placed before the license sentence.

**Issue 3**
- Problem: The origin of the red seal was cut, and "Thursday's" now points at a seal she cannot have. The Day 0 (Thursday) fare bag went to the courier sealed at Pruett on Friday morning (Ch 01), and the island has had no mail since, so a spent seal from that bag cannot be in her coat pocket on Sunday. The old text had the credit union cut an earlier seal off a fare bag and send it back in the empty pouch. The Master flag already warns Ch 07 about this, and the draft makes it worse.
- Location: "came out with Thursday's spent seal, the red one." and "Thursday's fare bag. Fare bag's red. That's the boat's."
- Suggested fix: Restore a half-sentence that says where the seal came from (cut off an earlier fare bag by the credit union and sent back in the empty pouch) and change "Thursday's" to "an old" or "last week's".

**Issue 4**
- Problem: The bar and the Ashby-to-cove route no longer appear in Ch 04, and Ch 05 s1 treats them as known. The old S5 introduced "Ashby had one road out. Beth wanted a back door" and the bar. In this draft the fish house is rejected for flooding, and nothing says how anyone gets from Ashby to the cove. Ch 05 s1 then states the plan with "Bar's knee-deep at half tide", and Dane accepts it with no question. The reader meets "the bar" with a definite article for the first time in a line of dialogue.
- Location: Ch 04: "Fish house at the head of Calloway Cove's had the cove over the sill since Friday. Floor goes under at the top of every tide, and there's one door." Ch 05: "Bar's knee-deep at half tide, and half tide's twenty to eleven."
- Suggested fix: Add a clause in Ch 04 4.2 after the fish house line, such as "You can walk to it off the point at low water, over the bar," or add the same clause to Ch 05 s1's plan line.

**Issue 5**
- Problem: The fish house is described as a shed on posts. The bible has Calloway's fish house on granite blocks just above the high-water line. The tarred posts belong to the fish houses behind Marr's (Ch 03 S2). The same passage says the floor "goes under at the top of every tide", while the bible limits the flooding to the surge tides of Days 1-3, which suits Sunday but is stated as a standing condition.
- Location: "A fish house is a shed on posts. He's checking houses."
- Suggested fix: Change to a phrase that matches the bible, for example "A fish house is a shed on a rock. He's checking houses."

**Issue 6**
- Problem: The man finds nothing, but the shed carries fresh signs of use that the page lets him pass without comment: an open bleed screw weeping fresh diesel to within an inch of his boot, a wrench taken off the fitting (never placed afterward), and the emptied fuel can. F2 option (a) depends on his crossing Ashby off. Beth's "Every shed on the island stank of diesel" covers the smell and nothing else. A reader who notices the puddle will ask why he does not read it.
- Location: "His boot stood an inch from the edge of the diesel." and "He looked at the dark behind the housing."
- Suggested fix: Give the wrench and the can a place out of his line (under her body, behind the housing) in one clause, and note that the puddle runs toward the sill in shadow.

**Issue 7**
- Problem: The one-clause explanation for no tracks contradicts Ch 03. Ch 03 S3 puts snow on Ashby on Saturday night ("stood on the step beside him in the snow", "his breath go up and come apart in the snow"). The new chapter says no snow lay on the point at all on Sunday afternoon, with no overnight event to explain it.
- Location: "The gale off three sides of water had scoured the point to frozen gravel and ledge; no snow lay on it to hold a track, theirs or his."
- Suggested fix: Make it overnight ("had scoured the snow off the point overnight") so the change from Ch 03 S3 is an event on the page.

**Issue 8**
- Problem: Ned acquires two small behaviours that read as tells in a chapter whose rule is "no new Ned tell" and whose F5 says "Ned's light is not a signal." His beam "lay across the two of them in the shed door" and then goes toward the harbor, which looks like a light shown at the shed to anyone watching the north point. He also stands "with his nose up" in the door, a sniffing gesture with the man's oilskin-and-outboard smell still in the shed.
- Location: "stood in the door with his nose up." and "The beam swung up once and lay across the two of them in the shed door, then went on toward the harbor."
- Suggested fix: Cut "with his nose up" and word the look-back as a single arc toward them that does not rest on the shed door (the old "a single arc" worked).

**Issue 9**
- Problem: Dane says "Down" to two people who are already lying flat. Beth used "Down" for Russ a few lines earlier, and the context makes this order hard to read: down the hill, or flatter?
- Location: "\"Down,\" he said. \"Now. That deck has binoculars too.\""
- Suggested fix: Change to "Off the top. Now. That deck has binoculars too."

**Issue 10**
- Problem: A sentence fragment arrives before the action it describes. "Leather between teeth." precedes "He pulled his glove off", so the reader sees the teeth before the glove and can take it for a description of the man's face.
- Location: "Leather between teeth. He pulled his glove off and laid his bare hand on the housing, a foot above her head."
- Suggested fix: Reorder to "He pulled his glove off with his teeth and laid his bare hand on the housing, a foot above her head."

**Issue 11**
- Problem: Plants the ledger lists as placed in Ch 04 are no longer on the page, and the ledger still carries them as open or paid. (a) "Picnic boat ... Paid (the Ruthie C, Ch 04 S3)": the draft never mentions a summer man or a picnic boat, so the Ch 01 plant is unpaid, and the draft never says the *Ruthie C* is a boat. (b) "Tess asked Russ twice where Beth sleeps; wrote it on her hand → Ch 05 (the fight)": it is gone, and Ch 05 does not use it. (c) Dane's three reasons for refusing private boats (bible rule 34: any boat could be theirs; he cannot assess an island boat in those seas; a witness who leaves on a neighbour's boat leaves a trail) are down to two.
- Location: "She gave him the *Ruthie C*. When the bank took her, her father signed every place the bank man's pen showed him" and "Any boat here could be theirs, and a wake across that bay leads straight to her," Dane said.
- Suggested fix: Update the ledger (picnic boat open; Tess/Russ plant retired); add "a summer man's picnic boat now" to the *Ruthie C* paragraph only if the pie ending can be kept.

**Issue 12**
- Problem: Russ's lines lose his bible signatures. He is meant to say "somebody" for gossip and to ask a question and answer it himself. His first long line contains neither, so only "I'm not a complicated man" is recognisably his.
- Location: "Saw the rowing from my kitchen," Russ said. "Came up for a look. After breakfast I'll take the *Second Wind* out and ask the *Margaret Ann* what she wants with a skiff."
- Suggested fix: Put "somebody" into his first line ("Somebody rowed out to that dragger at sunup").

## Ch 05 (with the seven edits)

**Issue 13**
- Problem: Nothing in Ch 05 carries "the night with no heat". Ch 04 ends on Dane's unanswered "Do we run it?", and the beats say Ch 05 opens on the answer. Edit 1 explains "You were blue an hour ago" only by a squall. The page never says they went without heat on Sunday night, and never says why the generator was not run after Ned's two cans were poured. The hum that rises from the floor on Monday night is the first sign the set ran.
- Location: "Her jeans hung steaming on the stove rail, soaked to the thigh by the squall that had caught her on the shore at first light, and the wet wool cooked." and "\"You were blue an hour ago.\" He put on another split and shut the door on it. \"It stays lit till you aren't.\""
- Suggested fix: Add a clause to the first sentence or to Dane's line that says the generator stayed off all night and the house had no heat.

**Issue 14**
- Problem: "Soaked to the thigh by the squall" reads as a wade, not rain. Rain soaks from the shoulders down, and a thigh-high line is a water line. The bar wade was cut, so the sentence leaves a trace of it. The page also does not say where Dane was while she stood on the shore at first light with the hooded man active.
- Location: "soaked to the thigh by the squall that had caught her on the shore at first light"
- Suggested fix: Change to "soaked through by the squall" or to spray ("soaked by the squall and the spray off the ledge").

**Issue 15**
- Problem: Edits 3 and 4 give Beth two incompatible kinds of knowledge. In the kitchen she states the bar's half-tide depth as fact. At the water she has "walked this bar dry since she was six and never once with water on it", and then finds the gut's lip by foot as a discovery. The bible has the gut waist-deep at low water and a stride-inside route, so a woman who has walked the bar since six would already know where the gut drops.
- Location: "Bar's knee-deep at half tide, and half tide's twenty to eleven." and "She had walked this bar dry since she was six and never once with water on it." and "Then her left foot came down on ledge, hard and square under the weed, and past the edge of it found nothing at all."
- Suggested fix: Make the kitchen line a reckoning ("Bar'll be knee-deep at half tide, by the table") and make the discovery where the lip runs under water, not that the lip exists.

**Issue 16**
- Problem: Tess's line says Ned has not left the boat, while the bible's CASE FACTS (and F2's option (a), which makes the Monday noon call "more necessary") have Ned on the store's back step at noon on Monday giving up Ashby. Two hours later Tess says he has not come off the boat and asks whether he "comes up" for his phone, which sits at the store. This is older text, not one of the seven edits, but F2 now leans on the noon visit.
- Location: "He hasn't come off that boat since he hauled two cans of diesel up the north road. I carry it down."
- Suggested fix: Change "He hasn't come off that boat" to "He hasn't come up to the store for his own phone", so that both she and the noon call can be true.
