## Where I got bored

- **Russ's middle paragraphs.** "Who's emergency management on this island? Tess is. Since town meeting. Slow putting her hand down. She had LifeFlight on the line yesterday..." Here I stopped watching people and started taking notes. Russ isn't talking to anyone. He's reading out a file on Tess: emergency management, LifeFlight, the only working phone, asked where Beth sleeps twice, wrote it on her hand. Each item is a clue, and they come one after another until the scene turns into a list.
- **The pouch questions.** "Who sees the slip?" / "Tess. Me. The teller." / "Flap's never sealed? Not once?" / "Never." / "And between Tess and the courier, who's alone with it?" It's a deposition. I could see exactly what Dane was doing, so the back-and-forth took longer than my understanding did. The red tag rescued it.
- Mild drift in the Ned scene between "Bit higher" and the license paragraph. Pouring diesel by flashlight isn't much to hold onto.

## Where I got confused

- **Where and when does the chapter open?** We're flat on granite in blueberry with binoculars, and I don't know how we got up there, what time it is, or which hill. Later Russ says "at sunup", so I worked out it was Sunday morning. The chapter never says Sunday. I had to count back from "Until yesterday" and "a marshal, Friday."
- **"A man on her afterdeck took his painter."** Whose afterdeck? The last "her" in my head was Beth. I read it twice before I took it as the *Margaret Ann*. Then "So he had a boat now" confused me again. He's had a skiff since Chapter One.
- **Who says the "Who's emergency management" paragraph?** It follows Russ's line with a paragraph break and no tag, then a third untagged paragraph ("And she asked me where you were sleeping"). For a moment I thought Beth or Dane had taken over.
- **How does Dane know Russ will say "somebody"?** He's met Russ once, in a store, soaked. It's a good joke, but it makes Dane seem to know the island better than the book has let him.
- **Geography of the bar.** "From the Ashby shore toward Calloway Cove... hers end to end." Is Calloway Cove where her house is? I thought her house was on the hill over the harbor. I couldn't picture where the back door leads.
- **"The only one on the island that works."** Tess's phone is the only working phone? Beth called the trooper from her own wall phone in Chapter One, and Dane's cell works under open sky. I took it to mean the power-out had killed the landlines, but I had to guess.

## Where I stopped believing

- **It's Monday, and nobody mentions the grand jury.** Chapter Two said: "the grand jury sits Monday. It's the last sitting before Pelletier walks." That was the clock. Now "Monday came in between squalls" and Beth is scouting a tidal bar with no word about it. Did Pelletier walk? Did the sitting move? Does Dane care? This is the biggest problem in the chapter for me. Either the stakes evaporated without anyone noticing, or the book forgot them. I can't tell which.
- **Dane tells Russ the feds want him off the *Margaret Ann*.** His orders say "Observe only." He then tells the most talkative man we've met that federal people are interested in that boat. By lunch the whole island will know. For someone this careful with screens, it's a strange slip, and the book doesn't treat it as one.
- **The hood searching her house in broad daylight**, front and back, unhurried, on an island where "the two yellow windows at the bend" watch everything. Then he pushes his hood back and shows his face to the hill. Either he knows nobody will talk, or he's being shown off on purpose. I'd believe either, but nobody in the scene reacts to how brazen it is.
- **Wading to the waist in November** to scout a back door, then standing around talking in wet clothes. She's a pro and I mostly bought it. But "Ninety seconds, if you don't stop" and then walking back to a cold house is a hypothermia setup the book doesn't seem worried about.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Dane's narrating voice has become a set piece.** He narrates the kitchen through the binoculars ("He's crossing your window, he's back again...") and then the bar ("You're in the dark part... you're swearing at Connecticut, which seems fair to me"). It already happened with the cable and behind the brick in Chapter Two. Four times now, the same device: low, even, running commentary to calm her. It was charming the first time. By the fourth I could predict the rhythm.
- **Dane's tics are tics now.** "Okay." to start a line (twice here, several times earlier). "I'd like it noted" / "For the record" (both here, both in Chapter One). "Talk to me" (here and Chapter Three). He sounds like a man with three bits.
- **Repeated phrasing across chapters.** "with his whole face" (Ch 1: watching the boat; Ch 4: listening turned up from the floor). "'Beth,' he said into her hair" (Ch 3 had "against her mouth" and "into her hair"). "low and even" / "low and steady" for his voice. "'Him,' Beth said." word for word, Chapter Three and Chapter Four.
- **Ned's exit gesture again.** He turns at the foot of the drive and lifts the light. Chapter One: he turns and lifts the hand. Chapter Two: he turns and nods. It's the same exit three times.
- **Every scene ends on a closing beat.** "and he didn't clear it." / Russ taking the other path / "Close it... The red tag rode on his wrist above the wrench." / Ned's lifted light / "And you stay inside where I put you." Each one sounds like a final line. Together they make the chapter feel assembled.
- **"Then save up."** A good line, but it's too finished, the comeback a screenwriter gives her.
- **Recycled backstory.** The *Ruthie C* paragraph is strong. But "man with a box of folders, polite for an afternoon" and "picnic boat" retell Chapter One's "a man in a good coat... in an afternoon, in blue pen" and "Picnic boat." Told twice, the second telling lands softer.

## What I want to know next

- **Who taught the hood the latch?** Friday night he lifted and stuck. Sunday he opens it on the first try. Somebody showed him, or he practiced. Beth only showed Dane. This is my biggest question.
- **"As if somebody had told him where."** He looked at the top of the hill, where they were lying. Who knew they were up there?
- **What is Q's request?** "Log daily. Do not disclose to witness." Now Dane is asking Beth about money, debts and the pouch. I suspect the log is about *her*.
- **The pouch.** The flap is never sealed. Tess carries hundreds in her vest. The boat is alone with the bag. Is the ferry moving cash, with Beth as the carrier?
- **Did Pelletier walk?** (See above. I want to know, and I'm a little annoyed I have to ask.)
- **The back door across the bar.** She's planning a night escape at half tide. From what, and when?
- **Will she say "Put my name in"?** It's a new want, and I'd like it to matter.
- **"A not yet. One I can't afford."** What makes him unable to afford it: the log, the job, or something he's done?

## Listener pass (heard as audio)

- "A man on her afterdeck took his painter" will lose a listener completely. With no eyes to reread, "her" is Beth.
- Russ's three untagged paragraphs need a voice actor to carry them. In a flat TTS read, "Who's emergency management on this island? Tess is." will sound like someone else answering.
- The phone screens (*Pike: Q says hands off...*, *Sold. Sorry.*) work aloud only if the reader shifts tone. "Pike" and "Q" are thin, near-identical sounds as names, and I'd mix them up by ear.
- "Is this going in your log? It was in her mouth, ready." The italic thought disappears in audio. It may sound like she said it.
- "She handed Dane the glasses." A listener may hear eyeglasses for a beat.
- Dane's long commentary runs ("Okay. He's in your kitchen... It isn't going to take him long.") are tiring aloud. They're one breathless sentence each time.
- These hold up well aloud: the *Ruthie C* paragraph, the embrace after the bar ("from the waist down... from the waist up there was him"), and "Son of a bitch," she told it.
