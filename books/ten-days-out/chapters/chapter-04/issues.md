# Chapter 04 Issues (draft.md)

**Issue 1**
- Problem: The heat scene's central change depends on Dane having "talked the whole length of the shore," and Beth calls it "the first time all night" he has shut up. The page contradicts both. Dane speaks only four short lines in s1, goes quiet under the boathouse, stays silent across the open stretch, and Beth puts her hand over his mouth in the dark loft. The setup is told, not shown, and Beth's line is wrong by the chapter's own account.
- Location: "He had talked the whole length of the shore. Now he had her face in both hands, and he said nothing at all." / "\"First time all night you've shut up,\" she said." (contradicted by s1: "Dane went quiet for the first time since the woodpile.")
- Suggested fix: Put Dane's low, steady talk on the page across the open stretch by the church and again in the pitch-dark loft (two or three more short present-tense lines each), and change Beth's line to something true, such as "First time since the shore you've shut up."

**Issue 2**
- Problem: Dane's reason for not firing, which the reader already has from Ch 03 ("He couldn't see the man, and behind him was a road of houses full of sleeping people"), is restated in s1 narration. Dane then explains it again to Beth in the s2 fight. She was flat on the ice under him and knows it. That is three tellings, and the second is one character explaining to the other something both witnessed.
- Location: "He didn't fire. He couldn't see the man, and beyond him lay the dark houses of the road." / "\"I couldn't see him. There were people asleep in every house behind him.\""
- Suggested fix: Cut the reason from the s1 narration (leave "He didn't fire."). Keep it only in the fight, where it is an argument and not information.

**Issue 3**
- Problem: Dane's self-blame is wrong against Ch 03. By ten that night he had turned the kitchen light off ("so nobody on the road could see in"), and the only light in the kitchen was the stove's glow through its door. Russ's was the only lit house, not a lit window. The one restatement the beat sheet allows carries a wrong detail.
- Location: "\"Fair.\" He looked at the tarp over the window. \"I put you in the only lit window on the harbor road and let the whole road watch. That's on me.\""
- Suggested fix: Change "the only lit window on the harbor road" to "the only lit house on the harbor road," which is Ch 03's own wording.

**Issue 4**
- Problem: Scene 3's and-chain follows the structure of bible Sample D beat for beat: wind on the roof or metal, a man out there, "thinking about that," then not, and she notices she isn't. The bible forbids lifting a sample's sentences or details. "She showed him" also echoes Sample D's "took his hand and showed him where."
- Location: "The heater ticked, and the wind boomed on the metal over them, and his mouth was at her throat, and somewhere out on the harbor road a man with a bad foot was walking in the snow, and then the man went out of her head entirely, and she noticed it go, and what was left was wanting this, and him, and his hand exactly where it was for as long as the night lasted, and she wasn't steering any of it."
- Suggested fix: Rebuild the chain from this scene's own details (the net's rope under her shoulders, the tape on his arm, the bait smell from the floor, the dragging step she can no longer hear) and drop the "man out there, then not, she noticed" progression. Change "She showed him." to a different action.

**Issue 5**
- Problem: The choreography limit (never more than two sentences in a row about who is touching what before a thought, sound or line) is broken at the opening of the heat scene, where three consecutive sentences are movement and touch. It is nearly broken at the climax, where three consecutive touch sentences run across a paragraph break.
- Location: "He came up off the bench into the kiss and walked her back until her heels hit the pile of old net by the heater. They went down onto it together. The net was rough through her coat, so she shoved the coat off." / "He kept his hand where it was until she stopped." / "Then she moved her hand on him, faster, the way his breathing asked her to. He came with his forehead down on her collarbone."
- Suggested fix: Insert a thought or a spoken line after "They went down onto it together." In the climax, put one line of her interior or a sound between "He kept his hand where it was until she stopped" and "Then she moved her hand on him."

**Issue 6**
- Problem: "That's one" is the payoff of Dane counting Beth's laughs, but nothing earlier on the page establishes laugh-counting. Ch 03's "I'm keeping count" counted witnesses and kisses. A listener hears "That's one" with no referent, which breaks the plain-language rule (World rule 6: no plot or joke fact left to inference).
- Location: "\"That's one,\" he said."
- Suggested fix: Make the referent audible in Dane's line: "That's one laugh. I'm counting."

**Issue 7**
- Problem: Ch 03 ends with the boss's "Six tomorrow morning, then. Me and nobody else." That is a fixed check-in. The loft scene has Dane unable to call, but the dawn scene, at about half past six, never acknowledges that the six o'clock call has been missed. The open thread is silently dropped, and the boss's reaction to a silent marshal is a live threat.
- Location: "\"Then somebody else can think of it,\" Dane said. \"We leave now.\"" (and s2: "I can't call my boss, and I won't go out on that stair to try with him on the road.")
- Suggested fix: Add one clause in s4, such as Dane saying he is already late for his six o'clock call and has to get to signal, as part of his reason for leaving now.

**Issue 8**
- Problem: A contrast shape ("X didn't. Y did.") in narration, which the zero-tolerance contrast rule covers in spirit. It also states the emotion where an action could carry it.
- Location: "He didn't care. She did. She moved his arm to lie along her hip, off the rough net, and kept going."
- Suggested fix: "A thread of blood came through the tape. She moved his arm to lie along her hip, off the rough net, and kept going."

**Issue 9**
- Problem: A narrator sentence states the conclusion of the dawn exchange (anyone on the island could walk into that kitchen) one beat before Russ's closing line says the same thing. The hook is pre-empted. This also breaks the beat sheet's "no narrator explaining the irony" rule. Russ's last line then repeats what he has already said ("Off the one on her kitchen wall." / "Back door was open.").
- Location: "Dane looked at Beth. She knew that kitchen. So did everybody on the island who had ever carried Mrs. Dyer's groceries up her hill." followed by "\"Somebody walked in and used her phone.\""
- Suggested fix: Cut "She knew that kitchen. So did everybody on the island who had ever carried Mrs. Dyer's groceries up her hill." and let Dane's look at Beth go straight into Russ's last line.
