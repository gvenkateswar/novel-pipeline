## Flags before the beats

1. **Where the second "Cancel it" goes (outline vs. caller).** The amendment puts "Cancel it" on her step, with Russ's blood on her hands. The caller's binding order puts Beth's second ask *after* Mercer. The outline allows only one ask in this chapter. **Taken:** the ask comes once, face to face, at dusk on the town-office step (Scene 5), and Dane's refusal now has Mercer behind it. On her step (Scene 3) she keeps "I want you, Marshal. I haven't stopped." and adds nothing after it. That carries the romance, and the rupture is left to Scene 5.
2. **Tourniquet (DC §11).** The wound is "halfway up the thigh," not "high in the thigh," so there's room for a strap above it. Beth packs the wound as before. She tightens the strap with a windlass: **the ferry's wrench from her coat pocket**, which she has carried since the hatch on Wednesday in Ch 12. Dane says the time out loud. Over the radio the Coast Guard tells them to ease the strap in an hour if he isn't in shock, keep the packing and a tight bandage on, and tighten it again if it bleeds. Ch 14 then keeps its one clause: by noon Thursday Tess had swapped the strap for a tight bandage. *Dependency on the parallel Ch 12:* if the rebuilt Ch 12 doesn't leave the wrench with Beth, use a split of stove wood from the pile by her step.
3. **No helicopter (DC §10, binding).** On the radio the Coast Guard says nobody will fly a stable patient in this ceiling, and Cape Cod will come if he gets worse. There's one clause in Dane's head and no LifeFlight call. This replaces the old text's "We'll fly when the icing clears" and the line "No helicopter would come…".
4. **The stretcher (DC §12).** A pumper's back is a hose bed. Beth **drives Russ's pickup up the lane** when she hears the shot (her term was "If anybody bleeds, I'm coming up"), so Russ goes on the stretcher into the pickup's bed. Tess comes in the fire truck with the stretcher and her handheld, drives the pickup down, and tosses Dane the fire-truck keys without a word. The gloves-off image is kept: the gloves are already gone when Beth gets out of the cab.
5. **Mercer's beard against Russ's.** The bible gives Russ a "red beard going gray at the chin," and the binding gives Mercer a "red beard gone white at the chin." A listener may merge them. Russ is asleep off-frame in Scene 5, so Mercer's tags for the ear are **the orange float coat and the gold tooth**, with "Captain" as his voice. The writer never puts the two beards near each other and never compares them. *Orchestrator: consider "a gray beard gone white at the chin" for Mercer at the next bible review.*
6. **People cap (more than three present).** Scene 3 (the step) has Beth, Dane and Russ, and then Tess. Tess's arrival is binding there, because she makes the radio call from the step. Russ has one line once she arrives. Scene 5 has Beth, Dane and Mercer, plus the kid, who sees and never speaks. Ned sleeps against the pipe and Russ sleeps on the couch, each in one clause. Splitting either scene would break up the binding sequence, so both scenes are kept whole and each has its own turn.
7. **The back-room door (audit N3), paid as Dane's cost.** Dane leaves it open on purpose. From the front doorway, an open door is the only way he can see the road, the step and both prisoners at once. That's what lets Mercer look straight past him at the kid. In his head Dane puts it in a review board's report. Ch 15 can let him own it aloud ("you and the boy").
8. **Lines cut from the old text, with reasons:**
   - "Yesterday he'd told his boss on speaker… *She'll go.*" — the override; replaced per N8.
   - "No helicopter would come up through icing…" — Flag 3.
   - "She was the best thing he had ever seen at work" — a stated feeling.
   - "On this island it's finished" — unclear to a listener.
   - "Nobody watching this harbor would think to watch that" and "He didn't answer her" — they announce the climax.
   - "This was the boy Ned's phone had sent to every bed she slept in" — it recites plants.
9. **Dropped from the outline's Scene 5 thoughts:**
   - "A lost ferry looks like the storm" — it belongs to the kept Ch 15 text and tips the climax here.
   - The B5 helicopter clause, which had a contrast construction ("in the cloud, not on the water"). It's rewritten below.
10. **Lines to the kid (A15).** The fight has exactly two ("Let go of it." and "I know which one. I'm the one who shot it."). The kitchen question ("Who's coming for her?") and the Scene 5 question ("Is that him?") are one line each. Dane's running talk is still saved for Ch 15.
11. **The gauze.** Mercer's packet stays on the bottom step in the snow. Beth won't touch it (she owes nobody), so Ch 14 can keep the empty first-aid box.

## Scene 1

**Want:** Dane wants the kid to stay at the sink until Russ knocks. In the way are the glass, the light coming up, and a boy who has started looking at the window.
**Surface action:**
- **Keep the opening line word for word:** "Dane Reyes lay in the snow under Beth's kitchen window at first light and watched the kid wash her dishes."
- Keep the kid at the sink: the left arm bound to his body with one of her dish towels, a mug washed one-handed and set upside down, the pistol on the drainboard, his weight on the right leg.
- The stove smoke, compressed to two sentences (A21). He lit it before dark with the smoke going up for the whole village to see, so the cold has beaten him, and he's weaker this morning than he's been all week.

- **The 4 a.m. exchange, kept, with one new line.** Mid-scene, not at the opening.
  - "You wait in the truck behind the rise. Dirt stops a bullet. And if this goes wrong, I want to know where you are."
  - "I'll go. Don't flatter yourself." (arms folded) "If anybody bleeds, I'm coming up."
  - "Yes."
  - "You said yes fast."
  - "I've lost to you every day since the oar. I'm getting quick at it. Give me till Monday and I'll lose before you open your mouth."
  - **New:** "Except about Portland."
  - Then "Marshal—", and she stops on the word. She takes the first-aid box out of his hands without touching his fingers.
- **One line in his head (N8), and only one:** "Yesterday he had told her what he'd asked for. It still stood."
- The kid lifts his head. His eyes come to the window and rest on the snow outside it, and there's nowhere to be if he comes to the glass. A gull goes over. The kid goes back to the mug.
- Russ's fist hits the front door, hard, from beside it, behind the stone chimney, where Dane told him to stand. Dane told him twice, and the writer says so once here. Inside, the kid's wet hand closes on the pistol.

**Subtext:** Dane has her plan for the house in his head (which board talks, which window her family goes out of), so he is lying in her trust while she won't touch his fingers. "Except about Portland" tells him she has priced the hold to the word.

**Ends on:** **Cut.** Mid-action: Russ's fist on the door, and the kid's wet hand closing on the pistol.

## Scene 2

**Setting:** The same window and the corner of the house. Seconds later. Sensory anchor: grit and snow in his face from the shot beside his head, and a high whine in his left ear.
**Characters present:** Dane, the kid, Russ (his voice, then his body on the step).

**Plants:** the bullet hole in the front door (→ Ch 17); the kid alive in ties (→ S5, Ch 15).

## Scene 3

**Subtext:**

## Scene 5

**Setting:** The town-office step and the harbor road, Thursday early afternoon, then dusk. It is still snowing, steady and fine, with no lull (B5). Sensory anchor: the steel rod across Beth's knees, cold enough to burn, with the wire brush rasping on it. Inside, Russ is asleep on the couch, the strap off since noon. Tess is at her store for wood. Ned sleeps sitting up against the pipe. The kid lies tied on the back-room floor.
**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Hal Mercer; the kid (sees, silent).
**Want:**
- Dane wants the man in the orange coat to stay at the foot of the step and then go. He wants Beth inside, and she won't go.
- Beth wants to keep working on her boat's steering in the open air, because she won't sit in a room with Ned, and she won't be moved by any man.
- Mercer wants to see her face and let the kid see his.

**Surface action:**
- **Open on now, not on catch-up:** "Dane saw the orange coat when it was still a mile off, coming round the head of the harbor in the snow."
