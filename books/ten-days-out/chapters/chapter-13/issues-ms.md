## CHAPTER 12

**Issue 12-1** (listener)
- Line: "The crack off the wall was still going out across the field when Beth Calloway hit the bottom of the ditch with Dane's fist in her coat, and Ned came down on top of her."
- Problem: The chapter opens in the ditch with no setting. Nothing says it is Wednesday first light, that this is the east road, that Ned's wrists are roped, or that Beth is leading him. "The wall" and "the field" arrive undefined, straight after Ch 11's "Why?" in a dark kitchen. The rope shows up only at "The rope's end was still in Beth's bare hand" and "tied man" only at "He had a witness and a tied man in a ditch." A listener has no idea where they are or how they got there until a third of the scene is gone.
- Suggested fix: Put one sentence before the crack: the east road, snow to the knee, Ned's roped wrists, the end of the rope in Beth's hand, a stone wall on the uphill side.

**Issue 12-2** (POV)
- Line: "He didn't chase either. He had a witness and a tied man in a ditch."
- Problem: Beth's chapter states Dane's reasons for not firing and not chasing, with "he didn't shoot toward houses" as his policy. Beth cannot know his reasoning. It reads as Dane's interior.
- Suggested fix: Show what Beth sees (he lowers the rifle, looks at the roofs, looks at her and Ned, stays), or make it her inference ("He wouldn't shoot toward houses. He wouldn't leave her.").

**Issue 12-3** (listener, timeline)
- Line: "A man rang me on that phone the morning after," Ned said. "He told me the street my boy sleeps on."
- Problem: "The morning after" has no antecedent. The last event the listener has heard is "a calm night" two years ago, then "the beach", so "the morning after" parses as the morning after the beach, which is today. The log has the lawyer's call on Friday morning, after the crate split. A listener will also wonder why Ned said on Tuesday "Some nights I don't know where he sleeps" (Ch 09) if he was told the street on Friday. That needs one clause to read as hiding.
- Suggested fix: "the morning after the crate split." Optional: "I told you I didn't know where he slept. I knew."

**Issue 12-4** (listener)
- Line: "He said, after your deckhand's gone home, you take it back across alone."
- Problem: Ned quotes Cole saying "your deckhand" to Ned, but Ned is speaking to Beth, who is the deckhand. In audio it sounds like Ned addressing her.
- Suggested fix: "after Beth's gone home" or "after the deckhand's gone home".

**Issue 12-5** (listener)
- Line: "So I came down that beach to stand where he'd have to shoot me first."
- Problem: The "So" hangs on the previous line, "It comes in from offshore. A boat. I never saw it.", which has nothing to do with the beach. It reads as a non sequitur. The beat meant it as an unasked addition after the ditch.
- Suggested fix: Drop "So" and add a beat: Ned stops, looks at his boots, then "On the beach I came to stand where he'd have to shoot me first."

**Issue 12-6** (cross-chapter; see 13-11)
- Line: "In September Cole asked me who'd have the boat after me," Ned said. "I told him you. I was proud of it."
- Problem: Ch 13's Mercer says "Come June" and Dane concludes he could only have June from Cole. Ned's Ch 12 line never says June, so the listener has no plant to connect. The captain's job and June have been talked about only in Ch 08–09.
- Suggested fix: "who'd have the boat after me in June."

**Issue 12-7** (listener)
- Line: "You asked me who I told. I told her. Friday night, from my porch, before the shooting."
- Problem: "Who I told" has no object. A listener does not know what he told. In Ch 05 the question was who knew where Beth was sleeping; here that is missing, and the beat's line "who I told you were at my place" was cut.
- Suggested fix: "You asked me who I told she was sleeping at my place."

**Issue 12-8** (listener)
- Line: *the kid isnt answering ned. your son is. go finish it.*
- Problem: Read aloud, "your son is" has no predicate. It is unclear whether the son is answering, is next, or is the lever. The lowercase run-together does not survive text-to-speech.
- Suggested fix: Give the line a verb the ear can catch ("the kid isnt answering. ned, your son is next. go finish it.") or have Dane read it aloud once in a clause.

**Issue 12-9** (listener)
- Line: "That's mine," Beth said. "I say that."
- Problem: Dane says "Sit." Beth's "That's mine. I say that." does not tell the ear what "that" is. The chair is the clerk's, and no earlier line plants "Sit" as hers. Dane's next line, "I'm borrowing it", takes "that" to be the chair, so the exchange is muddled.
- Suggested fix: Make the claim land on one object: "Sit" is hers ("I say 'sit' in this room.") or the chair is hers.

**Issue 12-10** (craft: scene opening)
- Line: "At noon Dane gave his boss the arrest, the number and the son on speaker, with Beth three feet away."
- Problem: It opens the hold beat with a reported catch-up of an off-page call, in the exact slot the rules ban (a catch-up at the head of a beat). It is simple past, not past perfect, but it does the same job. It also carries the boss's first speech with no scene break and no time cue other than "At noon".
- Suggested fix: Start on the sound of the boss's voice in the room ("A lawyer who can find a man's son in a morning can find a woman on an island," the boss said) and let "Beth three feet away" be shown by where she stands.

**Issue 12-11** (timeline, plausibility)
- Line: "Dane stayed at the window. His thumb moved on the screen. Beth watched it. He sent." / "The phone rang. He set it on the sill on speaker." / "The prosecutor's agreed to ask," the boss said. "Two o'clock Monday."
- Problem: Between Dane sending the text and the boss ringing back with the prosecutor's agreement there are about eight short lines of dialogue (under a minute of story time). The boss told him "Call me back, Reyes" and the draft reverses that. A listener also does not know who "He sent" went to.
- Suggested fix: Name the recipient ("He sent it to the boss.") and give the callback a time cue ("Twenty minutes later, the phone rang."), with the stove ticking or Ned's silence through the door filling the wait.

**Issue 12-12** (domain check A12)
- Line: "Ninety-six Grant Street. Second floor, the door round the back."
- Problem: The manuscript domain check (A12 and §16) flags 96 Grant Street, Portland, as very likely a real home. The fix was not applied.
- Suggested fix: "A three-decker on Grant Street, second floor, the door round the back."

**Issue 12-13** (consistency with Ch 13, domain check A6)
- Line: "The rod from the wheel to the rudder hung loose, bent by the first storm and broken off. The pin at the top was frozen."
- Problem: The domain check wants the sheared pin to pay Ch 02's free-spinning wheel. Ch 13 says "The bend showed where the pin had sheared." Ch 12 has a frozen pin at the top and a rod "broken off", with no sheared pin. A listener hears three states (bent, broken off, frozen) and Ch 13 a fourth (sheared). The two chapters should agree on which pin broke.
- Suggested fix: Ch 12: "the pin at the rudder end sheared when the rudder took the pier, and the rod bent; the pin at the wheel end was frozen." Ch 13 can keep "where the pin had sheared".

**Issue 12-14** (listener)
- Line: "Co-op vise in the morning," she said. / "I'd lose you to keep you alive."
- Problem: Dane's reply answers her earlier argument ("It's still my life to spend."), but it comes after a practical remark about the vise and reads as a reply to that. The ear takes "I'd lose you to keep you alive" as a non sequitur.
- Suggested fix: Move "Co-op vise in the morning" to after the exchange, or give Dane a one-clause bridge ("I heard you. I'd lose you...").

**Issue 12-15** (custody, physical logic)
- Line: "One second to get his other arm in," Dane said behind her, "which is harder than it looks with a rifle, and then—"
- Problem: Ned is in Russ's cuffs, wrists together; one cuff has been moved to the rail and then freed again. "Get his other arm in" a coat sleeve cannot happen with his wrists cuffed. Ned is already wearing his coat (snow clings to the back of it in the ditch). The line is also hard to follow by ear.
- Suggested fix: Make it the cuff ("One second to get his cuff off the rail, which is harder than it looks with a rifle, and then—").

**Issue 12-16** (object)
- Line: "She sat on the edge of the hatch with the bent rod across her knees and put the wrench on the next bolt."
- Problem: "The next bolt" has no referent. No bolts have been mentioned. The rod is across her knees, so it is unclear what she is wrenching.
- Suggested fix: Name it ("put the wrench on the next fitting of the steering gear") or cut to the wrench in her hand.

**Issue 12-17** (custody)
- Line: "Dane put Ned's knife in her hand and closed her fingers on it with his own, a second longer than it took."
- Problem: Ch 13 has Dane produce "Ned's rope knife" for Beth ("Knife." / "Dane put Ned's rope knife in her hand."). Ch 12 never shows her giving it back, and Dane's last known pocket for it is the gesture here. A one-clause return is missing.
- Suggested fix: One clause after the cut: she handed it back, or he took it.

## CHAPTER 13

**Issue 13-1** (consistency with Ch 12, Ch 05)
- Line: "Under the woodsmoke her kitchen smelled of somebody else's two nights in it, wet wool and canned stew."
- Problem: The kid ran toward the village at Wednesday's first light and lit the stove "at dusk" Wednesday (Ch 13's own first scene). That is one night in her house. Tuesday night he was in the woods and wounded. "Two nights" is wrong.
- Suggested fix: "somebody else's one night in it" or "a night in it".

**Issue 13-2** (consistency with Ch 05)
- Line: "Russ had worked two summers beside that boy on Cole's boat, and he'd spent twenty years talking drunk men out of houses."
- Problem: Ch 05 says the kid "worked the back of Cole's boat two summers", Russ "never once saw him walk on land", "Everybody called him kid", and "I don't think anybody ever asked his name." Russ saw him from a distance and never worked beside him. The cold reader already flagged this contradiction once. The Russ confidence is Russ's motive for stepping into the door, so it matters.
- Suggested fix: "Russ had watched that boy two summers off the back of Cole's boat, and he'd spent twenty years talking drunk men out of houses." Keep "You know me off the harbor." so the kid knows Russ by sight.

**Issue 13-3** (dialogue tag)
- Line: "I'm not dying. Did I stand in the door? I stood in the door like a summer kid. You said the side." / "I did."
- Problem: Russ's speech is addressed to Dane ("You said the side"), but the paragraph before it is Beth's "Don't move," she told Russ. The next line, "I did.", has no tag, and the ear gives it to Beth. The planted exchange is Russ and Dane.
- Suggested fix: "'I did,' Dane said."

**Issue 13-4** (medicine, domain check §11)
- Line: "She ran the strap round the thigh above the hole, put the ferry's wrench from her coat pocket through the knot and twisted, one turn, two, until the bleeding under Dane's hands stopped."
- Problem: Three gaps. (a) "The strap" has no source: the first-aid box is already out of dressings and no strap has been mentioned. (b) The page applies no pressure dressing, but the Coast Guard's instruction is "Keep the packing in and a tight bandage on", and the box is empty ("The box lay empty on the step"). The advice presumes a bandage that doesn't exist. (c) "Hold this. Press. Harder." is said to nobody, and Dane's hands are not placed until "under Dane's hands".
- Suggested fix: (a) "She pulled the box's cloth strap". (b) One line: Tess wraps the leg over the packing with a bandage from her kit (or tears her own scarf). (c) "Dane put his hands where hers had been."

**Issue 13-5** (listener)
- Line: "Coast Guard, this is Cutter fire."
- Problem: To a listener, "Cutter" means a Coast Guard cutter. Tess is calling from Cutter Island's fire department. The ear cannot tell whether she is a boat.
- Suggested fix: "Coast Guard, this is Cutter Island fire."

**Issue 13-6** (tracks, consistency with Ch 12)
- Line: "One line of tracks came up through the new snow from the cove, a clean print and a long smear. None went out."
- Problem: "The cove" has meant the kid's east cove (Ch 10, 11) since Tuesday. In Ch 12 the kid ran west along the wall toward the village. The tracks coming up from "the cove" reads as the east cove, which contradicts his run. If it is a cove below Beth's house, it has not been introduced.
- Suggested fix: "from the shore below the lane".

**Issue 13-7** (borderline contrast construction)
- Line: "The hold still stood, and he gave her nothing back but his face."
- Problem: "Nothing back but his face" is a not-X-but-Y shape. It is the ruled-out construction in disguise and also the stock "gave her nothing but" cadence.
- Suggested fix: "The hold still stood. He kept his face still."

**Issue 13-8** (past perfect near a scene opening)
- Line: "It hadn't been there at eleven."
- Problem: The second paragraph of the scene is a catch-up clause in the past perfect, in a scene the beat sheet says must open on something happening.
- Suggested fix: Put the boat in the present of the scene ("A big fishing boat lay quiet in the lee of the point, where nothing lay in November.") and drop "at eleven", or move the time cue to Dane's thought later.

**Issue 13-9** (listener)
- Line: "That coat's never been wet," Beth said.
- Problem: The joke depends on the coat being new and stiff. The draft describes the coat only as "orange" and the man as "big, slow … gray-bearded and bare-headed". The ear has nothing to hear this by. The beat sheet's "a new orange float coat" was dropped.
- Suggested fix: One adjective in the description: "a stiff new orange float coat".

**Issue 13-10** (POV, geometry)
- Line: "Mercer looked past Dane, through the open back-room door, and smiled at what he saw on the floor. The kid looked at him, then turned his face to the wall."
- Problem: Dane has come down the step and is standing in front of Beth, facing Mercer. The back-room door is inside the building, behind him. He cannot see the kid's look, and the draft cut the beat's "Dane turned his head". Dane's POV sees something it has no sightline to. The kid's reaction is the chapter's turn.
- Suggested fix: "Dane turned his head. On the floor the kid looked at him, then turned his face to the wall."

**Issue 13-11** (inference, cross-chapter; see 12-6)
- Line: "He'd said June, and he could only have June from Cole."
- Problem: (a) Ch 12's Ned line never says June. (b) "Only from Cole" overreaches: June was said aloud on the north beach on Tuesday in front of Dane and Ned (Ch 09), Ned's retirement is plausibly island knowledge, and the Tess-radio channel (which Mercer used for "a man shot" and "the boy") could not have given it. Dane concludes without ruling out island gossip. The outline's own version is hedged ("only her people knew").
- Suggested fix: Plant "in June" in Ch 12 (12-6), then: "He'd said June. Only Ned had said June to Cole." Or soften: "and the only place June came up in that conversation was Cole's."

**Issue 13-12** (attribution)
- Line: "He sent the photo and the name to his boss on one bar. *Nothing to hold him on. I'll run the name.* Offering gauze on a public road wasn't a crime."
- Problem: The italic line is the boss's reply but sits right after "He sent" with no tag. In audio it sounds like Dane's own text. "Nothing to hold him on" also reads like his judgment.
- Suggested fix: "Her answer came back on the same bar: *Nothing to hold him on. I'll run the name.*"

**Issue 13-13** (state across chapters: custody and position hand-offs)
- Line: "Haskell's cuffed to the pipe in the back room," Tess told him. "On that hip he's going noplace."
- Problem: Ch 12 ends with Ned's cuff on the car-deck rail at dusk and Russ's truck at the foot of the lane. Ch 13 never says how Ned moves to the town office pipe, when Russ comes in off the road, or when Beth took the knife back. The carry-forward says Russ was radioed in at dusk and the snow watched overnight. None of this is on the page, so Russ appears at the front door from nowhere, and a listener will wonder who was guarding Ned and the road overnight, as well as why the kid was allowed to sleep a night in her house.
- Suggested fix: One line in Ch 13 s1 in Dane's head: Russ called in off the road at dusk; Ned back on the pipe; the house watched from the rise overnight.

**Issue 13-14** (cross-chapter: continuity of Ch 12's hold under Ch 13's ask)
- Line: "Yesterday he had told her what he'd asked for. It still stood."
- Problem: Not a contradiction, but the listener's only anchor is "what he'd asked for". Ch 12's hold was never named "the hold" by Dane; the boss said "It rests on my deputy's word", and Dane's scene calls it only "this one". The chapter heading is "The Hold" but no one says the word aloud in either chapter until Ch 13's "The hold still stood" and "The hold was right". A listener may not link "the hold" to a thing Ch 12 gave a name.
- Suggested fix: Give it its name once in Ch 12 (the boss or Dane: "the hold").
