# Reader Notes: Chapter 13, "Her Father's Kitchen"

**Draft read:** `chapters/chapter-13/final.md`, as of Nov 6, 2026. To get here I read chapters 01 through 13, `final.md` only, in order.

**Pull score: 3 of 5.** I'd keep reading, but mostly because there is one chapter or so left and I want the ending, not because this chapter pulled me in. The opening image grabs me. Then the chapter stalls in flashback. The capture of the man who has hunted Beth for twelve chapters is over in about a paragraph. The romance has stopped moving. I get Dane's ache repeated without any change in it.

---

## Where I got bored

- Straight after the opening, which is very good (the kid washing her mug one-handed). Then come four paragraphs of past-perfect catch-up: "The kid had lit the stove half an hour before full dark last night..." / "Beth had given him the house at four, as business..." / "At dusk Dane had radioed Russ..." / "At four Russ said..." / "Tess had answered through her door..." / "Then he had told Beth where she'd wait." The tension of a man lying in the snow under a window drains out while I work out who said what at which hour. I stopped feeling the cold and started doing a timeline.
- "A review board would read it as Dane's plan and Dane's constable. He would tell them Russ stepped in front of the door, and it would sound like an excuse, and it would be true." A man has just been shot, an armed kid is a wall away from Dane, and Dane is rehearsing his paperwork. Earlier chapters did the "Cole's lawyer in a good coat" thought well. This time the pause killed the moment.
- Dane's paragraph while Beth works on Russ: "The program was still the only way he knew to keep her alive, and this island might kill her before Monday, and she would stay on it. He didn't say it. It wasn't his to say yet." I already knew all of it from Chapter 12. I skimmed.

## Where I got confused

- "Beth had given him the house at four, as business." Four in the morning? Four in the afternoon? It took me a second read to see that she had briefed him on the house layout. "At four Russ said..." right after it made me think both happened at once, in the town office overnight. I couldn't place where everyone had spent the night.
- "At dusk Dane had radioed Russ to back his truck behind the rise... and come in." Come in where? The town office? Then Russ is back out with the truck at first light? I lost track of where the truck, Beth and Russ each were.
- "Nobody hiding lit a stove before dark." I followed the reasoning (he's so cold he's given up hiding), but the phrase "half an hour before full dark" made me stop and parse it.
- "Beth stayed on her knees on the step with his blood to her wrists." Whose blood? The chapter is in Dane's POV and three men are in play. I know it means Russ, but "his" landed on Dane first.
- Tess won't speak to Beth or look at her. Is she blaming Beth for Russ? Is it still the loan and the store? Is it the Tess-and-Russ secret that came out in Chapter 12? I don't know where Tess and Beth stand, and the chapter doesn't help.
- The pencil chart on the pantry door. Who drew it, and when? "My father's way home." Was it there all along, so the kid found it, or did the kid draw it? I think it's the father's, but it's presented as if the kid uncovered it.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Russ and the kid.** In Chapter 5 Russ said the kid "worked the back of Cole's boat two summers," that "I never once saw him walk on land. I wasn't sure till that drag," and "I don't think anybody ever asked his name." Now Russ "had worked the back of Cole's boat beside the kid two summers" and tells him "I drove you home all summer." If he drove the kid home all summer, he saw him walk, and he'd have known the limp on Thursday night. One of these is wrong, and it took me out of the scene at the exact moment Russ gets shot.
- **Nobody calls for help for Russ.** He's shot high in the thigh and "bleeding hard," and the only medical kit is empty. The morning is "still," so the storm seems to be over. In Chapter 9 Dane said no helicopter would come "for a woman who wasn't dying." Here a man might be dying, and nobody mentions a helicopter, a boat or Portland. Dane's text to his boss is "Have him. Alive." Nothing about his constable. Russ goes to the "town office couch." The kid, with a bullet in his arm for a day and a night, gets tied to the sink. The book has been careful about logistics until now, so this gap stood out.
- **Russ stepping into the door.** It's in character, and "Too easily" sets it up, but it was so clearly signposted that when it happened I thought "there it is" instead of flinching.
- **The capture is too clean.** Twelve chapters of this kid shooting through windows, burning houses and outlasting a marshal, and he comes out the one window Beth said he would, straight into Dane's hand. One wrist bend and he's done. I see he's half dead from blood loss, but it felt like the plan worked because the plan was written to work.
- Dane holsters his gun with the kid's lip "pulled back off his teeth," before the kid is tied. That's a small thing, but this book has trained me to watch Dane's gun hand.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **"He wanted her," four times in one chapter:** "He wanted her. He lay in the snow and wanted her..." / "He wanted her so much it hurt. He stayed where he was." (in the middle of a gunfight, with a man shot on the other side of the house) / "She was the best thing he had ever seen at work, and he wanted her." / "he wanted her so badly it was stupid. Then it passed, or he let it." Each one is the same beat in the same words. By the third it reads like a slot being filled, not a feeling.
- Feelings explained after they've been shown: "He had lost her in two words, and he'd done it himself." / "It wasn't his to say yet." / "Nobody on this island hung a mug like that." The mug moment didn't need the explanation. Beth's line already made the point.
- "The island had no doctor and no ambulance, so the fire truck carried the stretcher." That's the narrator explaining things to me.
- Tics that have built up over the book and show here: faces going **gray** (the kid gray, Russ gray, earlier Ned, Dane and Russ again); gloves pulled off **with her teeth** (Chapters 2, 4 and now 13); knots pulled tight with teeth; "since the oar" as a callback ("I've lost to you every day since the oar," after "He had known that since the oar" in Chapter 7). I noticed every one.
- Everyone talks in the same dry, clipped register. Tess: "Talking's extra." Dane: "I'm getting quick at it. Give me till Monday and I'll lose before you open your mouth." Beth: "You said yes fast." I like each line on its own, but the whole island sounds like one person doing deadpan.
- Another chapter ends on a one-line dialogue sting: "They've got a boat." Before it came "And me," (8), "Which one?" (9), "Why?" (11). By now I can feel each chapter lining up its last line.

## What I want to know next

- **The boat.** Is "they've got a boat" a threat that's coming now, in the calm after the storm, for Beth or for the kid? Or is it just the source Ned already told us about ("It comes in from offshore. A boat.")? Ned said it in Chapter 12, so the kid's big reveal landed half-spent for me. What I do want to know is whether it's on its way.
- **Getting off the island.** The ferry still has no steering, the float is gone, the island's boats are on the rocks, and Monday is four days away. How does Beth get to Portland?
- **Beth and Dane.** "Then they stop. And so do we." Is she really ending it? Does he ask to stay in Maine to her face, not just to his boss? Does she ever answer "Ask me in five days"? The five days are nearly up, and that's the question I care about most.
- **Russ.** Does he live? Nobody in the chapter acts as if he might not, and that bothers me.
- **Cole's lawyer.** He's the "good coat" the whole book has been imagining. Does he get caught, or does Beth have to face him in the room?

## Predictions (written before reading further)

- The pencil line on the pantry door is the climax. Beth fixes the ferry's steering, or takes some boat, and runs her father's private line through the harbor ledges, either to escape Cole's offshore boat or to wreck it on rocks only she knows. It's planted so heavily, in the last scene, with "Nobody's taken it since him," that I'd be surprised if it's anything else.
- Dane does something on that run that reverses the Chapter 7 rock (she can't hold on, so he holds on for both of them, or she lets him decide this one thing).
- Beth testifies Monday, refuses witness protection and stays on Cutter. Dane transfers to the Portland office or quits, buys the leaky wooden lobster boat, and turns up on her ferry. The epilogue has her captaining in June with Ned's letter to the board.
- Ned isn't redeemed but gets one decent act or line at the end. His son lives.
- The kid testifies against the lawyer.

If those predictions are right, the ending is earned but holds no surprises. What will decide it for me is how the "Ask me in five days" scene plays.

## Listener pass (read as if hearing it on audio)

- The flashback block at the top is the hardest part to follow by ear: "At dusk... at four... at five... at four... Then he had told Beth." Read aloud, those time markers blur together, and a listener will lose who is where before the knock on the door.
- Pronouns get slippery when three men share the stage: "Dane took the kid round him and in through her front door"; "with his blood to her wrists." In print I sorted them out. On audio I'd lose them.
- "He wanted her" repeated four times will sound to a listener like the narrator is stuck.
- What works aloud: the short exchanges over the wound ("Knife." / "Shit," Beth told the wound. "Hold this." / "Press. Harder."). Russ's "Did I stand in the door? I stood in the door." Tess's "I'll sit with Haskell. Talking's extra." The mug line. They have good rhythm and are easy to follow.
- "The board just inside the front door talked" is a nice touch on the page. Heard once, the reader might take "the board" to mean the review board mentioned a few lines later.

## Best moment

> "You put the mugs away wrong," Beth told the kid.
>
> She took one down, turned it, and hung it back with the handle in. She stood with her hand on it. One of her dish towels was round the kid's arm, and another was stuffed in the pane by her door, and she was holding a third.

She comes back into her father's kitchen after a week of being hunted from it. Russ's blood is on her hands, and the boy who broke in on the first night is tied to her sink. What she does is fix a mug. Nobody explains the feeling, and the three dish towels say it all. It's the best thing in the chapter and one of the best in the book. The runner-up is the opening line: a marshal in the snow watching the man who's trying to kill his witness wash her dishes.
