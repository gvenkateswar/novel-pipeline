## Flags before the beats (outline against the page, the log and the binding fixes)

None of these blocks the chapter. Each one is settled below. Flags 1–4 change small facts against the outline's Ch 13 entry. Nobody wins, loses or does anything different.

1. **Beth waits somewhere other than the fish shack (N7 against the outline's s1).** The outline puts her at her father's fish shack. N7, which binds every back-half beat sheet, rules that out. It's also unsafe: the shack sits on the rocks under the house, on the kid's own way up from the cove. **Settled:** Beth waits in Russ's truck at the foot of the lane, where the rise hides it from every window, with the ferry's first-aid box and Russ's handheld radio. That's the spot where Russ sat all Wednesday, so the truck at the foot of the lane comes back as an image. In s3 she "comes up the lane running" where the outline had "comes up from the shack."
2. **Nobody knows the kid's name (outline s2: Russ "shouts the kid's name").** Ch 05 and the bible say he answered only to "kid," lived in Cole's trailer, and nobody on the island knew his name. **Settled:** Russ shouts "Kid. It's Russ. Off Cole's boat." It's sadder, and the kid stays unnamed.
3. **No cuffs for the kid (outline s2: Dane "cuffs him").** Dane's cuffs went into the sea (Ch 07). Russ's only pair is on Ned at the town office (Ch 12). **Settled:** at four in the morning Dane took a handful of the long black cable ties off the hardware shelf in Tess's store. In s2 he ties the kid's wrists with them. **Downstream:** in Ch 14 s1 and Ch 15 s2, "the kid in cuffs" should read "the kid with his wrists tied." Ned keeps Russ's cuffs, which Ch 15 needs to cuff him to the rail.
4. **"Trauma kit" becomes the ferry's first-aid box (carry-forward).** Beth uses the box's last dressing on Russ. **Plant:** the box is empty after s3, which fits Ch 14 s2, where Dane wraps her torn palms in his shirt.
5. **Who guards Ned during the raid.** Nobody had said. **Settled (one line on the page, s3):** Ned is cuffed to the pipe in the town office back room with the door locked, and Tess sits in the front room with her radio and the fire truck parked outside. She leaves when she hears the shot, because a cuffed man of sixty-four with that hip isn't going anywhere on an island with no boats. Tess said yes to sitting with Ned at four in the morning (s1, the laugh).
6. **Russ comes in off the road overnight (carry-forward).** **Settled:** at dusk on Wednesday, after the smoke, Dane radioed Russ in. A man sitting in a truck under a house he can't see into, with an armed man inside who can see the truck, is a target and nothing else. The snow kept watch instead (s1). This is told in Dane's head in two clauses, inside the action and set off by what he sees. It is never a paragraph at the top of the scene.
7. **How the kid got in, and how Dane knows he's still in.** The back door still has Dane's board across it (Ch 02). **Settled:** the small pane beside the front-door latch is broken, and the kid has stuffed one of her dish towels into it against the cold. 8. **Two new set facts [plot-architect]:**

   Both are needed so that the outline's "behind the stone of the chimney" and Dane's view of the kid on the kitchen floor from the front step work. For the continuity log.
9. **Three outline scenes become four.** The outline's s3 (the front step, then the kitchen) splits into Scene 3 (the step: Beth, Dane, Russ, Tess) and Scene 4 (the kitchen: Beth, Dane, the kid). ## Scene 1

**Setting:** The snow under Beth's kitchen window, on the side of the house over the cove, first light Thursday. The snow stopped in the night. Fresh snow lies over yesterday's, and a single trail of dragging prints comes up from the cove toward the front of the house. **Sensory anchor:** the kitchen window lit orange from the stove inside, and the kid on the other side of the glass, one-armed, washing a mug in her sink. A second anchor: the cold coming up through Dane's coat into his chest and his bad right knee.

**Characters present:** Dane, the kid (through the glass). Russ, at the front of the house beside the chimney stone (out of Dane's sight; heard, not seen). Beth, off the page at the foot of the lane, in Russ's truck.

**Point of view:** Dane. His mouth is shut and his interior runs long.

**Want:**
- **Dane** wants the kid out of that house alive and in his hands before full daylight. Under that, he wants Beth, and he knows he's lost her.
- **The kid** wants to be warm one more hour.
- **In the way:** a pistol on her drainboard, and Russ, who thinks he can talk to the boy.

**Surface action:**
1. **The binding opener.** Then through the glass, plainly:
   - The kid at her sink, his left arm bound against his body in a dish towel, washing a mug one-handed and setting it upside down to drain.
   - His pistol on the drainboard beside the mugs.
   - His face grey under the hood, and his weight all on one leg.
2. **[A21] Dane reads the smoke, in his head, set off by the stove light.** The kid lit the stove half an hour before full dark last night, which no man hiding would do. He's been bleeding a day and a night and the cold is winning, so he's weaker today than he's been all week. That's why today. **Say it twice (Ch 12):** this boy is the last thing Ned's phone sent her. The truck was clean because Ned rang Cole from the boat, so her word was the whole case, so every bed she slept in got a visit. One sentence, with Dane's anger in it at true size.
3. **Why first light and why the window, woven in as Dane's eye moves.** Each is a clause set off by something he sees, never a paragraph:
   - In the dark the man inside has the house and the men outside have nothing. At first light the kid stands in a lit kitchen and can't see out into the grey, and Dane can see every move he makes.
   - The back door has Dane's own board still across it.
   - The towel stuffed in the broken pane beside the front-door latch shows how the kid got in (flag 7).
   - At five, in the dark, Dane walked all the way round the house: no tracks out in the night's snow, so the kid is in there.
   - Beth gave him the rest at four, at the town office, in a flat voice, as business. Anyone in that kitchen who has to run goes out the window over the sink, because it's on the far side from both doors and the drop is soft. She went out of it at sixteen, and the kid watched her go out of it the first night. And the board just inside the front door talks when you step on it.
4. **Russ, overnight (carry-forward, flag 6), two clauses.** At dusk Dane radioed Russ in off the road. A man in a truck under a house he can't see into is a man the kid can see. At four, at the town office, Russ said he had the kid on the back of Cole's boat two summers and the kid would come out for him. Dane told him twice: knock from the side, behind the chimney stone, never in front of the door. Russ said it back.
5. **The laugh, one recalled line.** At four Dane walked to Tess's door to ask her to sit with Ned while they did this. Tess, through the door, in her headline voice: "I'll sit with Haskell. Talking's extra." *(Optional, one clause: the cable ties off her hardware shelf, and she wrote them on his tab. Flag 3. If the scene runs long, keep the ties and drop the tab.)*
6. **Beth, and the longing (binding, plain).** At four he asked her to wait in Russ's truck at the foot of the lane, where the rise hides it from every window.
   - Beth: "I'll go. Not for you."
   - Then: "If anybody bleeds, I'm coming up." He said yes to that, so her coming up later is agreed and never disobedience.
   - **The longing, set off by her absence:** he wants her. Say it in those plain words, in Dane's long sentence that runs on "and" (her voice at four, how she took the first-aid box out of his hands without touching his fingers). Then the short clause that undercuts him: he has lost her, and he did it himself, on speaker, in two words.
   - **No program recap beyond one clause** (a new name in a new state; the Ch 12 restate). Nothing of her father's anywhere in this beat.
7. Through the glass, the kid lifts his head at something. Dane goes still. It's only the stove ticking, or a gull. The fear beat before the danger: give it one breath. The kid goes back to the mug.

**Turn:** none on the page yet. The scene's motion is the plan going in. Its small irreversible fact is that Russ is in place at the stone and there's no calling it back.

**Subtext:** Dane is doing the one thing he still has with her, his job, and doing it so well that nobody can say afterward it went wrong for want of thinking. He knows that's partly for the review board in his head. Beth's "Not for you" means she'll do the sensible thing, and that she won't let him have even that.

**Tension source:** Situational: a pistol in reach and a man who has shot at her four times. Internal: wanting a woman who has ended it, while lying in the snow under her window. Interpersonal, offstage: Russ, who said it back too easily.

**Ends on (ending type: cut, on a sound):** From the front of the house, out of sight, Russ's fist on the door: three bangs from the side, exactly where he was told. Inside, the kid's hand goes to the pistol. Cut.

**Texture budget (2):** reading the snow (no tracks out); the cable ties off Tess's shelf.

**Plants:** the cable ties (→ s2, Ch 14–15); Russ's "he'll come out for me" (→ s2); "If anybody bleeds, I'm coming up" (→ s3); the board that talks (→ s2).

**Word count target:** 480. **Floor 440.** If it runs long, cut step 5's optional tab clause, then shorten step 3 to the window, the board and the snow. Never cut the A21 smoke reasoning, "Not for you," the longing sentence or Russ's "he'll come out for me."

## Scene 2

**Setting:** The same snow under the window, the next ninety seconds. **Sensory anchor:** sound. Dane can't see the front door from where he lies, so he follows the fight by ear: the board inside the front door talking, Russ's voice moving, the shot, the sash of the window going up.

**Characters present:** Dane, the kid, Russ (heard, then glimpsed).

**Point of view:** Dane.

**Want:**
- **Russ** wants to bring the boy out with words, because he drove him home from the dock all one summer.
- **The kid** wants out.
- **Dane** wants to hold the window, even when every part of him goes toward the shot.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open in motion, by sound:** the kid is gone from the sink with the pistol. Then the board just inside the front door talks (Beth's board), so the kid is at the door. That's Beth's information working, unsaid.
2. **Russ's voice moves.** Russ starts at the side of the house and his voice comes from the stone. Then Dane hears it come round to the middle, square in front of the door: Russ has stepped into the doorway. He leaned the shotgun on the chimney stone so his hands would be empty. Dane sees that later in Scene 3, so it's never narrated here.
   - Russ, loud: "Kid. It's Russ. Off Cole's boat." (flag 2)
   - One more line from Russ at most, for example: "I drove you home all summer. Come on out."
   - In Dane's head, one clause: Russ thinks he can talk him out. **[A3]** It's his own choice, against the plan, said twice.
3. **The fear beat, half a breath:** Dane can't leave the window. If he runs to the front, the window is open to the kid, and Beth is at the bottom of the lane. **The longing under fire, binding, plain:** his first thought is where she is (the truck, below the rise, out of the line), and how much he wants her. One plain sentence, short.
4. **The shot.** One crack through the door. Russ's shout changes, and Dane hears him go down on the step. **Say it plainly for a listener:** the kid shot through the front door, and Russ is hit. (The thigh is confirmed in Scene 3. Here it's "low," or "Russ yelling about his leg.")
5. **Dane's jury habit, one clause, in the half-second he holds:** a review board will read it as his plan and his constable. He'll tell them Russ stepped in front of the door, and it will sound like an excuse, and it will be true. He holds the window.

   - No hood grab and no blow to the head. Dane takes the gun hand first as it comes over the sill, bends it back, and the pistol goes into the snow.
   - He puts the kid face down in the snow with a knee across his back, careful of the bound left arm only after the gun is clear.
   - He ties the wrists with Tess's cable ties.
   - Dane fires no shot. One clause on why alive matters: a boy with Cole's lawyer's number on his phone can say who sent him.
7. **Dane's two lines to the kid, no more [A15]:**
   - Line one, an order: "Open your hand." or "Let go of it." (if the pistol hasn't dropped yet)
   - The kid, face in the snow: "My arm."
   - **Line two, the laugh, dry, against himself:** "I know which one. I'm the one who shot it." (He hit it at the beach, Ch 09.) The kid hates him for it.
   - Then nothing more to the kid in this chapter.
8. Dane hauls him up and in through her front door into the kitchen, around Russ on the step. He sets him face down on the floor and ties him to the leg of the iron sink, or the stove's cold side, as the writer prefers. Plain and quick, no list of moves.

**Turn (binding):** Russ steps into the doorway. It's irreversible, it's his choice, and it costs his leg. Then the kid is taken alive.

**Subtext:** Russ wants to be the lawman the island never took seriously, and the kid's friend, both at once. Stepping into the door is how he tries to be both. Dane's whole job is not bending a plan for a person (Marisol, unsaid), and he keeps the window while a man he's come to like bleeds on the other side of the house. He doesn't get to know yet whether that was right.

**Tension source:** Situational: a shot he can hear and can't see. Internal: the window against the man down. Physical: the takedown.

**Ends on (ending type: a sound coming):** With the kid tied on the kitchen floor and Russ swearing low on the step, Dane hears running boots coming up the lane, uneven on a bad knee, and Beth's voice saying Russ's name. No dialogue from Dane after the second line to the kid.

**Texture budget (2):** the gun-hand takedown; the board that talks (Beth's knowledge doing work).

**Plants:** the kid's wrists tied (→ Ch 14–15); the pistol with Dane; Russ hit in the doorway (→ s3, Ch 15, Ch 18).

**Word count target:** 600. **Floor 560.** This is the chapter's danger scene; never compress the sound sequence (board, voice moving, shot) or the takedown. If long, step 5 drops to one short clause.

## Scene 3

**Setting:** Beth's front step and the trodden snow in front of the door, just after. The door has a splintered hole low in its lower panel. Russ's shotgun leans on the chimney stone where he left it. **Sensory anchor:** Russ's blood bright on the snow of the step, and steam coming off it in the cold. A second anchor: the fire truck's engine coming up the lane, loud in the still morning.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Russ, Tess (who arrives). The kid is on the kitchen floor through the open door, tied and silent, in Dane's view.

**Point of view:** Dane.

**Want:**
- **Beth** wants Russ's leg to stop bleeding, and nothing else, for three minutes.
- **Russ** wants to have been right about the boy, and knows he wasn't.
- **Tess** wants Russ, and nothing from Beth.
- **Dane** wants to tell Beth what he sees and can't. The reader learns this at the longing beat.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open in motion:** Beth comes up the lane running stiff-legged on the bad knee, with the ferry's first-aid box. She's pulling her gloves off with her teeth as she comes **[A5, gloves off, the plant paid]**. She drops to her knees on the step.
   - **Restated plainly for the listener:** Russ is shot in the thigh, high, and bleeding hard.
2. **Beth's bare hands, binding, shown as actions:**
   - She cuts his trouser leg with Dane's rope knife, which Dane hands her (Ned's, from his pocket).
   - She packs the hole with the box's gauze, two fingers deep, and leans her weight on it.
   - She ties a strap tight above the wound and pulls the knot with her teeth.
   - Her orders, two or three words each: "Hold this." "Press. Harder." "Don't move." This is her fear register. She goes quiet in between.
   - It's the last dressing in the box, said once (plant: Ch 14).
   - Beth's one allowed swear can go here, at the wound.
3. **The laugh (Russ's habit: he asks a question and answers it himself):** grey, on his back on her step: "Did I stand in the door? I stood in the door."
   - Then, to Dane: "You said the side."
   - Dane: "I did."
   - Nothing else between them about it (binding outline exchange).
4. **The longing (binding):** Dane watches her work. Show it as action: blood to her wrists, her braid fallen forward, her voice gone flat and sure.
   - In his head, plainly: she is the best thing he has ever seen at work, and he wants her.
   - A new name in a strange state would keep her breathing and kill her.
   - He doesn't say it. It isn't his to say yet (Ch 14 pays it).
   - Nothing of her father's in this beat. It's her step and her hands.
5. **Tess arrives in the fire truck as the ambulance.** Plain on the page: the island has no doctor and no ambulance, so the fire truck carries the stretcher.
   - Tess gets out and goes straight to Russ.
   - **She doesn't speak to Beth and doesn't look at her.** Show it: she takes the strap end from Beth's hand without a word, and checks the knot herself.
   - **Flag 5, Tess's one line, to Dane:** "Haskell's cuffed to the pipe. He's not going anywhere on that hip." That answers who was guarding Ned.
   - Second line, optional: "Lift on three, Marshal."
