# Chapter 13 · Issues (continuity-checker, draft.md)

Order is by weight. Where the bible disagrees with the outline or log, the outline and log were followed. Stale bible items are listed at the end.

**Issue 1**
- Problem: The chapter says two opposite things about Russ's truck at the foot of the lane. Dane pulled Russ in at dusk because a truck below the house "was a man the kid could see." Then Beth waits in that same truck at that same spot, and the page says the rise hides it from every window. Ch 12 also has the truck sitting in the open at the foot of the lane all Wednesday, visible from the town office steps. A listener will hear the plan contradict its own reason.
- Location: "At dusk Dane had radioed Russ in off the road; a man in a truck below that house was a man the kid could see." / "He had asked Beth to wait in Russ's truck at the foot of the lane, where the rise hid it from every window." / "the truck below the rise, out of any line from this house."
- Suggested fix: Add one clause that at five, in the dark, Dane had Russ's truck moved round behind the rise, and make the dusk line say only that the truck where it stood all Wednesday was in the kid's sight. Alternatively move Beth's wait to a spot the kid never saw the truck at.

**Issue 2**
- Problem: Factually wrong, and a recap in the third paragraph of the chapter. (a) Ned's phone never sent the kid to Beth's house. The kid came on his own, and Ned's texts named Russ's porch, the north-side house and the beach. "This" has no clear referent either, so a listener can't tell what "the last thing" is. (b) "Every bed she slept in got a visit" is false. The loft, the keeper's house and the dentist's house were never visited.
- Location: "This was the last thing Ned's phone had sent her. Ned had rung Cole when they tied up at Pruett, so the truck was clean and her word was the whole case, and every bed she slept in got a visit from the boy now washing her mug."
- Suggested fix: Cut the first sentence. Rewrite the rest as one clause with true numbers: Ned's call made her word the whole case, and Ned's phone had pointed this boy at her porch, her north-side house and her beach.

**Issue 3**
- Problem: The kid has been in her house for one night. Ch 12 ends on Wednesday dusk with the stove smoke as the first sign, her house "empty and cold for six days," and the kid on the east road at dawn Wednesday. Ch 13's own scene 1 says he lit the stove "last night." "Two nights" has no support.
- Location: "Under the woodsmoke the kitchen smelled of somebody else's two nights in it, wet wool and canned stew."
- Suggested fix: Change to "somebody else's night in it."

**Issue 4**
- Problem: The kid has one usable hand, and the page has it doing two things at once. He raises the sash, which takes a hand, and comes out "pistol first" in that same hand. The tying also doesn't work. The left arm is bound to his body in a towel, and he is face down, yet Dane "drew the good wrist round to the bound one."
- Location: "The sash went up over his head. The kid came out over the sink, pistol first in his good right hand, as Beth had said he would." / "He drew the good wrist round to the bound one and pulled a cable tie tight over both, and another over that."
- Suggested fix: Have the sash go up with the pistol hand and the gun come through after the arm. For example, "The sash went up. Then the gun hand came over the sill, and the rest of him behind it." For the tie, say Dane rolled him and tied the right wrist to the strapped arm across his chest.

**Issue 5**
- Problem: "Doorway" and "door" are used for the same place, but the door is shut. Russ stands in front of a closed door and the kid shoots through it. A listener hears "stepped into the doorway" and pictures an open door. It also clashes with "keep out of the doorway" and "square in front of the door."
- Location: "He had stepped into the doorway on his own, against the plan Dane had given him twice." / "Dane told him twice: knock from the side, behind the chimney stone, and keep out of the doorway."
- Suggested fix: Use "in front of the door" in all three places and leave Russ's own line, "I stood in the door," as his phrase.

**Issue 6**
- Problem: "The rest" has no antecedent. Nothing earlier says Beth gave him anything. A listener can't follow it, and the next sentences read as if the sentence before were missing.
- Location: "Beth had given him the rest at four, as business."
- Suggested fix: "Beth had given him the house at four, as business."

**Issue 7**
- Problem: Two lines a listener can't follow. (a) "He'd had the kid on the back of Cole's boat" reads as if Russ owned or employed the kid. Ch 05 and the beats mean they worked the stern together two summers, so "Off Cole's boat" in scene 2 lands late. (b) The unattributed quoted line follows a narration paragraph about Russ and Dane, so the speaker is unclear until "Dane told him twice."
- Location: "At four Russ said he'd had the kid on the back of Cole's boat two summers." / "\"He'll come out for me.\""
- Suggested fix: "At four Russ said he had worked the back of Cole's boat beside the kid two summers and the kid would come out for him." Attribute the quoted line with "Russ said."

**Issue 8**
- Problem: Dane's own gun never appears in scenes 1 or 2. He lies under the window of an armed man and takes the gun hand bare-handed. The beats say he "goes in close with his own gun." A reader will wonder whether he is armed and why he tackles instead of aiming.
- Location: "Dane came up off the snow and went in low to the wall under the sill." / "Dane took the gun hand as it cleared the sill."
- Suggested fix: Add one clause in scene 2 that his own gun is out in his left hand, or holstered so he can use both hands, so the takedown reads as a choice.

**Issue 9**
- Problem: Scene 1 is a block of about 330 words of past-tense setup (stove, Ned's call, the board, the snow, Beth's window, Russ, Tess, the ties, Beth's terms, the longing) with almost no present-moment event between "Dane wanted to put him through the glass" and "Behind the glass the kid lifted his head." The beats asked for each fact as a clause set off by something Dane sees. The rule against summarising what just happened applies to the Ch 12 recap in the first paragraphs.
- Location: "The kid had lit the stove half an hour before full dark last night." through "He said yes." (paragraphs 3 to 14 of scene 1)
- Suggested fix: Cut the block by about a fifth and split it with the kid's actions at the sink. Hang the towel, the board, the window and the tracks on what Dane's eye lands on, and keep the Tess and cable-ties clause to one sentence.

**Issue 10**
- Problem: Length. A hand count puts the draft at roughly 2,150 to 2,200 words against a 1,950 target. That is about 11 to 13 percent over, outside the 8 percent band. Scene 1 is roughly 600 against a target of 480. I could not run `wc`, so the orchestrator should check.
- Location: whole draft
- Suggested fix: Run `wc -w`. If over, take the trim from scene 1 (Issue 9).

**Issue 11**
- Problem: A line explains what the dialogue just showed. Russ's two spoken lines already show him trying to talk the boy out.
- Location: "Russ thought he could talk him out."
- Suggested fix: Cut the sentence. Keep the "against the plan" clause if it is needed.

**Issue 12**
- Problem: A line explains what the dialogue just showed. Russ has just shouted "My leg. Damn it, he got my leg," so the listener knows he was hit by the kid.
- Location: "The kid had fired through the front door, and Russ was hit, low."
- Suggested fix: Cut it. "The shot cracked through the door" followed by Russ's yelling carries it.

**Issue 13**
- Problem: Dane explains to Beth what she already knows. She knows the kid is the one sent, and she knows the town office back room, because she was there. The line carries no new information except "by noon," and that time clashes with the answer he gave Tess.
- Location: "\"That's the one they sent,\" he said to her. \"He'll be in the town office back room with Ned by noon. On this island it's finished.\""
- Suggested fix: Cut "That's the one they sent" and the back-room clause. Keep "On this island it's finished." Fix the time (Issue 14).

**Issue 14**
- Problem: The time is inconsistent. To Tess, Dane says he will bring the kid down immediately. To Beth, ten minutes later, the kid arrives "by noon," about five hours on. No reason is given for the delay.
- Location: "\"Town office couch,\" Dane said. \"I'll bring the kid down behind you.\"" / "He'll be in the town office back room with Ned by noon."
- Suggested fix: Change "by noon" to "within the hour," or give a reason for the wait.

**Issue 15**
- Problem: The line explains what the question and answer already showed, and it labels the beat for the reader. The next two sentences also say the same thing twice.
- Location: "It was the first thing she had said to him since Wednesday that wasn't business. Dane let it stand. He didn't answer it."
- Suggested fix: Cut the first sentence and keep one of the other two. If the beats' requirement stands, make it a single short action, such as him not answering.

**Issue 16**
- Problem: Tess tells Dane something he arranged himself at four, that Ned is cuffed and guarded. The line justifies her leaving her post, but it is delivered as news. The pipe is also new, because Ch 12 left Ned sitting against the wall.
- Location: "To Dane she said, \"Haskell's cuffed to the pipe. He's not going anywhere on that hip.\""
- Suggested fix: Make it an answer to a question from Dane ("Haskell?"), or cut the hip clause so it is only Tess's headline, "Haskell's on the pipe."

**Issue 17**
- Problem: Russ's swearing is over the beats' cap of one "damn." He says "Damn it" in his shout, and the narration adds a second swearing beat.
- Location: "Out on the step Russ swore at the sky."
- Suggested fix: "Out on the step Russ talked to the sky."

**Issue 18**
- Problem: Dane's thought contradicts what he said in Ch 12 and what the outline has him say in Ch 14. In Ch 12 he says "It's still the only life my job can give you" and "I mean to make you go." In Ch 14 s3 he says "I still think you should go." Here he thinks the program would "kill her," which reads as a reversal with nothing to bridge it. The outline's Ch 13 text contains this line, so the conflict is in the outline.
- Location: "A new name in a strange state would keep her breathing and kill her."
- Suggested fix: Keep Dane's view, and put the cost on her: "A new name in a strange state would keep her alive, and she would stop being her."

**Issue 19**
- Problem: Dane lies under the window and "watches the kid wash her dishes." From below the sill he could not see the sink, and if the kid looked down he would see Dane. The opener is binding, so the repair has to come after it.
- Location: "Dane Reyes lay in the snow under Beth's kitchen window at first light and watched the kid wash her dishes."
- Suggested fix: In the next sentence or the "lifted his head" beat, add that Dane had raised himself to the corner of the lowest pane, clear of the kid's line of sight.

**Issue 20**
- Problem: Tess is behind a closed door, yet Dane takes ties off her hardware shelf. The reader can't tell whether she opened the store, and the beats' optional "she wrote them on his tab" was dropped, so the gap shows.
- Location: "\"I'll sit with Haskell,\" Tess said through her door. \"Talking's extra.\" He took a handful of long black cable ties off her hardware shelf."
- Suggested fix: Add three words, such as "She unbolted the store," or restore the tab clause.
