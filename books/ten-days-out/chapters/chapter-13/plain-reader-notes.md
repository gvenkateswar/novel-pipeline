# Plain Reader Notes: Chapter 13, "Her Father's Kitchen"

Draft read: `chapters/chapter-13/final.md` (Nov 6, 2026), after `final.md` for chapters 01 through 12. I read nothing else.

## 1. Retelling

At dawn, Dane lies in the snow under Beth's kitchen window. Inside, the kid with the bad leg, the one who has been shooting at them all week, is washing her dishes, with his hurt arm tied up in her dish towel and his pistol on the counter. The plan is for Russ to knock on the front door from the side so the kid runs, and Beth has told Dane the kid will go out the window over the sink, the same way she escaped on the first night. Russ steps in front of the door instead, and the kid shoots through it and hits him in the leg. The kid comes out the window, Dane grabs his gun hand, puts him face down in the snow and ties his wrists with plastic ties, because Dane wants him alive to say who sent him. Beth runs up from the truck and packs Russ's wound, ties a strap tight above it, and Tess takes Russ away in the fire truck without speaking to Beth. Inside, Beth rehangs a mug the kid put away wrong, and they find her father's pencil drawing of the harbor rocks on the pantry door, with a line through them that was his private way home. The chapter ends with the kid on the floor saying, "You think I was what's coming? They've got a boat."

## 2. What's at stake

Beth is the only witness against Cole, and she has to reach Portland alive to speak to the grand jury on Monday or he walks free. The kid is caught now, but he says "they" have a boat, so it sounds like more of Cole's people are coming for her by sea, and she still won't leave the island.

## 3. The romance

His side came through, and it came through as an announcement. "He wanted her" is said four times in this one chapter, all inside his head:
- "He wanted her. He lay in the snow and wanted her..."
- "He wanted her so much it hurt."
- "She was the best thing he had ever seen at work, and he wanted her."
- "He wanted her so badly it was stupid. Then it passed, or he let it."

By the third time it sounded like a stamp, not a feeling. Nothing passes between them on the page. The line that did reach me was "He had lost her in two words, and he'd done it himself." That one hurt, because I remembered "She'll go" from the last chapter.

Her side: I didn't feel it. Her only moves are stopping halfway through "Marshal—" and taking the box "without touching his fingers." While listening, those are small gestures I'd miss. I couldn't tell you whether she still wants him or is just angry. "Heard her stop on that word again" didn't make sense to me. When had she stopped on it before?

- "the program" in "The program was still the only way he knew to keep her alive." I had to work out that this meant the new-name, new-state protection from Chapter 12. It slowed me down.
- "chimney stone" in "behind the chimney stone." I couldn't picture where that was compared with the door. It didn't stop me.

- "drainboard," "cable ties," "review board." I mostly knew these, so they didn't stop me.

## 5. Where I lost the thread

- **The back-story section in the first scene (lines 9 to 41).** It starts at dawn, then jumps to "half an hour before full dark last night," then "at five," then "at four," then "At dusk Dane had radioed Russ," then "At four Russ said," and then Tess, the cable ties and the talk with Beth in the truck, and then back to the snow. While listening I couldn't keep the order straight. I also couldn't tell whether "four" meant four in the afternoon or four in the morning. I only got the plan for certain when it actually happened.
- "and Beth was at the bottom of the lane." Then the next sentence puts her "in the truck behind the rise." I wasn't sure whether those were the same place.
- "Heard her stop on that word again." I don't know which earlier time this means.
- **The ending, "They've got a boat."** I got that it's a threat. I wasn't sure whether it means another boat bringing drugs (Ned said in Chapter 12 that the drugs come from a boat offshore) or a boat coming to kill her. Dane has just said "On this island it's finished," so I took it to mean it isn't finished. That's a good hook, but it's fuzzy.
- **The father's pencil line.** I understood it was his way through the rocks, but not why it matters now, or what "Nobody's taken it since him" says about how he died. I guessed it's being set up for later.
- Why Russ stepped in front of the door. I took it to be kindness, since he wanted the boy to see him, but the chapter doesn't say.

## 6. Verdict: PASS on the plot, with a warning on the romance

- I could retell the main event: the kid is caught alive, Russ is shot, and Beth saves him.
- I could say what's at stake.
- No plot fact depended on a word I didn't know or a picture I had to recognize. The plan, the window and the strap above the wound were all said in plain words.
- Romance: I heard his longing stated four times, and the repetition made it feel flat. I didn't feel hers at all. If this chapter is meant to move the romance, or to show that she still wants him, count it as a FAIL on that test. If it is meant to show them apart after the fight, the distance came through but her wanting did not.
