### Scene 1
**Setting:** The *North Reach*, moored starboard side to the stone pier. It opens in the crew mess at last light on Thursday (Day 7): the bulkhead lamp, the heater going, the mug ring on the table, the forepeak pump running under the steel. The night is carried in two or three sentences, then the scene picks up in the wheelhouse at the top of the tide, about four on Friday morning (Day 8), with the gale up and the snow back. Sensory anchor: the brow's wheels grinding on the granite at every surge, louder at high water.
**Characters present:** Beth, Ned, Dane (arrives).
**Surface action:**
1. Open on her hands, with no recap: she takes the second mug from Ned and wraps both hands round it, and her eyes go to the deck. She doesn't answer "Where's your marshal?" Within the first three sentences, Ned: **"Good. Don't tell me."** (Drop "Where is he?" per ruling 3.) He sets his own mug down by the notebook on the table, and his eyes go over the notebook and on to the coffee. That's all; no lingering.
2. Ned in his own register: weather first, subjects kept. The crew cabin is made up, and Kenny's spare clothes are in the locker ("Kenny's. He won't miss them. He's in Pruett, sick as a dog."). He doesn't ask about the wet jeans, the notebook or the hour. He goes on deck to the lines, and he isn't shown leaving (no doorway beat, no look-back).
3. The night, as interior (passage 1 of 3), plain and physical: the dry clothes too short in the arm, the bunk, the notebook under her pillow, the pump under her the whole night like a pulse she could count by and didn't. Toward the top of the tide she can't lie there any longer and goes up to the wheelhouse in the dark. The snow has come back with the gale and goes sideways past the windows Ned's rag has smeared (the rag: once, if used). **The snow must be falling again here** (Ch 07 s6 had it over). Ned's flashlight is down on deck at the lines.
4. Out on the pier's seaward foot a figure comes along the riprap from the town end, out of the store's sightline, slow. **One arm held across the body.** In the good hand, something square that takes Ned's light and shows red.
5. **The "and…and" peak (the chapter's one):** she is out the wheelhouse door and down the outside ladder before she has decided on any of it, and the iced rungs and the sleet in her collar and the brow lifting and dropping below her and Ned's light and the red of the case all run together in one long sentence. (The outside ladder is "my way down" in s5 and Ch 09 s4. This is its use in her body first.)
6. **Ned on the brow, in his way.** He stands mid-brow with the light on the man at its foot. His line is weather, and it's a wall: something like "Top of the tide, Marshal. She's lively on the brow." Dane answers him civilly ("Captain.") and doesn't come on. From the gate, Beth: **"Let him on, Ned."** Ned holds the light on Dane a breath longer, then backs up the brow ahead of him.
**Subtext:** She ran from Dane to the one man she never checks, and the first thing that man does is stand between her and Dane. The reader with Ned at high confidence reads a leak guarding his witness. The reader who loves Ned reads an old man protecting his Bethie from the man she ran from. Write it so both readings hold, and don't nudge either way: no look at Ned's face, no pause loaded for the reader. Dane came by road and the brow, in the open. He didn't hide doing it, and that is the first thing he tells her without speaking.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Three people on a moving brow, each in someone's way.
**Ends on:** Consequence, seen, in one long sentence of at least 20 words: Dane comes up the grinding brow and holds the red Pelican case out to her, **shut**, its catches done up, and she takes it from him in both hands. (He brings back her message, closed. Don't explain it.)
**Who knew:** Nobody learns anything new. Ned has known she's aboard since last light Thursday. His phone is on Tess's shelf at the store, so he can't pass anything on from the boat (World rule 12), and he doesn't leave the boat that night.
**Tide check:** Day 8 high water about 4:15 a.m. The ledges under the bank are awash, so Dane can't come by the shore. He took the cove road and the north road, then the Harbor Road, then the riprap below the pier (bible route map). Nobody wades.
**Texture (2):** the brow's wheels on the granite; Kenny's spare clothes.
**Word count target:** 560 (floor 540).

### Scene 2 — ACCOUNTABILITY
**Setting:** Open with the sleet for one line, coming in flat through the deckhouse door before she gets it shut behind him. Then the crew mess, a little after four on Friday. The heater, the bulkhead lamp, the red case on the table between them, the notebook under her hand. Ned is out on deck at the lines, out of earshot (the pump and the gale between). Sensory anchor: wet wool steaming off Dane at the heater, and the cold coming off the steel table under her forearms.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:**
Shape the scene this way. **He asks for the book. She doesn't give it. So he tells her what's in it, from memory, in order, and why.** That's his accountability: he knows every line he wrote about her. His register is very polite and slow, the one he uses when he's saying something once. Beth answers with verdicts. Once, she opens the book to a page and finds he has it word for word, then shuts it again. Each thing the reader already knows gets one clause; each new thing gets one sentence. Stay on her hands and the book, never on a list.
1. He names it first, plain: he investigated her. The money questions in the generator shed were for the case. He wrote down "every place we've slept." He went to bed with her and kept writing. "Walked the bar" was a lie (the third and last use of the phrase).
2. **The pouch, one line, the correction:** "On the ramp I didn't know there was any money. Nobody told me till Saturday." He was watching the pouch at Pruett because he watches whatever leaves a witness's hands. Leave that as an echo, not a quote.
3. **The money, one sentence of meaning** (the cold reader didn't get "They missed the one they'd paid"): "Somebody put nine thousand dollars in your envelope so you'd look bought on Monday."
4. **Day 5 night: why he left her, the shore light, the soaked jeans.** His report was due by two and needed sky. At the top of the tide nobody could cross the gravel bar to her, and from the ledge he could see the cove road, the only way in by land. So he left her asleep behind a door nobody could bar from outside: "Yesterday you did the same to me." The light at the Ashby end of the gravel bar, "Short. Then long.", was two men from the *Margaret Ann* on the burned shore, calling their skiff in to take them off. When the skiff's light swept the cove toward the ledge, he went down off the rock into the sea under the bank to keep out of the beam, and stood in it till the skiff had gone. That's the jeans. Then he came round to the door the long way.
5. **"Seal noted: red" (the second and last use in the book).** The book is under her hand, so he says the line from memory and gives *his* reading in one sentence: "You told me the fare bag's red. I took it for the teller writing down the one on top, and I didn't ask you, because asking was telling you what was in the other one." **Beth says nothing about her own reading.** Her hand stays on the book; she doesn't nod and doesn't open it to that page. Her reading stays out of his hearing (R3-1). Shown by what she doesn't do, without "said nothing".
6. **The fish house (pays "I'll do what I can," Ch 05).** "The fish house is in that book. It was never in a report. Portland has 'east shore.'" The book was the only place it was written, and she took the book. (This answers the Ch 07 cold reader's "taking the notebook doesn't un-report anything." Don't comment on it in narration.)
7. **The shot: one word, and no pull-out.** Pike's "Status?" got one word back: **"Graze."** His reason, in his sentence shape: if he sends Portland more than a word, Portland sends something, and whatever it sends comes down in the open, on the ballfield or at the float, where that deck can see it, and the water was shut by mid-morning anyway. "Not again today" meant the Coast Guard won't land under fire.
8. **Why nobody on the lit road came out:** Dane raises it ("A man got shot on a lit road at dawn and nobody opened a door."). Beth answers, flat and conclusion first: **"One shot over the water at first light in November. That's somebody's eiders. Nobody on this island gets out of bed for eiders."** (The scene's dark laugh. Domain check, see Flag 7.)
9. **How he found her:** "There's one boat on this island you'd sleep on." (Keep it to the boat. Don't point at Ned.)
10. **His report at four.** At the top of the north road, under sky, before he walked down: he told Pike he has been sleeping with his witness, and that she has his book. One or two sentences. Then: "That's all of it."
11. Beth stands, his phone in her fist (it's on the table where he set it down to take the case off his arm): "Give me Pike." (Or, without a question: "I'm calling her. Not you.")
**Subtext:** He's handing her the one thing he guards, a full account, because she can check it. She checks it against the page once, and he's right, and that's worse. He waited a whole night low for her at the fish house before he gave up and came by the road. Keep that unsaid here; s5 needs it as a cost. Under all of it she knows she left him exactly as he once left her, and he says so first.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. The book under her hand, held as the last thing she has over him.
**Ends on:** Cut mid-motion. She has the phone and is already out through the deckhouse door into the sleet, toward the seaward side, and he is getting up to follow with the arm held against him. The sentence is still moving when the scene stops.
**Who knew:** Not an attack. The shore light (Day 5) was the *Margaret Ann*'s men at the burned Ashby shore, signalling their own skiff. They didn't have the fish house and didn't find it (CASE FACTS: never given).
**Texture (2):** the notebook (by memory, one spoken line); the eiders.
**Word count target:** 760 (floor 720). This is the chapter's heaviest scene. If it runs to 800, take the words from s5.

### Scene 3 — VERIFICATION and CONDITION
**Setting:** The seaward side of the deckhouse, out of the harbor's sightline, where every daylight sat call is made. Friday mid-morning, about nine, the ebb running toward the late-morning low. The gale is backing, the sleet coming in squalls, open water to the south with nothing on it. Sensory anchor: sleet ticking on the phone's case held up between their two heads (the case, never a stove), and the deckhouse steel at her back.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Pike (her voice, on speaker).
**Surface action:**
1. Before she dials, Dane gives her the consequence in one line: what he told Pike at four goes to the defense whatever she says now. **"You'll hear it from the defense. Hear it from me."** (This sets up "Giglio" so the reader knows what it means when Pike says it.)
2. He can't hold the phone up past his shoulder, so she holds it. Speaker on, both heads bent to it. This is the chapter's one call. Beth: "This is Beth Calloway." Pike: "Ms. Calloway."
3. **Pike, in conditions and orders, never a question she doesn't need:** "I don't apologize for orders, Ms. Calloway. I'll tell you what they were." Then the orders, short: HSI asked for a daily log on her and Pike ordered it; she wasn't to be told; the money; "Q" is Agent Quist. Then the one that turns it: **"He's asked me for a second location every night since Monday. Every night I've said no. There isn't one that doesn't cross open water."** Pike also says that at four he reported himself, and stops one sentence short of something (her 1999 detail, unsaid; render it as a pause on the line and a change of subject).
4. **Verification, in Beth's own hands (interior passage 2 of 3):** while Pike talks, she opens the notebook in the lee and turns the nights herself, Day 2 to Day 7. Each night's entry ends in the same two words. Quote **"No indication"** once, then give the rest by what her thumb does: page after page, the same place at the bottom. No count, no list of dates, no numerals.
5. **Pike, the night's order:** nothing flies before first light tomorrow. Hold her in the most defensible place he has. Nothing else. (This is Pike refusing bait before anyone proposes it. Don't use the word "bait.") "Monday" once.
6. **Condition (Beth's, spoken to Pike, not to Dane):** **"Nothing about me goes on paper I haven't read. And everything Pike says, I hear."** (In her anger register: quieter, exact, subject back in every sentence. "I" stays in.)
7. Pike, after a pause: **"Then it's Giglio, Deputy."** Said to him, heard by her. That's the relationship on the record: the point of no return.
**Subtext:** She called Pike to catch him in a lie and caught him asking, every night, to get her moved. Being right about the order and wrong about the man is harder on her than the other way round would have been. He gives up his privacy and his clean record to give her the condition, and he lets Pike say it.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal, at one remove: three voices, one phone, sleet.
**Ends on:** Consequence, seen. The call ends. He holds out his good hand, and she puts the phone in it and the notebook back inside her own coat, against her ribs. He watches the book go and doesn't ask for it. One long sentence. Don't end on Pike's line.
**Who knew:** Portland now knows the relationship and that Beth has the book. The fish house stays "east shore" in Portland.
**Tide check:** Day 8 low about 10:30 a.m. The tide doesn't matter to this scene; they're aboard.
**Texture (2):** the Iridium on speaker, held to the sky; Giglio (said once, and its meaning given beforehand in Dane's s3 line 1).
**Word count target:** 520 (floor 495).

### Scene 4 — THE ORDINARY SCENE
**Setting:** The crew mess at noon on Friday. The mess's little oven open on a ham (Ned's, laid in for the crew's Thanksgiving; see Flag 8). The heater going, the bulkhead lamp, the pump steady under the steel, sleet on the deckhouse windows. Sensory anchor: clove and burnt sugar in a steel room, and the oven door's heat on her shins.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane; Ned comes in at the end.
**Surface action:** No plot business between the leads. Nobody mentions the case, the night, Tess as a suspect or the notebook.
1. Dane cooks with one arm and directs. Beth does the knife work with her own knife: he wants the fat scored in diamonds, she scores it in lines, and lines hold the glaze the same. (His plan first, her way at the edge; one exchange.)
2. **The laugh:** Dane: **"The ham is eleven pounds, and there are three of us."** Beth: **"That's a load."** Dane: **"It's lunch."** (See Flag 6. Never "That's not a reason.")
3. **The ordinary pleasure (interior passage 3 of 3; the bible's quiet register):** three or four sentences of room for one plain good thing. The oven door's heat on her shins in a cold boat, the smell filling the steel room, a fork of the first slice off the end, too hot, and she eats it anyway. She enjoys it without comment.
4. **Ned and Tess, as island talk (this plants the why; play it as gossip and a laugh, nothing more).** Dane asks where the third plate goes. Beth: Ned eats at Tess's on a Friday (new fact, Flag 4), and every New Year's he asks her to marry him. Dane: "What does she say?" Beth: **"'Ask me sober, Haskell.'"** Dane: "How long's he been sober?" Beth: **"Twenty-two years."** Dane, his prediction for the chapter (one at most): "He'll ask her this year." Beth: "He asks her every year." No narration about what it means.
5. **The longing:** there's glaze at the corner of his mouth. She wipes it with her thumb, and the thumb is slow coming away. She wants to ask him something about after the barge, has it ready and doesn't ask. Show it in one observable beat (she puts the knife down and picks it up again; she starts a sentence with "When" and turns it into one about the ham). One or two lines, no more. Don't use "kept her thumb"; "slow coming away" or an equivalent.
6. Ned comes in out of the sleet, stamping, and stops at the sight of the ham.
**Subtext:** Two people who spent the morning on the record take an hour off it. Ned asking out loud every year, and taking his answer, is the thing Beth can't do yet: ask for what she wants before she has checked it. The reader may hear the other thing too, a man who would do anything for Tess, and the page says nothing about it.
**Tension source:** Low by design. Friction only: diamonds against lines, his talk against her four words, and the question she doesn't ask.
**Ends on:** Comic, mid-action. Ned, weather first, at the ham: **"Wind's backing. Somebody expecting company?"** Then, before anyone answers, Dane is already carving him a slab off the end, one-handed, and the sentence keeps going as Ned sits.
**Texture (1):** the scored fat and the glaze.
**Word count target:** 470 (floor 445).

### Scene 5 — THE TURN
**Setting:** The wheelhouse, Friday afternoon, about half past three, the high water coming at about twenty to five. The squalls are thinning, the wind backing northwest, the light going early. Through the windows Ned's rag has smeared: the harbor, the *Margaret Ann* on her new mooring with the town landing in front of her bow, the store's windows yellow on its generator. In the lee of the pier wall, where the wind hasn't scoured the snow, one line of footprints still comes down the pier road to the riprap. Sensory anchor: the cold of the chart table through her sleeves, and the snow-light flat on the windows.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:**
1. **The plan for the night, as his friction against hers.** Dane proposes his plan first: the fish house at the night low, when the shore is dry from about eight. Beth, looking at the line of prints in the lee of the wall: **"Anybody can walk it backward."** One clause: his tracks run from that wall back over the north road and down the cove road to her father's door, and it stopped snowing at first light. Then: **"They burned Ashby."** (As fact, not a maxim.)
2. Her place, said as positions, conclusion first: **"Steel doesn't burn."** Dane: **"Everything in it does. Your captain told me that the first night."** Beth: **"Tonight the stern's watched, I'm awake, and the outside ladder's my way down."** (Pike's order in s3 was the most defensible place. This is it, in her words.)
3. **Her case for Tess, aloud.** Beth starts it herself, before he asks: somebody tells them where she is tonight, so here is who. Short, certain, conclusion first, each item a fact she has seen with her own eyes. Don't make it an inventory: no more than one sentence an item, and let Dane break in twice. Items, in this order:
   - the light on her back steps that Tess logged and sat on;
   - Ashby found by its smoke;
   - the truck in the Ashby drive the night of the fire;
   - the county call Tess kept to herself;
   - the hundreds in the green ledger and the night fuel;
   - **Ashby written on her hand Tuesday morning** (this pays Tess's hand aloud, once);
   - the truck keys on the hook and **the town ring by the safe**, behind a curtain that doesn't shut;
   - Tess out on her rounds every midday, the store shut behind her;
   - Arsenault at the counter: "Store like this lives on the boat," and Tess laughing.
   **No seal.** In her head, one clause only, while she talks: she leaves the seal out, because her envelope rode in Tess's bag and the teller wrote down the boat's. (This makes the Ch 07 s6 reading plain for the reader who missed it, and is never spoken. See Flag 2.)
