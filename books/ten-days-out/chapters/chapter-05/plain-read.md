# Plain read: Chapter 5, "Good Till Next August"

Draft read: `chapters/chapter-05/final.md` (Ch 01 to 04 `final.md` read first for context). Read as a half-listening text-to-speech listener.

## 1. Retell the chapter

On Saturday morning Beth buys a box of condoms at the island store, in front of Tess (who runs the store) and two fishermen, and Dane stands right behind her. Tess tells them Cole lent her money for her store, so Beth's testimony is going to ruin her. Then she hugs Beth anyway. Dane asks Tess about the fake call from the empty Dyer house last night. Tess says the man spoke low with his hand over the phone, and Dane thinks that means Tess would have known the voice. Tess's phone rings at the same moment Russ is on his phone outside, and Dane notices. Russ takes them to an empty summer house with a broken window, and they find where the man with the dragging foot has been hiding: stew, dishes washed, a chair at the window facing the road, and Beth's newspaper picture pinned to the wall. The picture came off Tess's board in the store. Russ says the man is "the kid" who worked on Cole's boat and lived in Cole's trailer, and nobody knows his real name. Beth insists on a real house with food, so Russ gives her the key to a north-side house. They tell Ned and Tess where they'll be, and Dane asks his boss for all three islanders' phone records. Tess says the picture went missing in early September, which means someone was after Beth months before she saw the drugs.

## 2. What's at stake

Beth is the only witness against Cole Varney, and the man with the dragging foot, plus someone on the island helping him, is trying to kill her before the grand jury on Monday the 23rd. If she dies or doesn't testify, Cole goes free. In this chapter the danger moves closer: the helper could be one of the three people she trusts most (Tess, Russ, Ned).

## 3. The romance

Yes, I felt it, mostly from Dane's side. The condom purchase made it plain that they're planning to sleep together tonight, and it was funny. The line that got me:

> "He wanted tonight: a bed, a door that locked, the whole night with her."

Also the truck: "Beth's thigh was against Dane's, warm through two layers of denim, and he wanted badly to put his left hand on it." And at the picture, she leans into his hand on her back.

From Beth's side I only got it through her buying the condoms, plus leaning into his hand. For the rest of the chapter she argues with him. The line "Her hand sat on his arm the whole time she fought him, and she hadn't noticed" is the kind of small thing I could miss while listening, but it's said outright, so I caught it.

## 4. Words I didn't know

None of these stopped me understanding what was happening, but two slowed me down (marked *).

- "I'll carry you the penny." I guessed it means "I'll cover you for the penny." Island talk.
- * "you just put my store under, Calloway." I took "put under" to mean "made me go broke," but I wasn't sure. I also didn't know why: does Cole call in the loan if he's charged, or does she just lose him as a customer? This is Tess's reason to betray Beth, so it matters, and it came to me half-understood.
- "You can expense it." An office word. I got the joke.
- "Your marshal looks at my hands like he's pricing them." I didn't get this one. Is he suspicious of her, or checking her for something?
- "Screen said Dyer." I took this as caller ID showing the Dyer house. Fine.
- "Then my office has nothing to run." I guessed "run" means look someone up in police records. Police talk, but I could guess it from "No name, no pay stub, no paper."
- "nine more nights on nobody." A strange way to say it. I took it as "nine more nights with nobody knowing where I am."
- "captain's license" / "lady captain." Not a hard word, but in Chapter 1 she chained down trucks on the ferry, so I didn't know she was a captain. Small surprise.

## 5. Where I lost the thread

- "Cole Varney lent me the money for my new fuel tanks... and you just put my store under, Calloway." Then she hugs Beth. I couldn't tell whether Tess was angry, joking or scared. The hug right after the accusation left me unsure how to read her, and she's a suspect.
- "Russ answered before he'd finished. 'Nobody. Who'd I tell?' His eyes went to the mirror, down the hill to the store roof." I think this means Russ told Tess, but I only got that because the next line says Dane watched who a man looked at. Listening, a glance at a mirror toward a store roof is a picture I'd have to build in my head, and I nearly missed it. If Russ's look is meant to point at Tess, it went by fast.
- "smoke was coming off the chimney of a house that ought to be dead cold" next to "the snow lay smooth on the front steps." I worked out that the smoke means someone is inside and the smooth snow means he came in the back. It took a second, but I got it.
- "That's four people who know where you'll be tonight." I had to count: Tess, Russ, Ned and the boss. Fine once I counted.
- The ending, "In September I hadn't seen anything." I understood what it means (someone was watching her before the drugs), but I don't know why, and I think that's on purpose. It made me want the next chapter.

## 6. Verdict: PASS

I could retell the main event: finding the kid's hideout with Beth's picture on the wall, the picture tracing back to Tess's store, and the September date. I could say what's at stake. The romance moved, and I felt it through the condom scene and Dane wanting the whole night with her. No plot-critical fact reached me only through a word I didn't know.

Two soft spots for the author:
1. Tess's motive ("put my store under") reached me half-understood. One plain clause saying what happens to her loan if Cole is charged would fix it.
2. The hint against Russ (his eyes going to the store roof in the mirror) is a picture, not a statement. A half-listening listener may not get it.
