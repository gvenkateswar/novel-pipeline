## Where I got bored

- **The second store visit.** From "The store was nearly empty when they came back in the early afternoon" to "Tess rang it up." It's a grocery list, Ned dropping by to say a few homely lines, and a bundle of candles. Nothing turns until Beth looks at the pale square on the cork board. I skimmed. Ned's visit in particular ("She'll feed you. She won't be nice about it.") felt like he walked on to be counted for the "three islanders know" line, then left.
- **The drive up the east road.** "Beth's thigh was against Dane's, warm through two layers of denim, and he wanted badly to put his left hand on it. He kept it on his knee." I've now read a version of "he wanted X, he didn't do X" in every chapter. By here it doesn't register as tension. It registers as filler.
- **The phone call with the boss.** Every call goes the same way: the boss says "Dane," says something curt, and hangs up. "The phone companies take three days," his boss said, and hung up. I knew how it would go before it started.

## Where I got confused

- **Tess's fuel-tank line.** "Cole Varney lent me the money for my new fuel tanks," Tess announced, "and you just put my store under, Calloway." Then she hugs Beth. Is she angry? Joking? Scared? Is she saying she owes Cole and can't pay now? I read it three times and I still don't know what she feels. Also, how do Tess and two random lobstermen already know Beth is the witness against Cole? I assume island gossip, but the book never shows it travelling, and that matters in a chapter about who knows what.
- **The dialogue order in the store.** Tess explains the Dyer call, then right away says "Your marshal looks at my hands like he's pricing them," to Beth's back, then there's a paragraph of Dane thinking about the Dyer house's sightline, and then Dane answers the earlier point. The lines don't connect in order. I lost track of who was answering what.
- **Why bring Beth to the summer house at all?** Dane leaves her alone in a running truck while both armed men go in after a shooter who might be inside. Then she walks in anyway, and he has no reaction to her disobeying a direct safety order. No comment, no flash of anger. Nothing.
- **Beth's job.** The photo is "the day she got her captain's license." But since chapter 1 she's been chaining trucks and tying lines while Ned captains. I don't know if that's a plant or a slip. I noticed it either way.
- **The footprints.** The man was sitting in the chair watching them drive up and walked out the back as they came in, but the prints already have "the edges already softening under new snow." Was he there just now or not?

## Where I stopped believing

- **Russ knew who the kid was all along.** In chapter 2 the Pruett boatman describes a "skinny young fella with a bad leg," and Beth says flat out the man from the truck drags his left foot. Russ is the constable on an island where everyone knows everyone. Now, two days and one shooting later, he says, "That's the kid off Cole's boat... worked the back of Cole's boat two summers... lived in Cole's trailer." He drove past that trailer at dawn. Why didn't he say this on the pier in chapter 2? Either he's hiding something, and the book doesn't seem to notice, or it's convenient timing. Beth works the ferry every day, so she must have seen this kid on and off the island for two summers. In chapter 1 she looked right at his face and didn't know him.
- **"I saw it at five, looking for you. Didn't think anything of it till the light came up."** Russ is a constable. His house was just shot up by a man who's still loose. He sees a broken window on a shuttered summer house and thinks nothing of it? No.
- **A witness out shopping.** Hours after a man shot through a window at her, the marshal walks his only witness into the island store at nine a.m. to buy condoms in front of an audience. That's charming as a scene. It doesn't fit the Dane of chapters 1–3, who couldn't stop thinking about exposure.
- **Dane gives up the secret location in about two lines.** He says "Nobody hears where you sleep tonight. Nobody." One beat later Beth says she needs a key and groceries, and he answers "Fair... I hate it, and it's fair." Within the hour the location has been said out loud to Russ, Tess, Ned and the boss. Russ's house got shot up because the whole road knew where she was, and the book itself made that point. So giving in this fast doesn't feel like his character. It feels like the plot needs her to be findable tonight. And because it's so obvious, I'm now sure the north-side house gets hit. That kills suspense for the next chapter instead of building it.
- **The Tess phone / Russ phone moment.** She answers on the first buzz, says "Not now" with her back turned, and through the window Russ is just lowering his phone. Then in the truck Russ glances at the mirror toward the store roof when asked who he told. It's laid on so heavily it reads as a red herring with a neon sign on it. I don't suspect them. I suspect the book is pointing me at them.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Declared desire, every chapter, same build:** "He wanted tonight: a bed, a door that locked, the whole night with her." / "he wanted badly to put his left hand on it." / "He wanted to put his mouth to her hair, here... and he didn't." That's three in one chapter. Earlier chapters do it too ("She wanted them on her," "He wanted her," "She wanted him").
- **Feelings narrated like a checklist:** "Dane was afraid, and he let himself know it." That's the same move as chapter 4's "she let herself shake." It tells me the character is allowing an emotion instead of showing me one.
- **Recycled gestures:**
  - Ned "turned once and lifted a hand" at the door. He did exactly that walking up the harbor road in chapter 2.
  - Russ takes his cap off and puts it back on, again.
  - Snow "came flat off the harbor." Snow has come flat in nearly every chapter.
  - Dane speaks "polite and slow" with "Ms. Calloway," which is his chapter-4 move again.
  - Cole's lawyer "in a good coat," asking "slowly," is the chapter-3 image again almost word for word.
  - "The white rope scar" gets a mention again right after its debut.
- **"Her hand sat on his arm the whole time she fought him, and she hadn't noticed. He had."** This is the chapter-1 trick ("she didn't seem to have noticed") with a neat little two-beat button on it.
- **Everyone talks in the same clipped, dry, laconic way.** Tess, Russ, Ned, Beth, Dane and the boss all speak in short flat declaratives with a wry edge. Without the tags I couldn't tell Russ from Ned. Tess has a bit more color, but she still sounds like the narrator.
- **Rhythm.** Nearly every sentence in the chapter is short and declarative. After five chapters the even beat lulls me. It's competent, and it never changes speed, not even for the photo on the wall.
- **Scene endings.** Each scene closes on a tidy button: "He wasn't sorry." / "He knew the face before he was close enough to see it." / "In September I hadn't seen anything." The last one earns it. The pattern is still visible.

## What I want to know next

These are real questions, and they're why I'd turn the page:

1. **Who wanted Beth's picture in September,** before she'd seen anything? This is the best hook in the book so far. It changes the story from "witness hunted for what she saw" to "someone was already fixed on her." Is the kid obsessed with her? Was she already a target for some other reason?
2. **What is the kid's name, and why does nobody know it?** "I don't think anybody ever asked his name" is quietly chilling.
3. **Who is the second person,** the one who knew the Dyer house was empty and made the call? The chapter offers Tess and Russ, but my money is on Ned. He was afraid in chapter 1, told her she "didn't see it," is her father's oldest friend, and is now one of the three who know where she'll sleep.
4. **What is Tess's debt to Cole really about?**

Prediction, which is also a complaint: the north-side house gets attacked tonight, probably during or right after the scene the condoms set up. I'm reading on to find out who sold her out, not to find out whether she gets found. The book has already told me she will.

## Listener pass (if heard aloud)

- The Tess section would be hard to follow in audio. "Tess told Beth's back" comes after Tess's own previous line with a narration paragraph in between, so a listener would lose who's talking.
- The boss's lines on speaker, Beth's interjection, and Dane's replies all run through "Dane said" with almost no voice difference. In audio it would blur into one person.
- The run of short flat sentences would sound like a metronome when read aloud.

## Best moment

The summer-house kitchen, all of it, but especially this:

> The stove was warm under his palm. Cans of stew, a phone charger coiled on the table, the dishes washed and stacked, wet wool socks hung in a neat row by the stove. A kitchen chair stood at the front window, facing down the road they had come up.

and then:

> Russ stood at the sink, looking at the dishes. "He washed up," he said. "I don't wash up."

The neat socks, the washed dishes and the chair aimed down the road are creepier than any of the gunfire in chapters 3 and 4. It's the first time the man feels like a person instead of a limp and a flashlight. Close behind it: "I look like I'm being arrested." / "You look like you're about to tell somebody to move." That's the one exchange in the chapter where both leads sound like themselves and like nobody else.
