## Flags (read before drafting)

1. **The Dyer house has moved, and the bible hasn't caught up.** Ch 04 final (and the log) puts it "out on the point across the harbor, an hour round by the road." The bible's binding geography still says "above the harbor road on the hill behind the village." These beats follow Ch 04. Ch 10–11 still work, because from the point its upstairs window looks back across the water at the church, the school, the loft and the boathouse. The bible line needs updating by the orchestrator or the author. **Ch 05 uses the new position for a new fact:** from the point you can see Russ's lit porch across the water (Scene 1).
2. **Scene order is changed inside the chapter; no outcome changes.** The outline has the next bed decided at the store before the search. Here it's decided at the camp, after the photo, and Tess and Ned are told when Beth goes back to the store for groceries in the early afternoon. Three reasons. (a) Ch 04 ended on a talk scene (Russ's Q&A, which the cold reader called out), and the outline's s1 would make a second one in a row. (b) The agent's people cap: the outline's s1 puts four or five named people at one counter. (c) The photo gives Dane his strongest argument for telling nobody, so Beth's win costs more. What stays the same: the same four people know the north-side house on Day 2. Beth tells Ned herself, at the store counter, in front of Dane [OD], and she tells Tess and Russ the same way. The lobstermen are gone before the bed is decided (NB2). Dane asks for the records on the store steps with Beth arguing against it.
3. **People cap.** Scene 1 has Dane, Beth and Tess, with Russ outside on the steps, seen through the window. Scenes 2–3 have Dane, Beth and Russ. Scene 4 is staged so only three named people are ever present: Tess is in the back room filling the grocery box while Beth tells Ned, Ned leaves, and then Tess comes out. If the orchestrator wants a literal split, cut Scene 4 at Ned's exit. The chapter total (1,725) can't carry a fifth scene of any size.
4. **Two new facts that later chapters must honor (new commitments).**
   - **(a) Tess's account:** the caller spoke low, as if his hand were over the phone, and said to send Russ because *he could see his light from there*. That's true to canon, because Ned made the call from the Dyer house, which looks across the harbor at Russ's lit porch. **Ch 12 should let Ned own it in one clause** if the beats have room ("I sat at her window and watched Russ's porch till I could see you'd gone in"). Ch 10 gets an echo for free when Beth and Dane sit at that same window.
   - **(b) The photo** on the kid's wall is Tess's own copy, from the board by her register, and it **has been gone since the first week of September**, two months before Beth saw anything. That's the chapter's new end question. **Recommended payoff: Ch 16 s3,** the kid cuffed on the cabin floor: "Cole had me take her picture off the store wall in September so I'd know her. He said if anybody ever looked in a crate, it'd be the girl on the boat." That keeps the answer on Cole and on Beth's competence and off Ned. A heavier option for Ch 12 is for author approval only: Ned says Cole asked him in August who on the boat would notice, and he said nobody but Bethie. **Fallback if the orchestrator won't take a new commitment:** Tess says the picture went "a while back. I figured some summer kid," and the chapter ends on the outline's line plus Dane: "And two of them were on the phone to each other this morning." That fallback loses the new question.
   - Check against World rule 12: September points at Cole, who has had Beth on his deck every Thursday for six years. The kid could walk into the store openly in September and took the picture himself. Nobody on the island had to hand it to him. In Dane's head the suspicion stays with "the islanders as a group."
5. **Outline wording vs the page.** The outline says "an unheated loft." Ch 04's loft has a kerosene heater, so use "a loft Russ already found, with no food in it." The bible says landlines work through Day 1 night, so the phone Tess snatches up and hangs up is **her cell, face up on the counter**. The Dyer call she describes came in Friday night on the store line, so her "screen said Dyer" is fine.
6. **Opening line.** The cold reader flagged both-full-names openings as a template (Ch 02, 03, 04). Use: **"Dane Reyes stood behind Beth at the island store at nine in the morning while she bought condoms in front of Tess Marr, two lobstermen and God."** It still names the POV character first (World rule 4).
7. **The clock.** The bible asks for the clock once a chapter, and the reader is tired of it (Ch 03 notes). Resolution: no clock sentence this chapter. The deadline appears only as Beth's count "nine more nights" (Scene 3), which does a different job. Nobody says "Cole walks," "the grand jury" or "her eyes are the case."
8. **Outline lines checked for contrast constructions.** All pass and can be used word for word. One change: the bible gives Russ "Who'd I tell? Nobody. Who'd I tell." (twice, the second to himself). The cold reader flagged Russ's self-answering as a tic (Ch 02, 03), so he says it **once**: "Nobody. Who'd I tell?"
9. **Title and motif cap.** The title "Good Till Next August" plus Tess's line puts `good till` at its cap of 2 if the audit counts headings. No third use anywhere in the book.

### Scene 1
**Setting:** The island store, nine in the morning, Saturday, Day 2. The store is kept at fifty-eight degrees. There's the coffee urn on the woodstove, the bell on the door, and the cork board by the register crowded with notices. The power has been out island-wide since Friday afternoon, so the light is gray daylight from the front windows. The anchor is the smell of coffee and kerosene.
**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Tess. Two lobstermen are at the counter, unnamed, with no lines. Russ is outside on the steps, seen through the front window, not in the room.
**Want:** Beth wants to buy the condoms in public and not be embarrassed, which is the joke (bible: she says the embarrassing thing on purpose). Dane wants to learn from Tess exactly how the Dyer call came in, and Tess's speed is in the way. Tess wants Beth to know she's angry and loves her anyway, and she wants her phone call to stay private.
**Surface action:**
1. Open on the line above (Flag 6). Beth sets a small box on the counter and puts down a five. The lobstermen look at the box, then at Dane. Tess announces to the whole store: **"Four seventy-nine. Good till next August."** Dane: **"I'd have asked for a receipt."**
2. Dane's head, in one long sentence: Cole's lawyer in a good coat asking the two lobstermen, slowly, what the witness bought on Saturday morning. Then, plainly, he isn't sorry. Longing (form: memory into want): the net loft, the orange heater, her bare feet on his shins, and that she's buying these for tonight. Say what he wants in plain words, once.
3. Beth takes her change, a penny short, and Tess lets her owe it, which Beth hates. Beth zips the box into the zip pocket inside her coat **(plant, Ch 07).**
4. Tess, bitter, loud: **"Cole Varney lent me the money for my new fuel tanks, and you just put my store under, Calloway."** Then she comes round the counter and hugs Beth hard, eyes shut, cheek on her shoulder. The lobstermen leave. The bell.
5. Beth goes to the urn on the woodstove for two coffees, ten feet away and in earshot. Dane takes the chair by the stove with its back to the wall. Beth takes the other one without being asked (bible), then gets up for the coffee.
6. Dane asks Tess about the call. **Tess's one speech this chapter (World rule 19), four sentences at most:** "Screen said Dyer. A man, talking low, like he had his hand over it. He said send Russ, he could see his light from there." Then her defense: **"I sent Russ because the man said a tree was through the roof. What was I supposed to do, ask for ID?"**
7. Dane watches her hands squaring a rack of lighters that's already square. Tess: **"Your marshal looks at my hands like he's pricing them."** (laugh 2)
8. Dane, in his head: the Dyer house is out on the point across the harbor, and from its windows you'd look straight across the black water at the one lit porch on the harbor road. Somebody had sat in Mrs. Dyer's dark house and watched Russ on that porch, and then picked up her phone. Out loud: **"A man covers the phone when you'd know his voice."** Tess, too fast: **"Well, I didn't."**
9. Tess's cell, face up on the counter, buzzes. She has it before the first buzz is done, back half-turned, low: "Not now." Beth comes back with the two coffees. Tess hangs up and turns the phone face down. Through the front window Dane sees Russ on the steps lowering his own phone into his coat.
10. At the door, low, Dane tells Beth what he saw (World rule 9: nothing kept from her): **"Her phone rang while Russ had his at his ear."** Beth's flat verdict: **"Russ talks to Tess every day of his life."** (That's true and innocent, and it's the Book 2 couple hiding in plain sight.)
11. Dane opens the door. Russ on the steps, snow on his shoulders: **"Summer house up the east road has its back window out. I saw it at five, looking for you."**
**Subtext:** Tess is scared, because the call she relayed nearly got Beth killed and she's half thought about whose voice it was. Her speed is guilt about the wrong thing (the hang-up is Russ). Dane is thinking about the islanders as a group: everybody on this island is keeping something, and two of them are keeping it together. He accuses nobody in his head (World rule 12). Beth refuses to let the gossip shame her, and the purchase is a public claim on Dane.
**Tension source:** Comedy with an edge, because the whole island will know by noon. Then Tess's account turns cold: someone sat in a dark house watching. Then a phone hung up too fast.
**Texture budget:** (1) The caller ID on the store line Friday night ("screen said Dyer") shows the call really came from that house. (2) Tess's cell, face up and then face down, shows a private call she won't take in front of Beth.
**Point of view:** Dane.
**Ends on:** *Dialogue (consequence).* Russ's line about the broken back window. It sends them up the east road.
**Word target:** 475

### Scene 2
**Setting:** The east road and the shut summer house the kid broke into. Late morning, in falling snow. Russ's truck has three across the bench seat. The house has shingles gone silver and a back window boarded with nothing; a drift has come in under it since Thursday night. The anchor is the warmth that hits Dane's face when he opens the kitchen door, in a house that's supposed to be dead cold.
**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Russ.
**Want:** Dane wants to find the man, or proof of where he went, without putting Beth in range. Russ wants to be taken seriously as the constable and not be asked where he was. Beth wants to see it herself, and she wants Dane back out of that house.
**Surface action:**
1. In the truck, climbing the east road. Beth sits in the middle, her thigh against Dane's. Longing (form: the body, plain): he notices the heat of her leg through two layers of denim and wants nothing on earth so much as to put his hand on it. He keeps it on the door handle, because his gun hand is the one beside her.
2. Dane, eyes on the road: **"Who'd you tell she was sleeping at your place?"** Russ answers before Dane has finished: **"Nobody. Who'd I tell?"** His eyes go to the mirror, back down the hill toward the village and the store roof. Dane notes who a man looks at before he answers. Nobody says anything more. Beth looks out her side.
3. Russ stops below the house. The dread beat, which the 15% slowdown asks for: the house is shut, the snow is untracked on the front steps, and a stove's worth of heat is coming off the chimney. Dane plainly is afraid: there's a man with a gun who walked up to a window and looked in before he fired, and Beth is in the truck.
4. Dane to Beth: **"Engine running, doors locked. If you hear a shot, you drive to the store and you don't stop."** Beth, quiet, the way fear takes her: **"Go."**
5. Dane takes the front, Russ with his shotgun the back. Dane talks low the whole way in so Russ knows where he is (his fear register), one room at a time: kitchen, front room, the back bedroom. Nobody.
6. What's there (bible's camp list), seen by Dane's eye for what hands have done: the stove still warm, canned stew, a phone charger coiled on the table, the dishes washed and stacked, wet wool socks hung neatly in a row by the stove, a kitchen chair pulled up to the front window that looks down the east road the way they came.
7. Out the back door, a single line of boot prints goes down through the snow toward the shore rocks: one clean print, then the long smear the other foot leaves, already softening under new snow. **The turn:** he sat at that window, watched Russ's truck come up the road, and walked out the back.
8. Russ at the sink: **"He washed up. I don't wash up."** (laugh, dry, with the creep in it)
9. Dane turns from the window and sees a square of newsprint pinned to the wall by the stove with a thumbtack. A face. He knows the face before he's close enough to see it.
**Subtext:** Russ's lie sits in the truck with them all the way up. Dane can't prove it and has to turn his back on Russ with a shotgun in the next room anyway. The ordinary neatness frightens him more than a mess would: this is a calm man.
**Tension source:** Situational dread (is he inside?), then the realization that he was watching them come, and then the face on the wall.
**Texture budget:** (1) The sharp-then-softening edges of the prints in falling snow show how recently he left. (2) The chair turned to the window shows that he watches the road.
**Point of view:** Dane.
**Ends on:** *Open image (prop).* The newsprint face on the wall, unnamed in this scene. (This is one of the chapter's two allowed prop or withheld endings.)
**Word target:** 325

### Scene 3
**Setting:** The same kitchen, minutes later. Wet socks steaming faintly by the stove, gray light from the front window, snow ticking on the glass. The anchor is canned stew and wet wool drying.
**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Russ.
**Want:** Beth wants to look at it without falling apart in front of Russ. Dane wants her out of this house and then wants nobody told where she sleeps tonight. Beth wants a key and groceries and her own people. Russ wants to help and to be useful.
**Surface action:**
1. Beth is in the kitchen doorway. She came up when Dane didn't come out, which he should have expected. She sees the picture. It's her at thirty in her orange work coat at the ferry's wheel, on the day she got her captain's license, from the island newsletter. She goes silent (her fear register). Her bare hand comes up toward it and stops an inch short.
2. Dane puts his hand flat at the small of her back. She leans back into it and lets it stay. **Longing anchor (form: restraint):** he wants to put his mouth to her hair, here in the kid's kitchen, and he doesn't. He keeps his hand where it is and his eyes on the back door.
3. Beth's flat verdict, voice not quite right: **"I look like I'm being arrested."** (laugh, the one that hurts)
4. Then she looks properly, at the brown ring from a coffee mug across one corner and four old pinholes. **New fact:** **"That's Tess's. Off the board by her register. She put it up the week it ran."** Three years on Tess's board. Beth never looks at it, because she hates it. She doesn't know when it went.
5. Russ, looking at the picture and not at her, gives the kid's history in his slow way: **"That's Cole's kid. Worked the back of Cole's boat two summers for cash and lived in that trailer of Cole's out the east road. It's been empty since Thursday. Everybody called him kid. I don't think anybody ever asked him his name."** Dane, one line, closes the records option: no name, no pay stub, no paper, so the office has nothing to run.
6. Russ: **"I could sit on this house."** Dane: **"He watched your truck come up the road. He won't come back to tire tracks at the door."** He'll go to another shut house, and there are plenty.
7. Dane, at the window with his gun out, wants out of here now: **"Nobody hears where you sleep tonight. Nobody."** Beth's argument, four lines, face to face, fast: **"Russ sat on his porch with a shotgun in a storm. The whole road knew where I was, and anybody could walk into the Dyer house. The loft's found, and there's no food in it. I can't do nine more nights without a key and groceries."** Then the line that wins: **"If Russ wanted me dead, he had me asleep in his kitchen."**
8. Dane has no answer and says so: **"Fair."** Longing, second touch: she's rude to him and right, and it's the thing about her he likes best. Keep it to one clause, no verdict.
9. Beth turns to Russ in front of Dane: **"You've got the key to the north-side house."** Russ is already working it off his ring. She takes it in her bare palm, over the rope scar.
10. Dane, polite and slow, the anger register: **"Then my boss hears it too. And I'm asking for all three of their phones."**
**Subtext:** Beth is terrified and won't let the kid take her people from her too. Dane knows a shared secret is a leaked one and loses anyway, because she's right that they can't live nine nights on nothing. Russ is glad to be needed, and he has a lie sitting under it.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. It's the chapter's real fight, argued to their faces, and both are partly right (outline one-talk sentence for Ch 05). It happens in a room where the gunman's socks are still drying.
**Texture budget:** (1) The coffee ring and pinholes identify Tess's copy. (2) The key coming off Russ's ring makes the choice irreversible in his hand.
**Point of view:** Dane.
**Ends on:** *Dialogue (consequence).* Dane's two lines, the counter-move Scene 4 carries out.
**Word target:** 500

### Scene 4
**Setting:** The island store, early afternoon, then its front steps. The store is nearly empty, with the urn ticking on the woodstove. Tess is in the back room filling a cardboard box from Beth's list. On the steps the snow is coming flat off the harbor, and there's one bar of signal. The anchor is the cork board by the register, where a pale square on the cork has four pinholes at its corners.
**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Ned (first half); Dane, Beth, Tess (second half); the boss on speaker (on the steps). Never more than three named people in the room at once (Flag 3).
**Want:** Beth wants to tell her people herself, the way she'd want to be told. Ned wants to give her something; he always does. Tess wants to be told and to be useful. Dane wants the records and wants to say, once, what four people knowing means.
**Surface action:**
1. Ned at the counter with his coffee, big and stooped, both hands round the mug. Dane watches the hands: steady, a ferry captain's. Beth tells him straight: **"We're at the north-side house tonight. The one Russ looks after."**
2. Ned answers with the weather first (bible): **"She'll blow till Monday, Bethie."** Then: **"Russ keeps that place tight."** He pushes his coffee across the counter to her. She takes it. To Dane, in his slow understatement: **"She'll feed you. She won't be nice about it."** (laugh) *World rule 12: Ned gets two short lines and the coffee and nothing else. No extra detail, no pause, no look the narration lingers on.*
3. Ned goes. At the door he turns back once and lifts a hand (the plant), and the bell rings behind him.
4. Tess comes out of the back with the box: canned beans, onions, a stick of butter, candles. Beth tells her: **"North-side house. Russ's. Food for two."** Tess: **"I put in candles."**
5. Beth looks at the pale square on the cork board. **"When did my picture go off your board?"** Tess: **"First week of September. I figured some summer kid wanted the island's lady captain."** Beth: **"It's on a wall up the east road."** Tess's hands stop on the box. **"Beth."** It's the first time she's used the name all chapter, and Dane hears that it means this is serious.
6. The steps. Dane calls his boss on speaker, Beth beside him with the box on her hip and Ned's coffee in her other hand. It's his second call of the day; the morning report happened off page between the loft and the store, so mark it with one phrase from the boss at most and no recap. Dane gives the north-side summer house and says three islanders know it: Tess Marr, Russ Whitcomb, Ned Haskell. Then: he wants all three phone records.
7. Beth argues it to his face over the phone: **"Those three are mine, Dane."** Dane: **"Then the records will say so, and I'll say I was wrong in front of Tess."** The boss: **"The carriers take three days."** The boss asks no other question.
8. Longing (form: closeness under argument): she's leaning on his arm on the cold step while she fights him, and she doesn't seem to know it. He does. One plain sentence.
9. Phone away. Dane: **"That's four people who know where you'll be tonight."** Beth: **"Three of them would die for me."** Dane: **"Somebody wanted your face on a wall in September."** Beth: **"In September I hadn't seen anything."**
**Subtext:** Beth gives her people the only thing she can, her trust out loud, and she watches Dane take a little of it back with the records. Dane knows the records are three days away and tonight is tonight. Neither of them says what September means, because neither knows.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal, and both are partly right. Under it, the new fact: someone picked her out before Thursday.
**Texture budget:** (1) The speakerphone on the steps, with one bar: he reports in front of her (World rule 9). (2) "The carriers take three days," which says plainly what records are and when they come.
