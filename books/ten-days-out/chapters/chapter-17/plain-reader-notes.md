# Plain Reader Notes: Chapter 18, "Full Price"

**Draft read:** `chapters/chapter-18/final.md` (Nov 6, 2026). I read chapters 01 through 18 first, only the `final.md` in each folder, in order. I read nothing else.

## 1. Retelling

It's Thanksgiving morning. Beth is bailing water out of the old wooden lobster boat Dane bought over the phone without seeing it. He can only scoop with a coffee mug because of his stitches, and he finally has proper boots, which Tess charged him "the marshal price" for. They bet a dollar on whether the boat will be dry by noon. Beth loses and pays him, and he keeps the dollar. Back at her house Dane cooks a ham. Beth opens a short pencil letter from Ned in jail: his son is in the clinic, and Ned has taken his name off his letter backing her for the ferry captain job, because a word from him would hurt her now. So Beth needs someone from the island to vouch for her before the board decides in June, and she decides to ask Tess. Russ comes on a cane and Tess comes with a pie, still barely speaking to Beth. Over dinner Dane says he starts at the Maine office in January and asks Russ and Tess if he can live on the island with Beth. Russ says yes, and Tess says "Coffee's a dollar, Marshal. Same as everybody," which I took as a yes. Washing up, Beth asks Tess to speak for her. Tess has already written to the board, but the two of them are still sore with each other. At the end there's a light out on the water where the big boat was. Dane says the kid didn't plant the phone on the ferry, so someone on the island did, and Russ and Tess say they'll find out who. Then Dane bolts the door and Beth asks him, "Are you any good at your job?" He says, "Ask me every night."

## 2. What's at stake

Beth could lose the captain's job in June, because the man who was going to recommend her turned out to be the man who sold her out, and someone else from the island has to vouch for her. Beyond that, the drug people are still out there: the boat with one light is back, Cole's people burned a witness's house in New Bedford after she testified, and someone on Cutter (not the kid) planted the tracking phone on her ferry and hasn't been caught. Dane no longer has a gun.

## 3. The romance

Yes, I felt it. It's the settled, happy kind now, not the hungry kind. This chapter has no kiss and no sex, but I never doubted they're together and staying together. The moments I felt it:

- "She wanted him, in those boots, on his leaking boat. She kept bailing." It's told outright, so I got it.
- Dane at the table: "I'd like to stay on this island and live in this house with Beth, and she's said yes." That's said out loud in front of everyone, and I couldn't miss it.
- "Under the table Beth's knee was against his. She watched his mouth while he said it, and she didn't care who saw."
- The last lines: "So. Are you any good at your job?" / "Ask me every night." I caught this because in Chapter 1 he told her "Ask me again in ten days," and the chapter reminds me with "Your ten days were up on Monday." It also fits her rule from Chapter 15 that he has to ask her every time. That's where the ending landed for me.

One small one I'd only half catch while listening: he folds the dollar into his shirt pocket "over the stitches." I took it to mean he's keeping it close to his heart, but I wouldn't have missed anything if I'd missed that.

## 4. Words I didn't know

None of these stopped me understanding what was happening.

- "He sat on the engine box with his coffee mug." I guessed it's a box over the engine. That's fine.
- "It'll take up in a week." I didn't know the phrase, but the next line explains it in plain words: the planks "swell until the seams closed." Good.
- "cedar planks" and "seam": I know these well enough.
- "rubbed it along the wooden runners": the runners on a drawer. I mostly got it, because the drawer sticks and then slides.
- "evidence locker": I know it from TV, but the sentence says plainly that his gun is in Portland and he doesn't have it, so I didn't need the word.
- "the board": I know from earlier chapters that it's the ferry board that hires the captain. If this chapter were heard on its own it might need a few more words, but in the book it's fine.

For the whole book: earlier chapters used a few words I didn't know and that weren't explained, such as "choke" (Ch 3), "damper" (Ch 10) and "vise" (Ch 14). None of them were plot-critical. The plot-critical words were all said plainly: "fentanyl" in Ch 1 and 17, the "float" (Ch 2), the "ledges" (Ch 10), the "rudder" (Ch 14) and the "grand jury" (Ch 2).

## 5. Where I lost the thread

- "Tess set her fingerless gloves on the windowsill, one on top of the other, beside Beth's own pair, stacked the same way." I can tell this is supposed to mean something (they're alike? they're still close?), but half-listening I'd let it go by. It's not critical, because the letter carries the real point.
- "That's what the letter says. The rest isn't in it." I got that Tess backs her for the job. I had to think about "the rest". I took it to mean that Tess's hurt feelings and any forgiveness aren't in the letter. A little foggy, but I got enough.
- "...and by the time it did, she knew." For a second I wasn't sure what she knew. The next lines answer it (she'll ask Tess), so I only lost it for a moment.
- "The phone I found on your car deck, behind the bait truck's wheel." Dane has just been talking to Russ, so for a beat "your" sounded like Russ's. Then I worked out it means Beth's ferry. I remembered the phone from Chapter 15, so I followed it.
- The one light on the water: I understood it's meant to be ominous, because the text says it's "out where the Coast Guard had looked all night for the big boat." What I didn't know is whether it really is that boat or just a light. I think the book wants me unsure, and I was.
- The planted phone is a loose end, and the chapter says it is. It made me want a next book more than it made me feel cheated, but if this book is meant to stand alone, a listener could feel one question was left open on purpose.

## 6. Verdict: PASS

- I could retell the main event: Thanksgiving, the boat, Ned's letter, Tess's letter, Dane asking to stay, and the warning about the planted phone.
- I could say what's at stake: her job in June, the drug people still out there, and a traitor still on the island.
- No plot-critical fact reached me only through a word I didn't know or a picture I had to recognize.
- The romance was there in plain words, and the last line paid off a promise from Chapter 1 that I remembered.
