# Reader Notes: Chapter 18, "Full Price" (the last chapter)

**Draft read:** `chapters/chapter-18/final.md`, Nov 6, 2026. To get here I read every `final.md` from chapter-01 through chapter-18 in order. I read nothing else.

**Pull score: 3 of 5.** As an ending: **7 of 10.**

I finished it gladly, and I finished it without leaning forward. The book's suspense ended in Chapter 16 and its love story was settled in Chapter 17. This chapter is an epilogue at chapter length. A warm epilogue is the right shape for a romance, and most of this one is warm and earned. But the only pull left is "who planted the phone," and it shows up in the last page and a half.

---

## Does the ending satisfy?

Mostly yes.

What I wanted closed is closed:
- **Dane stays.** He moves to the Maine office, lives in her house, and gets a worse boat that is "tied up where you can see it from the ferry." That last part pays off in this chapter: "From its cabin she would see this boat every time she came in."
- **Beth's career.** She is running the ferry until June. Ned withdraws his letter, and Tess has already written hers.
- **The boots.** The book's first joke comes back: "Those are the right boots."
- **The ten days.** The title gets resolved with "Your ten days were up on Monday, Marshal."
- **The bookend.** "Are you any good at your job?" / "Ask me every night" answers Chapter 1's "Ask me again in ten days." I saw it coming as soon as she brought up the ten days. I liked it anyway. It is earned.

What is not closed:
- **The kid.** In Chapter 5, Russ says nobody ever asked his name. Nobody asks it here either. He has been in the book since page one and he leaves it nameless. I don't know if that is a choice or a dropped thread. On the page it reads as dropped.
- **The boy Beth taught to row.** He is the moral reason she called the police in Chapter 1, and the ending never touches him. Ned's son is in a clinic, which is near that thread. Beth never thinks about the other boy.
- **Cole's trial in the spring.** It isn't mentioned at all. Beth will have to testify again with Cole in the room, and nobody brings it up at a dinner where everyone is talking about how safe she is or isn't.

The Ned thread lands best. "Ned Haskell had sold her the first night, from the ferry at Pruett, and a page in pencil didn't change what that added up to." That is hard and right. I was glad the book didn't forgive him.

The Tess thread lands second best. It doesn't fully reconcile them, which is right too: "That's what the letter says. The rest isn't in it."

## Is the series hook clear? Does it cheat the happy ending?

**Clear: yes.** There are three pieces:
1. One light sits low on the water past the harbor mouth.
2. Dane reveals that the kid swears he never planted the phone, and the kid was tied up the whole time.
3. Russ says "I will" and Tess says "We will."

I understood right away that someone on the island is still working for the smugglers, and that Russ and Tess will hunt for them. I also read Russ and Tess as the next couple: the jacket in Chapter 12, "she looked at Russ, and he looked at her," and "We will."

**Cheating the happy ending: mostly no, with one sour note.** Beth and Dane end committed, living together, and accepted by the island. The hook belongs to other people. But the hook is a traitor on Beth's own island, and the book has told us twice what happens to witnesses after they testify (New Bedford). That makes their peace feel provisional. "Not always. So I watch the door," with no gun, is a nice line, but it puts the threat inside their happy ending and not next to it. I'd call this a happy-for-now with a shadow on it. Some romance readers will mind.

**The sour note.** I did the math on who could have planted the phone, and I didn't like where it pointed:
- Ned was cuffed to a pipe.
- The kid was tied up.
- Russ was on a couch with a bullet in his thigh.
- Tess was walking around free all of Saturday.

So when Tess says "We will," a suspicious reader hears a suspect volunteering to investigate. If that is the hook, it is a good one, but it quietly poisons the dish-washing reconciliation two pages earlier. If it isn't the hook, the book has handed me a false lead without meaning to.

**The light on the water.** It made me roll my eyes a little. The big boat was holed and limping, and the Coast Guard searched for it all night. Would it really come back and park in view of the harbor on Thanksgiving? I took it as atmosphere, not plot. It reads like a movie's last shot.

## Where I got bored

- **"They ate. Russ had seconds and then thirds. Tess talked to Russ and Dane about heating oil and passed Beth the potatoes without looking at her. There would be enough left for the week."** The dinner the whole last act built toward ("Tess says Thanksgiving is at Beth's") gets summarized. My attention dipped here and only came back when Dane put his forearms on the table.
- **"The planks had dried out all fall on land, and in the water they'd swell until the seams closed."** Beth has just said "It'll take up in a week." This line explains her line. It stopped the scene's bounce.
- **"Nobody from Cutter had spoken for her to the board now. In June they'd decide."** I was told in the boat scene that the board decides in June. Hearing it again felt like the book checking that I was keeping up.

## Where I got confused

- **The exchange at the sink:** "He got shot on your step." / "He did. I can't pay that back." / "No. You can't." I had to read it three times to work out who was speaking. On my second pass I thought Tess said "I can't pay that back," which flips the meaning. It is the most important exchange in the scene, and nobody is tagged.
- **"Somebody has to go first, and I've got the badge. I don't mind."** I don't know what Russ is saying yes to. Going first in giving his blessing? Being the first to take Dane in? It's close enough that I got the gist, but it's foggy at the moment it is supposed to land.
- **"It was the boat at Pruett he'd come up to see the day after the call came."** In Chapter 1 he was going to see it on Saturday, and the call was Thursday night. "The day after the call came" made me stop and count. Then "I'd seen eleven. How different could the twelfth be?" made me wonder whether he had seen this one or not. I worked it out, but it was a speed bump in the first page.
- **The opening joke.** "The doctor had said he could lift a mug" is followed straight away by "I can't lift anything... Doctor's orders." I got that it's a joke, but the two sentences sit so close that for a second they read like a contradiction.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Dane's speech at the table.** "I'm on leave till January, and then I start at the Maine office. I'd like to stay on this island and live in this house with Beth, and she's said yes, and she told me to ask the two of you, because it's your island, and I'd like to hear it from you." This is a man talking off a note card. Dane has been loose, funny and sideways for eighteen chapters, and at the most emotional moment he gets formal and complete. I believed the idea of asking (it calls back "you ask me, every time"). I didn't believe the words.
- **Dane's career.** In Chapter 17 he said he had been looking at boats for two years "because I wanted a way out of this job." Here he's going to the Maine office as if that were the point. Staying a marshal and being out of the job are not the same thing, and the chapter doesn't notice.
- **The light on the water** (see above).

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Every scene ends on a button line.** Here are all five:
  1. "There's a cup left, Marshal."
  2. "It ran smooth."
  3. "Coffee's a dollar, Marshal. Same as everybody."
  4. "Dry, Calloway."
  5. "Ask me every night."

  Each one is good on its own. Five in a row felt like a pattern by the third. The book has done this the whole way through, and the last chapter does it most.
- **"She wanted him, in those boots, on his leaking boat. She kept bailing."** Across the book, "She wanted him." / "He wanted her." has been dropped into nearly every scene as a flat statement. In Chapter 18 it read like a box being checked. By now I know she wants him, and the sentence adds nothing.
- **Recycled gestures.** These became tics over the book, and Chapter 18 uses them again:
  - "Russ took his cap off and put it back on." This is at least the fourth time.
  - "Tess scrubbed at a pan that was already clean." This copies Chapter 5's "squared a rack of lighters by the register that was already square."
  - "Tess looked at Dane's hands first." The book has a running "watched his hands" motif.
  - The dollar bets come back for a third chapter running.

  Each callback is fine. All of them at once feels like the book gathering up its props for a curtain call.
- **"...and by the time it did, she knew."** The drawer business is lovely. Then this line tells me a decision happened instead of letting the next line ("Tess.") do it.
- **"Then somebody on this island did, and somebody has to find out who." / "I will." / "We will."** This is a trailer line. People don't stand at a window and say it in that order.

## The listener pass (heard aloud, as with text-to-speech)

- **It works well read aloud** in the boat scene and at the sink. Short sentences, clear physical action, and the sounds carry it: the bucket scraping, the church bell, the water running.
- **Russ's voice is the most distinct thing to the ear in the whole book.** He asks a question and answers it himself: "Did you fix that? You did." / "Didn't they? They did." Even through a flat synthetic voice, you know it's Russ without a tag.
- **Tess is clear by ear too.** She calls Beth "Calloway," talks in clipped declaratives, and speaks about money.
- **It fails at the sink exchange.** The three untagged lines ("He got shot on your step" / "He did. I can't pay that back" / "No. You can't") can't be followed by ear at all. A listener will assign them wrong.
- **The opening sentence is a mouthful.** "Beth Calloway bailed out Dane Reyes's brand-new old boat" stacks "Reyes's" against "brand-new old," and it trips in the mouth. Chapters 15 through 18 all open on a character's full name. By ear that pattern is noticeable.
- **Ned's letter will read fine aloud.** It's short, and the italics don't matter. Russ's text in Chapter 17 works the same way.
- **Names in the last scene.** "Marshal" and "Calloway" come round often enough as terms of address that a listener starts hearing them as punctuation.

## What I want to know next

- **Who taped the phone under the bait truck?** And is it Tess? This is the real question, and it's the one that would get me to buy book 2.
- **Are Russ and Tess the next book's couple?** I think so. I'd read it to see Tess talk to someone she's angry at.
- **Is that light the boat?**
- **What happens at Cole's trial in the spring,** and does Dane get pulled off the island for it?
- **What's the kid's name?** I mean that. The book made a point of nobody asking, and then never asked.

That's a real list, but it is thin and it all comes from the last page and a half. Nothing in the first two thirds of the chapter makes me want a next book. It makes me content.

## Best moment

Ned's letter:

> *My boy's at the clinic in Portland. First week. He says it's the worst place he's ever been. I told him that's how you know.*

It's eight words of Ned at his best and worst, and Beth's response is exactly right: "No sorry. Nothing asked." Then she fixes the drawer. That is the best quiet passage in the last third of the book.

A close second is Tess at the sink: "Best hand on that water. I won't have a stranger at our wheel. That's what the letter says. The rest isn't in it."

---

## Short verdict on the ending

- **Does it feel earned?** Yes for Dane and Beth. Yes for Ned. Mostly yes for Tess, though the phone math undercuts her.
- **The character I'll remember:** Russ, for his voice, and because he stood in the door. Tess is next. Then Ned, more than either lead.
- **Where in the book I'd most likely have stopped:** not in this chapter. The riskiest stretch was Chapters 14 and 15. The witness-protection fight that ends "And so do we" in Chapter 12 gets settled over an eleven-pound ham, and the stakes felt like they were being let out slowly. I kept going for the ferry and the ledges, and Chapter 16 paid that off.
