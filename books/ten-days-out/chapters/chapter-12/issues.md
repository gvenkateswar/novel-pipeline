# Chapter 12 Issues (draft.md)

Checked against: beats.md, outline.md (including Audit round 2 fixes and the Ch 12 carry-forwards), continuity-log.md (Ch 11 entry), chapter-11/final.md, and the bible. Where the bible disagrees with the outline or the log, the outline and log win; stale bible items are listed separately at the end.

Caps verified clean: no swears from Dane or Beth; no "Bethie"; no "your marshal"; "looked back" once (Ned, Scene 2); "Ms. Calloway" once (Scene 4); no "Okay", "Fine.", "I'm asking", "Dollar says", "Cole walks", "ten days", "good coat"; every boat is "it". Pockets are correct (Ned's phone in Dane's left coat pocket). The rope-to-cuffs handover is present (see Issue 5 for its mechanics). The word count was not measured here; the orchestrator's `wc` check still applies.

**Issue 1**
- Problem: The line overstates the hunt. The kid did not come to every bed. He never came to the net loft (Ch 04), the keeper's house (Ch 07, he thought they had drowned) or the dentist's house (Ch 10-11; Ned came, not the kid). A reader who remembers those chapters will catch it.
- Location: "So the police had only her word, and that was why a boy with a gun had come to every bed she slept in for six days."
- Suggested fix: Change to "had hunted her for six days" (or "had come after her for six days").

**Issue 2**
- Problem: Narration restates Ned's answer in the sentences right after the dialogue, so the line does not land as new. The "police had only her word" half also re-runs the Ch 02 "she is the whole case" fact. This is the chapter's required "true size" reaction, but it is told as logic. Only the slack rope is body.
- Location: "Ned had warned Cole. The truck had been clean because of him."
- Suggested fix: Cut "Ned had warned Cole. The truck had been clean because of him." Let the slack rope and her stopping carry the beat, and keep only the "that was why" consequence sentence (after Issue 1's fix).

**Issue 3**
- Problem: Narration explains Ned's line, which repeats the plant. "Too" has nothing to refer to, because Scene 1 has no other boat (beats: "No boat in Scene 1").
- Location: "So there was a boat out there, too, and nobody had seen it."
- Suggested fix: Cut the sentence and go straight to "He got his good knee under him…", so Ned's line is the plant.

**Issue 4**
- Problem: Russ's confession is thinner than the plant list requires. (a) "I told her" never says what he told Tess (where Beth was sleeping, the north-side house). A listener who has lost Ch 05 cannot tell why this matters. (b) The beats and the outline put "he kept the harbor road on Tuesday because every house on it stood empty" in this exchange. It is the listed payoff for Russ keeping to the harbor road (Ch 08, Ch 09) and it is missing.
- Location: "You asked who I told. I told her." His ears had gone red. "I went to her after the Dyer house, and the phone she hung up on Saturday was me."
- Suggested fix: Add one clause, e.g. "I told her the north-side house" after "I told her.", and one short line, e.g. "And Tuesday I kept the harbor road because every house on it was empty." Keep it to two sentences more at most, and keep the dropped "Who'd I tell" off the page.

**Issue 5**
- Problem: The handover mechanics don't work. Ned's wrists are tied with rope, so Russ cannot cuff "in front" and then untie the knot. Cuffs can't close on wrists that are bound together. Ch 11 also made this "the knot that only pulls tighter", and here it is picked loose in seconds without comment.
- Location: "Russ cuffed Ned's wrists in front and picked her knot loose."
- Suggested fix: Reverse the order and give it a beat, e.g. "Russ worked her knot loose, slowly, since it only pulled tighter, then cuffed Ned's wrists in front." Do this before the rope burn is shown.

**Issue 6**
- Problem: The beats pay the Ch 09 plant "Dane can't hand anyone to Russ (on my list)" with one clause. The draft has none. Russ is never visibly cleared by Dane. Dane rings him, trusts him with Beth's lane, and Beth (not Dane) hands over the rope, so the payoff is unmarked.
- Location: "Beth held out the end of the rope. Russ put his hand on it."
- Suggested fix: Add a half-sentence, e.g. "Dane stood back and let her give it to him", so the reader sees Dane hand a prisoner to Russ.

**Issue 7**
- Problem: "I did." answers Beth's "you didn't even ask" and reads as Dane claiming he asked. That is false (the page shows he didn't) and out of character (the bible: he never lies to her). The beats intend an admission ("I said it because I mean to make you go"). It lands as a denial first.
- Location: "I did. Ms. Calloway, I'll say why once, slowly, because I'd like you to have all of it."
- Suggested fix: Replace "I did." with a plain admission, e.g. "I didn't ask." Keep "Ms. Calloway" as the single use.

**Issue 8**
- Problem: Narration asserts the boss knows what Beth is to Dane, and explains the "not you" line. The beats called for no narration explaining a line. The Ch 03 log entry does not record the boss learning of the relationship, so the claim is unestablished in the log. It is also unclear how Beth, the POV character, knows what the boss knows.
- Location: "She knew what Beth was to him."
- Suggested fix: Cut the sentence. If the boss's knowledge must show, let the boss's tone on "and not you" carry it, with no gloss.

**Issue 9**
- Problem: The same image of Beth wanting his mouth appears twice in the chapter. Both are "put/haul to her mouth" shapes, which makes a tic by Scene 4. The beats gave Scene 3 the interior longing and Scene 4 "one plain physical sentence", and the two read as the same sentence.
- Location: "Beth wanted to cross the room and put her mouth on his while he was still talking." / "she wanted to take his coat in both fists and haul him over the desk to her mouth."
- Suggested fix: Keep Scene 3's, and change Scene 4's to a different part of him (his coat in her fists, his grey face) with no mouth. Alternatively, in Scene 3 use the beat sheet's "bad knee against the desk" detail with no mouth.

**Issue 10**
- Problem: The last image states as fact that the kid is in the house and how he got there. Nobody on the page has seen him. Beth and Dane have only smoke and Russ's truck. The end question is "Who's in her father's house?", and this sentence answers it before the image asks it. It also narrates what the smoke means.
- Location: "The boy had come up from the cove below her house, the way he came the first night."
- Suggested fix: Make it Beth's inference ("The only way past Russ was the cove below her house, the way the boy came the first night"), or cut it so the smoke and the truck are the whole image, as the beats' ending type (open image, "no narrated question") requires.

**Issue 11**
- Problem: Dane's stated reason for posting Russ does not fit the timing the page shows. The kid ran west along the wall toward the village before the walk on to the rise and the harbor road (a long stretch of walking, the call, the handover). He would have passed or reached the Calloway lane well before Russ is sent there, and Dane calls hers "the first house he comes to." The page gives no reason Russ is not already too late. A listener will ask.
- Location: "because hers is the first house he comes to from the east, and it's the only one on this island he knows in the dark."
- Suggested fix: Add a short clause, e.g. that Dane doesn't know where the kid went to ground and a hurt boy goes to what he knows, or keep "first house" and add "if he hasn't gone to ground already".

**Issue 12**
- Problem: Signal contradiction in the town office. Scene 3 sends Dane to the window "where the signal was" to ring Portland. Scene 4 has the boss on speaker from Dane's phone lying on the desk, a few feet away. The room is described as one small front room. No reason is given for why the signal differs by the length of a desk.
- Location: "Dane went to the window, where the signal was, and rang Portland police on his own phone." / "Her voice came small and flat out of the phone face up on the desk"
- Suggested fix: Put the desk phone (or a bar at the desk) into Scene 3, or have Dane carry the phone back to the window sill for the speaker call, or have Scene 4's phone lie "on the sill".

**Issue 13**
- Problem: "Monday" is spoken twice (the boss, then Dane). The beats allow it at most once on the page. It is also the one date line that the carry-forward says to keep scarce.
- Location: "If she says no, our part ends after Monday. Will she take it?" / "I'm still your guard until Monday."
- Suggested fix: Keep the boss's "after Monday" and change Dane's to "I'm still your guard until then" or "I'm still your guard."

**Issue 14**
- Problem: The Ch 11 plant that explains how the kid knew to be at the wall is not paid. Ch 11 plants "A lit lantern on that road was a light anybody in those woods could see." The beats list it as the chapter's information channel (World rule 11). On the page the kid is waiting at the stone wall at first light with no stated reason, which reads as coincidence or a second leak.
- Location: "The crack came off the wall. Snow jumped off the road ahead of Ned."
- Suggested fix: In Scene 1 add one clause tying the wall to the lantern, e.g. in Dane's reasoning, "a lantern had walked this road in the dark, and anybody in the fields could have seen it."

**Issue 15**
- Problem: Two lines are comparison-shaped ("rather… than"). The beats approved both (the bible's angry line 3 is word for word, and "I'd rather lose you than bury you" was kept). If the zero-tolerance rule on contrast constructions is applied literally, these are the only candidates in the chapter. Reported for the orchestrator's decision. Not a drafting error.
- Location: "You decided I'd rather be alive in Ohio than be me, and you didn't even ask." / "I'd rather lose you than bury you."
- Suggested fix: If the rule holds, rewrite Dane's as "I'd lose you to keep you alive." Beth's line is the bible's own and needs an author decision. Otherwise leave both.

**Issue 16**
- Problem: Ned's "That's him. Cole's lawyer." repeats the identification Ned made in Scene 1 ("Cole's lawyer rang me the morning after"). It does not land as new. The only new fact in the exchange is the match of the numbers, which Dane's action and the call history already show.
- Location: "That's him," Ned said from the floor. "Cole's lawyer."
- Suggested fix: Cut "Cole's lawyer." and keep "That's him." (The Friday-morning call history just above already ties it to Scene 1's call.)
