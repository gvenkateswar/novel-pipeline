## Flags before the beats (outline against the page, the log and the orchestrator's corrections)

   - It uses the full-name opening the Ch 08 and Ch 10 readers flagged.

   **Settled:** a new opener (Chapter frame) names Beth, starts on her hand on the rope, and covers Ned's silence from four o'clock to first light in one clause, as the Ch 11 beats asked. "They have my boy" is still the first thing said aloud.

In Ch 11, Beth tied Ned's wrists with rope from the dentist's woodbox, using the knot that only pulls tighter. **Settled:** Ned walks the whole east road tied, Beth holding the end of the rope. At the town office door Russ's cuffs replace the rope. The outline's "Dane cuffs them" (Ch 11) and the bible's "Russ cuffs him on the float" are both retired.
3. **"Could see his light from there" is already used.** Ned said it in Ch 11 s3, so it isn't repeated or restated here.
4. **The people cap.** The outline's s2 puts five named people in one beat (Beth, Dane, Ned, Russ, Tess). The calibration log shows five people on one page as this book's worst-scoring scene: the Ch 03 store scored 2 on six reads. **Settled:**

## Scene 1

**Setting:** The east road, first light Wednesday. Snow is coming flat across open fields on both sides. On the uphill side an old stone wall runs across the top of a field gone white. On the downhill side is a shallow ditch half full of snow. There are no trees near the road (N12). **Sensory anchor:** the rope in Beth's bare hand, stiff with cold, jerking a little at every step Ned's bad hip takes. A second anchor: the dry squeak of new snow under three sets of boots.

**Characters present:** Beth, Ned, Dane.

**Point of view:** Beth. Her fear goes quiet and listens. Her questions are short, she already knows the answers, and she asks them one at a time and waits.

**Want:**
- **Beth** wants the why from Ned, all of it, before they reach the village and people. The reader learns this when she asks her second question without letting the first one settle.
- **Ned** wants her to have it, and can only say it to the snow.
- **Dane** wants them across open ground before full light shows them to anybody.
- **In the way:** a boy with a pistol somewhere on this island who saw a lantern on this road last night.

**Surface action:**
1. **The binding opener**, then Ned's first answer: "They have my boy." No "Bethie."
   - Beth asked "Why?" at four in the morning, and this is the answer.
   - Ned reports other men's words, his habit: Cole's lawyer called him the morning after, on the phone Cole gave him for the night runs, the only number Cole ever called him on [A11]. "He said where my boy sleeps. He said the street."
   - **Say-it-twice for Ch 11, one clause inside the action:** Beth's eyes go to Dane's coat pocket, where that phone is. It's the phone that sent Russ's porch, the north-side house and the beach at three. One clause, and it's never a list of suspects.
2. **Why the road at first light, one sentence in Beth's head:**
   - Dane waited for light because in the dark the kid knew the island better than they did.
   - He picked the road because Ned's hip couldn't do the shore rocks in snow with his hands tied.
   - From the road you could see a long way across the fields on both sides.

   Also one clause: Dane carries the ferry's first-aid box Ned brought, because his own kit has no gauze left (flag 7).
3. **Beth's second question, straight on:** "Why was the truck clean?"

   Ned: "I called Cole from the boat before you'd got your phone out. I told him you'd call. I know you." Then, from the bible, too much detail, his tell: he thought if the truck was clean there'd be nothing for her to have seen.
4. **Beth's reaction at true size, plain and physical, landing short.**
   - The truck was clean because of him. So her word was all the police had, and that's why a boy with a gun had come to every bed she slept in for six days.
   - The rope goes slack in her hand because she has stopped walking.
   - Don't use the phrase "eyes are the case." Say it new and plain.
5. **The fear beat before the danger** (the author's 15% slowdown goes here). Dane has stopped too. He's looking up the field at the stone wall. Beth hears what he's heard: nothing, and in a storm that's wrong. Give it a breath.
6. **The shot.** One crack from the stone wall at the top of the field. It's wild, and it goes somewhere you can see: a spray of snow off the road ahead, or a whine past. It's **one-handed**, because the kid is hit in his left arm.
   - Dane gets them both down into the ditch on the downhill side, Beth first, Ned after.
   - Ned goes in hard on the bad hip with his hands tied, and he rolls so his body is between Beth and the field. He's doing the beach again.
   - Beth shoves him off her: "Get off me." It's her one line in the danger, two or three words, her fear register.
7. **Dane on the job.** Up on one knee at the lip of the ditch, he fires once at the wall.
   - A shape breaks from the far end of the wall, bent over, and goes west along it toward the village. The bad foot plows a line through the snow behind him. That's the marker the listener tracks him by.
   - Dane doesn't fire again: past the wall there are houses (his rule since Ch 03).
   - He doesn't chase. He has a witness and a tied prisoner in a ditch, and the kid is running toward people. One clause.
   - Optional, one line from Ned, watching the shape go: "That's the boy?" There's no exchange between them, ever.
8. **N6, the call.** At the top of the rise where the road first sees the village, Dane's phone finds one bar. He rings Russ, who answers fast and sounds indoors. Dane's instructions:
   - Put it on the island radio: everybody indoors, doors locked, the kid is coming into the village from the east side with a pistol, and nobody goes out to help a hurt boy.
   - Meet them on the harbor road.

   Dane says nothing about where Beth is or where she'll be (bible: nobody says where she sleeps on the radio). Keep it to four lines or fewer.
9. **In the ditch or on the rise, the rest comes, each answer landing before the next:**
   - Beth: "Why was my picture on that wall in September?" Ned: "In September Cole asked me who'd have the boat after me. I told him you. I was proud of it."
   - Optional, one line from Dane, his ear for how a jury hears things: Cole wanted to know who he'd have to buy next. Use it if the listener needs the plot fact said plainly (World rule 6).
   - Dane's one question, which is his job: where does Cole get it? Ned answers Beth, and never the marshal (Ch 11 set this): "It comes in from offshore. A boat. I never saw it."
   - **Unasked, the beach**, while Ned gets himself up out of the ditch on the bad hip: "If I warned you, they'd know it was me, and my boy was in their hands. So I came to stand where he'd have to shoot me first." This is Ned's one longer speech. Beth has just watched him do it again in the ditch, and the page doesn't say so.

**Subtext:** Every answer makes Ned smaller and more human, and Beth can't afford either. She asks fast so she won't have to feel the last answer before the next one comes. Ned is giving her the only thing he has left, the truth, and he can't look at her while he does it. The rolling-over in the ditch is the man she loved, and she pushes it away because she can't take one more thing from him.

**Tension source:** Situational: open ground, a pistol, a man tied and slow. Interpersonal at full size: each answer takes something more of Ned away from her. Internal: the truck answer, that her six days were his doing.

**Ends on (ending type: cut, in motion):** They get up and walk on toward the village, Ned first on the rope, Beth stiff-legged on the bad knee. Dane's hand comes flat on her back once, between her shoulders, and goes. On the harbor road below, a door shuts, then another: the village has heard the radio. Cut while they're walking. No line of dialogue after the beach speech, and no interior verdict.

**Texture budget (2):** the rope and its knot (the rope burn starting on Ned's wrists); the single return shot (and Dane's rule about houses).

**Word count target:** 700. Floor 650. This is the chapter's danger scene, and the answers can't be rushed. If it runs long, step 2 drops to two clauses and the optional lines go. Never cut the truck reaction, the fear beat before the shot, the shove, or the beach line.

## Scene 2

**Setting:** The harbor road at Tess's store, about seven, full grey light, snow thinning. The doors along the road are shut. **Sensory anchor:** Russ standing in the snow in his shirt sleeves, because Tess has his brown work jacket on, the cuffs down over her fingers.

**Characters present:** Beth, Russ, Tess speak. Dane is present and silent. Ned is present and silent, on the rope (see flag 4).

**Point of view:** Beth. Her one-sentence verdicts on people are her habit, and she can update one here.

**Want:**
- **Russ** wants it out before anyone says it for him.
- **Tess** wants nobody to say one word about it.
- **Beth** wants, for one breath, something on this island to be what it looks like and nothing worse. Here it is.
- **In the way:** Ned on the end of her rope, and that every one of them is now someone Beth suspected.

**Surface action:**
1. They come along the harbor road. Tess's door opens, and Russ comes out with his shotgun and his radio, Tess behind him in the doorway in his jacket. Everyone stops.
2. **The laugh (binding):**
   - Beth: "Late."
   - Russ takes his cap off and puts it back on: "Later than that."
   - Tess: "Not one word, Calloway."
3. **The secret, in two lines, Russ to Dane.** Dane has had Russ on his list since Ch 09, and Russ knows it. Russ starts with the lie from Ch 05, said straight this time: "You asked who I told. I told her." Then, in one more line:
   - he went to Tess's after the empty Dyer house;
   - the phone Tess hung up on Saturday was him;
   - he kept the harbor road on Tuesday like he said, because every house on it stood empty and he wasn't leaving hers.
   - Keep it plain, two sentences at most. Never use "Who'd I tell."
4. **Beth's verdict.** Optional, one plain sentence in her head about the two of them, a new verdict that breaks an old one. Her habit, once.
5. **The handover at the town office door, a few steps along.** Russ gets out his own cuffs.
   - Beth holds out the end of the rope and doesn't let go of it straight away. Then she does.
   - Russ cuffs Ned's wrists and unties the knot. One clause: Dane can hand a man to Russ now (paying Ch 09 without quoting it).
   - Before the cuffs go on, **Tess goes back inside and shuts her door.** She doesn't watch Ned walked in. Beth sees the door shut.
6. **Ned looks back once** from the town office doorway, at Beth. His hands are cuffed, so it's a nod or nothing. This is the chapter's one "looked back." Then he's inside.
7. **N6, Dane's one instruction to Russ, short:** take the truck and the shotgun to the foot of the Calloway lane and sit on it. Hers is the first house the kid reaches coming in from the east and the only one he knows in the dark. Russ goes.

**Subtext:** Tess is braced for Beth to say what Tess said to her at the counter about the condoms (Ch 05). Beth gives her one word and lets her off, and that's love. Tess hasn't forgiven her for the records and the list, and she shows it by turning her back on Ned's handover. That's a refusal to watch the man who used them all, and it's a door shut on Beth too. Russ is relieved and ashamed at once, so he tells it to the marshal, the man who suspected him.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal and comic, with a dark edge: the last of Beth's three suspects, cleared in the silliest possible way, while the guilty one stands tied beside her.

**Ends on (ending type: action):** Russ's truck pulling away up the east side of the village toward her lane, shotgun on the seat. Beth turns back to the town office door. No dialogue after Dane's instruction.

**Texture budget (2):** Russ's cuffs replacing the rope; the island radio, said once as the reason the road is empty.

**Word count target:** 300. No floor. If the chapter runs long, this scene drops to the "Late" exchange plus the handover (Pacing flag 3). Never cut the laugh, Tess's door, or Russ being sent to the lane.

## Scene 3

**Setting:** The town office, mid-morning. A woodstove ticking in the front room. Ned on the floor of the back room with his back to the wall, the door open, the rope burn red on both wrists above Russ's cuffs. **Sensory anchor:** the cheap phone buzzing against the desk where Dane has laid it beside the kid's phone, a small hard rattle on old wood.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Ned.

**Point of view:** Beth.

**Want:**
- **Dane** wants to beat the lawyer to Ned's son, and to do it himself, inside the hour.
- **Ned** wants his boy alive, and can't do one thing about it from a floor in cuffs.
- **Beth** wants not to care whether Ned's son lives, and she can't manage it. The reader learns this when she speaks to Ned at the end.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open in motion:** the cheap phone buzzes on the desk. One clause: Dane has kept it switched on, because whoever else uses that number would try it.
2. **The text on the page** (italics, lowercase as typed, binding): *the kid isnt answering ned. your son is. go finish it.*
3. **Said plainly for a listener** (World rule 6), because "your son is" is hard to catch read aloud:
   - Dane: they're telling Ned they can reach his son.
   - Beth, two words: "He means me." That's "go finish it."
4. **The match [A10], shown with hands.** Dane holds the cheap phone beside the kid's phone: it's the same number that called the kid four times since the beach and never texted. Then he opens the cheap phone's call history, and that number is there once more, an incoming call on Friday morning (flag 6). Ned, from the back-room floor: "That's him. Cole's lawyer."
5. **Dane on the job [A8], with no speaker.** He rings Portland police himself on his own phone, at the window where the signal is.
   - He gets a desk, then a sergeant. He gives his name and his badge, and says a man's life is under threat and he needs a car at an address now.
   - His anger register is polite and slow.
   - He turns to the back room: "Ned. The address." Ned gives his son's street and number from the floor, every word. Numbers in words.
6. **The longing beat, one Beth interior.** Dane is on the phone to a city she has been to twice, making a desk in Portland do what he says in that polite, slow voice. He's good at this, and he's doing it for the man who sold her. She wants him so badly it scares her, standing there with her bad knee against the desk. Let it run as a plain physical sentence and land short. Don't use "She wanted him." Don't use "watched his hands."
7. **The wait.** Show it on Ned, through his hands only: his thumbs working at the rope burn on each wrist, over and over. He never looks at the phone. He looks at Beth. Nobody counts. The stove ticks.
8. Dane's phone. He listens. "He's alive. They've got him in the car. They're taking him somewhere safe." Then, once, after the fact: it took eleven minutes.
9. Ned's thumbs stop. He looks at Beth, the way he has looked at her before every answer since the dentist's house. Beth, a flat verdict: "You heard him."

**Subtext:** Beth's "You heard him" is the last kind thing she'll do for Ned, and it's dressed as impatience. Dane is doing for Ned's son what nobody did for Beth's two island kids, and he knows it. Beth knows he knows it.

**Tension source:** Situational: a clock nobody names aloud, with a life at the end of it. Internal: Beth's hate and pity for Ned, both at true size.

**Ends on (ending type: consequence):** After "You heard him," Ned bends forward over his cuffed hands until his forehead is on his knees, and stays there. Beth pulls the back-room door half shut, not all the way, and goes back to the stove. The scene stops on what saving the son has cost and given Ned. No further line.

**Texture budget (2):** the two phones side by side, with the call history; the Portland police call.

**Word count target:** 355. No floor. If short, the room goes to the wait (step 7). If long, step 6 shrinks to one sentence, but the longing beat never goes.

## Scene 4

**Setting:** The town office front room, late morning, then the steps at dusk. Dane's phone lies face up on the desk on speaker. Snow keeps on past the window over the harbor road. **Sensory anchor:** the boss's voice, small and flat out of the phone, filling a room built for one clerk. At the end, the cold of the town office steps through Beth's jeans, and the last light going blue.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Dane's boss (a voice on speaker). Ned is behind the half-shut back-room door, off the page.

**Point of view:** Beth. In anger she gets plainer and bossier and uses his full name. Her interior here is shorter than in the rest of the chapter.

**Want:**
- **Dane** wants her alive after Monday more than he wants anything, including her. The reader learns it at "She'll go."
- **Beth** wants her life, which is this island, and him in it. When she has to choose, she won't pay with the island. The reader learns it at "That's Cole winning. Slower."
- **The boss** wants an answer she can write down.
- **In the way:** both of them are right.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open in motion, mid-call** (speaker call one of two in the back half [A8]). Dane has just told her the arrest, the lawyer's number and the son. Beth is in the room and hears every word (bible: no misunderstanding, he reports in front of her). The boss, short conditions, no questions she doesn't need:
   - "A lawyer who can find a man's son in a morning can find a woman on an island."
   - Then the program, in the domain-checked wording, glossed plainly the first time ("witness protection" is in the jargon table): "A new name in a new state. She can't come back here, and she can't tell anyone from here where she is. Not her friends, not this island, and not you." The "not you" lands on both of them, and on the boss's knowing what Beth is to him (Ch 03 plant).
   - One more condition: if Beth says no, the Marshals' part ends after Monday. Plain words, said once. "Then they stop" needs it.
   - Then the one question she needs answered: will Beth take it?
2. **The condemnable choice, to her face.** Dane looks at Beth, three feet away. He doesn't ask her. "She'll go."

   **Binding: no narration after it** explaining what he did or what it means. Beth's body answers, one observable thing (her hand flat on the desk, or her standing up off the chair on the bad knee).
3. **Beth ends the call herself:** "Hang up, Dane." The boss is already saying "Call me back." Dane takes it off speaker and ends the call. That keeps the fight face to face, with no third party listening.
4. **The fight, face to face, both right, each said once:**
   - **Dane** (long sentences, because and which, polite and slow, and here "Ms. Calloway" comes back, once):
     - New Bedford: Cole's people burned down a witness's house after she testified (Ch 09, named again).
     - The pistol coming up at her on the cove rocks on Tuesday, and the shot off that wall this morning.
     - The man who loved her most on this island sold her for his son, and the next one will sell her for less.
     - "I'd rather lose you than bury you."
     - He also says plainly that he said "She'll go" because he means to make her go, so there's no misunderstanding to clear later.
   - **Beth** (short, plain, giving orders, "Dane Reyes"):
     - The bible's angry line 3, word for word: "You told your boss I'd go. With me standing right here. You decided I'd rather be alive in Ohio than be me, and you didn't even ask."
     - Then: "That's Cole winning. Slower."
     - She won't leave her island because shooting at a witness works. That's her "would never run," in one sentence in her own words.
   - **The one-talk test, made visible:** neither of them is mistaken about a single fact. He's right that it's the only safe life his job can give her. She's right that it's the one life she won't live.
   - **The longing, once, in the fight:** one plain physical sentence in Beth's head, of wanting him across this desk while she ends it, landing short. Not "She wanted him."
5. **The turn:**
   - Dane, plain: if she says no, after Monday his office stops.
   - Beth (binding): "Then they stop. And so do we."
   - **No narration after it.** Dane takes it standing. He doesn't argue the second half. He says one thing he can check, his bible habit: he's still her guard until Monday. Beth doesn't answer it, or answers with a task. No "Fine."
6. **One line carries the afternoon**, without a summary paragraph: he stays on the other side of the room all afternoon, the rifle against the wall beside him and his back to it, and neither of them says anything they don't have to.
7. **Dusk, the town office steps.** Beth comes out for air with her bad leg straight on the step. Dane is in the doorway behind her, because he's her guard. Up on the east rise above the village, over the roofs:
   - **smoke is rising from her own chimney**, her father's house, in the last light;
   - at the foot of the lane below it, **Russ's truck is still parked where he's sat all day.**
   - One clause of Beth's knowledge, no more: the kid came up from the cove below her house, the way he came the first night. Dane comes down a step beside her. They both see it. Nobody speaks.

**Subtext:** Dane decides for her because he has watched a gun come up at her twice in two days, and deciding is the only thing his fear knows how to do. He calls it his job. Beth isn't angry that he wants her alive. She's angry that he answered for her, and that he's right about the danger. "And so do we" isn't a punishment. She can't stay with a man who would say "She'll go" for her, and he can't stop being that man by Monday. The smoke is her father's house, and someone else has lit the stove.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal, at full adult size: two true things that can't both be kept. Situational, at the end: the kid inside her house, past the guard, and her chimney giving him away.

**Ends on (ending type: open image):** The smoke from her chimney rising pale into the dusk over the village, with Russ's truck small and dark at the foot of the lane beneath it, and Beth and Dane on the steps looking at it together, apart. **No dialogue, and no narrated question.** It sets up the outline's end question (*Who's in her father's house?*) and Ch 13's opener (Dane under her kitchen window at first light).

**Texture budget (2):** the program terms (domain-checked wording); New Bedford.

**Word count target:** 600. **Floor 575.** This is the rupture, and it never compresses below the floor. The fight (step 4) gets the most room. If the chapter runs long, steps 6 and 7 shrink to the image alone, but the smoke and the truck both stay.
