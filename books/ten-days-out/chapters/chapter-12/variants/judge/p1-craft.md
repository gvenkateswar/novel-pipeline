# Judge: Craft, pair p1 (A vs B), Chapter 12

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A stays with Beth the whole way and lets the fight over the hold stay one long fight, from the office out to the hatch, and Dane gets to be funny right when she shuts the door on him. B stops halfway to walk me through Dane's reasons in his head, and that took the heat out of it.

3. **Where A lost me:** around 23%, the ditch: `"So I came to stand where he'd have to shoot me first," he said. Nobody had asked.` It's cut off from Ned's last line, so for a beat I didn't know who "he" was, and "Nobody had asked" is the narrator nudging me. It got me again at about 33%: `"You asked me who I told you were at my place."` I had to read that sentence twice. After that it held me. The hatch scene stacks a lot of talk (the boss's callback, her refusal, his New Bedford speech, her answer), but it's all one fight with her halfway up a ladder, so I kept going.

4. **Where B lost me:** at 60%: `"Dane took the phone off the sill and stayed at the window. He could ask her again. On the tower she had made the calls. On the cove rocks she had jumped with a pistol on her. Last night she had lain down under a quilt..."` That's a list of the plot so far inside his head, and it explains a choice I'd already have understood from watching him send the text. Two lines later, still in his head, comes `"She didn't step back. That made her angrier than the text had."` Now I'm in her head too, and I lost track of whose scene it was. Moving the "prosecutor's agreed" call into the office also costs B the slammed door. Instead it ends on `"Reyes?" the phone said to the room.` That's a nice line, but it's a smaller exit.

5. **Confidence: medium.** These are close. B's opening and hatch are tighter line by line, and `He could have watched those hands the rest of his life, and he had just made sure he wouldn't` is the best single sentence in either version. But B's recap paragraph and the slip into Beth's head cost it more than A's clumsy spots cost A.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Most of the sentences are identical. One is a re-cut of the other.
