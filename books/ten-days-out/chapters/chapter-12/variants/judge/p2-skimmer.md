# Judge: Skimmer, pair p2 (A vs B), Chapter 12

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** In B I could hear the shot coming. The island goes quiet, Dane stops, then the crack. And when the door bangs shut on Dane mid-sentence, it woke me up right when the office stuff was making me drift. A starts with the gunfire and then stops dead for Ned's two-year confession while the shooter is still running off, and with my ears alone I lost who was answering what.

3. **Where A lost me:** "\"Snow's set in for the day,\" Ned said. Then he answered her." (~9%). I'm in a ditch, someone just fired, and now Ned is answering a question I never heard her ask. Then two Ned paragraphs in a row ("On the beach I lied to you..." / "A man rang me on that phone...") and I couldn't tell from listening whose turn it was. I came back for the town office, then my thumb went again in the long boss-and-prosecutor stretch: "It rests on my deputy's word that you'll be killed if you go home. Only his word takes it back." (~62%). The deal-making at the window took me out of the room.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Friday night, from my porch, before the shooting. Saturday at the store, when her phone buzzed and she hung up on it, that was me telling her I'd found you alive." (~33%). It's a list of days, a man reading out his own record, and I let it run. I came back quickly, because the chair-and-"once" bit right after is good. A smaller dip came when the boss rings again over the hatch ("The prosecutor's agreed to ask... Two o'clock Monday," ~80%). A legal update in the middle of her fighting the pin.

5. **Confidence: medium.** A has better single lines ("you're lying in my spot. I'd like it back", "Then walk faster"), and some nights its gunshot opening would win. But B's order is easier to follow by ear, and B lost me later and for less time.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Most of the lines are word for word the same, just put in a different order.
