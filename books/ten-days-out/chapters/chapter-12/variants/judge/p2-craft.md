# Judge: Craft, pair p2 (A vs B), Chapter 12

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** B gives Ned his confession while they walk, lets the island go quiet, and then fires the shot, so the ambush lands on "Beth stopped walking." A opens on the shot, then sits in the ditch for a calm Q&A, which wastes the bullet and the confession both.

3. **Where A lost me:** "'Snow's set in for the day,' Ned said. Then he answered her." (~9%). Someone has just shot at them off a wall, and Ned starts a two-year confession to a question I never saw asked. After that the whole exchange reads like an interview held in a ditch with a gunman still out there. "Up. Before he works out he can stop running" comes too late to make it feel dangerous. A wins a few lines back later ("somewhere with fewer windows" / "Then walk faster," and the phone saying "Reyes?" to an empty room), but I was already reading it as cooler than B.

4. **Where B lost me:** "His phone rang at the rail, where the one bar was. He held it out over the hatch on speaker." (~79%). The boss calls in the middle of the hatch scene, gives procedure, hangs up, and then Dane launches straight into the New Bedford speech, which is itself a list of grievances. For about a dozen lines this turns into the couple doing business. The bent rod and "I'd lose you to keep you alive" pull it back.

5. **Confidence: medium.** B's version of the ambush, plus "he drew his elbow in to let her by. There was room without it" and Dane's line cut off when the door bangs shut, give it the edge. The two share most of their lines, so this comes down to where things sit in the chapter, not to better sentences.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. They're the same draft with the scenes reordered, and most of the dialogue is word for word.
