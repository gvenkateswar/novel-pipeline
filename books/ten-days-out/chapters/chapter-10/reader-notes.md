## Stop line

None in this chapter. I finished it, and I'd have finished it at this point in any book: it's the last chapter and I'd come nine chapters to see who leaked.

The closest I came to drifting was the start of Quist's statement: "The captain states that at noon on Monday—". At that point I knew I was about to be told things I'd mostly worked out in chapter 9.

---

## Where I got bored

- **Quist reading Ned's statement aloud.** The long paragraph that begins "—while Ms. Marr was on her rounds, he came up the back way for an impeller…" repeats a confession we already sat through in chapter 9, and now it's in police-report syntax. Two new facts (the truck key on the back step, "lift it and lean on it") are buried in a list. I skimmed for the new part.
- **The bedroom scene, from the condom on.** It follows the same pattern as chapters 5 and 6: a consent beat ("Yes. I want you inside me"), a logistics beat ("Arm?" "Fine."), then one long run-on sentence of sensations that ends in a dissolve ("and then none of that was anywhere"). In chapter 6 the dissolve was "somewhere in all of it she had stopped listening for the door." I recognised the shape and my eyes sped up. What's new here is the lamp in the window and "I love you". The sex in between is filler between them.
- **The grand jury corridor.** It's three short paragraphs of summary. "Inside, the prosecutor had asked her about the money once." The big outside event the book's clock counted down to ("Monday the twenty-third") gets a sentence of reported speech. I didn't mind much, because the book was never really about the courtroom, but the countdown fizzled.

## Where I got confused

- **The opening of scene 1 has two starts.** "Dane stood beside her at the head of the landing ramp…" is followed by a paragraph of backstory about the EMT. Then "A little before eleven, near the low, Beth stood by the locked box at the ramp head." I wasn't sure whether we'd jumped in time or were still in the same moment.
- **"Tomorrow I'm a passenger," then he goes down the ramp with the deputies.** I assumed he was being taken off the island right then. Then he turns up at her back steps that night. Where did he go, and why did they let him come back?
- **Why isn't she on the lifeboat?** Pike said "First light Saturday." The lifeboat has been at the float since eight. She stays another night and crosses on her own ferry Sunday. I can feel why the book wants that, but nobody on the page says why the plan changed. Once the bad guys are in cuffs the urgency is gone, I suppose, but I had to supply that myself.
- **"Tess said it first. 'Whitcomb's.'"** Whitcomb's what? It took me a second to realise this meant the white bait bucket by Quist's boot, and that Whitcomb is Russ. His surname appeared once, back in chapter 4. Then "She nudged it with her boot," and I still don't know why Quist is carrying a clean bait bucket around, or why Russ has bought her a new one in the epilogue. I can see it's a running joke, but I never got the first half of it.
- **"Kenny… back over on yesterday's boat."** Which boat was yesterday's? The lifeboat? The Marine Patrol boat? In chapter 8 Kenny was in Pruett.
- **"the first time since she was twenty-two"** (cleaning Ned's smears off the windows). Was Ned not letting her? Was it a superstition? I couldn't tell what it was telling me, only that it mattered.
- **"She sits low at the stern," Dane said. … her mouth did something he couldn't read.** I couldn't read it either. Is something stowed aft? Is it a boat joke I'm too landlocked for? It reads like a set-up with no punchline.
- **The final paragraph.** She's the master now, yet she's on the car deck with her hand on his on the binder while "the deckhand" raises the bow ramp. Who's in the wheelhouse? After a book that taught me the certificate means master plus one deck and that she does the corrections herself, it bothered me that the captain was down on the trailer chain as the gap opened.

## Where I stopped believing

- **An agent reading a suspect's sworn statement aloud, in full, to a witness who is also a person of interest, across that witness's own counter.** That is a device for informing the reader. No investigator does it.
- **"Goddamn," Quist said to the page. "How many nights did your float fuel a boat carrying fentanyl, Ms. Marr?"** That's TV-cop dialogue. It's also the first time the drug is named, at the very end, in a line written to explain it to me.
- **Tess's secret ledger.** She kept two years of the dragger's night fuel, with Lyle's threats in the margins, in her safe. That's in character, since she writes everything down. But it means she sat on evidence the whole week while Beth was being shot at. The book lets that slide with "Count the hundreds." I wanted Beth to feel it, or Tess to pay something for it.
- **The deputies idling out front all night while the suspended marshal sleeps with the witness the night before she testifies.** The book already admitted, through Giglio, that everything goes to the defense, so I half-accepted it. "Deputies'll hear" acknowledges it without making it plausible.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Every planted object comes back in this one chapter, as if ticked off a list.** The listing sheet, the boots, the dollar bet, the ham (now fourteen pounds), the bait bucket, Ned's gloves, the window smears, the dolphin correction, "Put my name in", "I'd still put Haskell on the list", the ramp-gap image, "too strong to drink." Each one is fine on its own. Together the ending feels as though it's closing tabs. A real book would let two or three of these go.
- **Two lines in a row do the same "since the ramp" job.** "I've been wrong about you every single time since the ramp," then, in the epilogue, "the first move of hers he had called right since the ramp." The second sounds like the first one's echo, written to make a matched pair.
- **"It's the best run I've had in four years. Don't fix it."** It's a bit too composed for a man lying in bed after sex with a dislocated-feeling arm. Dane was always the talker, and this is his talk with a ribbon on it.
- **Scene endings.** Nearly every scene closes on a button line: "Tell him yes." "Who's taking her home?" "…and her own pencilled *Teresa Marr* at the end." Then the final image. They're good buttons, but there are six in a row.
- **Voices.** Ned, Tess, Russ and Beth all talk in the same clipped coastal shorthand, with the subject dropped and the verb last, a little proverbial. Tess is the most distinct of them (headlines, prices), and Dane is the only one built differently (long, polite, self-mocking clauses). By chapter 10, Quist arrives and also speaks in short, flat island cadences ("Nobody stays off the boat"). She doesn't sound like a federal agent from Portland.
- **Recurring tics that have become furniture:** "too strong to drink", "the bad hip", "since she was in diapers" (twice in the book), "Steel doesn't burn / Everything in it does" (three times by chapter 9), and someone's hand closing on someone's wrist or sleeve "once".

## Prediction section

**What I expected going in:**
- That Ned was the leak. I'd suspected it since chapter 6 (Marr's price sticker on the impeller box, "Me and God," the confession about the reefer contract), and chapter 9 confirmed it. So chapter 10 had no whodunit left to give me, only consequences.
- That Ned would be taken away and Beth would get the boat. Chapter 4 set that up ("She still couldn't say *Put my name in*").
- That Dane would lose his gun and face a review for sleeping with his witness.
- That the notebook, which I'd read as a betrayal in chapter 7, would turn out to protect her ("No indication").
- An "I love you".

**What surprised me:**
- **Ned telling her to put her own name in, and adding "My saying so won't carry what it did."** I expected her to ask. I didn't expect him to turn her down in a way that was also a blessing. That was the best surprise.
- **Ned can't swim, and Dane is the one who steadies him over the water.** The book planted Ned's not swimming early and quietly, and here it pays off in an image instead of a line.
- **Tess's ledger.** I'd been half-suspecting Tess all book, as the book wanted me to. That she had been quietly building a case was a real turn, even if it arrives as a prop under the counter.
- **"I sold you condoms."** It's both the funniest and the most hurt line in the chapter. Tess is telling Beth: I helped you, and you wrote my name down for the police.
- **Pike is a woman in her fifties.** I'd pictured a man for nine chapters. It's a small, pleasant reset.
- **Dane is the POV in the epilogue, not Beth.** It worked. Seeing her through someone who still can't read her mouth was the right last angle.
- **The boat is called *Pier Pressure*.** It made me laugh, and I wasn't sure the book wanted that laugh at that moment. It's a slightly goofy note in a book that has been dry all the way through.

## Listener pass (read as if hearing it on audio)

- The opening paragraph is a mouthful aloud: "The kicks had opened the furrow, and at about two the fire company's EMT had closed it again on Tess's counter with fresh strips and a pressure dressing and checked the bone twice." That's four facts and three "and"s, before we even know where we are.
- "Tess said it first. 'Whitcomb's.'" A listener will have no idea what this refers to, and "She nudged it with her boot" has no visible "it" on audio.
- Quist's statement reads aloud like a deposition. On audio it would be the place I reached for the skip button.
- The run-on climax sentence in the bedroom actually works better aloud than on the page, because the rhythm carries it. The problem is that listeners heard the same rhythm in chapters 5 and 6.
- The switch to Dane's POV in the last scene is flagged clearly by "Dane Reyes stood…", so a listener won't get lost. Chapter 9 already used a Dane section, so it isn't a cheat.
- "Who's taking her home?" lands cleanly aloud. So does "You jumped."
- Names in this chapter: Quist, Pike, Haskell, Whitcomb, Marr, Kenny, Lyle, plus "the deputies", "the officers" and "the inspector". That's a lot of names and titles for a listener in a chapter meant to wind down.

## How the ending lands

**The mystery: earned.** The leak is Ned, and the book played fair. The clues were all on the page: the impeller box, "me and God", the red seal on the store bag, the box key, "Which tide?". The red-herring suspect (Tess) is cleared by her own evidence rather than by a speech. The money in the envelope is explained. The log is reversed from betrayal into proof of her honesty without anyone lecturing me about it. I don't feel cheated anywhere.

**Beth's arc: earned, and the best-built part of the book.** It started with a hundred-ton license with sharp corners, never used, and a woman who couldn't say "Put my name in." It ends with her cleaning Ned's windows and taking the correction off Pruett alone: "nobody stood beside her to make it after her." That line is the book's real ending, and it comes in scene 4, not the last scene.

**The cost: real, but mostly paid by Ned and Tess, not by Beth.** Beth loses her surrogate father to a cell and has her pencilled *Teresa Marr* in a federal file. Tess gets a split lip and a proposal accepted by proxy to a man going to prison. That's heavy and good. Beth herself comes out with the boat, the man, the indictment and the bet. That's a little clean for a book that kept telling me nothing on the island is free.

**The romance: mostly earned.** "Since when?" / "You jumped." is right, because it reaches back to the first scene and to who she is: she fell for the man who did the stupid brave thing. The lamp left burning in the window, with Tess free to write it down, is a lovely inversion of the whole book, which has been about lights being logged and hidden. I didn't need the sex scene to tell me any of that.

**The final image: earned.** The ramp rising, the gap of black water opening, her hand on his on the binder. It mirrors the jump without explaining that it does, which is what I want from a last line. The logistics of who is driving the boat nagged at me, but the image held.

**Overall:** the ending pays for the book. It doesn't overpay. The chapter spends a lot of its length tying off threads and too little letting anything hurt, apart from the first scene.

## Would I buy book 2?

**Maybe, and it depends on whose book it is.**

The hook on the page: Quist is building "a task force, every boat in this bay," with Russ on it. Dane is on leave while "the referral sat in somebody's inbox in Arlington." There's a new boat that's bad luck to rename. Ned is awaiting sentencing, with Tess's "yes" pending.

- If book 2 is Beth and Dane on this island, with Dane's referral hanging and Beth running the boat Ned lost, I'd buy it. I want to see those two in a week without a gun.
- If book 2 is Russ and Quist, I'm less sure. Quist got one scene and spoke like an islander. Russ is charming in small doses ("I'm not a complicated man"), but I don't know what he wants.
- The hook is soft. Nothing on the last page pulls me forward. The book ends at rest, which is right for this book and weak for a series opener. I'd buy book 2 because I liked the voice and the island, not because a question was left open.

## What I want to know next

Honestly, not much, and that's the right answer for a last chapter. The live questions are:
- What happens to Ned, and will Tess really marry him through glass?
- Does Dane keep his badge, and does he want to?
- Does Tess forgive Beth for writing her name down? The chapter has her say "You put my name in" and then never comes back to it.

None of these would make me order book 2 on release day.
