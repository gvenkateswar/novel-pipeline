---

## Where I got bored

- **The sex scene, from about "He was as careful with her as he had been with the strips on her knee" on.** This is the fourth intimate scene, and by now I know the routine: he checks for consent ("Beth?" / "That's a yes"), there's foil from a coat pocket, she tells him where to put his hand, she comes, then he comes with "nothing said," and then somebody is watching a door. The "both at once" move with his fingers already happened in Chapter 7. I skimmed until "I'll take the first watch." The two new things here are him looking over his shoulder at the door and her turning them around so he can see it. Those are good, and they're spent in the first few lines. After that it repeats.
- **"She watched his hands."** It's in here three times, plus "I've watched your hands all day." By the third time I felt the book pointing at its own motif.
- **The opening argument behind the split rock.** "Every night so far the kid's picked the place. He's hit and bleeding... For once he's weaker than we are, because he's the one with a hole in him." This is Dane laying out a plan to the reader, not arguing with a woman he loves. The scene is the two of them agreeing to do what I already knew they'd do. I was waiting for the trail.

## Where I got confused

- **"Now there's one way off this island." / "And it's my boat."** Which boat? In Chapter 2 the ferry's wheel spun with nothing on the other end. Nobody has fixed it on the page since. So either the ferry got fixed and I missed it, or Beth means some other boat. It reads like a strong line, and I had no idea what it was promising.
- **The fight at the cove.** Dane tears the rifle strap off the kid and kicks the rifle away. Then "The kid tore loose with a high sound." Tore loose from what? I never saw Dane get hold of him. I had to reread the paragraph and still can't place their bodies.
- **The kid's phone.** Dane "worked his thumb on it before the screen went dark," and hours later he has it open and reads it. I guess he turned off the auto-lock, but the book doesn't say so. With the one piece of evidence the whole mystery now hangs on, the hand-wave stood out.
- **"The Pruett dentist's house... I closed it up in October. The key's on my ring."** Since when does Beth look after summer houses? Russ was the caretaker for the north-side house. Her phone drowned on the rocks in Chapter 7, but her key ring apparently made it through the sea, the fire and the beach. It works, but the right key turns up exactly when they need one.
- **"My boss knew the lighthouse on Monday. This never sent it."** It took me a second to follow the logic: the boss knew the hiding place, the kid never got it, so the boss isn't the leak. Fair enough, but Chapter 8 ended on the boss saying "And me," which I took as the book pointing at her. One line of deduction clears her now, and it felt like the thread got cut instead of paid off.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Dane lets an armed, bleeding man escape twice and never mentions handcuffs.** He's a deputy U.S. Marshal with the only suspect at his feet, and his only move is grabbing a hood. The kid pistol-whips him and walks off uphill. I don't need a shootout, but a professional would at least think about restraints, or about shooting a man aiming a pistol at his witness. His rule about not firing toward houses doesn't apply here. They're in an empty cove.
- **A head wound bad enough to bleed into his eyebrow, an open knee, a storm, a gunman loose inland, and then slow sex on a daybed.** I believed Chapter 7 because of the hypothermia logic. This one I believed less. Dane being the one who says "Door first" helped. The scene that follows didn't.
- **"Records on her three came back clean."** The phone companies "take three days," and these came back clean exactly when the plot needs a burner number. That's convenient, though not fatal.
- **The kid is still "the kid" in Chapter 10.** In Chapter 5 it was a nice touch that nobody knew his name. Now it looks like the book has no plans for him beyond being a limp and a gun.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **"She was the case, and he had no other move."** That's the author explaining the threat at the moment it should only happen.
- **"Then it's your shore and my trail." / "Fine."** Every negotiation between these two ends this way: a balanced line, then "Fine" or "Done." I've seen it in almost every chapter.
- **"They looked at each other's blood and said nothing."** It's composed to be quoted. So is "His did." after "to see whether they knew what they were doing."
- **The scene breaks land on neat lines:** "And it's my boat." / "Door first." / "Then look at it." / "Somebody she had loved all her life was awake in a dark house on this island, waiting for a boy to write back." Each one alone is fine. Together, every scene closes with a click.
- **The two leads talk alike.** "I've been good at this the whole time. You've only seen the bad days." "I'll dry." "It will tomorrow." Dane's dry one-liners and Beth's dry one-liners come from the same mouth. Earlier, Dane was the one who talked too much and Beth was the one who went quiet. Here he "said nothing at all," she does the deadpan, and I couldn't tell the voices apart.
- **"Beth Calloway said" in the first line.** Using her full name in Chapter 10 sounds like a narrator reintroducing her. I noticed it.

## What I want to know next

- **Who sent those texts?** "constables. ill get him off the porch." / "north side house. russ has the key." / "north beach 3." It's someone who talks about Russ in the third person, someone the kid answers with "ok." That's real fuel.
- **Who is the second number,** the one that called four times and never texted? Cole's people checking on their hired man?
- **"Answer him," and Dane reaching for his own phone.** Are they going to set a trap with the kid's phone? That's the hook, and it works, but it's a quiet one. It ends on Dane reaching for a phone, not on anything happening.
- **Where is the kid?** He's bleeding inland in a storm, with no boat and no rifle.
- **On the romance side,** "Don't say it yet" against "Ask me in five days." I want to see the day she answers. I care about that less than the mystery right now, because the sex scenes have used up a lot of the tension.

The mystery has been "which of my three" since Chapter 6, and Chapters 6, 8, 9 and 10 each restate it. This chapter finally narrows it with evidence. If Chapter 11 goes back to restating, I'll lose patience.

**Prediction:** Ned's confession in Chapter 9 makes him the decoy. The texts sound like someone who sees Russ's porch and Russ's keys from the outside. Tess said the Dyer caller was a man, and she grabs her phone on the first buzz. Russ answered "Nobody" too fast and said "Late." My money is on Russ, and I'm about 55% sure. If it's Tess, she lied about the caller being a man, and I'd want the book to have earned that.

## Best moment

> Dane lifted a rock off the beach and put it through the bottom of the skiff, twice.
>
> "Now there's one way off this island."

There's no explanation and no quip, and it's a decision you can see. Right behind it, the texts on the kid's phone, and this:

> She put her thumb over the number, as if that would keep it from being anybody.

That one sentence holds everything the book has been building about her three people.

## Listener pass

- The long run-on paragraph in the sex scene ("The door was across the room with the chair under its knob...") works read aloud. It's the one place the prose changes gear.
- The texts are lowercase italics with no names. Read aloud, the lowercase cue disappears, and "constables" sounds like a plural noun instead of "constable's." A listener may not catch that the first text means Russ's house.
- With "Fuck," Dane said and "Fine" / "Done" exchanges in every chapter, the dialogue tags start to sound alike in audio.
