# Chapter 10 — First Boat: Beat Sheet

**Days:** Day 9 — Saturday, Day 10 — Sunday, Day 11 — Monday, then the coda (a Friday in December). Heading form per World Rule 3: **Day 9 to Day 11 — Saturday to Monday**, with the coda under its own subhead (*Three weeks later, a Friday*). Never print the year.
**Target:** about 2,850 words across six scenes (Beth for scenes 1–5, Dane for the coda).
**Chapter promise to deliver (outline, series row):** the coda in Dane's POV. A Friday night freight run in December. Dane, transferred to the District of Maine and back from suspension, stands on the passenger deck. Beth checks binders by feel. Her hand goes to his. A clear declaration in words. Quist hands Russ the photocopy of Tess's ledger: night fuelings for boats from three harbors, payments to a mainland buyer known only by an account. She wants him on the task force.

## Continuity and outline conflicts (flagged before the beats)

1. **Who is at the wheel on Day 10.** The outline says "the first ferry runs with Ned at the wheel," and the bible says Ned "captains the first boat on Day 10." The Ch 09 flag and this brief want Beth at the wheel. **Resolution used here:** Ned is master in command, on his license, in the wheelhouse. Without being asked, he gives Beth the wheel. This keeps the bible true (he captains) and pays off her unused 100-ton license ("asking Ned for the wheel would feel like asking him to be old"). She never has to ask.
2. **Who fired from the skiff on Day 7.** The Ch 09 flag says "Arsenault or the second crewman; decide and say." The bible says Doucette: his entry has him firing the Day 7 shot, and World Rule 11 says "Doucette a handgun (Day 7 shot from the skiff at about 60 yards)". Ch 09 left it open ("Nobody saw the shooter"). Doucette was free that morning (the radio-guided walk came that night), so nothing on the page conflicts. **Resolution used here:** Doucette fired both rounds. The *Margaret Ann*'s second crewman ran the gray skiff's outboard. Quist says it plainly on Day 10. If the orchestrator wants Arsenault or the crewman as the shooter instead, World Rule 11 and Doucette's entry would have to change, which needs the author.
3. **"Cross-examination" at the grand jury.** Research note 23 says a grand jury has no judge and no defense lawyer, so there is no cross. The bible has AUSA Moreau ask Beth about Dane (one question, one answer). **Resolution used here:** Moreau asks the question himself and says it is the one a defense lawyer will ask her at trial. That puts her answer in the record, which is the point.
4. **"Friday night freight run" in the coda vs Ned losing the freight contract.** The lost contract is the dealer's **Thursday** night reefer run. The Friday run in the coda is the island's ordinary weekly freight: propane, fuel, groceries. The prose must make that difference clear in one clause, or readers will think the contract came back. The slip's transfer bridge is rehung by December 11 (crane barge, new cables), so trucks are loading again. On Day 10 it is "passengers and light freight" only, as Ned announced in Ch 09, plus the two storm-held trucks, which drive off at Pruett.
5. **Quist and the word "fentanyl."** Research note 39 lets Quist name it in Ch 10, but the Master Continuity Flags forbid it. The flag wins: nobody says "fentanyl" in this chapter. The bible says Tess learns what the pills were from Quist on Day 9, so Quist describes them without the word (scene 2).
6. **The fish house in testimony.** World Rule 7: nobody is told where it is. When Beth testifies about the relationship she says "a place of my family's," never the fish house or the cove.
7. **Quist's two appearances.** The outline puts Quist on the float at noon on Day 9. This brief has her meeting the first boat at Pruett on Day 10. Both happen: she leaves with the prisoners on Day 9 and is waiting at the Pruett ramp on Day 10 to take Beth to Moreau.

Spent or capped items carried into this chapter: Dane's two swears are spent, so he has none here. Ned's "goddamn" is spent. Tess gets her one real swear here, when she breaks. Quist gets her one "fuck" here, about the ledger. That makes two strong swears, inside the cap of four. Ned's third coffee is unspent and is used in scene 4. Beth's counting: at most twice, and both slots are marked below. "Not asking": once, marked in scene 3. Button endings: two, marked (scenes 5 and 6). Do not use any retired tic: "put it where she put things," "longer than X needed," "the way she'd [boat task]," "right side clear," neat closing lines elsewhere, "wet/hard," "let go of everything."

### Scene 1
**Setting:** Day 9, noon. The stone pier beside the wrecked slip, where the *North Reach* lies starboard side to with the gangway down. The harbor is flat and bright under a hard blue sky, and the air is still after the northwest blow. Sensory anchor: two engines coming in across water so still the wakes reach the pier long after the boats have stopped. A gull walks the twisted transfer bridge.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Quist, Ned, Russ, the two relief deputies (unnamed per the bible), Marine Patrol officers (unnamed). Tess, Arsenault and Doucette are aboard, unseen.
**Surface action:** A Maine Marine Patrol boat and a Coast Guard boat come in. Quist steps across onto the pier first, gray-faced from the crossing, in a navy HSI raid jacket over a down vest. She says nothing about the sea. The relief deputies come up the brow. On the gangway, in front of Quist and with Beth watching from the rail, the senior relief deputy formally relieves Dane: Pike's instructions, read flat. He is off the detail and off the case. There is an internal-affairs referral, and he reports to Portland when he lands. Dane hands over the detail in his own order: the witness, the locations, the prisoners. He does not mention the fish house. Then Quist holds out an evidence bag, and Dane takes the Rite in the Rain notebook from his inside pocket with his good hand and puts it in. Quist: "It goes to Moreau. All of it goes to the defense, Reyes." This is the first time she calls him Reyes. He answers, "I know." Marine Patrol take Doucette and Arsenault off the ferry to their boat. The second crewman is already aboard it in cuffs, taken off the *Margaret Ann* on the way in.
**Subtext:** The cost of the clearing stops being words on a sat phone. Beth watches the notebook that broke her trust go into a bag where strangers will read every line of it, including "Subject and I." She finds that she minds, for him. Dane does not look at her during the handover because he is still on duty for one more minute, and he means to finish it properly.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and institutional. The formal machinery arrives and starts taking things: his case, his book, his place beside her. Quist is a stranger to Beth in person, and she is the woman who suspected her.
**Ends on:** An action, not a line. The evidence bag's seal pressed shut under Quist's thumb, and Dane's empty hand going to his inside pocket out of habit and finding nothing there.
**Word count target:** 450. Advances plot: relief, Dane's costs made concrete, prisoners moved.

### Scene 2
**Setting:** Day 9, early afternoon. The passenger cabin of the *North Reach*. The stove is lit and the windows are steamed at the corners. Tess sits upright on the bench by the stove where Ch 09 left her, in the red vest, the red band still on her right wrist. Sensory anchor: the stove ticking. A paper coffee cup Tess hasn't touched, a skin on top.
**Characters present:** Beth, Tess, Quist. One relief deputy is outside the door.
**Surface action:** Quist comes in with Beth behind her. Quist sits across from Tess and asks one plain question: does Tess know what was in the cartons? Tess: "Pills." Quist describes them without naming them: blue tablets, pressed to look like a pharmacy's, each one enough to stop the breathing of someone who thinks they're taking something for a bad back. Tess's real swear, her one in the book, comes out small, not shouted: "Oh, fuck." Then "Oh, Bethie," to the floor. Quist tells her she is under arrest and tells her why. Before anyone asks, Tess says where the ledger is: under the floor of the back office, beside the booster shelf, in a gallon freezer bag. "Every boat. Every gallon. I add it twice." Quist writes it down. Then Tess looks at Beth, and the one exchange happens:
- Tess: "They said they'd only scare you." What she told herself, said once, without defending it.
- Beth: "You warned me."
That is all on the subject. No speech, no apology asked for or given. Then the one thing Tess asks. It is not forgiveness. It is for someone else: "Make Ned eat something that isn't coffee." Beth answers with a task and not a promise: she takes the cold cup off the bench and pours it over the side outside the door. That is a yes, and both of them know it. Quist cuffs Tess in front, gently, avoiding the bruised wrist. The relief deputy walks her down the brow to the Marine Patrol boat. Ned stands at the pier end with his hands in his jacket pockets and the same foot of air between them as in Ch 09. As she passes, Tess's hand lifts an inch toward his sleeve, against the cuffs, and stops.
**Subtext:** Both of them love each other, and that is what makes the scene unbearable. Tess's ask shows who she still is: she remembers what people need, as she remembers coffee orders. Beth won't give her absolution and won't refuse her the small thing. Beth also learns, watching Tess's face at the description, that Tess truly did not know what she was moving. That doesn't excuse her. It makes it worse.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and internal. Beth has to stand in a room with the person who sold her location four times, and that person was the first to warn her. Quist's procedural flatness is the only thing holding the scene upright.
**Ends on:** An image. The Marine Patrol boat's stern line coming off the cleat, and Ned on the pier not raising his hand. Do not end on a line of dialogue.
**Word count target:** 500. Advances plot: Tess arrested and cooperating, the ledger located (the Book-2 hook planted), Tess's fate. Deepens character for Tess, Beth and Ned.
**Rules:** Tess speaks at most six short lines in total. Her guilt and her love both show in hands: the cuffs, the sleeve, the cup. She must not explain her motive. The reader assembled it in Ch 08.

### Scene 3
**Setting:** Day 9, about 3:30, the light already going long and yellow. The stone pier at the foot of the brow. Marine Patrol's second trip is alongside with its engine idling, ready to take Dane and Quist to Pruett. Sensory anchor: at dusk the island's grid comes back as the cable crew restores power (World Rule 5). Porch lights and kitchen windows come on house by house up the Harbor Road. The store's generator out back stops, and the silence where its roar was is louder than the roar.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Quist, Ned. Russ is up the pier, out of earshot.
**Surface action:**
- **Ned's fate, decided on the page (about 180 words).** Quist stops Ned on the pier with her notebook out. He gives his statement Monday, everything about the year he looked away. He took no money. If his statement reads the way he told it to Beth, she sees no charge, and she will not be asking the Coast Guard to look at his license. He asks one thing: "The Thursday run." Quist: the dealer's license will be gone by Monday, and his contract with it. There won't be a Thursday night run, not on that contract. Ned takes it the way he takes weather. He turns his hearing aid off with one finger. That is his tell that the conversation is over, and it is the first time Beth has seen him use it on bad news instead of on people.
- **The parting (about 270 words).** Dane comes down the brow with his go-bag on his good shoulder and stops in front of Beth. People are watching: Quist, the deputies, Ned. He doesn't touch her. He tells her the costs plainly, in his own voice, as fact and not complaint: off the case, the referral, and probably a suspension. He has asked for Portland. "I've asked for Portland. Whatever else they do to me." Beth puts her palm flat on his chest, through the soft-shell, the way she reads a truck or a man she thinks is lying. She holds it there and reads him. He holds still and lets her. True. She says, "Come on the Friday boat." He says, "Which Friday." She says, "Any of them." She does not ask him to promise. This is the chapter's one "not asking" beat. He covers her hand with his good one for the length of one breath, then lifts the bag and steps down into the Marine Patrol boat beside Quist.
**Subtext:** This is a parting, not a breakup, and both of them make sure of it without saying "love" yet. He won't kiss her in front of the record, not out of shame but because she is still a witness until Monday, and he has learned what timing costs. She checks him with her hand because that is the only proof she has ever trusted, and this time she does it openly, in front of everyone.
**Tension source:** Situational and romantic. The boat is idling, the costs are real, and nothing is guaranteed. Portland might say no.
**Ends on:** An image of motion, not a button line. The Marine Patrol boat's wake reaching the pier long after the boat is small, and the lights coming on up the hill, Beth's own cottage among them, with nobody in it.
**Word count target:** 450. Advances plot: Ned's fate, Dane's costs, the transfer request, and the setup for the coda's Friday boat.

### Scene 4
**Setting:** Day 10, Sunday, first light, about 6:30. The *North Reach*'s wheelhouse, then the crossing, then the Pruett Harbor slip under its sodium ramp lights, still burning in the half-dark. The harbor is glass. Sensory anchor: the diesel coming up to 900 rpm through her socks and boot soles. Burnt coffee and wet wool. Ned's rag-smeared windows.
**Characters present:** Beth, Ned, Kenny Thibodeau (walk-on; well again, working the lines on deck), the two relief deputies (passenger cabin), Quist (at Pruett).
**Surface action:**
- **The wheel.** Ned climbs the ladder slowly with two cups. It is the third coffee (his apology tic, the last of three). He sets one by Beth's hand. Then, instead of taking his chair, he stands aside from the wheel and says, "Take her off." Nothing else. She doesn't thank him. She takes the wheel.
- **Competence in motion, one last time.** There is no slip, so this is a pier departure: starboard side to the stone face, flat calm, the wrecked bridge off her bow. She has Kenny single up and hold the aft spring, puts the wheel over, and walks the bow off the pier against the spring with a touch ahead. When the stern is clear she calls "Let go aft" and backs her out past the twisted bridge with feet to spare. Ned's hands stay in his pockets. She passes the *Margaret Ann* on her mooring, dark now, with Marine Patrol's yellow tape across her rail, and the fuel float, dark, the way it should be. **Counting slot 1:** on the crossing she knows the minutes to Pruett, and the number matters because the grand jury is tomorrow and she is going to be on time for it.
- **Pruett.** She brings the *North Reach* into the Pruett slip and lowers the bow ramp from the wheelhouse controls. Then she goes down to the car deck herself and releases the two storm-held trucks: the fire company's propane truck and the store's box truck, chained since Day 1. She does it by feel, though the sodiums are on, because her hands know these binders. She passes the welded patch in the deck plate without looking at it.
- **Quist at the ramp head.** She stands on the transfer bridge in the orange light, steady now that she's on land. As they walk up together she says it plainly, as one item in a list: Doucette fired both rounds at the landing on Day 7. The *Margaret Ann*'s second crewman ran the gray skiff, stolen out of another harbor down the bay. He has been talking since Marine Patrol took him off Saturday. Doucette's gun is at the lab with the round from the bait-shed wall. Then Beth says, plainly, "You thought I took the money." Quist: "I did. Now I don't." She does not apologize, and Beth respects that more than she would an apology (bible: Beth and Quist).
**Subtext:** Ned gives her the wheel in place of every word he can't say: about the reefer, about Tess, about her father having asked him to hire her. It is also an admission that the boat's future without the Thursday contract may need her license. Beth takes it without ceremony because ceremony would make him say something. The boat she knows blind is the one thing in the ten days that never lied to her.
**Tension source:** Mostly release, with a thread of situational tension: a pier departure with no slip in front of the man who taught her, and tomorrow's room in Portland ahead.
**Ends on:** Not a button. Beth getting into the back of the relief deputies' car at the ramp head with the subpoena from under the barometer folded in her float coat pocket, and through the rear window, Ned in the wheelhouse wiping the glass with his rag and making it worse.
**Word count target:** 600. This scene warrants the most words because it carries the outline's "competence in motion" promise and three plot items (the wheel, the shooter, Quist).

### Scene 5
**Setting:** Day 11, Monday morning. The grand jury room in the federal courthouse on Federal Street, Portland. Use that phrase, not the building's name or address (Research note 27). It is a plain room with rows of chairs for the jurors, a table, a witness chair, and one tall window with real daylight in it. Sensory anchor: central heating that is too warm after ten days of woodstoves and a 58-degree store, and the dry smell of radiator dust.
**Characters present:** Beth, AUSA Daniel Moreau, the grand jurors (unnamed, as a body), a court reporter. No judge and no defense lawyer, per Research note 23.
**Surface action:** Keep it brief and stated plainly. Do not dramatize a trial.
- She tells the night freight in order: the dark display, the box's temperature, the sprung lid, the fourteen cartons, the plate at 9:44. **Counting slot 2:** the fourteen cartons, said aloud as a fact for the record. That is the last time she counts in the book.
- **The $9,000, cleared.** Moreau puts a copy of the Day 1 credit union deposit slip in front of her. Her paycheck line is in her own handwriting. The cash line under it, $9,000 in hundreds, is in another hand: square figures pressed hard enough to dent the paper. Beth knows that hand from nineteen years of store receipts and from the totals Tess added twice. Moreau states for the record that Teresa Marr has given a statement that she placed the cash. Beth says once that she never saw it, never knew of it, and that her balance before that paycheck was $412.
- **The question about Dane.** Moreau says it is the question a defense lawyer will put to her at trial, so he is asking it first. While Deputy Reyes was protecting her, did she have a sexual relationship with him? One answer, as the bible says. Beth: "Yes. I chose it. He told me before it happened that he'd report it, and he did." She says it in daylight. That pays off her Ch 08 line "I'll tell them I chose it. In daylight." She does not name where (World Rule 7). Moreau has no follow-up. A juror in the second row writes something down.
**Subtext:** The thing she was most afraid would be used against her is now in the record in her own words, which means nobody can ever find it. She has done what Dane did on Day 8 and taken the cost to be believed. The room is the opposite of the boat: someone else's ground, someone else's rules. She is steady in it anyway, and that is the growth.
**Tension source:** Situational. A room without the exits she'd choose, strangers judging her credibility, and the two documents that could have sunk her (the slip and the relationship) both laid on the table.
**Ends on:** **Button ending 1 of 2.** Her answer stands, and Moreau says "Thank you, Ms. Calloway. That's all." Stop there. Do not follow her out to the street and do not summarize what it meant.
**Word count target:** 350. Advances plot: the testimony given, the $9,000 cleared, the relationship on the record. The main plot is closed.

### Scene 6 — Coda (Dane POV)
**Subhead:** *Three weeks later, a Friday.* (December 11. The weekday may appear; never print the year.)
**Setting:** The *North Reach*'s open passenger deck on the Friday night freight run out of Pruett: full dark, about 6:30, a hard frost. The run is the island's weekly freight (propane, fuel, groceries), not the dealer's Thursday reefer run, which is gone. Say that in one clause. The transfer bridge at Cutter has been rehung. Sensory anchor, in Dane's terms: the orange sodium floods on the car deck below, the sightline from the rail down onto the line of trucks, and his own boots, which are XtraTufs now. He finally bought deck boots.
**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Quist, Russ (on the Cutter float at landing), Ned (in the wheelhouse, seen as a shape behind smeared glass).
**Surface action:**
- **Where he stands.** Dane is at the passenger-deck rail with his back to the deckhouse wall and his view of the deck clear. That is his old habit, and he notices that he no longer needs it. Quist stands beside him, seasick and upright, the ring on its chain inside her collar. In passing, in his head, we get the facts of his costs as they finally landed: a suspension served without pay, a letter in his file that will be there when he retires, and the District of Maine, which took his transfer because it was short of deputies anyway. He is transferred and disciplined, not promoted. This is the third Friday he has taken this boat, and he goes back on the Monday first boat. A pink seam on his left arm pulls when he leans on the rail.
- **The mirror of Ch 01.** Below, under the floods, Beth walks the line of trucks with her gloves off, checking chain binders by feel: palm on the handle, a quarter turn taken up without breaking stride. In his POV it comes through hands and decisions, not numbers. **Dane does not count** (bible rule). He thinks that he spent ten days reading her hands and only now gets to watch them without writing anything down. The concrete future comes in here: since the first of the month she has stood relief watches at the wheel on the district's Sunday runs, on her own license, because Ned asked the district board, which he will never mention to her. And she is coming to Lowell on her two days off at Christmas, where his father will ask, as he does every year, whether he has a horse yet. No proposal, no moving in (World Rule 14).
- **The hook (Quist).** Quist says she is going out to see Whitcomb and shows Dane the edge of a manila envelope. She can say this much to him now, since he is off the case: a photocopy of Tess's ledger. Two years of night fuelings, boats from three harbors, cash routed to a mainland buyer known only by an account number. Her one swear, about the ledger: two years of it in that woman's hand, every column added twice, and she has never seen a cleaner fucking ledger in her life. She wants Russ on the task force: "Three harbors. One account. And he knows every boat in this bay by its exhaust note." Dane notes, as a reader of people, that she has already decided and is only practicing the ask.
- **Her hand.** Beth comes up the outside ladder still in her float coat, stands at the rail beside him, and her hand goes to his on the cold rail. It is her move, unprompted, in front of Quist.
- **The declaration.** He says it in plain words, the first time either of them has said it: that he loves her. It is the one rule he now breaks on purpose and on the record. She starts to answer the way she always has, with a task ("Hand me—"), and stops. Then she says it back, plainly, a statement of fact like a weather call. A kiss, brief, on an open deck in the cold. No heat beyond that (HEAT RULES).
- **Landing.** The *North Reach* comes into the Cutter slip. Under the ramp sodiums Russ is on the float with his cap in his hand, the habit he uses to buy time. Quist goes down the brow with the envelope, and Dane watches Russ take it and put his cap back on. Up the hill the fuel float at the store is dark, the way it should be after six.
**Subtext:** Every one of Dane's tics has turned over. He reads a room and finds nothing he has to guard. He watches her hands and the watching is no longer an order. He says the thing out loud instead of withholding it "for her own good." The hook is seen from outside the case, by a man who isn't on it, which keeps Beth and Dane's story closed while Book 2 opens in front of them.
**Tension source:** Low by design. The only edge is whether she will answer in words or deflect with a task, and that is what she chooses to change. Russ taking the envelope carries the forward pull for Book 2.
**Ends on:** **Button ending 2 of 2.** The final image belongs to the couple, not the hook. After Russ takes the envelope, Beth turns from the rail to go down and lower the ramp, which is her job, and she doesn't let go of his hand until the ladder makes her. Or end on one short line of hers that gives him a task, the way she has given him positions all book, this time something domestic and small. Choose one; do not use both. Do not end on Quist, the ledger or a question.
**Word count target:** 500–550. Delivers the outline's series chapter promise: HEA bookend, clear declaration, concrete future, Book-2 hook (the ledger, Russ, Quist).
