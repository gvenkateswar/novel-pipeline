### Scene 1: The split rock

**Setting:** The big split rock on the north shore where the land turns, out of sight of the beach. Tuesday, about half past three (sunset is a little after four). The second storm is a black wall to the south, coming in, and the wind is getting back an edge it didn't have at three. **Sensory anchor:** the first hard grains of snow out of the south hissing on the rock beside her face. (Not her clothes crackling; Ch 09 used that.)

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Opening line (Flag 17):** *"'Not Ned,' Beth Calloway said, and heard how it sounded out loud."*
2. **The Ned exchange, four lines and no more (A19):**
   - Beth: "Not Ned."
   - Dane: "He knew the hour. So did two other people you love."
   - Beth: "He stood in the sea for me."
   - Dane: "I know. Every night so far the kid's picked the place. Not tonight."
   Then Ned leaves the chapter until s5.
3. **The hunt argument, whole, face to face.** Dane crouches to her level and makes his case in his long sentences, built on "because":
   - The kid is hit and bleeding, and the snow holds his trail until the storm fills it, which is under an hour.
   - Every other night he has had the dark and the choice, and tonight is the one night he's weaker than they are.
   - She's wet to the neck. Sitting behind a rock while the storm comes in will freeze her, and the lighthouse is a mile back the wrong way (Flag 5).
4. Beth's case, plain and short: it's nearly dark, there's a storm, and he's a man with a gun walking ahead of her into the woods.
5. Dane answers each point. He goes first. If the trail turns toward houses, toward people, they stop and go back. He doesn't follow it anywhere he can't see.
6. Beth sets her own term, and it isn't a concession. Where the trail runs near the shore, she leads, because it's her shore and she knows where a man can go on it and where he can't.
7. **The longing (an act, not a statement):** partway through his case, she puts her cold hand inside his coat, flat against his ribs, for the warmth. He doesn't stop arguing, and he doesn't move her hand.
8. "Fine." She stands up first. It's her choice too.

**Subtext:** Beth wants it to be anybody but Ned, and she knows that saying "Not Ned" out loud is what makes it sound possible. Dane doesn't push on Ned. He moves her off the question by giving her something to do with the fear, which is what she would do herself. Under the argument, both know that going after the kid ends the hiding.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal (a real disagreement in which he's right about the odds and she's right about the dark), situational (the storm wall, the light going, her body getting colder), and internal (the name she has just said).

**Texture budget:** (1) The storm wall to the south and the snow hissing on the rock, the clock made visible. (2) Her hand on his ribs through his wet shirt, the want made physical.

**Ends on:** *Motion.* She stands up first and goes, and the last beat is a physical one: she is already down off the rock onto the shore stones, heading east, and he is coming after her. "Fine." is not the last word; one plain action follows it.

**Word count target:** 280 (compress here first if the chapter runs long).

### Scene 2: The trail and the east cove

**Setting:** The woods above the east shore, then the shore rocks under the bank, then the east cove: a short curve of round stones between two arms of rock, spruce running down to it, the one place on this stretch where a skiff can sit out of the sea. Last light, about a quarter past four, the gray going out of everything. **Sensory anchor:** the blood, black in the failing light against the snow. Then, at the cove, the sound before the sight: a skiff's flat bottom grinding over stones in short drags.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, the kid (unnamed on purpose; marker: the dragging left heel).

**Surface action, in order:**
1. They cut up from the shore to the tree line and find the trail where Ch 09 left it, going east. **Dane reads it in silence (A15)** and shows her with his hand, never in words:
   - a smear of blood on a spruce trunk at shoulder height, where the kid leaned on his left side, so it's the left arm (Flag 11);
   - a body-shaped hollow in the snow and a handprint, where he fell;
   - the bad foot plowing a groove the snow hasn't filled yet (the heel, once, in new words);
   - a turn **downhill**, away from the houses inland.
2. **Beth says it out loud,** because she's the one who knows the shore: "He's going to his boat. Between here and the village there's one cove a skiff can sit in" (Flag 4).
3. **Beth leads**, her term from s1, down off the trail onto the shore rocks under the bank and along them. The woods and the road above them are the kid's (Flag 7); the shore is hers. It brings them at the cove from the north end, below the kid's sightline: he'll be watching his own trail behind him, the way anyone would come. Dane follows with his own gun out, silent. Her knowledge plus his reading gets them there ahead of the dark.
4. **The cove, from the rocks at its north lip.** The kid is dragging his skiff down the stones toward the water one-handed, his rifle slung across his back by its strap. His phone is lit in his good right hand, held to his ear. He has switched it on to call someone, and it rings and rings, and nobody answers. In its light Beth sees his face: young, scared, and grey.
5. Dane's hand on her shoulder means *stay*. He goes down the rocks fast and quiet. One order: **"Put it down."**
6. The kid drops the phone onto the stones and swings the rifle round on its strap, and Dane is on him before it comes round. The rifle goes onto the stones, and Dane kicks it away down toward the water.
7. The kid tears loose (his hurt arm, a sound out of him) and goes **up the rocks at Beth**, because she is the case and he has nothing left, dragging a pistol out of his hoodie pocket with his right hand as he climbs.
8. **Why Dane doesn't fire (Flag 10), one clause in Beth's POV:** she's on the rocks right behind the kid, in the line, and she sees Dane see it.
9. **Beth's choice:** silent (her fear register; no words), she throws herself sideways off the rock, out of the line, and comes down hard on the stones below. Her knee hits a rock edge and opens. She doesn't feel it yet.
10. **Dane's second swear of the book,** one word, aloud, as he goes after the kid up the rocks (Flag 3).
11. The kid is a step from her, and the pistol is coming up. Dane catches him by the back of the hood and hauls him backward off the rock. The kid swings the pistol back and down across Dane's head. Dane goes to one knee on the stones, and the kid gets free into the spruce, going east and **inland**, uphill toward the woods above the road, gone.
12. **Dane goes to Beth, not after him.** His hand is on her before she has sat up. Blood is coming through the torn knee of her jeans, and blood is running out of his hair into one eyebrow. They each look at the other's blood. Almost no words.
13. Dane gets up. He takes the phone off the stones while it's still lit and works his thumb on it before the screen can go dark (Flag 12; no explanation). He picks up the rifle, checks it and slings it.
14. **The skiff (A2).** Beth looks at the sky to the south and then at the skiff:
    - Beth: "He'd have been swamped at the ledges. An hour across is the ferry. In that, it's three, and the storm's here inside the hour."
    - Dane: "He was going to try."
    - Beth: "He's got nothing to lose."
    - Dane lifts a rock off the beach and puts it through the bottom of the skiff, twice.
    - Dane: "Now there's one way off this island."
    - Beth: "And it's my boat."

**Subtext:** Dane is doing his job where she can finally see it, in silence and without a joke, and she watches his hands the way she watches anybody's hands, to see whether they know what they're doing. They do. The kid went for her, not for Dane, and both of them understand what that means. The skiff exchange is two right people naming two costs. Hers: the kid would have drowned trying. His: wrecking it traps the kid on the island with them, cornered, and the only boat left for anyone who wants off, or wants her, is her ferry.

**Tension source:** Situational and physical: the dark coming, a wounded man with two guns, a pistol a step from her face. Then the realization that the danger hasn't ended, it has changed shape.

**Texture budget:** (1) The trail signs Dane shows her with his hand (bark, hollow, groove, the turn downhill): competence a listener can see. (2) The kid's lit phone at his ear, ringing out unanswered, which plants the second number without anyone saying so.

**Ends on:** *Image.* After "And it's my boat," one or two plain sentences: the skiff settling on the stones with sea coming in through the hole, and the first hard snow arriving sideways out of the south, all at once. Nothing spoken after it. No sound of the skiff knocking (that's Ch 11's).

**Word count target:** 620. **Floor:** 580. The cove fight is the chapter's action climax and must play beat by beat in real time, with no cutaway.

### Scene 3: The dentist's house (about 400 words; never the scene that gets compressed; runs into Scene 4 with no break)

**Setting:** The Pruett dentist's summer house on the hill above the east cove: gray shingles, every window shuttered for the winter. Beth closed it up herself in October. It has a **front door** facing the east road up the hill, which she bolted from inside, and a **back door** into the kitchen off the cove path (Flag 6). Inside it's a shut-house cold, colder than outdoors, and it smells of someone else's soap and cold ashes. There's a woodstove, and a daybed against the wall beside it, across the room from the back door. **Sensory anchor:** their breath showing white in the phone light in the cold kitchen.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **The storm hits as they climb** the path from the cove: snow out of the south, sideways, the wind back all at once.
2. **Why this house (A1, Flag 7),** two or three short lines between them on the path:
   - The road is out: the kid went into the woods above it, and the road is his tonight.
   - The shore path back to the village is two hours in the dark, along rocks he could shoot down onto.
   - The blood went east and inland, away from here. Dane: "He won't come back to a boat with a hole in it, toward a man who's got his rifle."
3. **Her key (A5b):** on her own ring. She closed this house for the summer people in October. That fact is said once, plainly, and it isn't a joke.
4. **She starts it at the back door (A5c).** The kitchen door swings in on the black, and before he can clear the house or either of them can light anything, she turns in the doorway and kisses him, out of the cove: hard, cold-faced, the rifle strap between them, his blood on her cheek. He kisses her back.
5. Then, against her mouth: **"Door first."**
6. **He makes the house safe:**
   - He clears it fast: the kitchen, the front room, the back bedroom.
   - He checks the front door's bolt, the one she set in October, and the shutters.
   - He wedges the back door with a chair under the knob.
   - He stands the rifle against the end of the daybed and lays his own gun on the chair beside it.
7. **The stove (Flag 9):** Dane says to light it and gives his reason in one line to her face: the kid already knows where they are, the storm hides the smoke, and her being wet is the bigger risk tonight. He lights it, and Beth tells him which damper. The stove door stays open a crack, so the room has light, the first lit room in the book (Ch 04 had a heater's glow, Ch 07 had total dark).
8. **The first aid, as its own beat (Flag 2):**
   - He sees the blood through her torn knee in the light and sits her on the daybed.
   - He cuts her jeans open above the knee with the kit's scissors, cleans the cut, and closes it with thin strips that pull the edges together, then a pad and tape. He's quick and sure.
   - She watches his hands. That's all the page says about her watching. **No "that's what she'd been waiting for."**
   - **The laugh,** while he works. Beth: "You're good at this." / Dane: "I've been good at this the whole time. You've only seen the bad days."
