## 1. Retelling

After the shooting on the beach, Beth keeps saying it can't be Ned. Dane says they have to go after the kid now, because the kid is shot and bleeding, and the snow will show his trail until the storm covers it in about an hour. They follow the blood east and find the kid at a little cove trying to get his small boat into the water. He's on his phone and nobody is picking up. Dane jumps him and gets his rifle away. The kid breaks loose and comes at Beth with a pistol. She throws herself off a rock so Dane can get at him and cuts her knee open. Dane drags the kid back by his hood, but the kid hits Dane on the head with the pistol and runs off into the woods. Dane keeps the kid's phone and rifle and smashes a hole in the boat, so the kid can't leave the island. The storm hits, so they go to an empty summer house Beth has the key to. Dane patches her knee and they make love. When he starts to say "I love you," she stops him: "Don't say it yet." Later they read the kid's texts. One number sent him every place Beth slept and the hour of the beach pickup. Dane's boss says the phone records for Beth's three friends came back clean. Then a text arrives on the kid's phone, "where r u. answer me," and Beth tells Dane to answer it.

## 2. What's at stake

Beth is the only person who saw the drugs, so she's the whole case against Cole. The kid, and whoever is feeding him her locations, need her dead before the grand jury meets on Monday, six days away. If she doesn't testify, Cole goes free. Under that, someone she has loved all her life is selling her out, and it looks like one of Tess, Russ or Ned.

## 3. The romance

Yes, I felt it clearly. They want each other, and it's more than sex now.

- At the dentist's door: "She turned in the doorway and kissed him, hard and cold-faced, with the rifle strap between them and his blood on her cheek." I felt it right away.
- He keeps looking back at the door, and she swaps places so he can watch it instead of making fun of him: "That's a yes. I turned us round for it." It's said out loud, so I didn't have to work it out myself.
- The big moment: "Beth. I—" / She put her hand over his... "Don't say it yet." I caught it because of chapter 8, where he said "I'm in love with you" and she said "Ask me in five days." This is the same promise, still waiting to be kept.
- "I've watched your hands all day." That line pays off the tracking scene, where she watched his hands to see if he knew what he was doing.

## 5. Where I lost the thread

1. Line 259: "Records on her three came back clean." Right before that, at line 249, the texting number is "none of" her friends' numbers. Then at line 265 Beth decides "Somebody she had loved all her life" sent the texts. While half-listening, "clean" sounded like her three friends were cleared, so I didn't see why she still thought it was one of them. I worked out that one of them must have a second, secret phone, but nobody says that. One plain line from Dane or Beth would fix it.
2. Line 245: "My boss knew the lighthouse on Monday." I'd been carrying "Monday" as grand-jury day, so for a second I thought he meant the future. It's yesterday. "Knew the lighthouse" is also an odd way to put it. I got his point (the boss knew where they were and the kid never got that, so the boss is cleared) only after replaying it in my head.
3. Line 251: "Another number's called him four times since the beach. Never once texted." I don't know whose number that is, or whether "where r u. answer me" comes from it or from the number that sent the texts. So when Beth says "Answer him," I wasn't sure who "him" is: the traitor, or a second person.
4. Line 83: "An hour across is the ferry. In that, it's three." I tripped on it. I think it means the ferry takes an hour, but a small boat in a storm would take three. It took a second listen.
5. Line 157: who lies where on the daybed. I couldn't picture it ("went over him on her good knee... at the outside edge, facing the room... his back to the wall"). It didn't matter, because "Can you see it?" / "Yes." tells me what I need to know.
6. Line 171, the long run-on sentence. It's meant to blur, and it did. That's fine as mood. On audio I lost where I was, then caught up again at "the shutters shook."

## 6. Verdict: PASS

I can retell the main event: they track the kid, he gets away, they take his phone and sink his boat, and the texts show one number has been giving him Beth's locations. I can say what's at stake.
