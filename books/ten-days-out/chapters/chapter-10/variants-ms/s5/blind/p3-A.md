"Who carried the bags up from the car deck that Thursday night?"

The prosecutor waited with his pen over the pad. Three rows of strangers waited behind him.

Beth's left hand closed on the edge of the table, the white scar across the palm hard against the wood.

"The captain," she said. "Edward Haskell."

He turned a page. "That's all. Thank you."

In the corridor the stone floor gave her good shoes back to her all the way to the tall window, and the radiators had the air hot and dry. A woman in her fifties got up off the bench.

"Ms. Calloway."

Beth knew Pike by the voice before the face.

"They voted it," Pike said. "It goes back to the magistrate this afternoon. With an indictment there's no preliminary hearing, so Pelletier stays held."

"The truck."

"Troopers on Route One found one brick, missed in the bottom of a crate. The rest was gone before they stopped him. Without you, it's one brick."

Beth had her coat off the bench and one arm in it.

"Did they have his book?"

"Yes. He told me you'd ask. He wanted you to hear it from me first."

The other arm went in. Beth did up the top button and started for the stairs.

"District rang my office," Pike said behind her. "The island's had no boat since Sunday. Car's at the side door. Deputies drive you to Pruett and stay on you till you're aboard, because whoever paid for that load reads the indictment tonight."

Beth was on the stairs, two at a time, her shoes loud on the stone.

"Call my office when you're off the slip," Pike said.
