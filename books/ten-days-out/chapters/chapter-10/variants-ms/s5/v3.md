"Who carried the bags up from the car deck that Thursday night?" the prosecutor said.

"The captain," Beth said. "Edward Haskell."

The stenographer's keys ran on after her voice had stopped. Beth laid both hands on the table, the scarred palm down, and kept them there.

Out in the corridor the stone floor gave her good shoes back to her, and the radiators had the air hot and dry. A woman in her fifties was up off the bench by the tall window. She started talking before Beth reached her, and Beth knew Pike by the voice before the face.

"They voted it. It goes back to the magistrate this afternoon, and with an indictment there's no preliminary hearing. Pelletier stays held. Troopers on Route One found one brick in the bottom of a crate. The rest was gone before light. Without you, that's one brick and a driver with a lawyer."

"Did they have his book?" Beth said.

"Yes. He told me you'd ask. He wanted you to hear it from me first."

Beth waited for the rest. Pike buttoned her coat.

"My page," Beth said. "Tess's name."

"It's in the file, and it stays in the file. If the defense wants it, they ask a judge, and they get it." Pike looked at the stairs. "The district wants its boat home tonight, and you're who they've got. Car's downstairs. A deputy drives you to Pruett. You sail at three."

Beth had her coat on and was at the head of the stairs before Pike finished, the stone ringing under her going down.

"Three o'clock, Ms. Calloway," Pike said behind her. "After three, the district hires a relief captain."
