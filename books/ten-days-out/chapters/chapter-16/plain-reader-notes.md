# Plain Reader Notes: Chapter 16, "Inside the Ledges"

Draft read: `chapters/chapter-16/final.md` (Nov 6, 2026). Chapters 01 to 15 `final.md` were read first, in order, for context. No other files were read.

## 1. Retelling

The big fishing boat that had been waiting off the point comes after the ferry, much faster than the ferry, and starts shooting out its windows. Dane climbs up on the open roof with the rifle and talks to Beth over the radio, telling her where the boat is. Beth calls the Coast Guard, who say they are an hour away, and the bad guys come on the same radio and tell her to stop and hand over "the girl and the boy." The boat keeps pushing her toward the rocks off the point, so she uses that. She knows a secret path through the rocks, the pencil line her father drew on her pantry door. Dane lies on the radio that the steering is broken, and she lets the ferry drift so it looks true. That pulls the big boat in close behind her. Then she cuts hard through the gap. The big boat follows and hits a rock, and it limps away. Dane goes quiet on the radio in the middle of a word. Once she is clear, she uncuffs Ned, leaves him at the wheel, and finds Dane on the roof, shot along the ribs but awake. The Coast Guard shows up and chases the fishing boat. The kid confesses that Cole's lawyer paid him and that the boat brings the drugs in. They land at Pruett around four in the morning. Ned and the kid are taken away, and Dane goes off in an ambulance after asking if she will go in at ten without him. She gets into a police car with six hours to go.

## 2. What's at stake

Beth is the only witness, and if she isn't in front of the grand jury this morning, Cole goes free. Cole's people on the fishing boat want to kill her and the kid, or wreck the ferry so it looks like the storm did it. Now Dane is hurt too, and she may have to walk in alone with Cole's people knowing her face.

## 3. The romance

Yes, I felt it. It wasn't a big kiss. It was these lines:

> "You left the wheel," he said.
> "You stopped talking."

That landed, because the whole book has been about him talking so she knows he's alive. Then it says it plainly: "She wanted him, here, bleeding on the upper deck of her boat in a borrowed shirt, and she had never wanted anything so much in her life." The "you still owe me an answer / on the other side" thread also works. "Is this the other side?" / "Pruett's the other side. Stay alive till then." was easy to follow. Her hand on the stretcher all the way up the pier came through too.

One thing: he never gives the answer he owes her, and she never answers his last question. That reads as on purpose and it pulls me forward. Just flagging that the romance ends this chapter unpaid.

## 4. Words I didn't know

- "Empty brass rolled by the rail." I don't know what "brass" means here. Probably something from the gun. Didn't stop me.
- "She found the handset" / "keyed the handset." I took this to mean the radio mic and pressing to talk. Mostly got it from context. Didn't stop me.
- "The ferry swung side-on to the sea." Not sure what side-on means, but the next words ("its nose came round to the right") carried me. Didn't stop me.
- "The wall box by the ladder was empty." What box? I guessed a first-aid box because she then uses her hat on the wound, but the page doesn't say. Didn't stop me.
- "Alton's way." **This one stopped me briefly.** I don't know who Alton is. Nobody named Alton has come up before, and her father has never been named in 16 chapters. "You've seen it on the door" saved me, because I remembered the pencil line on the pantry door, so I worked out Alton was probably her father. Half-listening, I'd likely think a new character just came in.
- "ledges" was explained back in Chapters 10 and 13 (the rocks just under the water), so I was fine with it here.

## 5. Where I lost the thread

- "Alton's way. You've seen it on the door." As above: who is Alton? One plain clause like "her father's name" would fix it.
- "as it had off the pier on Friday night under the fire truck's light, with the boat off the point watching." This is a long sentence that asks me to remember the steering sticking two chapters ago and that the boat saw it. I got it on a careful read, but by ear it's a lot at once. It matters because it's why the lie works.
- "Ashore, Tess heard every word and stayed off the air." How does Beth know this? It's a small jump in point of view. It didn't really confuse me, but it made me wonder.
- "It floated." This is a strange little sentence on its own. I think it means the big boat didn't sink. It took me a second.
- "Ten o'clock. Will you go in without me?" / "Six hours." The book had told me the grand jury hears her "that morning" and the hearing is at two. Ten o'clock is new. I worked out that ten is when she goes in and six hours is from four a.m., but only because I did the math. Half-listening, "Six hours" might go right past me.

## 6. Verdict: PASS

I could retell the main event: the chase, the fake broken steering, the secret gap through the rocks, the big boat hitting the rock, Dane shot. The stakes are clear. No plot-critical fact came only through a word I didn't know. "Ledges" was explained earlier, and the gap is tied clearly to the pantry-door drawing. The romance came through in plain words. Small fixes worth making: say who Alton is, and maybe say plainly that ten o'clock is when she goes in to the grand jury.
