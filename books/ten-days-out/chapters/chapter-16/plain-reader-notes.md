## 1. Retelling

Beth walks into the federal courthouse in borrowed shoes that are too small. Cole's lawyer, the man in the expensive coat, steps into her path and sneers that the marshal has been "keeping you warm." She says yes, and that she'll say so in there. Two deputies walk in right then and arrest the lawyer, because the kid signed a statement saying the lawyer paid him. Upstairs, Beth tells about twenty ordinary people what she saw on her deck. The prosecutor asks her straight out whether she's in a relationship with the marshal. She says yes, and that she saw the drugs six hours before she ever met him. A juror in a work jacket asks whether Dane ever told her what to say. She says he tried telling her what to do once and it didn't take, and the room laughs.

Outside, Dane has walked out of the hospital and is waiting on a park bench, where nobody can say he walked her in. His boss has texted that the jury voted to send Cole to trial. Before he can show her, Beth covers the phone and asks him to stay. He says he has applied to the Maine office, wants a cheap boat tied up where she can see it from the ferry, and wants her. He says the danger isn't over and asks to be the one who watches her door anyway. She says "From inside," then reads the text. They go to a warm room (I took it for a hotel), make love carefully around his stitches, and he finally says "I love you." That evening Russ texts that Thanksgiving is at Beth's, and Tess is coming. Tess says it "isn't forgiveness."

## 2. What's at stake

If the grand jury hadn't heard Beth this morning, Cole would have walked free at two. She testified, the jury sent him to trial, and the lawyer is in a cell. Beth is still in danger from the people on the big boat, which got away. The woman in New Bedford had her house burned after she testified, so Beth isn't safe yet, and she will have to testify again in the spring.
