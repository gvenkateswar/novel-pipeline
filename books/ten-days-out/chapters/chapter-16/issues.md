## Answers to the two direct questions

**Beth's love answer re-staged as new?** No. See above.

**Where the New Bedford risk goes.** It has a natural place in Ch 17: the bench, right after Dane's risk list ("And after today nobody pays me to watch your door.") and before "Can I be the one who watches it anyway?" Reasons: (1) That is where he names every risk he won't leave out, and New Bedford is the one he used to justify "She'll go," so leaving it out reads as dropped. (2) The ask only means something if he has named the worst case. (3) Ch 18 is the holiday table, a leaking boat, Ned's letter, Tess's "Calloway" and the light offshore. Putting a fear conversation at the start of that chapter would soften the HEA and compete with the series hook. Size: one exchange, sixty to eighty words, bought by cutting the duplicated trial line (Issue 11). Content: Beth says "New Bedford." Dane says what is different (the lawyer who reached Ned's son is in a cell and the kid has named him; the boat's people are named to the Coast Guard) and what is not (the boat is still out there, the trial is ahead). He does not say she is safe, and he does not say "trust me." Then "Can I be the one who watches it anyway?" lands as the answer to a stated danger. Ch 18 would then carry the lights offshore only as the series hook.
