# Plain Reader Notes: Chapter 11, "Somebody's Boy"

Draft read: `chapters/chapter-11/final.md` (Nov 6, 2026). I also read chapters 01 to 10 `final.md` first, in order, for context. I read nothing else.

## 1. Retelling

Dane and Beth are holed up in the dentist's house with the kid's phone. Somebody on the island has been texting the kid where Beth sleeps, and they want to trap that person. Dane writes a fake text in the kid's style: "marshal shot me, bleeding bad, can't walk, come alone, bring something for blood." Beth won't let him put a rolled-up quilt on the bed as a decoy. She lies there herself, because whoever comes will be someone she loves and she wants it to be her face they see. His boss says go. Someone texts back "coming." They wait in the cold dark, get a scare from the sunk skiff knocking on the rocks, and Dane whispers to her about the old boat he wants to buy. A lantern comes down the lane, bobbing like a big man with a bad hip, and Beth says she knows whose it is. It's Ned. He kneels by the bed calling her "son," spreads his good blanket over her and pulls his knife to cut a sleeve. Beth sits up. Dane rings the leak's number and Ned's coat buzzes. Ned admits to all of it: Russ's porch, the north-side house, the beach at three, the fake call from the Dyer phone. Dane arrests him. With no handcuffs, Beth ties Ned's wrists with the knot he taught her when she was nine, and asks him why.

## 2. What's at stake

Beth is the only witness against Cole, the drug dealer. If she doesn't testify to the grand jury in Portland on Monday, Cole walks free. That's why his people want her dead. Tonight there's more danger: the wounded kid is still out there with a pistol and no boat, and the person who kept sending him to her turns out to be Ned, the man closest to her in the world. Even if she lives, she has lost him.

## 3. The romance

Yes, but quietly. This chapter is mostly about the trap and Ned. The moment I felt it was put in plain words:

> "He kept on in a whisper about the soft boards, and under every word was how much he loved her, and that whoever walked down that road tonight would be somebody she loved too, and that nothing he knew how to do would make it anybody else. It was all he had to give her, and it was a boat."

That got me. Before it there's the banter ("I'd leave you with anybody. You'd have them sitting down by the stove in a minute.") and "Talk to me," which I took as her wanting his voice. The kiss on her knee, with her hand on the back of his neck, is clear enough when you hear it. Her shoulder touching his at the shutter is the kind of small gesture I'd miss while listening. The boat line carries it, though. If the book hadn't already told me he loves her, I might have felt less here, but I didn't have to work it out.

## 4. Words I didn't know

None of these stopped me from following what happened.

- "damper" (line 45): "Beth leaned over and shut the stove's damper, and the red behind its door began to go dull." I don't know what a damper is, but the sentence tells me what it does: the fire dies down. Fine.
- "the leak's thread" (line 5): "Besides the leak's thread, the phone held a few short ones..." "Leak" for the person passing on secrets I can just about work out. Opening the chapter on it, plus "thread," made me rewind. I worked it out, but it's a rough first line to hear.
- "drew the front door's bolt" (line 33): I wasn't sure whether "drew" meant locked or unlocked. From "you go out the front when I say go," I guessed unlocked, so she could run.
- "hinge side" (line 85): explained by "so the door would swing in and hide him." Fine.
- "favoring one hip" (line 129): "favoring" is an odd word, but "a big man... one hip" plus Ned's bad hip from earlier chapters was enough.
- "rope knife" (line 155): explained on the page ("for cutting a rope in a hurry"). Fine.
- "daybed": I know it roughly, a couch you can sleep on. Fine.

## 5. Where I lost the thread

- Line 5 to 7, the opening: "Besides the leak's thread, the phone held a few short ones to numbers with no names..." and "Like the kid's, it came on only to send or to wait for an answer. So the one way to get it switched on..." Two sentences of phone logic right at the start, said in the order Dane thinks it. I had to replay it to understand that the leak's phone is usually off, so they need to make the person turn it on, carry it, and answer. Once Ned's coat buzzed, it all made sense looking back. On first hearing, that was my weakest moment in the chapter.
- Line 7 vs line 77: "At a quarter past two he had rung the leak's number... and it was off" and later "At a quarter past it had been off." The repeat is fine, but the times (quarter past two, half past two, three) stack up and I stopped tracking them.
- Ch 10 mentioned "Another number's called him four times since the beach. Never once texted." Here Dane sets the phone to silent "so the number that kept calling couldn't ring." I still don't know who that second caller is. I'm not sure if I'm meant to wonder or if I forgot something.
- Line 187: "Wind's going round to the west." Ned answers Beth's first question with weather. I took it as him dodging, the way he always gives the weather first, and it worked. A half-listener might just think it's a line about the storm.
- Line 205 to 207: "I could see his light from there. I told Tess so." The page reminds me this is what the caller said to Tess on Saturday. Good that it does, because I wouldn't have remembered on my own.
- Line 181: "Cole's lawyer was already asking twelve people from Maine to picture an old man on his knees with a blanket." I got roughly that the trap might look bad in court, but not exactly why it worries Dane.
- "He's somebody's boy, Bethie." I tied this to Ned's son in Portland on drugs (Ch 9) and it hit. A listener who missed that line in Ch 9 would just hear a kind old man.

## 6. Verdict: PASS

- I could retell the main event: they lure the leak with a fake text from the kid's phone, it's Ned, he confesses, Dane arrests him and Beth ties him up.
- I could say what's at stake.
- The plot-critical fact, that Ned is the leak, reached me in plain words and sounds: the lantern and the hip, Ned's voice, the coat buzzing when the leak's number is rung, and "That was me." / "Me." / "Me." No unknown word or TV picture was needed.
- I felt the romance at the boat speech, which says outright that he loves her.

Small fixes I'd want: make the first two paragraphs (the phone logic) easier to follow on one hearing, and say clearly which way "drew the bolt" goes.
