**Chapter: 7 of 10.** I would keep reading. The last scene is the best confrontation in the book so far. What holds the score down is that I knew it was Ned before the chapter started. When the lantern dips "on every other step," I wasn't surprised. I was waiting for the book to admit it.

**I'd keep reading, but:** the mystery is spent. From here what pulls me is "why," and the kid is still out in the woods with a pistol. Nobody in the house is asking "who." If Ned's answer to "Why?" is just the son in Portland, which Chapter 9 already told me, the next chapter has nothing new to give.

## Where I got bored

- The opening two paragraphs. "At a quarter past two he had rung the leak's number from his own phone, and it was off. Like the kid's, it came on only to send or to wait for an answer. So the one way to get it switched on..." This is the plot explaining its own plumbing. I reread it to work out the phone logic and still wasn't sure. Then a quarter-past-two timestamp lands right after "by half past two" in the first sentence, and I lost track of the clock.
- "By three the stove had gone black and cold to look at, and their breath showed in the kitchen. Dane laid a hand on the iron once. It was still warm." My attention dipped here. I've had a lot of stove in this book: Russ's stove, the kerosene heater, the onions, the dentist's damper. This paragraph is the stove again.
- The knee kiss. It's sweet, but it comes right after the decoy argument and right before the boss text, and it held up the setup scene for one more tender beat when I wanted the trap set.

## Where I got confused

- "If it's the kid, the plan is I shoot him. If it's Tess, the plan is you explain." / "If it's Tess, the plan is run." / "You'd leave me here with her." / "I'd leave you with anybody." I couldn't work out who was running, who was being left, or why Tess got singled out. It reads like a joke whose setup got cut.
- "the leak." The word shows up with no introduction ("Besides the leak's thread"). It's fine as Dane's shorthand, but it reads as if I'd missed a scene where someone named it.
- "all small letters and no names... a period after most of it." Small letters and no punctuation, then periods? It reads like a contradiction, though it might be a deliberate detail.
- "He meant to cut a sleeve off a wound." Whose knowledge is that? We're behind the door in Dane's head, and Dane can't know what Ned meant. It defuses the knife before it can scare me.
- Ned's hour on the road. Ned's house is somewhere near the harbor, the dentist's house is up the east cove, and in a blizzard, on that hip, he walks it in roughly the time between "coming" and the boot on the step. That works, but I had to stop and check whether it did.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Dane uses the only witness as bait, in the dark, with an armed kid loose on the road outside.** He's spent ten chapters refusing to let her stand anywhere exposed: the beach terms, "I'm between you and the trees," "you stay behind this rock." Now she lies on a daybed under a quilt, alone across the kitchen from him, waiting for a stranger to kneel over her. Her argument ("A quilt doesn't breathe") is good, and he gives in after one line. I wanted him to fight it longer, or I wanted it to cost him on the page.
- **The boss answers "Go." at about half past two in the morning, within moments, to a plan to lure a suspect with the witness as decoy.** That's very convenient. It also quietly drops the "And me" sting from the end of Chapter 8, which had made me suspect her.
- **Ned sold the porch, the house and the beach, then stood chest-deep in the sea in front of Beth, and tonight walks an hour through a storm for the boy who was sent to kill her.** I believe each of those as a single act. I'm not yet sure I believe them all in one man. The chapter is betting that the "Why?" answer will make them fit. If it doesn't, Ned falls apart as a character.
- **Ned's bait text sends him to "the house up the hill" at "east cove."** He finds it in a whiteout on the first try. Islanders would know it, so this is minor.

## Where it sounded like AI

- "He kept on in a whisper about the soft boards, and under every word was how much he loved her, and that whoever walked down that road tonight would be somebody she loved too, and that nothing he knew how to do would make it anybody else. It was all he had to give her, and it was a boat." The boat monologue just before it was working because it left the feeling unsaid. This paragraph explains the subtext, then ends on a neat line.
- "Neither of them said it was nothing." Another neat scene-button.
- "Dane, who talked through everything, stood by the door of a stranger's kitchen with his mouth shut. This was hers. He hated watching her do it... He held his tongue. He was bad at it." The book is telling me something the scene already shows.
- "Whatever Ned said next, about why he had sold her, would take the last of him away from her, and Dane could not stand in front of it." Same thing: the feeling explained right before the final line.
- "It came out rough. He liked the man." Three short sentences of telling.
- **The ", which..." quip tic.** "You're right, which I'm going to hold against you." "By stove light, with you arguing, which isn't how they teach it." Across the book Dane has done this a dozen times ("which will look great in my report," "which you'll hate," "which tells you about my day"). It's his signature, but by Chapter 11 I can hear the template.
- **Hands flat.** "one hand flat on the daybed," "Her hand stayed flat on the daybed, pressed until the fingertips went white." Somebody's hand has been flat on something in nearly every chapter: on a chest, a forearm, a rock, a stomach, a back.
- **"Here's how this goes."** Dane used this exact phrase in Chapter 1. If the callback is deliberate, it didn't land as one.
- **"The cuffs at the bottom of the bay."** Chapter 10 told me, and this chapter tells me again at the arrest. The second time works because it pays off in the rope. The first mention made it feel like a reminder.

## What I want to know next

1. **Why?** Is it only the son in Portland, or did Cole have something on Ned: debt, a threat to the boy, the two years of cash? What answer could square selling her three times with standing in the sea for her?
2. **Did Ned take her picture off Tess's board in September?** If he did, he was setting her up two months before she saw anything, and that's much darker than a father protecting his son.
3. **Who has been calling the kid's phone four times without ever texting?** That's a second number, so is there someone above Ned?
4. **Where is the kid?** He's shot, he has a pistol and no boat, and a lit lantern just walked down the lane in view of "anybody in those woods." I'm waiting for a boot on that step again.
5. **The ferry.** Beth said there's one way off the island, "once I fix the steering." Ned's job is hers in June. Does she take his wheel to get herself to Portland, with Ned tied up below?
6. **Ned and the grand jury.** Does Ned end up testifying against Cole, so Beth isn't the whole case? That would change what Dane's "jury" fear means.

## Prediction section

What I think happens, so the author can see how far ahead of the book I am:

- Ned's answer is his son: Cole has the boy, or owns his debt, or threatened him, and the September photo was Ned's doing under pressure. He'll say he never thought the kid would really shoot. (Confidence: high.)
- The kid comes back to the dentist's house that night or at dawn, drawn by Ned's lantern or desperate for a way off. Either Ned gets hurt shielding Beth a second time, or the kid is taken alive. (Confidence: medium-high. If Ned dies saving her, I'll feel it coming a page early.)
- Beth repairs the ferry's steering and runs the crossing herself as captain to make Monday, with Dane aboard and the kid and Ned as passengers or prisoners. (Confidence: high. Chapter 10 practically promised it.)
- Ned's testimony makes Beth no longer the whole case, which defuses Dane's "good coat" fear about the two of them.
- Ending: Beth says "ask me again" or the five-days answer ("Ask me in five days," Chapter 8). Dane takes a Maine posting and buys the leaky wooden boat. (Confidence: high.)

If even three of these land as I've written them, the last stretch of the book will feel like it's completing a form rather than surprising me.

## Listener pass (heard as audio)

- **The timestamps blur.** "Half past two," "a quarter past two," "at a quarter past," "four minutes later," "by three." On the page I could go back and check. By ear, I lost which came first and whether the leak's phone was on or off.
- **The bait text doesn't survive being read aloud.** "marshal shot me. bleeding bad. cant walk. east cove the house up the hill. back door open. come alone. bring something for blood." Read flat by a narrator, "east cove the house up the hill" sounds like a misread. That might be fine for the kid's voice, but a listener won't know whether it's the narrator's mistake.
- **"He" gets ambiguous once Ned is in the room.** "He saw the marshal and the gun, and his eyes went to Beth and stayed there." "He set it on the chair by the daybed." With two men in the scene, I had to work out every "he" from context. "He held his wrists out together. He held them out to Beth" works, because the repetition is the point.
- **The pocket inventory works aloud:** "The knife on the right, Ned's phone on the left, the kid's phone over his heart, the gun in his hand." The rhythm carries it, though it's on the cute side.
- **The "Me." exchange is the best thing in the chapter to hear.** Short questions, one-word answers, with weather as the first dodge. A narrator can't ruin it.
- **"The dentist's" repeats:** the dentist's quilt, the dentist's floor, the dentist's lane, and the dentist's soap from the last chapter. By ear it starts to sound like the dentist is a character.

## Best moment

> "And tonight you walked an hour in this. What for?"
>
> "He's somebody's boy, Bethie."
>
> Beth pulled his blanket off her legs and folded it square.

The title lands, and the line is the most damning thing Ned could say and the kindest at the same time. She doesn't answer it. She folds his blanket. Then she ties his wrists with the knot he taught her at nine. I believed every word of this part.

Close second: Ned kneeling and talking all the way down ("I've got the box off the boat, son... You're cold, that's all, so I brought my good blanket"). Before the reveal is confirmed, the book makes me love him one more time, and that's why the reveal hurts.
