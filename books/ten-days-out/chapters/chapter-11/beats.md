## Flags before the beats (outline against the page and the log)

None of these blocks the chapter. Each is settled below the way the outline means it.

1. **The stove.** The outline's room note says "the stove has gone out and the house is black." Ch 10 ends at about two with the stove "a red bed behind its door." **Settled:** they let it die on purpose as part of the bait. A boy bleeding alone in a shut house couldn't have lit it, and a dark room makes the shape on the daybed all anyone sees. Beth shuts the damper in s1, and by the time the lantern comes the stove is black and the room is cold.
2. **Beth's way out.** The outline has her "go out the back when he says go." The bait itself sends the visitor to the back door. **Settled:** Dane draws the front door's bolt in s1 (the bolt Beth set in October, Ch 10) and leaves that door shut and unbolted. If it's the kid, she goes out the front.
3. **Where the lantern comes from.** The outline says it "comes up the hill." Audit fix N5 says Ned comes by the road, which takes an hour. **Settled:** the lantern comes along the east road and turns down the dentist's lane. They see it through a gap in the road-side shutter. "The hill" becomes "the lane."
4. **Three scenes, not 4–6.** The outline sets three scenes. This chapter is one hour in one room, and the cold reader flagged a neat kicker line at the end of every scene. Adding scene breaks would add more of those. **Kept at three.** Each scene has its own event and its own ending type.
5. **Stale bible items, overridden by the outline:**
   - World rule 13 puts Dane's second swear in Ch 11 s2 (the church fire). Both his swears are now spent (Ch 09 s2, Ch 10 s2). **This chapter has zero swears from Dane, and none from anyone else either.**
   - CASE FACTS puts the bait on the ferry in Ch 13, the arrest on the float in Ch 12, and "the good blanket" in Ned's Tess story. All three are retired. The good blanket is now Ned's own, carried into this kitchen.
   - The bible has Russ cuffing Ned. The outline [DC] has Dane cuff him here. Russ's own cuffs come in Ch 12 s2.
6. **Numbers ledger, for the continuity checker.** The ledger lists texts from the leak's number as "three, plus a fourth at two." Ned's reply *coming* is a **fifth** text from that number. Log it.
7. **"Bethie" is near its cap (10 in the book).** On the page so far: Ch 01 ×1, Ch 02 ×2, Ch 05 ×1, Ch 08 ×1, Ch 09 ×2, for 7. Ch 16 needs two. **Ch 11 gets exactly one**, in "He's somebody's boy, Bethie." Ch 12 gets none unless an earlier one is cut. "Your marshal" (2 of 3 used): none here. "Good blanket" (cap 2): once here. Any form of "look back": none here, because Ned's look-backs belong to Ch 12 and Ch 16.

## Scene 1

**Setting:** The dentist's kitchen, about half past two. The stove is still red behind its door. The only other light is the kid's phone in Dane's hand. Sensory anchor: the cold coming back into the room as the stove dies, first at their feet and then across the floorboards.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth. Dane's boss is a text on the screen.

**Event:** They build the bait together, his boss says go, and he sends it.

**Surface action:**
1. Open on the binding line. The kid's other threads on the phone are a few short ones to numbers with no names: no capitals, no apostrophes, a period after everything, "cant," "ill." There's nothing in them about Cole, the ferry or Beth, nothing a court could use, and no new person. One clause is enough to say a boy who saved nobody's name.
2. One line of what Dane already tried. At a quarter past two he rang the leak's number **from his own phone** [N3], and it was off. The leak's phone, like the kid's, is only on to send or to wait for an answer [A13]. The only way to get it switched on, in somebody's pocket, in a room with them, is to give it something to answer. Dane turns the kid's phone to silent so nothing from the second number can light up or ring in the dark.
3. The bait is built as they work, never at a table. Beth knows what brings an islander out: "Nobody on this island lets a boy bleed to death in the snow." Dane writes the boy's way.
4. They argue the room while they set it, and every line of plan comes with a hand doing it:
   - **Dane** wants a rolled quilt on the daybed for the shape.
   - **Beth** won't have it. Whoever comes will kneel and put a hand on it, a quilt doesn't breathe, and she wants to be the one they find. She's right on the merits, and Dane gives in with the reason said out loud, never "Fair."
   - **Dane's cost line** (this restates "one of her three" from Ch 10 in one line): whoever walks in will be one of hers, brought here by their own kindness. She knows. She says so.
   - **Dane's other risk** (this restates "one way off"): it's the kid's cove, the kid has no boat now, and he's somewhere on this island with a pistol. If the door opens on the kid, she goes out the front when Dane says go. **He draws the front door's bolt now** and leaves the door shut.
   - **The laugh (binding):** "If it's the kid, the plan is I shoot him. If it's Tess, the plan is you explain." / "If it's Tess, the plan is run."
   - **Beth shuts the stove's damper.** She knows that stove from October. Dane carries the kid's rifle off the end of the daybed and stands it in his own corner behind the back door, out of reach of anyone kneeling at the daybed. Beth is never armed (World rule 17).
5. **The longing beat (new, specific to Ch 10's injury).** By phone light he checks her knee dressing before she lies down, and he puts his mouth on her knee above the tape, once. It isn't the way into anything (F5: first aid is never the way into bed). Neither of them makes a joke of it.
6. **The report in front of her [A12].** At the kitchen window, where the one bar of signal is, he texts his boss the plan with the screen turned so Beth can read it. Then he waits. The answer: *Go.*
7. He sends the bait from the kid's phone. Text on the page, italics, lowercase as typed (binding):
   *marshal shot me. bleeding bad. cant walk. east cove the house up the hill. back door open. come alone. bring something for blood.*
8. Four minutes later: *coming.* In one clause at most, Dane notes that the phone was off at a quarter past and on again now, like a man waking to look on the half hour [N3]. No reasoning about schedules beyond that.
9. Dane pulls the chair out from under the back door's knob. The storm leans on the door.

**Subtext:** Dane would like a dummy in that bed and Beth in the back bedroom. He knows she's right and hates that she's right. Beth wants to see the face. If she has to lose one of them, she won't let it happen with a quilt standing in for her. Neither says that she's choosing to be the thing her own people come to rescue.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal, and adult. Both are partly right about the room. Situational: they are about to open a door in the dark with an armed boy loose on the island and no way off it.

**Ends on (ending type: action, no dialogue):** The chair comes out from under the knob, cold comes in around the door's edge, and Dane sets the kid's phone face down on the chair beside his gun. Its light shows only at the edges. Don't end on the *coming* text. The text is the event. The scene closes on hands.

**Texture budget (2):** the kid's way of writing; the boss's one-word go.

**Plants:** paid: the kid's phone (Ch 10); phones only on to text [A13]; the report to the boss in front of her [A12]. Placed: the front bolt drawn (for s3, if it's the kid); the rifle in Dane's corner (Ch 15–16 carry it); the phone on silent (Ch 12 s2, the second number).

**Word count target:** 460. This is the scene that compresses if the chapter runs long. Never cut the laugh, the knee, the go or the bait text.

## Scene 2

**Setting:** The same kitchen, now black. From about three to about four. The stove is dead, and the room has gone as cold as outside. The only light is the face-down phone's edge. Sensory anchors: total dark, and the sea below the house. Then a knock and a scrape on stones.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth.

**Event:** A sound down at the cove freezes them. Then a lantern comes down the lane with a walk they both know.

**Surface action:**
1. **Positions, shown once and briefly.** Beth is on the daybed at the dead stove, under the dentist's quilt the way a hurt man would lie, bad leg straight. Dane stands in the corner behind the back door, on the hinge side, so the door will open and hide him. The gap between them is the whole kitchen. **Give the dread a beat** (the author's 15% slowdown goes here): the cold, his scalp aching with it, and the wind in the chimney.
2. **The false alarm [A6], at the center of the chapter's danger.** Below the house, on the cove stones, something knocks and scrapes. Dane is at the cove-side shutter in two steps with his gun up. Beth has stopped breathing under the quilt, and he can hear that she has. The sound comes again.
   - Beth knows the island by sound, so she names it in two words from the dark: "The skiff."
   - It comes again and again, timed to the sea. It's the holed skiff, half full, lifting on the tide and dropping back onto the stones.
   - Dane waits for one more to be sure. Show that as listening, never counting.
   - **They don't laugh.** Nobody says it was nothing. Dane goes back to his corner.
3. **The romance beat (binding), the one time in the book Beth asks for his talking:** "Talk to me." She says it into the dark, quietly. **No narration about what it means that she asked.** He starts talking.
4. **What he talks about is his boat**, the one he means to buy, said in whispers across the black kitchen. One or two plain specifics at most:
   - **The kind:** an old wooden lobster boat, about the size he worked on at sixteen.
   - **The price:** thirty-some thousand dollars, in words.
   - **What he'd fix first:** something a listener can see, like the floor at the back where you stand to haul, which on an old boat is always soft.
   - Optional: the Saturday boat in Pruett he never got to see.
   - Plain words throughout. No engine terms, no "transom," no "stern" [jargon budget].
   - Beth answers with one flat verdict: "It'll leak." / "They all leak." This is the optional second laugh, and it seeds Ch 18 ("It'll take up in a week").
5. **His interior while he talks:** one long Dane sentence running on "and." He is giving her the price of a boat in the dark so she has his voice to hold onto, and what's under it is how much he loves her and how little he can do about what's coming up that road. It ends on a short clause that undercuts him. No flat "He loved her," and never "in his head, plainly."
6. **The lantern.** Through the gap at the road-side shutter, where a hook doesn't seat, a light comes along the east road a long way off and turns down the dentist's lane. Beth comes off the daybed on her good leg to the gap beside him, and her shoulder is against his. **The walk is readable in the light itself:** the lantern dips on every other step, the way a lantern swings for a big man favoring one hip. They both see it.
7. **Plant for Ch 12, one clause in Dane's head:** a lit lantern on that road is a light anyone in those woods can see.
8. Beth: "I know whose it is." Nothing more from either of them. No name.

**Subtext:** Asking him to talk is Beth reaching for him without moving. Talking is the only touch the room allows. Dane hears that she knows who's coming before she says it, and he can't do one thing to make it someone else.

**Tension source:** Situational: the open door, the noise in the kid's own cove, the light on the road. Internal, for both: the wait is the moment before she loses someone, and both of them know it.

**Ends on (ending type: action):** Beth goes back to the daybed, lies down the way a hurt boy would and pulls the quilt over her head. Dane steps back into his corner behind the door with his gun down along his leg. Outside, a boot on the back step. Don't end on "I know whose it is." Let one physical beat follow it.

**Texture budget (2):** the wrecked skiff's sound; the boat he'd buy.

**Plants:** paid: the wrecked skiff (Ch 10) as the false alarm; talk against silence (Ch 01, 03) as "Talk to me"; Dane's boat (Ch 01, 08). Placed: "It'll leak" (→ Ch 18); the lantern seen from the woods (→ Ch 12 s1, the shot on the east road).

**Word count target:** 620. If the writer runs short, the room goes to dread and to the boat talk. It never goes to the plan or to who the leak might be.

## Scene 3

**Setting:** The kitchen, a little after four. Ned's lantern lights it for the first time: low orange from the floor, every shadow going up the walls. Sensory anchors: kerosene from the lantern, and snow melting off a green wool jacket onto the dentist's floor.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Ned.

**Event:** Ned comes in to save the boy, finds Beth, and she faces him down. Dane arrests him.

**Surface action:**
1. **The door opens.** The back door swings in and hides Dane. Snow blows in, then the lantern, then Ned's voice (binding): "Son? It's all right, son." He's carrying the lantern, the ferry's first-aid box and a blanket over his arm. He's breathing hard from an hour of road. He doesn't see Dane.
2. **He kneels at the daybed.** He sets the lantern on the floor and goes down slowly on the bad hip (Ch 02). He talks to the shape too much, the way Ned does when he's hiding something: the box off the boat, it's all right now, and the binding detail, "my good blanket." He lays the blanket over the shape.
3. **The danger beat.** His hand goes to his belt, to the rope knife every man on the ferry wears to cut a rope in a hurry (plain gloss, once). He means to cut a sleeve off a wound. In Dane's view it's a blade coming out over Beth in the dark. Dane's gun comes up.
4. **Beth sits up out from under both blankets:** "Hello, Ned." Ned's hand stops on the knife.
5. **Dane shuts the door behind him.** Ned turns his head, sees the marshal and the gun, and looks straight back at Beth. From here on, Dane notices that **Ned looks at Beth and never at the gun.**
6. **The proof, and the call [N3].** Dane rings the leak's number **from the kid's phone**. Earlier, one clause: a man walking an hour to a hurt boy keeps his phone on in case the boy writes again. Ned's coat buzzes. "Take it out." Ned takes out a cheap phone, lit, shaking in his big hand, with the kid's number calling.
7. **The knife.** Dane takes the rope knife off Ned's belt in one move, then holds out his hand for the phone, and Ned gives it to him. Dane pockets both (Ch 12 s2 needs Ned's phone in Dane's pocket). After that he says nothing.
8. **Dane's quiet (romance beat, binding).** This is hers, and he notices himself keeping quiet: the man who talks through everything, standing still with his mouth shut. One long Dane sentence, ending short. He hates watching her do it and won't take it from her. Optional, one sentence at most: how Ned's next words will sound to twelve people from Maine.
9. **Beth faces him down, one place at a time.** She uses short questions she already knows the answers to, one at a time, and waits after each (her syntax profile):
   - "Russ's porch."
   - "The north-side house."
   - "The beach at three."

   Ned answers each one shortly. He may answer the first with the weather, once, because that's his habit. Beth's face in the lantern light shows it at its true size, through one observable thing (her hand flat on the daybed, her stiff leg, how still she sits). Don't explain it.
10. **The Dyer phone.** Beth names it. Ned owns it (binding): "I could see his light from there. I told Tess so. I wanted Russ off that porch, and I didn't want him hurt." One clause in Dane's head: those were Tess's words at the store counter (Ch 05), and now they're his.
11. Beth: "You stood in the water." / Ned: "I did that too."
12. Beth asks why he came tonight, an hour in this. Ned (binding, and **the chapter's one "Bethie"**): "He's somebody's boy, Bethie." Dane's narration says nothing about Ned's son. The line carries it.
13. **Beth gives the blanket back.** She pays every favor back at once (bible: Owing), and she won't keep anything of his. She folds his good blanket and puts it in his arms. He sets it on the chair.
14. **The arrest.** Dane: "Ned, you're under arrest." These are his first words since "Take it out," and they cost him, because he likes the man. **Ned holds his wrists out to Beth**, and Dane cuffs them [DC].
15. **The cost line, then the question (World rule 16).** One line in Dane's head tells the cost before the answer comes: whatever Ned says next takes the last of him from her. Then Beth asks "Why?" Make the line clear to a listener: she's asking why he sold her, because the coming-tonight question has already been answered. Ned looks at the dead stove and doesn't answer. The chapter stops there.

**Subtext:** Ned came because he couldn't let a boy bleed. He sold her for the same reason, for a different boy, and Beth half sees it before he says it. Beth's questions are a deckhand's checklist, because a list is the only way she can get through this. Dane wants to stand between her and this the way he stood between her and the pistol, and the decent thing is to stand still.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal, at full size: the man who raised her alongside her father, caught by his own kindness. Situational for a breath: the knife coming out over her. Internal for Dane: he has to stay quiet.

**Ends on (ending type: the outline's question, owned):** Beth's "Why?", with Ned silent and cuffed in the lantern light. The final line names its speaker (the Ch 09 reader couldn't tell who said "Which one?"). It should be a full sentence with her name or an action in it, so the chapter doesn't close on a one-word button. One example shape, for the writer to beat: *Beth asked him the only thing left, "Why?"* Don't write Ned's answer here. **For the Ch 12 architect:** Ch 12 opens on the road at first light with "'They have my boy,' Ned said." Account for his silence between four and first light in one clause, for example that he didn't say a word until the road.

**Texture budget (2):** the rope knife; the cuffs.

**Plants:**
- **Paid:**
  - "Could see his light from there" (Ch 05).
  - Cole's night-run phone (Ch 01, off page), now in Ned's coat.
  - The three texts (Ch 10) as Beth's three places.
  - "You stood in the water" (Ch 09).
  - Ned's hip (Ch 02).
  - The leak comes in person (Act 2's unpriced card).
- **Placed:**
  - Ned's phone in Dane's pocket (→ Ch 12 s2, the lawyer's text).
  - Ned's why, unanswered (→ Ch 12 s1).
  - The kid still loose inland with a pistol (→ Ch 12 s1).
  - The good blanket left on the dentist's chair (open, for any later use).
- **Say it twice** (restated in Ch 12 s1): Ned's phone; the three places; the Dyer call. Each is said plainly here.

**Word count target:** 760. This is the chapter's longest scene, and it never compresses below 700. If the chapter is short, the extra goes to Beth's three places and the silences between them.
