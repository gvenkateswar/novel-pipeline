# Chapter 11 issues (continuity check of draft.md)

**Issue 1**
- Problem: Dane's hands do not add up in the arrest. At the point he rings the number he holds his gun (it came up on Ned and was never put down or holstered) and the kid's phone, one in each hand. He then takes the knife and holds out a hand for Ned's phone with no hand free. The kid's phone, still calling, is never hung up or put away. "Pocketed both" is also unclear: it could mean the knife and Ned's phone, or Ned's phone and the kid's phone. What is in whose pocket matters for Ch 12, which needs Ned's phone in Dane's pocket.
- Location: "Dane took the knife out of his other hand in one move and held out his own for the phone. Ned gave it to him. Dane pocketed both and stepped back to the door."
- Suggested fix: Before he takes the knife, show Dane ending the call and putting the kid's phone in his coat, and laying his gun on the chair, so both hands are free. Then write "Dane pocketed the knife and Ned's phone."

**Issue 2**
- Problem: A pronoun and a clock problem in the "coming" beat. (a) "that phone" follows "the kid's phone lit in his hand", so it reads as the kid's phone, but it means the leak's phone. (b) It repeats what the chapter said at the start ("it was off" at a quarter past). (c) The opening puts the writing of the bait at about half past two, and Dane then argues the room, gets the boss's go and sends. The text therefore goes out after half past two, and a reply "four minutes later" cannot be a man "waking to look on the half hour."
- Location: "At a quarter past, that phone had been off. Now it was on, like a man waking to look on the half hour."
- Suggested fix: Replace with one clause that names the phone and leaves out the clock, for example: "The leak's phone had been off at a quarter past. Now a man was holding it on, waiting."

**Issue 3**
- Problem: "the other number" is ambiguous. Ch 10 set up two numbers: the leak's (the thread) and a second number that called the kid four times and never texted. This chapter never names the second one. The sentence follows a paragraph about the leak's number, so a listener will read "the other number" as the leak's, which is the number Dane wants to ring in. Silencing the phone would then cut off the very reply he is waiting for, and the plant for Ch 12 (the lawyer's number) is lost.
- Location: "He set the kid's phone to silent, so nothing from the other number could ring in the dark."
- Suggested fix: Say it plainly: "so the number that kept calling the kid couldn't ring in the dark," and keep the leak's texts as lit screens.

**Issue 4**
- Problem: A woodstove that was hot enough at about two to warm the room does not go "dead" and leave the kitchen "as cold as the yard" within about half an hour of its damper being shut. Iron holds heat for much longer. The chapter then relies on "dead stove" three times (the wind down the chimney, and Ned looking at it). It also puts wind "down the chimney" through a damper Beth just shut.
- Location: "By three the stove was dead, and the kitchen was as cold as the yard."
- Suggested fix: Write "By three the stove had gone black and cold to look at, and the kitchen was cold enough that their breath showed," with Dane laying a hand on the iron once, which gives the room the cold it needs without a stretch. Drop the wind "down the chimney into the dead stove" or make it wind on the shutters.

**Issue 5**
- Problem: One-talk test gap. Dane states the exit terms as an order. Beth, who refused the back bedroom and wants to be the one they find, never answers or agrees to leave when he says go. The next beat is Dane drawing the bolt. The terms are not settled to both faces, and Beth leaving when told is at odds with her profile (she does not leave a job half done). Her "If it's Tess, the plan is run" is a joke about Tess and does not accept the term for the kid.
- Location: "If that door opens on him, you go out the front when I say go."
- Suggested fix: Give Beth one line of agreement with her own condition, for example: "If it's the kid, I go. If it's anyone else, I stay till I've seen his face."

**Issue 6**
- Problem: Narration explains a beat the dialogue already carried. "Talk to me" and his whispering about the boat are the beat. The sentence tells the reader what the talking is for, and the beat sheet says no narration about what it means that she asked.
- Location: "he was giving her the price of a boat in the dark so she'd have his voice to hold onto"
- Suggested fix: Cut that clause. Keep the rest of the sentence (the soft boards, the long *and* run about how much he loved her and who was coming), ending on "It was all he had to give her, and it was a boat."

**Issue 7**
- Problem: Beth states the irony and the theme aloud. "He's somebody's boy" already carries it, and Beth's line spells it out again right before the arrest. It also does the cost line's job before the cost line comes, so the chapter ends with the same point said twice. The beat sheet said the line carries it and the narration should not explain it.
- Location: "You'd walk through a storm for a boy you never met," Beth said. "And you sold me."
- Suggested fix: Cut the line. If the listener needs "Why?" to mean "why did you sell me," let the cost line before it do that work: "Whatever Ned said next would take the last of him away from her" can name the sale ("his selling her") in plain words.

**Issue 8**
- Problem: Characters tell each other what both already know. Beth set up the premise herself ("Whoever comes will kneel and put a hand on him"). Dane then tells her whoever comes is one of hers, and she says "I know." He also tells her the kid's cove, pistol and lack of a boat, which she was present for. (The outline asks for "say it twice" restatements here, so the facts stay, but they should come inside action or with new information, not as announcements.)
- Location: "Whoever walks in here is one of yours," he said, "and they'll come because they're kind." and "This is the kid's cove. He's got a pistol and no boat."
- Suggested fix: Fold each into an action line with a new fact attached. For the first, cut "I know. That's why it'll work." and let Beth answer with the thing she will do: "Then it has to be my face they find." For the second, put it in the same breath as drawing the bolt and add what he has not said before (the road is the kid's too).

**Issue 9**
- Problem: Injury continuity. Dane's scalp has a split and a lump from the pistol (Ch 10: Beth cleaned it and, in the love scene, kept her fingers in his hair "clear of it"). Here her hand "came down on his head" and stays, with no wince and no care taken of the wound.
- Location: "Her hand came down on his head and stayed there until he stood up."
- Suggested fix: Put the hand on his shoulder or the back of his neck, or have it settle on the unhurt side of his head.

**Issue 10**
- Problem: Repeated position detail turned into a tic: "bad leg straight" three times in under two thousand words, each restating a fact the reader has. "The way a hurt man would lie" and "the way a hurt boy would" also appear, with man and boy swapped.
- Location: "Beth sat on the daybed with her bad leg straight." / "Beth lay on the daybed under the dentist's quilt the way a hurt man would lie, bad leg straight." / "Beth sat with her bad leg straight out and one hand flat on the daybed beside her."
- Suggested fix: Keep the leg only in the first (where the knee is dressed) and cut it from the other two. Use "the way a hurt boy would lie" in both lying lines, since Ned is expecting the kid.
