**Issue 1**
- Problem: Scene 2 is set at dusk, before the bridge falls (about 9 p.m.). The grid is still up at that point (bible, Phones: "Grid power fails about 9 p.m. Day 1 with the bridge"). Yet the store generator is already roaring and Tess has the Pinkhams "on candles". Scene 3 then has the power go "in the same surge", and the generators start afterward. Scene 2 contradicts that sequence.
- Location: "The store's generator roared out back, and Beth felt it come up through the step into her heels." and "I've got Whitcomb aboard his boat, the Pinkhams on candles, and nothing from you."
- Suggested fix: Cut the generator sentence and make the Pinkhams' candles a choice ("the Pinkhams saving their diesel"). Or move the generator's roar to scene 4 or 5, after the surge. Anchor the sensory beat in scene 2 on the bell or the wind instead.

**Issue 2**
- Problem: The back-door latch is placed on different doors. Scene 4 puts it on the outer door and Beth lifts it from outside. Scene 1 has Dane already inside the mudroom, fighting the door "between them" (an inner door), and Beth goes "by him onto the step" through the outer door. Scene 5 has the intruder "inside the outer door" and then lifting the latch (which must be the inner door's). It also says the outer door "knocked against its latch" and has Dane lift and shove to see "the outer door swinging". A reader cannot say which door sticks, which knocks, and which one Dane or the intruder is working.
- Location: "Dane stood in the mudroom with snow in his hair and his right hand on his hip under the jacket." / "He shut the door between them and tried it" / "In the dark she went in through the outer door, found the latch under her thumb in Ned's wool, lifted it and shoved, and the door went open without a sound." / "Somebody was inside the outer door, holding it, standing in her mudroom in the dark" / "The latch lifted. It stuck. It lifted again."
- Suggested fix: Fix one door as the sticking thumb-latch, the one between mudroom and kitchen (it carries lift-and-shove and the 11:40 lift). The outer door only knocks. Change scene 4 to "found the inner latch under her thumb in Ned's wool" after she has crossed the mudroom. Keep scene 1 and scene 5 as the inner door. Also name the outer door as a plain door in scene 1.

**Issue 3**
- Problem: Dane's position in the front yard is geometrically impossible and contradicts the outline's logic answer. Beth sees a glow under his chin through the front-room window, so he faces the house. The text then puts "the harbor side of the house behind him", but if he faces the house the harbor side is in front of him, beyond it. The outline's logic-hole answer says he goes to the road side to watch the road the truck used. The draft has his back to that road.
- Location: "a faint glow under his chin, his back to the road the truck had taken, the harbor side of the house behind him."
- Suggested fix: Keep him facing the house, so the glow shows. Delete "the harbor side of the house behind him" and say instead that the house stands between him and the harbor, which is why he can't see the back steps. Reconcile with the outline's "watching the road" by giving him a glance up the road before he turns to the glass.

**Issue 4**
- Problem: Pronoun ambiguity. The previous sentence's subject is Beth ("Beth watched her own name dry"). The next sentence, "She dug in her vest for the pen cap", therefore reads as Beth digging in her own vest. It is Tess's pocket. The reader gets the cash beat's owner only two sentences later.
- Location: "She dug in her vest for the pen cap. A fold of bills came up with it"
- Suggested fix: Replace "She" with "Tess" ("Tess dug in her red vest for the pen cap.").

**Issue 5**
- Problem: The cash beat quantifies Tess's money and weighs it. The beats say "no count, no total" and no interior verdict (Flag 15). "More than the store took in a week in November" implies a sum and tells the reader what to conclude. "Tess was brighter all at once" also labels her stress register rather than showing it.
- Location: "hundreds on the outside, more than the store took in a week in November." and "Tess was brighter all at once."
- Suggested fix: End the bill sentence at "hundreds on the outside." Delete "Tess was brighter all at once." Let the run of prices ("Milk's up a quarter...") carry the change.

**Issue 6**
- Problem: The bow-line action is unreadable. Beth throws the line "across the gap to the granite pier", but nobody is ashore (Dane is aboard), so nothing receives it. Dane "hauling it toward himself" would pull it off the pier. Beth's correction "Other way" cannot be followed. The set piece's action beat is unclear.
- Location: "She threw the bow line across the gap to the granite pier beside the slip, and it went over the first time. Dane had the tail and was hauling it toward himself."
- Suggested fix: Have her drop the eye over a pier bollard with the throw, and have Dane take a turn on the bow cleat with the tail. Beth's "Other way" then corrects his direction of turn. Show that the line works.

**Issue 7**
- Problem: Speaker mismatch with the bible's assignment table and the beats. "Nobody's coming over that stern in this." is assigned to Ned. As drafted it follows Dane's line with no tag, then Ned is shown silent ("didn't answer"), so the line reads as Beth's. The beat's Dane/Ned exchange is lost. Beth is also given a flat refusal that sits oddly with her own caution.
- Location: "\"Anyone with a skiff,\" Dane said.\n\n\"Nobody's coming over that stern in this.\"" and "Ned looked at the stern a long time and didn't answer, which on this boat was a yes."
- Suggested fix: Tag the line to Ned ("Ned said"), introduce him at the stern before it, and then have Dane's polite answer win the point.

**Issue 8**
- Problem: Ned adds weight in a chapter where he must not (orchestrator instruction: nothing in Ch 2-3 adds weight to Ned). The narration lingers on Ned's long look at the open stern, which is Ch 9's way in, and glosses his silence as a concession.
- Location: "Ned looked at the stern a long time and didn't answer, which on this boat was a yes."
- Suggested fix: Cut the sentence. Move to Dane's walk up the gangway after Dane's line, or give Ned a plain weather remark ("Lumpy.") in its place.

**Issue 9**
- Problem: The Ned look-back plant (2 of 3) is diluted. Beth turns first, and Ned's turn is a reply ("turned once too"). The plant is that Ned looks back on his own at every parting, as in Ch 1, where he turns at the top of the ladder unprompted. Here a reader could take it as courtesy to her turn.
- Location: "At the top of the gangway she turned. Ned had gone a few steps forward toward his pumps, and he turned once too, and nodded to her, and went on."
- Suggested fix: Make Ned the one who turns first, with Beth already walking up the gangway and not looking. "Behind her Ned, going forward to his pumps, stopped and turned once and nodded."

**Issue 10**
- Problem: Beats say "no heel detail" in scene 5 (the heel belongs to Ch 3's opening). "Fast and uneven" is a limp cue. Combined with the figure on the dragger's afterdeck, it all but identifies the runner as the hooded man. It also pre-empts Ch 3 s1.
- Location: "Feet went down the back steps, fast and uneven, and away toward the harbor."
- Suggested fix: Replace "fast and uneven" with "fast" or "two steps at a time."

**Issue 11**
- Problem: Dane's scene 1 dialogue is clipped, verbless fragments in Beth's drop-subject mode, not his profile (whole sentences with "because" and "which"; "which" and a dry last clause). Read with the names stripped, "A door that locks. Somebody awake." and "Last sitting before Pelletier walks." could be Beth's. This is the strip-the-names risk the bible names.
- Location: "The grand jury sits Monday. Last sitting before Pelletier walks. They want you in front of it." and "Then tonight it's somewhere with one way in from the land. A door that locks. Somebody awake."
- Suggested fix: Rebuild as one Dane sentence ("The grand jury sits Monday, which is the last sitting before Pelletier walks, and they'd like you in front of it, which is a sentence I've been practicing since Pruett.").

**Issue 12**
- Problem: Scene 1's reply to the Monday news does not close private boats. The beats require Beth to close them with the sea ("Nothing's crossing in this."), so the line answers "They want you in front of it" and reads as a non sequitur. The reader doesn't know the question being answered.
- Location: "\"Nothing's crossing in this.\""
- Suggested fix: Have Dane say first, in one clause, "Any boat that'll take you," and have Beth answer "Nothing's crossing in this."

**Issue 13**
- Problem: The Master Continuity Flag says Ch 2 s1's yard call is where Dane reports the limping man to Pike, and the beats make the call "his report". The draft says nothing to show what the call was. The reader gets no link between the skiff man and the call, so Pike's learning of him is not on the page.
- Location: "He made the call there under open sky. Through the kitchen window she watched his mouth and couldn't hear a word of it, and she put a stick in the stove it didn't need."
- Suggested fix: Add one line when he comes in that shows the content without relaying it, e.g. "\"They have the heel,\" he said." Do not name Pike.

**Issue 14**
- Problem: The scene 5 teasing exchange departs from the beat. The beat is Dane guessing which house talks about *her* most. The draft has him say "the house that's talked about me most today", so the answer "You." means Beth talked about him all day. The reader saw her say almost nothing about him, so the joke has no setup. It also drops the prediction frame that makes it Dane's.
- Location: "\"The house that's talked about me most today.\"" and "\"You. Since this morning.\""
- Suggested fix: Make the question the beat's: "The house that talks about you most." Beth waits. Dane: "You. Since this morning." Or keep "me" and add one earlier cue of Beth's remarks about him.

**Issue 15**
- Problem: Information-channel inconsistency. The bible's CASE FACTS have Ned reporting the bridge at the store (about 10:45), staying behind "to use the phone", and calling the casualty to Sector that night. Here Ida Dyer "would have heard Ned call the Coast Guard" on her scanner, which implies he made the call over VHF on the air. That contradicts the cell-only, store-only channel, and it gives the island Ned's report earlier than the bible allows.
- Location: "Ida Dyer had her scanner on, the way she'd had it on since Carter, and she'd have heard Ned call the Coast Guard."
- Suggested fix: Change to what Ida would hear without the call: "she'd have heard the bridge go" or "she'd have heard the net," and keep Ned's Coast Guard call off the air.

**Issue 16**
- Problem: Unrecorded named characters are introduced. The Pinkhams (Earl and Doris), Grace Grindle and Ida Dyer's scanner "since Carter" are not in the bible's cast, and the bible rules out a quirky ensemble. Grace Grindle is not Walt Grindle, the bible's fire chief. The names need recording, or they set up facts later chapters could contradict.
- Location: "The Pinkhams were on candles, because Earl begrudged the diesel and Doris liked candles and he liked Doris. The two yellow windows past the co-op were the Grindles, and Grace Grindle would have the pair of them married by Tuesday."
- Suggested fix: Add the Pinkhams and Grace Grindle (Walt's wife) to the continuity log as walk-ons. Keep each to the single line they have here.

**Issue 17**
- Problem: Small continuity drift on an object. Ch 1 places her father's alarm clock "off the shelf" in the kitchen. Ch 2 puts it "over the sink" and adds green hands. The reader cannot tell it is the same clock. Ch 1 has Beth wind and set it for the first boat, so a ticking clock that was set to ring also needs a line if it is to sound.
- Location: "Her father's clock over the sink, going on." and "The green hands of her father's clock stood at twenty to twelve."
- Suggested fix: Write "Her father's clock on its shelf, going on." Keep it consistent in the later mention, and name it the alarm clock once.
