# Chapter 02 Issues (draft.md)

**Issue 1**
- Problem: The reason the two trucks are on the car deck contradicts Ch 01. Ch 01 has the ferry make a full round trip Thursday night (island to Pruett, back by about eleven) with Cole's truck as "the last truck onto the ferry that night." Trucks parked aboard "since yesterday afternoon" would have crossed to Pruett and back on that run, or been unloaded at Pruett. As written, the stated reason ("a first run that wasn't going to happen") does not fit the night the reader just saw. Beats Flag 3 left this for the orchestrator to confirm.
- Location: "They'd been parked aboard since yesterday afternoon for a first run that wasn't going to happen."
- Suggested fix: Change to "They had been driven aboard after the last crossing last night, for a first run that wasn't going to happen," so they board after the ferry is back at the island and Ch 01 is untouched.

**Issue 2**
- Problem: PLAIN LANGUAGE budget broken (two boat, legal or police terms per 1,000 words). In the stretch from the grand jury line to the float gloss, the page uses grand jury, constable, harbormaster, floating dock (the unglossed form of the budget term "float"), and then float and car deck. That is three or four different terms inside one 1,000-word window. "Harbormaster" is not on the allowed list and is never glossed. The beats planned only grand jury and constable for the first half.
- Location: "Pruett harbormaster just phoned me," he said.
- Suggested fix: Replace with plain words, for example "The man who watches the Pruett dock just phoned me," and cut "floating dock" in s2 and s3 to "the dock" so "float" arrives once, glossed, in s4.

**Issue 3**
- Problem: Listener confusion. Russ is standing on the island pier beside the island's own floating dock, which the page has just named, and he says "the floating dock over there" for the Pruett dock across the water. A listener will hear it as the dock below them. Rule 6 (no plot fact left to infer): the key fact is that the kid left Pruett before Beth called.
- Location: "A skiff went out from the floating dock over there at half past ten last night."
- Suggested fix: "A skiff went out from the Pruett dock, across the bay, at half past ten last night."

**Issue 4**
- Problem: "Six hours" has no antecedent on the page. The midnight call is not restated until two exchanges later, so the line does not land. It also collides with the book's other "six hours" (the crossing in the window).
- Location: "Six hours," Beth said, "and I'm already 'the witness.'"
- Suggested fix: Anchor it: "I called at midnight," Beth said. "Six hours, and I'm already 'the witness.'" Or change the number to "One night."

**Issue 5**
- Problem: Dane's reasoning does not hold. In the same scene Dane drops the leak theory ("Nobody had to tell anybody anything"). Then, with no new reason, he locks his own office out and reports only to the boss. The plant (Dane reports to the boss alone, paid Ch 05, 08, 10) has no cause on the page, and the bible's Rule 12 allows his suspicion to stay general. The scene needs a reason a listener can repeat.
- Location: "And I report to you. Nobody else in that office. You're the one person there I'd trust with my life."
- Suggested fix: Add one clause of reason before the line, for example "The skiff explains tonight. It doesn't explain who else knows where she is tomorrow. So I report to you. Nobody else in that office."

**Issue 6**
- Problem: Ambiguous for a listener. "Nails" reads as fingernails. The joke she is covering for (his hammered nails) is lost, and checking a man's fingernails to hide a laugh makes no sense when heard.
- Location: "She almost laughed. She covered it by checking his nails."
- Suggested fix: "She almost laughed. She covered it by running her thumb over the nail heads in the board."

**Issue 7**
- Problem: Plain-fact gap in the high-danger beat. The page gives no reason Dane goes over the pier edge onto the ferry, and a "new rope" appears in Beth's hands with no one fetching it. A listener cannot follow what Dane is for in the rescue (Rule 6; beats: Dane "jumps ... to catch the rope Beth throws him").
- Location: "He was going over the edge of the pier." and "She threw him the end of the new rope."
- Suggested fix: Add one line before the jump, for example Beth, from the stone: "Get the back rope. Take it round the post on the deck." Then Dane's jump has a cause, and the rope is the one she dragged up from the pile.

**Issue 8**
- Problem: Geography contradicts the bible. Beth's house stands on the east side of the village above its own cove, and the back door faces the hill that runs down to the cove and the fish shack. From the pier at the west end of the harbor road she could not see the board nailed across the back door.
- Location: "She could see the board Dane had nailed across the back door."
- Suggested fix: "She could see the house, small and gray on its rise, and she knew the board was across the back door." Or move the image to the front window or the porch.

**Issue 9**
- Problem: Contradicts Dane's bible background. He worked two summers at 15 and 16 on his mother's cousin's lobster boat out of Rockport, which is the source of his calluses and his want of a boat. The draft says he knows no knots at all. It also sits oddly beside the same chapter's "careful, like he'd done it before."
- Location: "He didn't know a single knot, and he didn't pretend to."
- Suggested fix: "He knew only the knots a fifteen-year-old learns, and not the ones she was tying, and he didn't pretend otherwise."

**Issue 10**
- Problem: Ned's injury is out of scale with what he does next. A hip that "goes with a sound like a stick breaking" reads as a fracture. Seconds later he leans his full weight on the rope, then climbs the steel stairs, then limps home. The bible has "bad hip, worse after hauling Beth back."
- Location: "and she heard his hip go with a sound like a stick breaking."
- Suggested fix: Change to "and she heard him grunt, a sound she had never heard from him, and saw his hand go to his hip," which keeps the cost and keeps him mobile.
