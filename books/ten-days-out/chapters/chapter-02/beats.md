# Chapter 02 — The Weather Hold: Beat Sheet

**Heading for the prose:** Day 1 — Friday
**Target:** about 3,200 words across six scenes (Dane POV for Scene 1 only, about 850 words; Beth POV after that)
**Chapter promise (from outline):** How did they know she stayed, and is it him?
**Heat:** no sex, no kiss. Attraction gets one or two lines per scene at most (hands, breath, a look held too long).
**Swearing budget:** Dane none. Ned no swearing on air. At most one mild swear from Beth, aimed at weather or gear. Tess gets one "Christ on a bike" if it fits.

---

## Pre-flight flags (conflicts and how this sheet resolves them)

1. **The "11:30 boat" in the outline's Leak logic table conflicts with the Ch 02 entry and the friction beat, which both make the 2 pm the departure Dane plans for.** Resolved: Day 1 has two runs. The 8:00 out of Pruett lands about 8:45 and turns around for Pruett at 9:15, carrying the bank bag. The 2 pm is the afternoon departure. There is no 11:30. Dane turns down the 9:15 for stated reasons (Scene 2). Once the 2 pm is cancelled the whole island can work out that Beth is still on Cutter. What the network needs, and what Tess gives them, is **which house she is sleeping in**: her own, as opposed to somewhere a marshal would plausibly move her. Dane tells Beth he thought about moving her and decided against it, and that he has told nobody (Scene 3). That keeps "Who knew you were here?" / "You." honest. Ned knows she didn't board the 9:15. He does not know where she spent the night. The orchestrator should use this wording in the Ch 02 continuity entry.
2. **Who takes the bank bag.** The outline says "the deckhand". Kenny is sick (Ch 01), the bible keeps Kenny to an off-page phone voice on Day 1 at most, the mate is off-island, and Beth has been told to stay home. Resolved: Ned is working the boat short, as he said he would, so **Ned comes down the outside ladder and takes the bag at the rail himself**. Dane reads it as a captain doing a deckhand's job. This is also the moment Ned won't meet his eye. If the orchestrator would rather have Kenny back on deck, gray-faced and unnamed in Dane's POV, the beat still works, but the bible's Kenny limit would need an exception.
3. **Ned's one "goddamn" at the bridge collapse** (Language Rules) can't happen in this chapter. Beth hears the collapse only over the VHF, and the VHF carries no swearing. Recommend moving it to Ch 03, when Ned tells the story at the store ("and then the goddamn bridge"). Flag it for the Ch 03 beat sheet.
4. **Subpoena date.** Dane serves the grand jury subpoena in the morning (Research note 23), so it already shows Monday, November 23. Pike's evening call doesn't introduce the date. It turns the date into a deadline: last sitting, no continuance, the defense won't waive, Pelletier's Rule 5.1 clock. Dane says it out loud that night (World Rule 4).
5. **"Fentanyl" gets one use in the book** (Research note 39: Dane in Ch 02 or Quist in Ch 10). This sheet gives it to Dane in Scene 2. After that it is "the pills" everywhere, and Quist must not say it in Ch 10. Log it.
6. **Names now on the page:** Pelletier (Dane uses it in Scene 2 and again in Scene 5). The *Margaret Ann* (Dane reads the name and GLOUCESTER, MASS. off her transom in Scene 1). Pike (named in Scene 5). The hooded man and the skipper are still unnamed. Log all of these.
7. **The chair under the latch** (Master flag) is moved in Scene 3, in daylight, for a reason both of them state. Dane asks her to leave it off: the back door is their only second exit, and a latch that sticks is an alarm. Scene 6 pays this off.
8. **Landline vs. sat phone.** The landlines work all of Day 1 (World Rule 5). Dane makes his last landline call to Pike mid-morning (Scene 2). After the bridge goes, he tries the kitchen phone and gets a fast busy, because every islander is calling out at once and the phone cabinet is jammed. So he takes the Iridium to the yard. That makes both porch calls a necessity rather than a choice, as the bible requires.
9. **Scenes 4 and 5 run under 500 words** on purpose. They are hinge scenes, and the chapter needs its words in Scenes 1 and 6.

### Scene 1
**Setting:** The open passenger deck of the M/V *North Reach*, coming into Cutter harbor at about 8:40 am. The sky is low and the color of wet slate, with the wind starting to back. Spray comes over the bow ramp. Dane is in the wrong boots, standing aft of the passenger cabin with his back to the bulkhead. Sensory anchor: the diesel's thrum through his soles, and the cold hydraulic smell coming up off the car deck.
**Characters present:** Dane Reyes (POV), Ned Haskell, Tess Marr (not named; Dane doesn't know her).
**Surface action:**
- Dane counts the people on the island float as the boat noses into the slip. There are seven. Two lobstermen in orange are there for freight. Somebody's grandmother is waiting for a parcel. Others are watching just to watch. He sorts them by what their hands are doing: pockets, coffee cups, one man with a phone held up, getting no signal.
- A short woman in a red vest, glasses pushed up into cropped gray hair, comes down the ramp laughing at something somebody said. She passes a canvas zip bag with a seal tag up over the rail. Ned has come down the outside ladder from the wheelhouse to take it, because he is working without a deckhand. Dane files it as island banking, the way a town without a bank does it, and moves on. Give it one plain sentence and no lingering.
- Ned looks at Dane's soft-shell, his boots and his bare head. Then Ned looks at the bag, the ramp, the sky, everywhere but Dane's face. Dane notes it: a man who has been told who is aboard and doesn't want to be the one to say so.
- On the guest mooring under the hill sits a red-hulled steel dragger with a white wheelhouse, lying to her mooring with her stern swung toward him. He reads the transom: MARGARET ANN, GLOUCESTER, MASS. An out-of-state boat on a guest mooring the morning after. Her deck lights are still on in daylight.
- Before he walks off he runs the job through in his head as distances and times: one-day transfer, a second deputy meeting him at Pruett at 2:45, the safe site. He has the subpoena and the Rite in the Rain notebook in his inside pocket and the Iridium on his belt.
- He walks up the Harbor Road and counts sightlines as he goes: the float, the store dock, the church, the cottage's kitchen window that looks down on all of it. The door of the gray-shingled Cape opens before he reaches the step. She has watched him the whole way up.
**Subtext:** Dane is good, and the reader should trust his eye. That is exactly why the bag reads as nothing: he sees it clearly and gets it wrong for sound reasons. The order doesn't exist yet, so this POV has no secret to hide. Keep his thoughts entirely on the job. Marisol is not named. At most, his eyes rest on a parking lot at the Pruett end in memory for half a line, or skip it.
**Tension source:** Situational. He is alone on a job meant for two, the weather is coming in early, and there are too many eyes on a float for a boat that brings in one man. Ned's averted face is the first wrong note.
**Ends on:** The door opening before he knocks. She is in the doorway in a watch cap, one hand on the frame, and has clearly been watching him come up the road. He realizes she counted him the way he counted the float.
**Word count target:** 850

### Scene 2
**Setting:** Beth's kitchen, about 9:00 to 10:15 am. The woodstove is going. The barometer by the kitchen door reads 994 and is falling. The kitchen window looks down on the harbor, and through it the *North Reach* backs out of the slip at 9:15 with the bag aboard. Sensory anchor: woodsmoke and wet wool, and her father's clock in the front room.
**Characters present:** Beth (POV), Dane.
**Surface action:**
- Dane shows his credentials. He calls her "ma'am" once. She shuts it down in one line ("Beth or Calloway. Not ma'am."). He goes with "Ms. Calloway", which she lets stand without liking it.
- He serves the grand jury subpoena, a single sheet. She reads it standing, sees *Monday, November 23*, and pins it under the barometer. On the way past she taps the glass with one knuckle.
- He gives her the case in a few lines. The driver, Pelletier, is in custody. The bricks were pressed pills. "Fentanyl." This is the book's only use of the word, said flatly; she does not react on the page beyond her hands going still on the counter. "You're not in the program. You're in the waiting room for the program. I'm the waiting room."
- The plan: the 2 pm boat, a second deputy at the Pruett terminal at 2:45, a safe site until the grand jury. She asks why not the 9:15, which is leaving right now. He tells her the deputy can't be at Pruett before 2:45, and he won't sit her in an open terminal lot with one gun for five hours. His hand stops on the counter for a second when he says "lot". She notices and doesn't know why.
- He uses her kitchen landline to confirm the 2 pm with Pike. Keep it short and offstage-ish: she hears his half of the call, "two o'clock, Pruett, two forty-five." This is his last landline call of the book.
- When he hangs up, she says it: "It won't run. Pack if you want. You'll be unpacking by two." He asks why. Conclusion first, then reasons, and only because he asked: the wind's backed northeast, the glass is down four since breakfast, and Ned won't take her into that slip on a falling tide with the sea building. He repeats it back in fewer words: "Northeast. Four millibars. Two o'clock's dead." He doesn't agree. He doesn't argue either.
- He asks to walk the house. She takes him through it. In the mudroom he sees the chair wedged under the back-door latch bar and looks at it, then at her. She tells him she wedged it last night. He touches the latch once. Leave the chair where it is for now.
**Subtext:** Both of them are reading the other's competence and deciding whether to defer. She wants him to be good at his job so she can stop doing it herself. She is also braced for him to be one more person who tells her how things are going to go. He wants her compliant and packed, and he is starting to suspect she won't be either. Neither wants the other to see how much the morning's news (fentanyl, a federal subpoena) has landed.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal friction over the boat. Each of them is right inside their own field, and they don't yet trust each other's.
**Ends on:** His hand on the latch above the chair, and the latch giving its iron clack as he lets it drop. She says, "It sticks." He says, "I heard."
**Word count target:** 450

### Scene 3
**Setting:** The cottage and yard, about 11:30 am to 2:00 pm. Sleet has started, the first of it rattling the north windows. The woodpile is out the back door under a lean-to. Beth's ferry-issued Icom handheld is on the kitchen table, set to 16 and 13. Sensory anchor: sleet ticking on the shingles; the handheld's squelch.
**Characters present:** Beth (POV), Dane; Ned (voice on VHF only).
**Surface action:**
- Dane goes out to walk the property: road to the door, back step to the alders, the line from the guest mooring to her bedroom window. From the kitchen window Beth watches him stop at points and look back at the house, measuring. She knows what he's doing because it is what she does with a deck: where's the exit, what can see you, how many steps. (Romance beat: she notices him doing it.)
- She needs wood in before the weather gets worse. She pulls the chair out from under the latch, shoves the latch up (it sticks, then gives) and carries in three armloads. Dane comes back around the house while she's stacking.
- He asks her to leave the chair off. He gives her the reason and something she can do instead. The back door is the only way out of the house apart from the front door onto the road. A barred door traps them if trouble comes to the front. And a latch that sticks is a better alarm than a chair: nobody can lift it quietly. She keeps an exit in every room, and she agrees because his reasoning is her reasoning. She sets the chair by the stove. This is the move the Master flag needs.
- At 1:15 the handheld: Ned, formal and clipped. "All stations, all stations, this is the Cutter Island ferry *North Reach*. The fourteen hundred departure is cancelled. Next departure to be announced. *North Reach* standing by one-six and one-three."
- Dane looks at the radio, then at her. "You were right about the boat. I'm going to stop arguing with you about water." She doesn't gloat. She answers with a task, telling him where the lamp oil is.
- He runs through the options aloud and closes them. A private boat: any island or visiting boat could be part of it, he can't assess a lobster boat in those seas, and a witness who leaves on a neighbor's boat leaves a trail of who took her. LifeFlight: medical only, and it lands on the ballfield in view of half the island. Moving her to another house tonight: he has no house he's walked, and this one has the chimney mass and a door he can hear. "We stay here tonight. I haven't told anyone where. I won't." Beth agrees on the sea alone: nothing small is getting out of that harbor today.
- Attraction line, one only: his hair wet from the sleet, and her noticing she has handed him a dish towel before deciding to.
**Subtext:** Respect turns over. He has conceded the water to her, and in the next scene he will extend that to the island, which is the mistake. "I haven't told anyone where" sets up "You." in Scene 6: in her mind the circle is now exactly one man.
**Tension source:** Situational (the trap closing as the ferry is cancelled), set against an interpersonal thaw.
**Ends on:** Beth tapping the barometer as she passes it. It reads 990. The chair sits by the stove, not under the latch.
**Word count target:** 500

### Scene 4
**Setting:** The cottage at about 5:30 to 5:50 pm. It is full dark (sunset was a little past four). The shades are down, one kerosene lamp is lit in the kitchen, and the stove is loaded. The kitchen landline is on the wall with its 12-foot cord. Sensory anchor: the cord's coil, cold against her wrist; the wind now in the north gable.
**Characters present:** Beth (POV), Dane (outside for the call), Tess (voice on the phone).
**Surface action:**
- Earlier, Beth has explained the island rule to Dane in two lines: at six Tess runs the check-in net on 68. Miss it and Walt Grindle's fire crew is on your porch by seven. Anyone on the net hears every name. Dane: "Then nobody hears yours." Beth: "Leave it to me." He takes that, because he has just stopped arguing with her about her island. Show a brief stop in him (a hand on the notebook in his pocket, not opening it), then he lets it go.
- Dane goes out to the road to check what the lit house shows through the drawn shades. That puts him outside on purpose while she makes the call. Image to land: he is checking what can be seen from outside while the information leaves by wire.
- Beth takes the receiver into the mudroom doorway on its cord and keeps her voice low. She dials the store. Tess answers bright and fast ("Marr's"). Beth: home, don't send anybody, keep it off the air. Tess: of course, hon, she'll count her by phone. Beth hears the pencil going. Tess asks if she's okay, mentions she heard Bethie had company off the morning boat, and Beth says, "Don't ask me, Tess," and Tess doesn't. Tess's fear shows once, small enough to reread later: a laugh that comes half a beat late, or a coin dropped on the counter at her end.
- Tess plants, in her voice: she's net control tonight, "and emergency management director, since town meeting. I was slow putting my hand down." One line about money (the generator's eating forty dollars of gas a day and the tank loan doesn't care about storms). Then: "Channel 9 if the phones go. Like the old days."
- Beth hangs up and stands with her hand on the phone. Dane comes back in, shaking sleet off. "Shades are good." Beth: "I've taken care of the check-in." He nods. He doesn't ask how.
**Subtext:** Beth is careful with the marshal and careless with her own. She keeps the case from Tess ("Don't ask me") and gives her the location without counting it as information. Tess is family, and telling Tess where you are is like telling the house. Channel 9 is a girl on her father's boat and a woman on the float, and the line should carry that warmth. The reader has the bank bag, the fuel float from Ch 01 and now the EMD title, and should feel a small cold prickle that Beth does not.
**Tension source:** Dramatic irony. The smart, correct choice (stay off the air, keep the fire crew off the porch) is the leak.
**Ends on:** "I've taken care of the check-in," his nod, and her turning the lamp down a notch.
**Word count target:** 450

### Scene 5
**Setting:** The kitchen at 9:00 to about 9:50 pm. High water is near and the storm is at full strength. At 9:00 a shriek of parting cable carries up from the slip, and then every light in the harbor goes out at once. The ferry's own deck floods stay lit, swinging. The handheld is on the table. Sensory anchor: the boom of the sea under the slip, felt through the window glass under Beth's palm.
**Characters present:** Beth (POV), Dane; Ned (VHF voice); Pike (sat phone voice, heard only as Dane's half of the call or reported).
**Surface action:**
- Beth is at the kitchen window, palm flat on the glass, when the sound comes: steel, then a long grinding, then the grid going dark (World Rule 5: power fails at about 9 pm). In the ferry's floods she can see the transfer bridge's seaward end down and twisted, half in the water.
- She has her float coat half on before she has decided anything. Ned is alone down there. She stops with one arm in the sleeve, because a witness running down the Harbor Road in the dark is exactly what someone is waiting for, and because the radio will tell her more than her eyes can. She takes the coat off. Dane sees her do it and says nothing. This is the decent-competent choice the bible requires; give it two sentences, no internal debate.
- Ned on 16, then 13, formal: the slip is damaged, the vessel is secure, no further service. Then, slower: "Ten days, if the state gets a barge out." Beth repeats it out loud, once.
- Dane tries the kitchen landline and gets a fast busy, because the whole island is calling out. He takes the Iridium from his belt, steps out into the yard where the sky is open, and calls Pike. Beth watches him through the gap at the edge of the shade, a dark shape with the stubby antenna up, his back to the wind.
- He comes back in and tells her plainly, slowing down so she'll listen. Pike says the grand jury is Monday, November 23, Day 11, the last sitting before Thanksgiving week. If they don't indict Pelletier that day, his preliminary hearing deadline runs out and the case starts coming apart. No continuance. No relief can get to the island before the sea goes down. "If the ferry's back on Day 10, we're on it. If it isn't, I'll find another way, and it won't be a neighbor's boat." Pike will call back at 11:20 for his nightly check. The Iridium only works outside, so he'll be on the porch.
- Beth taps the barometer. It reads 986. She looks at the subpoena under it.
**Subtext:** The trap closes. She is on her own island and can't get to her own boat. The deadline gives the ten days a shape: ferry back on Day 10, testimony on Day 11, one day to spare. Dane on the porch with the antenna up is presented as pure necessity, so that when Scene 6 turns it into suspicion the reader feels the unfairness and the logic at once.
**Tension source:** Situational. The slip is wrecked, the grid is down and the deadline is fixed. Internally, Beth's urge to go to Ned, held back.
**Ends on:** Her knuckle on the barometer glass, and the subpoena under it with the date showing.
**Word count target:** 400

### Scene 6
**Setting:** The cottage at 11:15 pm to about midnight, with no power. The stove is banked to a red line and the north gable takes the gusts. Beth is in her bedroom off the hall, dressed, under a blanket and awake. Dane is on the front-room couch with his boots on. Sensory anchor: the latch's iron clack. Second anchor: the cold of the chimney brick through her shirt.
**Characters present:** Beth (POV), Dane; the person at the door is never seen or named.
**Surface action:**
- At 11:20 Beth hears the front door and sees, through the front-room window, Dane step off the end of the porch into open sky with the Iridium's antenna up, notebook in his other hand, facing the road. His voice is low and the wind takes the words. It lasts a few minutes. He comes back in, and she hears the couch take his weight. She notes the time by her father's clock because she always notes the time. Give it one plain paragraph and don't color it with suspicion yet.
- 11:40. She is still awake. From the mudroom: the latch lifts, sticks, lifts again. It is the sound of someone who doesn't know you have to lift and shove. (Use Style Sample 2's rhythm here, but not its exact wording. Here she is awake when the latch goes, so this is a sound she hears, not a missing sound that wakes her.)
- She doesn't reach for the lamp or a flashlight. Feet on the cold boards, shoulder to the wall so the floor won't talk, four steps to the bedroom door and seven down the hall. Dane is already sitting up. She leans in until her mouth is at his ear: "Back door. Someone's on the latch." He doesn't ask if she's sure. He points once at the chimney mass, and she gets behind the brick and puts her back to it. She is the one who knows the house, and she lets the armed man go to the door. (Danger beat: exact, and smart.)
- From behind the brick she hears it all by ear: Dane's weight crossing the kitchen, the latch going still, the mudroom door yanked inward, the storm coming in loud, and then running feet on wet ground, going away. Silence. One beam of his flashlight, finally, down on the step.
- He comes back and tells her. Sleet is already filling in the prints: boots, one set, across the yard toward the alders and the shore path. She says, "That latch sticks. Whoever that was has never been in this house." He says her name for the first time: "Beth." Then: "Who knew you were here?"
- "You." She says it before she thinks, and it's true as she means it. The reader should catch her eyes passing over the wall phone and its coiled cord without stopping. Do not put Tess in her head.
- In her head, with no commentary: him on the porch twenty minutes earlier, antenna up, facing the road.
**Subtext:** This is the point of no return. The only safe place left is a house someone just tried to walk into, and the man who answered the door is the only person she has told herself knew. She doesn't believe it's him. She can't stop placing the porch call next to the latch. He hears "You." and doesn't defend himself, and in her eyes that reads as either honesty or practice. Restraint as care: after she says it, he does something practical, like re-loading the stove or putting his own jacket over the chair by the door. He doesn't argue.
**Tension source:** Physical threat, then the first cold edge of suspicion against the respect built in Scenes 2–5.
**Ends on:** Beth still behind the chimney mass after the danger has gone. Her back stays on the brick while Dane stands in the kitchen doorway with the sat phone's antenna showing above his jacket pocket. She doesn't step out. Don't end on a question in narration (Forbidden clichés); end on the image.
**Word count target:** 650
