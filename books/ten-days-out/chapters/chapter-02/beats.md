## Flags before the beats (continuity log and bible against the outline entry)

None of these blocks the chapter. Each has a default, and the beats use it.

1. **The kid is never shown armed in Ch 01.** The approved opener says "a night that had included a man with a gun," and the outline gives Dane "he won't leave her alone on an island with an armed man." In the final Ch 01 the kid has a flashlight, and the only gun is Dane's. **Default used:** the second sentence of the chapter turns the opener into a joke: the man with the gun was the marshal, who sat up all night at her father's table with it in front of him. Dane's reason in s2 is worded as what he knows plus what he assumes: "a man who kicked in your door at three in the morning and came down a hill after you. I'm going to assume he brought more than a flashlight." Ch 03's shots then confirm it.
2. **The boss and Beth.** The bible says the boss never speaks to Beth directly until Ch 14, but the domain-checked line says "She wants **you** in the room." **Default used:** on speaker, the boss says "She wants **the witness** in the room." Beth hears herself turned into a noun, and that gives her a one-line verdict. The rest of the DC line is word for word.
3. **The two stranded trucks.** The outline says they were "left from the afternoon run when the storm warning came in." That doesn't say which run, or why they're still aboard. **Default used (one plain line on the page):** they've been chained on the car deck since yesterday afternoon, parked aboard for this morning's first run, which isn't going to happen. They can't drive off afterward because the ferry ends up tied along the side of the pier with the ramp at her front hanging over water. Ch 01's "the last truck onto the ferry that night" allows other trucks aboard. **Orchestrator:** confirm, or give the beats a different reason, and log it.
4. **Scene count.** The outline gives two scenes (900 + 800). Following the beat-sheet format (4–6 scenes), this sheet splits them into four. All of the outline's content and order is kept, and nothing is added to the plot. The waking moment from the "Opens with" line becomes s1. The outline's s1 becomes s2. The outline's s2 becomes s3 (the break-loose) and s4 (the cost, and the hold).
5. **Ch 03's opening line** ("Dane Reyes lost the argument about Beth's house in under a minute and won the one about Russ Whitcomb's") seems to say Beth's house won, while the Ch 03 body rules it out. This chapter ends with Dane saying "Not there" and Beth *not* arguing. **For the Ch 03 beat sheet:** read the opener as "Dane argued for something about her house (fetching her things, one more night with the door nailed) and lost," or reword it. The Ch 03 architect owns this. It's noted here so the handoff is clean.
6. **Boat words in the outline entry.** "Help with the lines" becomes **ropes** on the page. "On their moorings" becomes **tied out in the harbor**. Neither word reaches the prose.

**No conflict** with the continuity log (empty) or with Ch 01's final state. Ch 01 ends with Beth in Dane's coat at the shack and the wind rising. Per the bible's bed table she sleeps on her couch by the stove from about four, with Dane awake at the kitchen table and his gun on it. Her back door was broken in. Dane's forearm is bruised from the oar, and her feet are sore from the snow. All of that is carried forward below.

## What the sample reader warned about, and how this sheet answers it

*Warning: "she refuses the boat, a storm, a rescue, a long hug" reads like a stall.*

- **The refusal is an act, done with her hands.** Beth is the one holding the police boat's rope, and she gets one foot onto its edge before she decides. Then she throws the rope aboard herself and watches the boat go. That's a choice she can't take back, and it happens on the page.
- **The romance moves four times, each step bigger than the last:** she wakes wanting him (s1), then touches the bruise she gave him (s1). She's glad, plainly, that he stayed (s2). The first thing she looks for when she's hauled out alive is him (s3). Then the hold, where neither of them lets go and she says in her head that she's done calling it the cold (s4).
- **The storm is an event with a cost.** It's three things breaking in order: a rope parts, the ferry swings, and Beth is nearly crushed. Then the float is smashed, the steering is gone, and the island's lobster boats go up on the rocks. Each thing that breaks closes a way off the island.
- **The hug is short and busy.** It runs about 150 words, and it happens while they watch Russ's boat go onto the rocks, so it carries the loss as well as the touch.
- **No two talk-led scenes in a row:** s1 (action and touch) → s2 (argument with a physical act and a running engine) → s3 (action) → s4 (event and touch, then one question).

### Scene 1
**Setting:** Beth's kitchen, about half past five, still dark. The woodstove is down to coals. Sensory anchor: cold woodsmoke and the smell of her father's pipe that she tells herself is still there. Second detail: the sound of a hammer.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:** Open on the outline line exactly: "Beth Calloway woke wanting the marshal, which was the most inconvenient thing to happen to her in a night that had included a man with a gun." The next beat lands the joke from Flag 1: the man with the gun was the marshal. He'd sat at her father's table all night with it in front of him, and he isn't at the table now. She's on the couch by the stove under his coat. It smells of him, and she lies there one second longer than she needs to. Her feet hurt when she puts them down (the snow last night). The hammering is Dane at the back door, sleeves pushed up, nailing one of her father's boards across the door the man broke. On his forearm is a purple bar the width of an oar. She watches his hands and forearms and wants them on her, said in those words in her head. She crosses the kitchen in two pairs of socks and puts her thumb on the bruise without asking. He stops hammering. Neither of them says what that was. The laugh: Beth: "That's the only door on that side of the house." Dane: "Then he'll have to use the window, like everybody else in this house." His phone buzzes. It's the officer: the police boat is at the landing, and the sea closes in an hour. "Get your boots."
**Subtext:** She's starting to like being looked after, and she doesn't trust that. He did something useful for her house at dawn because he couldn't do anything about the man, and he's embarrassed to be caught at it.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and situational. The touch is a move neither of them names. The boat is waiting, and she already knows she isn't getting on it. The reader doesn't know that yet.
**Turn:** From sleep and want to the clock: the boat is waiting.
**Longing (can't miss it):** The first line, then her plain thought about his hands, then her thumb on the bruise.
**Laugh:** "Then he'll have to use the window, like everybody else in this house." Dane's joke, aimed at her and at himself.
**Plants:** The board across her back door (pays in s4: her house can't be her house tonight). The oar bruise (injury carried to Ch 03). Her sore feet (injury carried through Ch 02).
**Ending type:** Dialogue button. "Get your boots." Then cut.
**Ends on:** "Get your boots."
**Word count target:** **350** (floor 320). *If the chapter runs long, this scene can be squeezed to about 150 words and opened at the top of s2. It's the one scene the chapter could lose without losing plot. It does advance the romance (her first touch of him), so keep it if the words allow.*

### Scene 2
**Setting:** The landing at the island pier, first light, about six. The police boat's engine is running and tied at the floating dock below the pier (call it "the floating dock" here, not yet "the float"). The ferry is tied at the stone pier beside it. Snow is starting and blowing flat. Sensory anchor: the engine running under everything they say, and the officer's exhaust in the cold.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, the police-boat officer (unnamed, one marker: he keeps checking his watch), Dane's boss (voice on speaker), Russ (arrives partway through), Ned (seen on the pier with the ferry ropes, no lines).
**Surface action:** One argument staged against a running engine. Each piece of news gets one line, in this order:
1. **The officer, plain** (bible: he tells her what happens if she stays): ten minutes, then the sea closes the bay and he can't come back for days. She's holding his boat's rope in her bare hands, and he's waiting on her.
2. **Beth almost goes.** She gets one foot onto the boat's edge. Her fear at its true size: she wants to be on that boat and away more than she has wanted anything since her father died. Then she looks up at the pier, where Ned is alone with the ferry's ropes, the wind is coming round, and his hip makes him slow. She takes her foot back. "No."
3. **Dane puts his boss on speaker to change her mind, and it backfires.** The boss, in two lines (Lines as they now stand, with Flag 2's one-word change): the truck was clean, the cold storage on the Pruett wharf was hosed down, all they have is a little powder in the cracks of one crate, and her eyes are the case. "The grand jury sits Monday the twenty-third. The prosecutor won't go in on a phone call and a little powder. She wants the witness in the room, and she says if they don't indict him that morning, he walks." Dane glosses the grand jury for Beth in his own sentence: a room of ordinary people who decide whether there's enough for a trial. Beth's verdict on being "the witness" is one flat line. Then her interior, one line: Cole Varney paid for the flowers at the funeral of the boy she taught to row (bible flag 8). "The truck was clean" makes her angrier, and her mind is made up.
4. **Russ comes down the pier at a jog**, cap off and back on. Gloss in Beth's head: Russ Whitcomb, the island's constable, its one part-time police officer. Her one-sentence verdict: "Russ Whitcomb was a good man who was bad with money." His one line: the Pruett harbormaster phoned. A skiff went out from the floating dock at Pruett at half past ten last night, no lights, a skinny young fella with a bad leg in it. (The bad leg is the listener's marker. Don't name "the kid's" job or trailer; that's Ch 05.)
5. **Dane says it plainly, to her face (N6):** half past ten. She called at midnight. "Then he was on his way before you ever picked up the phone. Nobody had to tell anybody anything." Beth's reaction, plainly: she hadn't let herself think it until he said it was untrue, and her knees nearly go a second time. (World rule 12: Dane's earlier suspicion stays general, and here he drops it.)
6. **Her reasons, to his face (N7), in her short whole sentences:** she's the only one who saw the kid with the drugs. If she runs, Cole's people learn that shooting at a witness works. And Ned can't tie the ferry up for the storm alone, look at him. **His reasons:** "I can't make you get on that boat. Nobody can. It's your call." Then the boss: no second deputy can get out before the storm. Dane: "Then I'm staying." He won't leave her alone on an island with a man who kicked in her door at three in the morning and came down a hill after her ("I'm going to assume he brought more than a flashlight"). In front of Beth, he tells the boss he'll report to her and nobody else in that office, because she's the one person there he'd trust with his life. The boss gives a short condition, not a question.
7. **Flirting with an edge (Lines as they stand):** "You're the most stubborn witness I've ever had." / "How many have you had?" / "Forty-one, as of this morning."
8. **The act:** Beth throws the police boat's rope aboard herself. The officer backs out into the snow, and its lights go small and then out.
**Subtext:** She's staying for her island and for Ned, and, in one second she'd never admit, because he's staying. He tells his boss it's the case. The reader can't hear his head this chapter, but can see him not look at the boat once.
**Tension source:** Situational (the engine, the ten minutes, the snow) and interpersonal (both are right: she shouldn't stay, and she can't go). It's argued face to face, and nothing is withheld (World rule 9; outline one-talk sentence for Ch 02).
**Turn:** She's leaving, then she stays, and he stays. Act 1 ends (~9–10%).
**Longing (can't miss it):** As the boat's lights go out, she's glad. Say it in plain words in her head: for one second she was glad, and it was because he was still standing beside her. (The bible lists this as the thing she's afraid he'll find out. It stays in her head and drives nothing between them.)
**Laugh:** "Forty-one, as of this morning." The count is aimed at her.
**Plants:** The prosecutor's Monday (→ Ch 08, 17). "The truck was clean, so her eyes are the case" (said twice: here, then Ch 03 s1). The kid left Pruett before she called (→ Ch 12). The skiff kept at the Pruett float (→ Ch 14–16). Leaving at first light is closed: protection is voluntary, and her three reasons. Dane reports only to the boss (→ Ch 05, 08, 10).
**Ending type:** Image plus a one-line interior admission. The boat's lights go out in the snow, and she's glad.
**Ends on:** The police boat's lights gone into the snow, and Beth, glad for one second, because he stayed.
**Word count target:** **650** (floor 600). This is the most crowded scene in the chapter (outline Pacing flag 1). Keep each piece of news to one line inside the argument. Don't add a second line of case facts anywhere.

### Scene 3
**Setting:** The pier and the ferry, the storm's first hour, about seven. The wind has gone round and is pushing the ferry onto the pier. Sensory anchor: the hull grinding on the stone, then a rope parting like a shot. Second detail: ice on the posts.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Ned.
**Surface action:** Beth and Ned are doubling the ferry's ropes for the storm. Beth works fast and takes her gloves off to tie the knots that matter (bible habit; one use). Ned works slowly at the posts on the pier, on his bad hip. Give the fear a beat before it lands (World rule 8): she hears one rope groaning wrong before it goes. Ned answers her with the weather first. Then the rope parts like a shot, and the back of the ferry swings out and then in. Beth is on the floating dock between the hull and the pier, with a rope in her hands, as the steel comes at her. She goes silent. Ned, slow on land, gets her by the coat collar from the pier above and hauls her up and back. She hears his hip go, and the steel closes on the space where she was standing. Meanwhile Dane jumps from the pier onto the ferry, badly, landing on his knees on wet steel, to catch the rope Beth throws him. **Talk against silence (bible friction 1):** Dane talks the whole time, low and steady, saying where he is and what he's holding. Beth gives two or three words: "Rope. Hold it. Don't let go." The ferry's swing smashes the floating dock where the police boat was tied forty minutes ago. They get her tied along the side of the pier. Ned goes up to the captain's cabin and turns the wheel, and it spins with nothing on the other end.
**Subtext:** Ned would do anything to keep her alive. The reader takes that at face value, and it's true (World rule 12: this reads as an old man's love). Beth sees that Dane doesn't wait to be good at a thing before he does it, which is on her attraction list.
**Tension source:** Physical and immediate. Steel, rope, ice. Then a slower dread: every repair they could make needs a boat that works.
**Turn:** The safe harbor becomes the danger, and the boat they were saving is crippled.
**Longing (can't miss it):** When Ned drops her on the pier, the first thing she looks for, before her own hands and before Ned, is Dane. Say it plainly.
**Laugh:** Right after the ropes hold, Dane, still on his knees on the deck: "I want everybody to remember that I jumped." / Beth: "You fell toward the boat. It's different." Her verdict, aimed at his mainland softness.
**Plants:** The steering broken (→ Ch 13). The floating dock smashed (→ Ch 12, 13, 14: one broken section chained to the pier). Talk against silence (→ Ch 16). Ned's bad hip, worse now (injury, to the end). Ned saved her on the pier (→ the reader clears him, F11).
**Ending type:** A line of consequence. Ned at the wheel: "She's not answering, Bethie." (Or Ned's own understatement. Keep it to one line, and give the weather first if it fits.)
**Ends on:** The wheel spinning free in Ned's hands.
**Word count target:** **550** (floor 500). Real time, beat by beat, no cutaway (bible high-point register). Never more than two sentences of rope work in a row before a sound, a thought or a line.

### Scene 4
**Setting:** The pier, minutes later, snow blowing flat across the pier lights, spray freezing on their coats. View across the harbor to the rocks, and up the rise to Beth's house.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Ned, Russ.
**Surface action:**
1. **What's left, one plain line each, inside the action:** the float, the floating dock where every other boat tied up, is splintered down to one broken section chained to the pier. Two trucks have been chained on the car deck (the open deck where the trucks park) since yesterday afternoon, a lumber truck and the co-op's bait truck, parked for a first run that isn't going to happen. With the ferry tied along the side of the pier and her ramp hanging over water, they can't drive off (Flag 3).
2. **The tease (plant):** Ned sits on a post with his hip. Beth tells him he can't swim and still won't wear a life jacket. He answers with the weather or with slow understatement. (Suggested shape only; the writer owns it: "Never needed one. I don't go in.")
3. **The hold, while something happens:** Dane comes off the ferry and takes hold of her. Over his shoulder, across the harbor, the lobster boats tied out in the harbor break loose or drag and go up on the rocks one after another. Beth watches Russ's go with them. Russ is standing at the shore end of the pier watching it, his cap in his hand. Inside the hold, her interior, plain (bible interior line 1, which may be used as written): she wanted him, she'd wanted him since the oar, and she was done calling it the cold. Then: "You can let go." / "I know." He doesn't, not for another breath, and she doesn't step back either. About 150 words for all of it, interleaved with the boats.
4. **Say it once, with feeling (restating the chapter's closing facts for the ear):** in one line in her head, there's no way off now. No police boat, no ferry, no float, and the island's own boats are on the rocks. (This closes the lobster-boat safer option, B4.)
5. **Ned goes home (plant):** he limps up the harbor road, turns once, and lifts a hand. One sentence.
6. **Russ walks back over** and asks his line (bible): "Where are you sleeping tonight? I'm not asking for me. I'm asking because somebody will."
7. Beth looks up the rise at her father's house, at the board Dane nailed across the back door this morning. She has slept in that house every night of her life, and a man with a bad leg knows every step of that hill in the dark.
**Subtext:** The island she stayed for has just been taken apart in front of her. The only thing still standing next to her is a man from away. He's holding on longer than a marshal needs to, and both of them know it.
**Tension source:** Situational (no way off, and the man is on the island with them) and interpersonal (the hold neither ends).
**Turn:** The island shuts, and her own house stops being hers.
**Longing (can't miss it):** "She wanted him," plus the hold and "You can let go." / "I know."
**Laugh:** Ned's life-jacket answer (dry, slow, his). A small laugh. The big one was in s3.
**Plants:** Ned can't swim and won't wear a life jacket (→ Ch 09, 12, 15). Ned looks back (→ Ch 12, 16; place once more by Ch 05). The float smashed and the lobster boats on the rocks (→ Ch 13). The two trucks stuck aboard (→ Ch 15, 16). Russ's boat on the rocks (the bible's Russ thread; his certificate is Book 2, so not here).
**Ending type:** Open question, the chapter promise. Russ's question hangs. Beth doesn't answer. Dane, beside her, looking where she's looking: "Not there." She doesn't argue, and that frightens her more than the steel did.
**Ends on:** "Not there." and Beth not arguing. *Where does she sleep tonight?* Ch 03 opens on it.
**Word count target:** **405** (floor 370).
