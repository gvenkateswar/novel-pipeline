# Reader Notes: Chapter 02, "The Weather Hold"

**Draft read:** `chapters/chapter-02/final.md` after the tournament rewrite, Oct 29, 2026. I read `chapters/chapter-01/final.md` first for context. I read nothing else.

**Pull score: 4 of 5.** I'd turn the page. The last three lines hit hard. The middle third (the pier, the bridge coming down, Ned at the stern) is where the chapter is weakest, and if this were going to break, that's where it would break.

---

## Where I got bored

- **The door test at the top.** "He shut the door between them and tried it from his side: a clank, a rattle, the whole door bucking. Then he lifted and shoved, and it came, loud. The third time it opened without a sound." Three tries at a latch to open the chapter. I could tell it was being set up for later, and that made it feel like homework. Chapter 1 ended with his hand going to his hip and I wanted that moment. I got a tutorial on the door.
- **The bridge explained.** "Below her the ferry lay in the slip with her bow shoved up under the bridge, the steel ramp the cars drove aboard on, hung from two towers." I stopped to work out the machinery. It's a definition, not a scene.
- **Ned's walk to the stern.** "Ned took them aft with the light. At the stern the deck ran out low to the black water behind a chain..." By this point the bridge had already fallen, and walking aft to look at a chain felt like a second ending to that scene. The energy dropped here.

## Where I got confused

- **Bridge, ramp, tower.** In Chapter 1 it was the "bow ramp," and I understood that. Here it's the "bridge," which also has "towers," and the boat's bow sits "under" it. When the cable snaps and the bridge "folded onto the bow," I lost track of what was where. Then she throws off lines, "the boat moved under her," she throws a line to a post on the pier, Ned backs off and lays her alongside the pier. I couldn't picture where the boat ended up relative to the slip and the pier. I just read on and trusted that it worked out.
- **"The dragger."** Late in the chapter "the dragger lay in her deck lights." I had to go back to make sure this was the Gloucester boat. It's a new word for a boat I'd been given a different name for.
- **Why say "On the boat" out loud to Tess?** Beth says it with the marshal right beside her, Tess writes it on her hand, and it goes out on the net. Later Dane's whole reason for leaving the boat is that "the whole island's had her on this boat since the net." So why did either of them let her announce it? If it was a feint, the page didn't tell me.
- **"Who knew you were here?" / "You."** But Ned knew. She told him "Home. Don't tell anybody," two pages earlier. Either Beth is leaving Ned out on purpose (shielding him? not thinking of him?), or the book forgot. I couldn't tell which, and it muddies the ending a little. I also had to reread to confirm that Dane asks and Beth answers.
- **Monday vs. ten days.** The grand jury sits Monday, and it's "the last sitting before Pelletier walks." Then the ferry is out ten days. Does that mean Pelletier walks? Nobody says so. Dane's hand goes to his phone pocket, and I think that's meant to carry it. I wanted someone to say the cost out loud.
- **"Portland knows about your man with the heel now. They didn't this morning." He let her hear that.** Is he scolding her for leaving it out of her call, or hinting that information moves fast? I read it both ways.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Dane goes out on the front step to make a call.** This is the man whose last witness "is buried" in her own town. He has just heard a pickup with no lights idle under the house, and the back door is the obvious weak point. He leaves her alone on the floor and stands out front with a lit phone. He even says, "You can see me." I can see why the plot needs it, because it's the ending, and the ending half-acknowledges it. But it read as the character being moved by the plot. A careful man wouldn't do that.
- **The bridge collapse is very well timed.** A storm, sure. But it comes down the same night the witness is aboard, and the book doesn't yet tell me whether that was sabotage or weather. I'm holding it as a coincidence for now. If it stays one, I'll mind.
- **The fold of hundreds.** "Beth saw it. So did Dane." It's so plainly placed that it reads as a clue left out for me. Tess flashing cash at the moment the plot needs a suspect is a bit too handy.
- **Everyone has a comeback.** Tess: "Wrong's free, Marshal. Right goes on your tab." Ned: "Steel doesn't burn. Everything in it does." Ned: "Just the once." Dane: "I'd like to be wrong about it from a distance." Each line is good on its own. Together, a whole island where every person talks in finished, quotable lines, in the middle of an emergency, stops sounding like real people.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **"Said it to [an object]."** "Dane said it politely, to the chain." In Chapter 1 he said things to the barometer, to the lobster boat, and to the window. By now I notice it every time. The same goes for "politely": Chapter 1 has "Very polite," and this chapter has "as politely as he'd said anything all day" and "said it politely, to the chain."
- **The same gestures again.** His hand on his hip, again. His hand going to the phone pocket "and stayed there," again (Chapter 1 had "his hand stayed on the pocket"). "Wrong boots," again. Men "facing the hill," again (skiff, dragger). Ned turning back to give a nod or lift a hand, again. Some of this reads as deliberate echo, but there's too much of it.
- **The narrator explains Dane's tactics.** "He let her hear that." "He stood by the stove with his hands open and let her get there." The narration tells me he's managing her when the dialogue already shows it.
- **Scenes end on a snapped line.** "Boat." / "She was already on the gangway, the wheels grinding under her." / "Nobody who had been in this house made that sound." Every section ends on a button. The last one earns it. The others feel like they're there out of habit.
- **The callback is telegraphed.** "Nobody who's been in this house makes the noise you made first" is so obviously placed on page one that I knew it would come back. When it did, it was satisfying and neat at the same time, which takes some of the fear out of it.

## Listener pass (read as if hearing it aloud)

- **Boats and women both get "her," and the ear can't separate them.** "Dane's eyes went to her over Tess's shoulder and stayed." Heard aloud, "her" sounds like Tess or Beth. It's the Gloucester boat. Same with "Ned backed the North Reach off... and laid her alongside." On the page I can sort it out. By ear I can't.
- **The action geography gets lost faster by ear.** Bridge, tower, near tower, bow, pier, slip, post, cleat, gangway, all inside a page and a half. A listener drops it.
- **The final exchange has no tags.** "Who knew you were here?" / "You." Heard aloud, it's unclear who asks and who answers, and the line after it ("She saw it again...") has to rescue it.
- **What works aloud:** Dane's running commentary under the cable ("That's my knee by your hip, sorry about the knee... You're very good at lying down"). It's a distinct voice, funny and calming at once, and very easy to follow by ear. The "nothing, and nothing, and nothing" run in the mudroom also plays well aloud.
- **Tess's roll call, done by Beth in a whisper.** "Calloway, aboard. Marshal, aboard." [pause] "Allegedly." That pause carries by ear.

## What I want to know next

- **Did Dane's phone give her away?** Is it him, someone in Portland reading his texts, or his office (the reason the last witness died)? This is the big pull, and it's a real one.
- **Why did Beth say "You" and leave out Ned?** Is Ned in it? He sent her home in Chapter 1, he knew she'd be at the house, and he keeps giving her those goodbye looks.
- **Tess's cash.** Who's paying her?
- **The Gloucester dragger.** Is the heel man living on it? Who's aboard, and why are its lights on all night with nobody on deck?
- **The cable.** Was the bridge cut?
- **Monday.** Does she miss the grand jury? Does Pelletier walk? How do they get her off the island: a lobster boat, the boat he wanted to buy?
- **Beth and Dane.** The knee against hers, the laugh into the blanket, and now she might have to suspect him. I want to see how she treats him in the next scene.

## Prediction section

What I think happens, from the page alone:

1. The leak runs through Dane's side: his office, his partner, or whoever he texts. That's why his last witness died. Dane himself is clean, but Beth will doubt him for a stretch, maybe a chapter or two.
2. Ned is fine but gets hurt or is put in danger, probably on the boat, which is open at the stern and which everyone knows about. The repeated goodbye nods feel like a setup for that.
3. Tess's cash comes from Pelletier's people (fuel float at Marr's, lit at night, in Chapter 1), and Tess or her family is the island-side contact. Or it's a red herring and the money is innocent.
4. The heel man is working from the Gloucester dragger.
5. Beth gets herself across on a working boat by her own seamanship, maybe through Dane's "boat I can't be called off of" thread, in time for some sitting. Dane buys a boat on the island at the end.

If 1 and 4 both come true more or less as I've laid them out, the book is running on a familiar track. Right now I can see the track, but I'm still curious.

## Best moment

> The one after came hard off the water and shook the kitchen window in its frame, and in the mudroom nothing moved. That door had knocked in every northeast blow since she was born. Her father never fixed it, because he said it told him when the wind went round. It went quiet for one thing.

This is the best passage in the book so far. The danger arrives as a sound stopping. It comes from her father, from the house, and from her knowing it, and nobody else in the book could have noticed it. The scare is in "It went quiet for one thing."

Runner-up: "Calloway, aboard. Marshal, aboard." A small pause, exactly Tess's. "Allegedly." / "Does Tess know you do that?" / "Nobody does." It's the first time Beth is funny on purpose, it's private, and it's for him.
