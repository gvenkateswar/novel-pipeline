**Pull score (Ch 02): 3 of 5.** I'd keep reading, but more slowly than after Chapter One. Chapter One ended on a hook ("somebody had already given that man her name"). Chapter Two takes that hook back within two pages and gives me a storm in its place.

---

## Where I got bored

- **The boss phone call.** "The truck was clean when the troopers stopped it. Cole's cold storage on the Pruett wharf has been hosed down. All we have is a little powder in the cracks of one crate." This is a plot briefing read to me over a speakerphone by a man with no name. "Her eyes are the case" is a good line, but the rest is setup I'm being handed.
- **"The grand jury," Dane said to Beth, "is a room of ordinary people who decide whether there's enough for a trial."** That line is for me, not for Beth. She's an adult who lives in America. I drifted here.
- **The rope-doubling paragraph before the line snaps.** "Every rope that held the ferry to the pier got a second one beside it. Ned worked the posts up on the pier. Beth worked below him on the dock..." I could tell something was about to break, so I skimmed the setup to get to it.
- **The aftermath inventory.** "There was no way off now. No police boat. No ferry. No float. And the island's own boats were on the rocks." I had just watched all of that happen. Reading the list felt like being told what I'd seen.

## Where I got confused

- **The opening.** "a night that had included a man with a gun. The man with the gun had been the marshal." For a moment I thought the intruder had a gun. Chapter One gave him a flashlight. It reads like a trick, and it took me a beat to sort out.
- **The geography at the pier.** Pier, dock, "the dock below the pier," posts, the float, the stone. Then "Under them the dock... broke apart," and on the next page "The float was gone." Are the dock and the float the same thing? I couldn't picture where Beth was standing when the steel came at her.
- **"Dane! Get on the boat!"** Why is she telling him to get on the boat? Was it an order, a plea, or a plan? He jumps, and only afterwards do I work out that she wanted someone aboard to take a rope. In the moment it read as nonsense.
- **Russ's dialogue: "Saw her go. Where are you sleeping tonight? Who's asking? Me, for now. Somebody else will be, before dark."** Is he asking and answering his own questions? Did a line from Beth get dropped? I read it twice and I'm still not sure who says "Who's asking?"
- **The skiff from Pruett versus "he's from here."** In Chapter One Beth was sure the man was an islander, because "Nobody from off the island could find this cove in the dark." Now he came across from Pruett. Nobody mentions the contradiction. Is he an islander who lives in Pruett? Is she wrong? Nobody says.
- **Timing.** He crossed at half past ten and kicked the door in a little before three. What was he doing for four hours? Nobody asks.
- **"the scar in her palm."** It's new and unexplained. It reads like something I was supposed to know.
- **"He never once looked at the boat."** I don't know what this is telling me.
- **Dane's logic.** He says "Nobody had to tell anybody anything. I was wrong last night." A paragraph later he wants to report only to his boss and nobody else in the office, as if there's a leak after all. So which is it? The book seems to want both.

## Where I stopped believing

- **"Russ Whitcomb was a good man who was bad with money."** That is the author pointing at a suspect with a flashing arrow. Nobody in Beth's head thinks of a neighbor in a sentence like that, at that moment. It also repeats his full name in two straight sentences.
- **Russ's phone tip.** A man who minds the boats in Pruett calls the island constable at dawn about a skiff with no lights, and he can describe the passenger as a "skinny young fella with a bad leg." It's very convenient, and it shows up exactly when the plot needs Dane to un-say Chapter One's ending.
- **The stranding pile-up.** The police boat has ten minutes before "the sea closes the bay." Then a rope snaps, the dock breaks up, the ferry's steering goes, and every lobster boat in the harbor drags onto the rocks, all in one morning. I believe a storm. I don't believe a storm that ticks off every exit in order. It feels built to trap the two leads together.
- **Beth looks for Dane before Ned.** Ned has just hauled her out from under a hundred feet of steel and hurt himself doing it, and the text proudly says "Before her own hands. Before Ned. She looked for Dane." She never thanks him. It made me like her less, and the book doesn't seem to notice.
- **Banter under mortal danger.** "I'm at the back, on my knees, which wasn't the plan. Throw it." Then "I'm right here, Beth. I'll keep talking till you tell me to stop." This comes in the middle of holding a ferry off a pier with his bare hands. The second line especially sounds like a romance line pasted into an action beat.
- **Desire during the wreck.** The island's boats are rolling onto the rocks. Russ is standing there with his cap in his hand watching his livelihood go. And Beth's line is "She wanted him. She'd wanted him since the oar." The tone is off. Her neighbors are losing everything and the camera is on her crush.
- **"you're the one person there I'd trust with my life."** No one says that to their boss on speakerphone with a witness and a cop listening.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Desire is stated, not shown, over and over:**
  - "woke wanting the marshal"
  - "She wanted them on her. She thought it in exactly those words... and she didn't take it back."
  - "For one second she was glad. She knew exactly why, and it scared her."
  - "The first thing she looked for was Dane... She looked for Dane."
  - "She wanted him. She'd wanted him since the oar, and she was done calling it the cold."

  That's five announcements in one short chapter. The thumb on the bruise does the job better than all five of them put together.
- **The "X frightened her more than Y" tic.** Chapter One has "That frightened her more than anything." Chapter Two has "it scared her" and then ends on "That frightened her more than the steel had." It's the same construction, and the chapter closes on it as a neat button.
- **Explaining words the reader already has.** "the car deck, the open deck where the trucks park." "the island's constable, its one part-time police officer." "It had been the floating dock where every other boat tied up." Add the grand jury definition. It reads like a book for someone learning English.
- **Rhythm.** Short declarative after short declarative for most of the chapter. Chapter One had more variety. Here it starts to feel like a metronome.
- **Everyone quips the same way.** Dane, Beth, Ned and even Russ all speak in clipped, dry, half-joking lines. Ned's "It does what it likes" and "I don't go in" are good, but by the end I couldn't tell the voices apart by rhythm alone.

## What I want to know next

- Where do they sleep tonight? Clearly together, somewhere not her house. That's what the book wants me to want, and I do, a little.
- Is Russ dirty? The book has told me to suspect him so loudly that I half expect it's a misdirect, which isn't the same as being curious.
- Ned can't swim and won't wear a life jacket. That's a plant, and I'm waiting for him to go in the water. I'm dreading it, which is something.
- Where is the man with the bad leg now: Pruett, or on the island?
- What does Cole Varney do now that his truck came up clean?

These are real but weaker than the pull at the end of Chapter One. The leak question ("who gave him her name?") was the strongest thing pulling me forward, and this chapter mostly let the air out of it.

## Best moment

> Ned climbed aboard and went slowly up the steel stairs to the wheel. He put both hands on the wheel and turned it. It spun. It spun all the way round with nothing on the other end, and he let go and watched it turn.
>
> He slid the window open. "Snow's letting up a little," he called down. "She's not answering, Bethie."

No one explains anything here, and it lands harder than any line in the chapter. A close runner-up is the thumb on the bruise: "His arm was warm under her thumb. She left it there for one breath, then took her hand back."

## Listener pass (heard aloud)

- The boss's speech runs across two paragraphs with no tag between them. Read aloud, you lose who's talking.
- Russ's four-question line is close to unreadable aloud.
- "Russ Whitcomb... Russ Whitcomb" in back-to-back sentences sounds like a stutter.
