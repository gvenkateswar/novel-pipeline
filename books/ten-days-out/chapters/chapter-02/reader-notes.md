# Reader Notes: Chapter 2, "The Weather Hold"

**Drafts read:** `chapters/chapter-01/final.md` and `chapters/chapter-02/final.md` (v2 rebuild, Oct 29, 2026), in order. I opened nothing else.

---

## Pull score: 4 of 5 (about 7 of 10)

I would turn the page. The last third (the knocking door, the latch, the glow under his chin) is the strongest stretch in the book so far. The middle has two soft patches, the Tess stop and the walk down into the hull after the bridge falls, and the scene logic around "Boat" made me stop and reread. Nowhere did I think about putting it down.

Scene by scene:
- Back door and the skiff in the squall: 4
- Tess at the store, the storm net: 3
- Bridge cable and the collapse: 4 (it would be a 5 if I'd understood the bridge on first read)
- Ned in the hull, the stern chain, the gloves: 3
- The walk home in the dark: 4
- Kitchen floor, the door going quiet, the latch: 5

## Where I got bored

- **"She ran the storm net, the island's roll call on the radio every night of weather, so nobody's house went dark without somebody knowing why."** The narrator steps in to explain a thing to me. My attention dipped here. The whole Tess stop feels like it exists to hand me two clues, the net and the roll of hundreds, and then end. It's short, but I could feel it delivering clues.
- **"He handed Beth the flashlight and bent to the pump intake, his beard nearly in the water. 'Hold it there, Bethie. No, there.'"** The bridge has just fallen on the boat and we go below to hold a flashlight. "That's the bridge" / "Just the once" is a good exchange, but after it the scene goes slack, and then the stern-chain conversation repeats what I already knew: nobody can cross, it's lumpy.
- **The whispered house-by-house tour of the island.** "The house past the co-op was on candles... Russ was the *Second Wind*... Ida Dyer had her scanner on." Three new names in one paragraph, all people I've never met, and I skimmed them. "The two yellow windows at the bend in the road would have the pair of them married by Tuesday" pulled me back in. The rest was a roster.

## Where I got confused

- **"Dane."** Chapter 1 called him Reyes from start to finish. Chapter 2 opens with "Dane stood in the mudroom," and for half a second I thought someone new was in the house. Nothing on the page marks the switch. In her head they went to first names somewhere between the chapters, and I wasn't there for it.
- **The opening beat.** Chapter 1 ends with him heading to the back of the house with his hand on his gun. Chapter 2 opens with someone rattling the door from the mudroom. It took me a paragraph to work out that he'd gone outside and was coming back in. Maybe that's the intended jolt, but I didn't know where anyone was standing.
- **"Portland has your man with the heel now, which they didn't this morning."** I read "has" as "has in custody." Then the man turns up on the dragger, and I had to go back and reread it as "has been told about."
- **"Boat."** When she says it, I thought they would sleep on the ferry. Then they go home, and only Dane's line at the stern ("the whole island's got her aboard tonight, off the net. Nobody's looking at her house") told me "Boat" was a decoy. I still can't tell if it was a decoy from the start or a change of plan after the bridge damage. I had to back-read to rebuild the plan.
- **The bridge.** "The steel bridge the cars drove across from the shore... where it hung from its towers." I had to stop and build it in my head. When it said "the road off the island," for a second I pictured a causeway to the mainland, not the ferry's loading bridge. I got there eventually, but the most physical action in the chapter happened while I was still arranging the furniture.
- **"Beth let out all the air she had. Her shoulders came down from where they had been since the night freight."** Relief, while a bridge falls on her boat? I don't understand why this moment relaxes her. Maybe the waiting is over, but the page doesn't tell me.
- **"Every sea lifted the boat and shoved her bow up."** "She" and "her" mean the boat and Beth in the same paragraphs ("Ned backed her off the slip"). On the page I sort it out. By ear I mixed them up twice.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Nobody asks whether the bridge was sabotaged.** A cable lets go and the island's only loading bridge collapses on the witness's boat, the same day a man has been watching her house. Dane is a marshal who opened with "the last witness who knew her own town better than I did is buried in it," and he never wonders out loud. Neither does Beth, and neither does Ned. Either the book wants me to think it was just the storm, in which case one line saying so would help, or it's a clue nobody on the page has noticed, which makes them look slow.
- **Dane leaves her alone.** He sets the rule himself ("one way in from the land, a door that locks, and somebody awake behind it. I'll be the somebody"). Then, right after an unlit pickup idles under the house, he goes out front to use his phone with the back of the house open behind him. I saw why the plot needed it. I didn't believe this man would do it.
- **"Who knew you were here?" / "You."** Ned knew. She told him "Home. Don't tell anybody" a few pages earlier. Either Beth is lying to Dane to protect Ned, or she has forgotten, or the book is waving Ned at me. Whichever it is, her answer doesn't match what I just watched happen, and she shows no flicker of having left him out. If it's deliberate I want one beat of her choosing not to say his name. Without it, it reads like the author forgot.
- **No mention of getting her off the island another way.** "Any boat that would take you across before then" is the only escape anyone discusses. No helicopter, no Coast Guard cutter, no "the weather grounds everything." I'll accept it if they tell me, but nobody tells me.
- Smaller: the intruder runs from one word, "Evening." I bought it, since he lost surprise, but it is a soft scare.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Every scene ends on a placed image or a one-word button.** "Boat." / "the bell swung on its spring behind her." / "the dragger lay lit from end to end, as if nothing had happened." / "the kitchen door went open without a sound." / "She saw it again: the small glow under his chin." In chapter 1 too. Each is good on its own, but together they make a pattern I can feel coming.
- **"The boat was crippled and open at the stern, the storm was on, the truck could be waiting up the road, and the brick at her back and the gun on his hip were the best she had."** The narrator stops to sum up the danger for me. I already knew all of it.
- **Repeats inside two chapters:**
  - Dane's calming monologue "and it didn't stop" / "and he didn't stop" is used twice in this chapter (the cable, then the clock). Both run the same way: where I am, where you are, a dry joke, a promise. The second one is lovely ("it's still warm, which is good of it") but lands softer because I've already heard the pattern.
  - "Neither of them moved" / "neither of them let go" / "neither of them moved it" / "neither of them said anything." It closes every romantic touch and appears in both chapters.
  - "The way he always did" for Ned's turn-and-lift, in chapter 1 and again in chapter 2.
  - "Looked at it the way he looked at weather" (Ned at the crack), after chapter 1's "like a man looking at weather he meant to go out in" (Dane).
  - Tess's "free to X, two dollars to Y" bit: funny once in chapter 1, and on its second use it's already a tic.
  - "Lumpy" from Ned, now twice. That's probably intended as his signature, but it's close to the line.
  - "I'm told" from Dane: twice in chapter 1 ("I'm told it isn't my best one," "I'm told it's what I'm for").
- **The name motif is getting pushed.** Her name going down on a trooper's form, then the subpoena "spelled her right," now "Beth watched her own name dry on somebody else's skin." The third one is the best written, but I can feel the book underlining a theme.
- **The "and... and... and" rhythm** runs under every paragraph of action. Two chapters in, every scene has the same pulse whether it's a bridge falling or Ned handing her gloves.

To be fair: the two leads do not talk alike. Beth is clipped and literal ("Line." "She," "Other way. Unless you want to go with it."). Dane is wry and over-polite and says complete sentences. Ned and Tess each have their own sound too. That part does not read like a machine.

## Listener pass (heard, not seen)

- **Hard to follow by ear:** the Reyes-to-Dane switch at the top. The boat/Beth "her" in the bridge and slip passages. The bridge geometry. The roster of island names in the whisper scene.
- **Long single-sentence runs** go fine silently but leave a narrator out of breath: the bridge-coming-down paragraph, the walk home ("Sleet off the water in her face, rockweed under the snow, and the knee-high ledge below the church..."), and from chapter 1 the "Neither of them let go" paragraph.
- **Works beautifully aloud:** the knocking door. "A hollow knock, then nothing, then the knock" and "The door knocked" coming back as a refrain make you hear the gap when it stops. "Nothing, and nothing, and nothing." The fake net call, "Calloway, aboard. Marshal, aboard." / "Allegedly.", needs no tags. "Light." / "Still." Dane's one-word orders in the dark work well in audio.
- **A risk in audio:** *CALLOWAY — BOAT* written on Tess's hand only works if the narrator says it as writing. The italic radio call is fine.

## Romance check

- **What's there:** him flattening her under the bulwark, her fist closed in his collar the whole time. The blanket on the kitchen floor, knees touching. Her silent laugh into the blanket against his shoulder. His hand on her knee once before going back to the gun. And the best line of the romance so far: "The house that's talked about me most today." / "You. Since this morning." He's teasing her during a stakeout, and it's charming. It also tells me he's been paying attention to how she sees him.
- **What's missing:** chapter 1 gave me her wanting. She imagined him at the rail of a lobster boat, "getting his hands right on it," and made the coil too tight. Chapter 2 gives me contact but no thought. Not once does she think about him as a man. There's no "she noticed," no small lie she tells herself. The touches are all protection or comfort, and each one ends on "neither of them moved."
- **The ending helps the romance.** "Who knew you were here?" / "You." plus the glow under his chin puts real distrust between them, for the first time at the moment they've been closest. That's the right kind of trouble for a romance. I want to see her decide whether to trust him.
- **Heat level:** slow burn, closed door so far. Two chapters in, I believe they'll end up together, but I can't yet feel what she's afraid of losing by wanting him.

## Prediction: who I suspect, and why

**Who knew Beth was at home tonight, on the page:** Dane, Ned, and whoever is on the other end of Dane's phone.

1. **Ned: my main suspect, about 55–60%.** The page has given me more on him than on anyone:
   - Chapter 1: "Eleven years, and he had never once sent her home." He tries to send her home before the reefer is even chained, as if he knew what that load was.
   - "Ned kept his eyes on the black water" the whole crossing. Then he asks, "That crate of his come open, did it?" How did he know it was a crate? From the wheelhouse, maybe. Maybe.
   - He takes both bags, the fare bag and the store's with Tess's yellow tag, and won't let her run the store's up: "Go home. I'll lock up."
   - The fuel float at Marr's is lit "at an hour it never was," the same night.
   - Chapter 2: he's the only person she tells she'll be home. "Nobody but me and God" sounds like a line written to come back as irony.
   - Dane lays out the decoy plan in front of him at the stern.
   - The handheld on channel nine is the line to him: "You need me, nine. Like your dad." Her father lost his boat to the bank, and a man in a good coat wrote him down. Ned is tied up in her father's story somehow.
   - When Dane asks "Who knew?", Beth leaves him out. Either she can't see it, or she's protecting him.
   - The sympathy plants (the bad hip, can't swim and only she knows, the warm gloves every winter) read to me like the book making him lovable so it can hurt later.
2. **Tess: second, about 25%.** The fold of hundreds in her vest, gone "before the cap was on." "What's it cost to stay off it?" / "More than a marshal makes." She runs the net, so she knows where everyone sleeps. She's "brighter than the weather called for." Her light burns late after the store goes dark, and a flashlight goes east along the road to her house. Ned took her store bag off the boat in chapter 1. If Ned's dirty, I think Tess is his money end. But her net said BOAT, and the intruder came to the house. So either she isn't the leak tonight, or she got the truth from someone.
3. **Dane: the red herring, about 15%.** In chapter 1 he turns his screen away "as if he did it in his sleep" and types about her morning to "somebody she would never meet." The chapter ends by pointing straight at the glow under his chin. That's such a hard push in the last line that I don't believe it. I think his phone is the channel, maybe a leak in Portland or in the Marshals, and not Dane himself.

**The man with the dragged heel** is on the Gloucester dragger, the boat whose lights don't blink when the island's power goes. That clue was obvious, and I liked catching it. I assume he's who came through the mudroom, since the scuffs went down the steps "toward the harbor."

**What the page has given away:** a lot, about Ned. If the twist is Ned, I'm already halfway there after two chapters. If it's not Ned, the book has spent a lot of plants misdirecting me, and I'll want the real answer to be planted as carefully.

## Best moment

> "The mudroom door knocked, and the next gust went over the roof, and the door didn't.
>
> The one after came hard off the water and shook the kitchen window in its frame, and in the mudroom nothing moved. That door had knocked in every northeast blow since she was born. Her father never fixed it, because he said it told him when the wind went round. It went quiet for one thing."

The door gets set up as background noise for a whole scene, and then the silence becomes the threat. Her father is in it without being explained. I felt it before the sentence told me. Close second is the fake net call: "Calloway, aboard. Marshal, aboard." A small pause, exactly Tess's. "Allegedly."
