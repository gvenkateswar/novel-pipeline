# Judge: Craft persona, p3-A vs p3-B

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B, Tess shows up on the pier in person, writing names on the back of her hand, saying "Like the dog," with a roll of hundreds coming out of her vest. A gives me the same woman as a voice on a radio net. B's Dane is also funny when he's flat on top of Beth ("You're very good at lying down. Best thing you've done all day."), so for the first time the three of them sound like three different people.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 23%, the radio block: "the handheld on Beth's coat opened up, and she thumbed it off nine to the net. / *"Cutter storm net, this is Marr's, net control. Roll call by house. Calloway."*" Channel numbers, net control and roll call. That's the call-sign-and-procedure feel of A1. Then the definition at about 34% kept me skimming: "Over the bow hung the bridge: the steel ramp the cars drove down from the shore onto the *North Reach*, its sea end hung from two towers." The "Margaret Ann" coded call and the two clicks back are a real hook, and I came back for them. But I got to them through a list of radio procedure.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 49%, Ned's paperwork after the bridge comes down: "She's not carrying anybody till somebody from the Coast Guard has his hand on that crack. I'll call it in tonight, by the book." / "Ten days, if the state gets a barge out." It's a ferry-ops briefing right after the best physical beat in the chapter, and I drifted until "Steel doesn't burn." It's a dip, not a stop. B has one more small problem. Beth's "Allegedly" imitation of Tess's net works less well here, because B never lets us hear Tess on the air. In A it pays off.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The second half of the two files is almost word for word the same (the brick, the knocking door, the latch, "Who knew you were here?"), so the whole decision rests on the first 45%. A has lines I'd keep: "She stood there finding it," "She," Beth said, and "Your rope's under my hand. Line." But B puts people in front of me where A puts a radio and a machine.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same sentences, same rhythms, half the chapter shared verbatim. These are two drafts of one hand.
