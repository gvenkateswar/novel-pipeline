# Judge: p1, Buyer persona (opus)

Files: A = `blind/p1-A.md`, B = `blind/p1-B.md`. Also read `docs/calibration.md`.

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** In A he flirts with her while they're lying on the deck ("You're very good at lying down. Best thing you've done all day.") and she pictures him at sixty and lets herself want to be the one who sees it. That made me want them together. B gave me most of the same night with the jokes taken out.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 20%, the pier: "Below her the ferry lay in the slip with the bow shoved up under the bridge, the steel ramp the cars drove aboard on, hung from two towers at its seaward end." My thumb sped up through the ferry hardware and slowed again at "Like the dog." I skimmed it a second time at Ned's Coast Guard and barge talk (about 50%, "Ten days, if the state gets a barge out."). That part is two men and a boat, and the couple isn't the point.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 30%: "Beth went forward down the one lane of steel between the two trucks, the fire company's propane truck and the co-op's bait truck, chained aboard for the two o'clock that never ran." Right after it comes the paragraph explaining what the island calls the bridge. Two paragraphs of equipment in a row. I came back for the cable, and I liked "warm from his neck" and the line he says into her hair. But by then Tess had come and gone at the house without the pier scene's fun, and the "I'm watching it so you don't have to" monologue is sweet but plain.

5. **Confidence: medium.** From the blanket on, the two are almost word for word the same, so the choice comes down to the first 60%. B's "she wanted to put her fingers there" is the more direct line of wanting him, and some nights I'd take it.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** The whole back half, from the kitchen floor on, is the same text almost line for line.
