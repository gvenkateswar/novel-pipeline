# Judge: Craft persona (opus), p2-A vs p2-B

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A has the better ear all the way through ("trying to come back into her kitchen politely, and then less politely"; "She stood there finding it"). It also has the one moment in either chapter that made me sit up: Tess calling the dragger on the air "in the same bright voice, as if it were bread," and the two clicks that answer her. B swaps that for a wad of hundreds and a man saying out loud what it means.

3. **Where A lost me:** around 22%, at "she thumbed it off nine to the net. / *'Cutter storm net, this is Marr's, net control. Roll call by house. Calloway.'*" For a few lines it's radio procedure, and I started skimming. Tess's "Candles a dollar apiece" pulled me back right away. Dane's long reassurance speeches near the end ("I'd move you if I had anywhere better...") explain a bit more than they need to, but they didn't lose me.

4. **Where B lost me:** around 33%, at "Over the bow, the steel ramp the cars drove aboard on ran down from the shore onto the *North Reach*, hung at its seaward end from two towers. The island called it the bridge." It's a technical-drawing sentence, and it comes right after a list of trucks ("the fire company's propane truck and the co-op's bait truck"). Earlier, at about 5%, "she wanted to put her fingers there and find out whether it was as soft as it looked" says the longing out loud where A just shows her looking. Then "That was hundreds... Where does a store on an island take in hundreds in November?" states the clue for me.

5. **Confidence: medium.** From the boat scene on, the two are nearly word for word the same. The real difference is the opening scene. A also earns Beth's "Marshal, aboard. Allegedly." impression later because we actually heard Tess on the net. In B we never hear the net, so the impression doesn't land as hard.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** The second half is almost the same text, down to "nothing, and nothing, and nothing."
