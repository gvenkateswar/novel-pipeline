# Reader Notes: Chapter 09, "Car Deck, No Lights"

**Draft read:** `chapters/chapter-09/final.md` (3,047 words), read after `chapter-01/final.md` through `chapter-08/final.md`, in order. I read no other files.

**Where I came in:** By the end of Ch 08 I was sure Ned was the leak. The red seal, the timer key, the float question, "Not Ned" said three times, and "He didn't look back" all pointed the same way. So I came into the climax expecting it to confirm Ned and test whether Beth could act on it. That expectation shaped how this chapter read for me.

## Stop line

None. I would finish the book. But the main question I had been reading for, who the leak is, is answered here. What carries me into Ch 10 is the relationship and the wrap-up, not urgency. See the last section.

---

## Where I got bored

- **"His father, who drove a city bus for thirty years, said you could tell who'd make trouble by whether they had exact change."** This is a brand-new father, a brand-new theory, and a paragraph that ends "That proved nothing about either of them." In the middle of the climax I am being handed a clue the chapter itself throws away. My attention dropped here.
- **The Dane-lane recap.** "The first night the captain had stood at that stern... Steel doesn't burn, the captain had told him... This morning in the mess, when he gave her the seal line, she had looked at the heater grille, and he had filed that and not asked." Three callbacks in a row, each one explained. I remembered every one of them. The scene is walking me back through the book when I want it to move forward.
- **Ned's confession, from about the fourth "Ned said" onward.** Once it settled into one paragraph per plot point, I was reading to tick items off: the money, then Ashby, then the fish house, then the float, then tonight. Each answer is fine, and together they are a checklist.

## Where I got confused

- **Why does Beth kill the floods?** Her own plan was "Floods go, it's me," so I knew it was her. But she knows Dane has sat all night facing the floods, and the moment they go out he is blind and trips. Was she taking away the arsonist's light? Making the stern dark so she can't be seen as "one shape"? Whatever she meant, the effect is that she disables her own guard. It may be deliberate, but nothing on the page tells me what she was thinking when she threw the breakers, and her thinking is usually so precise.
- **Who is the radio call for?** She calls Tess on sixty-eight, which reads as a test of Tess, the suspect she wrote down in Ch 08. Then Dane says she told "everyone on this island with a radio." So was it a test of Tess, or bait for the whole island? I couldn't tell what result she was hoping for.
- **Why send Russ away?** Russ is the visible constable at the bollard. She hears Ned on nine from the store and sends Russ to "Get Tess," which leaves the pier empty just as someone boards from the water. I can partly reconstruct it (she suspects something is happening at the store), but it happens in one line and I had to stop and work it out.
- **Who is in Russ's truck?** "A man sat in the passenger seat with his hands behind him." Is it the boy out of the water, or Arsenault, who put Tess into the counter? If it's the boy, where is Arsenault? Where is the dragger? The chapter never says.
- **Where is Dane in the last scene?** He was kicked on his wounded arm twice and soaked in gasoline. Then the scene cuts to the store and he isn't there and isn't mentioned. For a romance, losing the hero at the climax with no word on whether he's hurt was strange.
- **How did Beth get from the deck to the store?** There's a hard cut from the line going taut to her hands on the counter. I accepted it, but I lost the pulling-in, the arrest, and Dane, which are the payoff moments.
- **"The score was zero for nine days."** What score? I went back and still don't know. Times he's made her laugh out loud? Times he's been right? It reads like a reference I was supposed to catch.
- **Tess and the safe.** "Behind it Beth heard the safe's dial turn." Is she getting the ledger for the police? The money? Hiding something? It's either a good loose thread or an unclear one. I can't tell which.

## Where I stopped believing

- **The binder chain across the lane.** In Ch 01 Beth won't let a binder sit a quarter turn loose, and she has been on this deck all evening. Then a chain is "left across the lane" exactly where Dane will trip in the dark. It's convenient, and it goes against the one thing the book has told me about her.
- **Dane joking while he's being kicked.** "He's on my left, he's heavy, he smells like a gas pump, and you'll want to remember I did what I was told, because it's a first." The book has sold me that he talks to calm her, and in Ch 02 that worked. But this comes right after a boot lands on his bullet wound, in the dark, in gasoline. The fear drained out of the scene because he plainly isn't afraid. And "I did what I was told, because it's a first" is the same joke as Ch 07's "First time all week I've done what you said when you said it."
- **"Jesus Christ, Beth... which I gather is how it's done out here."** A man who has just been kicked twice turns the climax into a wry tag line. I didn't buy that he'd have the breath for it.
- **The confession as a monologue.** Ned has just been unmasked in front of the woman he's loved for twenty-two years, with her lip split because of him, and the woman he raised. He talks in tidy, ordered, complete paragraphs. Nobody interrupts. Beth asks one question, "Tell me how," and then says nothing for the whole account. Real people would break in, especially Tess. The order is so neat that it read as the author clearing the board, not as a man breaking.
- **Dane is useless in every confrontation.** Swept by a sea in Ch 03, hidden behind the set in Ch 04, shot in Ch 07, blind and kicked here. I like that Beth wins it. But by now the marshal's gun has never once mattered, and I've started noticing it.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **The same joke shape for Dane, over and over.** Long, polite sentence, then a dry "which" clause at the end:
  - "which is a use of the place I hadn't considered"
  - "which makes it the first good seat I've had on this boat"
  - "which I want you to know is new for me on this boat"
  - "which I gather is how it's done out here"

  That's four in one chapter. In Ch 01 this was his voice. By Ch 09 it's a template.
- **Dialogue tags on everything.** "Beth said" and "Dane said" follow nearly every line in the first half ("They come for me, we catch one," Beth said / "And if they bring something we can't catch?" Dane said / "Look at it when it gets here," Beth said), even with only two people in the room. Earlier chapters left most lines untagged. This one reads like it was tagged by machine.
- **Nine paragraphs in a row ending "Ned said."** Consecutive paragraphs from the same speaker, each re-tagged. On the page it's mechanical. Aloud it's worse (see the listener pass).
- **Over-explained interiority in the Dane scene.** "It was the first call he had made about a person in four years and put his own name under." "...he had filed that and not asked." "...and here he sat on the steel between two trucks on her say-so." Each one tells me what the moment means when I already knew.
- **A neat closing line.** The last sentence lays out the irony in full: the boat is dark with no master aboard, and nobody in the store holds a master's ticket except the woman across from him. It lands, but it's tied off with a bow. Chs 05, 07 and 08 all ended on a tidy line too.
- **The scene that ends mid-word.** Russ's "mostly a—" is cut off by a scene break, not by an event. It felt like a writing trick, not something that happened on the boat.

## Prediction section

1. Ned is taken across in custody, maybe on the Coast Guard boat on Saturday, which "flies at first light."
2. Beth testifies Monday in Portland. Dane's notebook goes to the defense (Giglio), it says "No indication" every night, and it helps her.
3. The *North Reach* needs a master. Beth finally says "Put my name in." The hundred-ton license with the sharp corners gets used. I'm about 90% sure of this; the book has set it up hard.
4. Dane is disciplined or leaves the Service over reporting himself. He comes back for a working boat, maybe the Webbers Cove, maybe sternmanning, maybe crewing for Beth.
5. Tess says yes to Ned, or something devastating close to it, and waits for him.
6. Arsenault and the *Margaret Ann* are caught or slip away off the page. The book has already said Pelletier walks if the grand jury doesn't sit.

If Ch 10 does all of these, it'll feel like a list. If it skips the master's ticket, I'll feel cheated.

## Listener pass (reading it aloud / text-to-speech)

- **The "Ned said" run is the worst of it aloud.** Nine paragraphs with the same tag sound like a looping recording. A listener would hear "Ned said" more than they hear Ned.
- **"Beth said" / "Dane said" in the opening scenes.** Aloud, the tags thud on every line of the two-hander.
- **Dane's long sentences work aloud in quiet scenes and hurt in action.** "The gun's up and it's on your breathing, which I can hear from here, so I'd be grateful if you stood very still" is fine. The four-clause "I'm by the bait truck's back wheel..." speech, said while he's down on his wounded arm, makes the action stall.
- **The deck-crossing run-on works aloud** ("She crossed the black deck in her socks, the D-rings coming up under her soles and the gasoline running cold through the wool..."). The rhythm carries the panic.
- **Radio channels are easy to follow.** Nine is established as Ned and Beth's channel. Sixty-eight comes in once and is clear in context.
- **The scene-four switch back to Beth** after the Dane section is clear aloud only because of the timestamp ("At two minutes past eleven"). Without it a listener would lose track of whose head they're in. This is the first Dane POV scene in nine chapters, and aloud it's disorienting until his name settles it.
- **"The store bag had gone over wearing red, and the teller had written down red on Tess's bag."** I had to replay it to parse it. On the page I reread it twice.

## What I want to know next

- Is Dane hurt? He took two kicks on a bullet wound and then vanished from the page. This is the strongest pull I have, and it shouldn't have to be.
- What did Tess take out of the safe?
- Does Tess answer Ned? He asked her "Sober." I want her answer more than anything else in the store.
- Does Beth take the *North Reach*'s wheel?
- What happens to Dane for reporting himself, and does he stay?
- Where are Arsenault and the dragger?

To be plain: the question the book ran on, who on the island is selling her, is answered, and answered the way I'd guessed. What pulls me into Ch 10 is wrap-up curiosity and the romance, not dread. I'd read the last chapter, but because I want to see it end, not because I can't stop.

## Best moment

> "Bethie. Water side."
>
> She had the voice before the second word. Ned's.
>
> On the air Ned was another man. He used call signs and *standing by* and *one-six and one-three*, and he cancelled a run in the flat voice he'd have used to read the tide table to a stranger. Never once in her life had he put her name on the air.

In two words Ned betrays the people he's working for, warns her, and confesses that he's been inside it, and Beth hears all three at once. It also pays off the radio call from the first chapter without pointing at it. Runner-up: "I asked her tonight at supper instead. Sober." followed by the wrists held out across the counter.
