## Where I got bored

**The shape of the book, more than any one line.** This is the fourth time I've watched the same sequence: Beth tells her people where she'll be, the man with the dragging foot shows up, there's shooting, they escape, and then the two of them crouch somewhere and go over who could have known. Russ's house, the summer house, and now the beach all run this pattern. The net loft is the only stop where nobody came. When the boughs started dropping snow I sat up. Once the boat backed off, I knew what came next: they'd hide behind a rock and then argue over the list. And they did. "So it's one of your three, or it's one of mine" is the same conversation as chapter 6's "Three of the four were hers," with one more name added. The suspense has stopped growing and is just going round in a circle.

**The opening paragraph.** "At one o'clock he had climbed the walkway around the top of the lighthouse, and from up there he had found the one place the trees couldn't see: behind the rocks at this end." We already had all of this in chapter 8, with Beth looking down at the beach. Here it's repeated as background before the lantern scene can start.

**"Dane worked out how long the drive was from the Portland office to the Pruett ferry, and whether a man could ask to stay on in Maine..."** I drifted here. They're crouched behind a rock waiting to be shot at, and the book stops to plan their romance. Then "Does the ferry run all winter?" / "No reason." I saw that coming a page off, and it's the same cute dodge Dane has used before.

**"his eyes on the trees."** I stopped counting at about eight. "Watching the trees," "eyes went to the tree line," "laid his gun on the trees," "where he could see the trees," "opened them on the trees." By the end of the chapter it's background noise.

## Where I got confused

- **"Which one?"** I don't know who says the last line. The paragraph before it belongs to Dane ("Dane saw her do it and said nothing"), then there's a narrative line about the six days, then "Which one?" Is Dane asking Beth which of her people she was looking at? Is Beth asking herself? Is it general narration? The chapter's last line needs an owner, and I had to guess.
- **Ned's son.** "My boy in Portland's on that stuff." Ned has been on the page since chapter 1 as her father's best friend, and in eight chapters nobody has mentioned that he has a son, let alone an addicted one. It shows up at the exact moment Ned needs a motive. It doesn't feel like something being revealed. It feels like something being installed.
- **New Bedford.** "Two years ago in New Bedford, Cole's people burned down the house of a woman who'd testified. After." Same problem. This is the first I've heard of it, and it matters a lot. Did Dane know this the whole time? If so, why didn't he use it in chapter 2, when he was trying to get her on the boat?
- **State police or Marine Patrol?** The boss promised "a state police boat" in chapter 8. Here it's "the Marine Patrol boat." Small, but I noticed it.
- **The hiding spot.** The whole point of the tower climb was finding the one place the trees couldn't see. Then the shooter just moves along the tree line and shoots from directly above that same spot. I understood it, because Dane raised his arm and gave the place away, but the payoff of the tower plan felt like it vanished.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Ned's confession, right then.** He has a boat coming, a gunman possibly in the trees, and a marshal who just made him open his jacket at gunpoint, and he picks that moment to say yes to the job and also confess to two years of running drugs, in one tidy exchange. The job-ask beat ("You could have offered." / "You could have asked.") is earned and lovely. The confession on top of it is a plot delivery. "Then you'd better hear the rest, since you're going" does what the author needs, not what a frightened 64-year-old would do.
- **Ned clears himself by walking into the sea.** We're told over and over that he can't swim and won't wear a life jacket, so when he wades out chest-deep to stand in front of her, I saw the setup paying off from a long way back. It works as a gesture, but it lands exactly when it's needed. And it nearly takes him off the suspect list, which makes Beth's glance toward his lantern at the end feel forced, like the book wants him to still be a suspect.
- **Dane's one shot.** He's thigh-deep in a swell, aiming uphill at a muzzle flash in a tree line, and he hits with his only shot. Nine chapters of "he didn't fire" built toward this, and it still felt too easy.
- **The kid misses Beth again and hits the officer.** That's the fifth or sixth time he's missed her. He's an attack that always comes and never connects, and he's starting to feel harmless.
- **"No helicopter would come up through icing for a woman who wasn't dying."** This sentence is answering an objection the reader hasn't raised yet. You can feel the author checking off a box.

## Where it sounded like AI

- "Dane let her hold it. It was the last good thing that afternoon." This is a neat foreshadowing sentence placed right after the sweet beat. It tells me how to feel about what's coming.
- "He had been afraid for her that whole minute. Now she was shaking under him, alive, and he shut his eyes against her hair and was so glad it hurt." The scene already showed this, and then the narration explained it.
- "A man had no business working that out about a witness." / "That was no answer, and he knew it." / "Dane had no time to work out what that meant." That's three tidy little self-assessments by Dane in one chapter, and they all have the same rhythm.
- "he talked the whole way, low and steady." Dane has talked low and steady through every crisis since chapter 3. It's no longer a trait. It's a template.
- "where he had put her" / "Ned went where he was put." The same phrasing turns up twice in a few pages, and it echoes chapter 8's "You stand where I put you."
- Every chapter so far has ended on a short kicker line ("I know a place." / "In September I hadn't seen anything." / "And me." / "Which one?"). By the ninth one I can feel the formula coming.
- "Ned. He's sixty-four." Ned's age is repeated from chapter 2 as if the reader needed reminding.

## What I want to know next

I still have real questions, so I'd keep going. But I'm reading for the answer, not for the next scene.

- **Where was Russ?** He told Beth he wouldn't leave the harbor road, and he never came. The chapter never says his name, and that silence is the loudest thing in it. If the book wanted me to notice, it worked.
- **"And me."** The boss's line from chapter 8 still hangs there, and Dane finally puts his own side on the list. Is the boss dirty, or is that a red herring?
- **Is the kid dead in the woods?** There's a hand-sized patch of blood and a drag line heading east. Does he come back, or is someone else coming now?
- **Did Ned sell the hour?** He confessed to the drug runs but not to this, and then he walked into the sea for her. I'm half sure he's clean, and I suspect the book will make me wrong.
- **Six days with no boat.** How do they get through it, and where do they sleep now that every hiding place has been found?
- **"Ask me in five days."** That clock from chapter 8 is still running, and this chapter barely touched it.

The worry: I already know the next chapter will put them somewhere new, someone will learn about it, and the man will come. If the next chapter is that loop again, I'd skim.

## Best moment

The snow coming off the boughs:

> "Snow was coming off the spruce at the middle of the tree line. One bough dropped its load, then the next, then the next, a line of falling snow moving along the top of the beach toward the rocks, as fast as the boat.
>
> The next bough over let go, closer."

It's the best piece of suspense in the book so far. I could see it, it's specific to this place and weather, the villain is shown by the effect he has on the landscape, and nobody explains it. I held my breath.

Runner-up: "You could have offered." / "You could have asked." Two people who have dodged each other for years, in seven words.
