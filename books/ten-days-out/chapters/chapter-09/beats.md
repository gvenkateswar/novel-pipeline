## Flags (read before drafting)

1. **The log overrides the outline on where Beth stands.** The outline's s1 has Dane "move Beth to the waterline at the rocks' end" after the confession. In Ch 08 Dane already set the terms and Beth agreed: she stands at the rocks at the far end, out of sight of the trees, and he stays between her and the trees from the keeper's-house door to the boat. So she is at the rocks' end from the first line. After the confession, Dane's "move" is a tightening of his own plan: she goes down behind the rocks and stays out of sight until he says so, she goes into the water only on his word, and he puts Ned on the sand a step ahead of him and to his left, where he can see Ned's hands and the trees in one look. That is a decision he makes himself, not a concession.
2. **Ch 08 as written leaves two things out that the outline put there.** (a) The final text drops the boss's line about helicopters, so the helicopter is not yet ruled out on the page. Ch 09 rules it out in one line of Dane's thinking in s2 (World rule 11). (b) The final text drops "any time from noon to four." The only forecast on the page is Ned's ("It ought to lie down by tomorrow noon, or near it"). So the hour argument in s4 rests on something Dane saw for himself: the wind was down by one, and the man still waited in the trees for three. The writer must not quote "noon to four."
3. **Ch 08 calls it "a state police boat."** In Ch 09, say once that it is the Marine Patrol boat, the state police on the water, so a listener knows it is the same boat.
4. **Dane fires once at the beach.** The numbers ledger lists his shots by place (beach, pier, deck). The bible's line that a deputy takes the gun because "he fired it three times on the island" could mean three shots. One aimed shot that hits fits both readings, and it shows more skill than a burst. The kid fires three times. The orchestrator should confirm the ledger's meaning before Ch 14.
5. **The opening is not the outline's "Opens with" line, on purpose.** The caller and the Ch 08 reader both flagged the full-name-plus-action-plus-twist opening (eight chapters out of eight). The first sentence still names Dane (World rule 4). Two options are given in Scene 1.
6. **The kid learns which end she is at by watching the boat turn toward her.** That's a channel he can see, not the radio. Dane's Ch 08 terms ("Nothing about this tower, nothing about which end") mean all four sources gave the kid only the hour and the beach, so the mystery stays fair to all four. **Tess must never say on the radio where Beth is standing.** Her radio lines are about the water.
7. **New Bedford goes on the page in this chapter** (the bible requires it by Ch 09). Dane says it out loud to Beth in s2. It gets named again in Ch 13.
8. **Dane's swear.** This is the first of his two settled swears (World rule 13), in s3 when the officer is hit. It is the only one he uses this chapter.
9. **Scene lengths.** The chapter target is 2,070 (bible: 1,800 × 1.15). Four scenes, so most targets fall below the agent default of 700. The ambush is the chapter's climactic action and gets a floor. The outline's pacing flag 3 (s1 700, s2 1,100) is honored in spirit: confession plus the move run 1,000, and the ambush plus aftermath run 1,070.
10. **People cap.** No scene has more than three named people present. Tess is a voice on the boat's radio. The two Marine Patrol officers and the kid are unnamed by design (bible, Unnamed voices). They are named by role below, not as "a stranger."
11. **Outline lines checked against the contrast rule.** "If he sold the hour, they're in those trees now." and "So it's one of your three, or it's one of mine." both pass and are usable word for word. Every quoted line below passes too.
12. **Nothing in the confession reaches ahead to Ch 12.** Ned does not mention the warning call, the lawyer's call, or the prepaid phone. He confesses only to the cash and the night runs, and to his son (outline F11; World rule 12).

## Tics to keep out of this chapter (from the Ch 06–08 reader notes)

### Scene 1 — The lantern

**Setting:** The north beach at its far end, below the lighthouse, at half past two on Tuesday. Granite rocks run down into the sea at this end. Pale sand runs back to where the beach road comes down at the other end, and dark spruce stands along the top. The wind has dropped. The sky to the south is the color of slate. **Sensory anchor:** the swell sucking under the rocks, then going quiet, then coming again, slow now that the wind is down.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Ned.

**Point of view:** Dane (long interior sentences about people and how a thing will sound; short undercutting clause; in speech, longer sentences built on "because" and "which").

**Want:** Dane wants Beth on a boat in half an hour and off the island alive. In his way: a figure with a long pole coming out of the gloom, and then a confession that turns the one man Beth would never question into a risk. Beth wants to leave right: ask Ned for the job, say goodbye, owe nothing. Ned wants to set down what he's carried before she goes, and (unsaid, paid in Ch 12) to stand where the man in the trees would have to shoot him first.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. **Opening (first sentence names Dane; pick one or write an equal):**
   - A: *"A lantern came along the shore at half past two, low and swinging, and Dane had his gun on it before he could see whose hand it was in."*
   - B: *"'Somebody's on the rocks,' Beth said, and Dane already had his gun out."* (She hears it; he is already on it.)
2. One or two sentences, inside the moment, on what Dane has already done (not a recap): at one o'clock he watched the tree line from the tower walkway for forty minutes. From up there he found the stretch of sand the trees can't see, and that's where she stands. He brought her down by the shore rocks under the bank, never through the trees.
3. The figure comes clear: Ned in his green wool jacket, slow on the iced rock with his bad hip, a lantern in one hand and a boat hook in the other.
4. Beth: "It's Ned." Dane keeps the gun where it is and says, politely and slowly, to lean the boat hook on the rock and open the jacket. Beth objects in four words or fewer. Dane doesn't move the gun. Ned does as he's told. Dane watches Ned's hands before his face (his habit): big, red, empty, shaking a little.
5. **The laugh:** Ned: "Your marshal's got that thing on me, Bethie." Beth: "He had it on a seagull this morning." Dane: "The seagull knows what it did." He lowers the gun only after the jacket is open. (If a better line lands at the same target, the writer may swap it. Keep it one line from each, and Dane's short. Check the "your marshal" count against the audit; cap 3.)
6. Ned says why he brought the boat hook in his way, weather first, then the practical reason: to fend the boat off the rocks.
7. **The ask (paid from Ch 08):** Beth says it in a sentence with a verb in it: "I want your job in June, Ned. I'm asking for it." Ned answers with the weather first ("Sea's going down."), then: "It's yours. I've had the letter to the board written since the day you got your license. I was waiting on you to ask." Dane watches her face take it in. Give it two sentences of room; it's the last good thing.
8. Ned: "Then you'd better hear the rest, since you're going." **The confession,** his one short speech of the chapter (World rule 19), about 80–100 words, broken by one or two of Beth's short questions. He reports other men's words ("Cole said…"): for two years he took Cole's cash to run his trucks across at night and asked no questions. Cole said it was lobster for restaurants. He guessed what it was. His boy in Portland is on it, so Ned told himself it was lobster, because he couldn't stand it being the other thing. Beth: "That's why you said I didn't see it." Ned: "I didn't want you to have seen it." *(No warning call, no lawyer, no phone. See Flag 12.)*
9. **Beth's reaction at its true size (N15):** "Two kids, Ned. I taught one of them to row." She hits him in the chest with both fists, once. He takes it and doesn't step back. Then she goes still and silent (her fear and grief register).
10. Dane, through all of it, watches Ned's hands. They stay open. In his head, one fast, plain line of logic: a man who has taken Cole's money for two years has had three o'clock since yesterday afternoon. Then he says it out loud, to her face, with Ned standing there to hear it.

**Subtext:** Dane likes Ned and searched him anyway, and that has just turned out to be the right instinct, which gives him no pleasure. Beth got the yes she has wanted for three years in the same minute she lost the man who gave it. Ned is confessing to the smaller sin so he never has to confess the bigger one.

**Tension source:** situational at first (a figure with a pole, a gun on a friend), then interpersonal (Beth's verdict on Ned breaking in front of the man she's known four days), then situational again (the trees).

**Texture budget:** (1) The boat hook. It shows Ned knows how this landing will go better than anyone on the beach, and it gives Dane hands to watch. (2) Ned's jacket opened at gunpoint: the old green wool, nothing under it but a sweater. It shows Dane's competence, and that Ned has nothing to hide that a hand can find.

**Ends on:** *Dialogue.* Dane: **"If he sold the hour, they're in those trees now."** Stop there. No reply and no reaction line after it.

**Word target:** 550.

### Scene 2 — The boat comes in

**Setting:** The same rocks, from about twenty to three until three. Beth is down behind the biggest rock at the end, out of sight of the trees. The red of Tess's fire truck shows at the top of the beach road at the far end. The south sky is going from slate to black. **Sensory anchor:** first the engine, heard before the boat is seen. Then Tess's voice coming thin across the water from the boat's radio speaker as she talks it in.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Ned. Tess is a voice on the boat's radio. The two Marine Patrol officers are aboard, unnamed by design.

**Point of view:** Dane.

**Want:** Dane wants her in the boat without showing her to the trees for one second longer than he must. In his way: the boat can't come to the rocks, the only radio that can call the boat off is Tess's, up past the trees, and a doubt he has chosen to carry. Beth wants Monday to be the end of it, so she can come home. Dane can't give her that.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. Open in motion, no recap: Dane is already putting her down behind the rock with a hand on her shoulder. He tells her his reasons to her face (B2), in two or three plain lines. There's no signal on this beach, and the only radio is Tess's, at the top of the road past those trees. If he sends the boat away there won't be another before this next storm blows out, and the grand jury has to hear her Monday morning. **One line, in his head, rules out the helicopter (Flag 2):** no helicopter would come up through icing for a woman who wasn't dying, so it was this boat or nothing.
2. His tightened terms: she stays behind the rock until he says "Go," and on "Go" she goes straight into the water to the boat, never along the sand. Ned stands on the sand a step ahead of Dane and to his left, where Dane can see his hands and the trees in one look. Ned goes where he's put without a word.
3. **New Bedford (plant, Flag 7).** Beth, crouched and wet-eyed, holding on to something: "After Monday nobody needs me dead." Dane, low, to her face: "Two years ago in New Bedford, Cole's people burned down the house of a woman who'd testified. After." Beth, her flat verdict: "Then I'll keep a bucket by the stove." It isn't an answer, and he knows it.
4. **The longing, in Dane's interior** (a long "and" sentence ending short; no "he wanted"): he catches himself working out how long the drive is from the Portland office to the Pruett ferry, and whether a man could ask to stay in Maine, which isn't something a man works out about a witness. One more line can carry the September photo if it fits: somebody had her face on a wall in September, and he doesn't like that the boat will take her away from him and not from that.
5. The engine, then the boat, lifting and dropping in the swell off the middle of the beach, where the road comes down. Name it once as the Marine Patrol boat, the state police on the water (Flag 3). Tess's voice comes from its speaker, plain speech about the water only: a rock just under the surface off the point, keep it to your left, come in slow (Flag 6; no "over," no channel talk).
6. Dane steps out past the rock's edge and lifts one arm, so he shows himself and Beth stays hidden. The boat swings toward the rocks' end. In that moment Dane understands the cost of the signal: the boat itself is now pointing at her. He knows it and he doesn't call it back, because it's this boat or none.
7. The boat holds off the end in the swell, as close as the officer at the wheel dares. The second officer leans over the side, one arm out. Dane: "Go." Beth goes into the water. Dane wades in behind her to his thighs, between her and the trees, and his eyes go to the trees.
8. He sees the snow falling off the spruce at the middle of the tree line. One bough, then the one next to it, then the next, in a line, moving along the top of the beach toward the rocks' end, keeping pace with the boat.

**Subtext:** Dane is making his condemnable bet with his eyes open (bible: the secondary choice): he thinks he can cover her. New Bedford is his fear speaking early, and it plants the rupture. Beth's bucket line is her refusing to be afraid of anything after Monday, because she can't afford to be.

**Tension source:** situational (the boat has to come toward her to take her, and in doing that it shows the trees where she is) and internal (Dane chose this, and he can't take it back once the arm is up).

**Texture budget:** (1) The boat's radio speaker carrying Tess's voice over the water. It shows how the information travels (Tess talks the boat in) and puts Tess on the page without putting her near the shooter. (2) The snow sliding off the spruce in a moving line. It shows Dane reading the ambush before a shot is fired, and that the man in the trees didn't know which end.

**Ends on:** *Cut,* mid-action. The last line is the snow coming off the next bough over, closer. Nothing after it: no shout, no thought.

**Word target:** 450.

### Scene 3 — The ambush

**Setting:** The water off the rocks' end and the rocks themselves, at three. The water is about fifty degrees, waist-deep on Beth, thigh-deep on Dane. The light is failing under the storm cloud. **Sensory anchor:** the cold closing on his legs and the swell lifting Beth toward the boat and setting her back. Then the flat crack of the shots, smaller than he expected out over the water.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Ned. The two Marine Patrol officers. The kid in the trees, unseen, known by his muzzle flash and the snow.

**Point of view:** Dane. In fear he talks more, low and steady, in the present tense, **about the shooter and what she does next.** Beth goes silent, or two or three words at most. Short and medium sentences, cause before effect, so a listener can follow.

**Want:** Dane wants her out of the line of fire and the shooter down. In his way: waist-deep water, a swell that won't hold still, an officer's arm reaching across her, and Ned in places he can't predict.

**Surface action, in order (real time, beat by beat, no cutaway):**
1. Dane reads the moving line in the snow and acts before the first shot. He shouts to Beth to get down, and she drops to her chin in the water behind the swell. That shout is why the first shot misses her.
2. **First shot** from the trees: the officer leaning out for Beth's hand is hit in the shoulder and sits down hard on the deck. **Dane's swear here,** one word, aloud (the settled Ch 09 swear).
3. **Dane fires once** at the flash, aimed, braced in the swell, his gun dried by feel two nights ago (no comment needed). In the trees something lurches. The snow stops moving.
4. **Second shot** from the trees goes into the boat's side near the waterline; the officer at the wheel shouts that she's holed. **Third shot,** wild, slaps the water near Ned.
5. **Ned, who can't swim,** has come off the sand past Dane, empty-handed, into the sea to his chest, and stands between Beth and the trees. Dane's surprise, in one line of interior: he put Ned where he could watch his hands for a gun, and Ned walked into water he can't swim in with nothing in them, to stand in front of her (bible: Dane is still surprised; this keeps Ned unreadable).
6. Dane calls it, a decision nobody argues with. He waves the boat off: the officer is bleeding, the hull is holed, the swell is too high to get her up the side under fire. "Take him in. Go." He does not try a second time.
7. Dane gets Beth by the arm and Ned by the back of the jacket and drags them both through the swell and onto the rock, behind the biggest one at the end, talking the whole way about what he can see: where the man was, that he's hit or moving, that the snow up there has stopped. Not about himself.
8. **Romance beat:** behind the rock, his body over hers on the iced granite, his gun on the trees over her shoulder. When he's sure nothing is moving up there, he holds her while she shakes, from the cold and from all of it. One plain line of what he feels at its true size (fear, then the size of the relief) without the words "he wanted."
9. The boat backs off and turns away into the swell with the hurt officer aboard and water getting in. Tess's voice is still coming from its speaker, calling Beth's name, then "Calloway," and getting thinner as the boat goes.

**Subtext:** The rescue was Dane's call and it has just cost a man his shoulder. He did everything right and it still went wrong, which is the thing he fears most since Marisol (no name needed; never a monologue). Ned's act could be love or guilt, and Dane, the man who reads people for a living, can't tell which. Beth has stopped thinking and is just holding on.

**Tension source:** situational and physical: gunfire, cold water, the swell, a shot man. Under it is Dane's knowledge that he chose this.

**Texture budget:** (1) The swell timing: Beth rising and falling against the boat, and Dane bracing his shot between swells. It shows his skill under fire in plain words. (2) The bullet through the hull near the waterline. It's said once, plainly, because Ch 13 needs it ("laid up with a bullet through its hull").

**Ends on:** *Open image.* The boat going away with Tess's voice on its speaker getting smaller, until there's only the swell. This is the midpoint reversal landing, and the turn of the chapter. No spoken button after it.

**Word target:** 700. **Word floor:** 650 (the chapter's climactic action).

### Scene 4 — The last rock

**Setting:** First the rocks' end, then the tree line just above it, then a big split rock further along the north shore where the land turns, out of sight of the beach. About a quarter past three. The second storm is coming in black from the south, and the first hard snow is starting. **Sensory anchor:** Beth's wet clothes freezing stiff at the edges as they walk, and the crackle of it.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth, Ned (who leaves in the first beat).

**Point of view:** Dane.

**Want:** Dane wants her warm and moving and wants the truth said fairly, including about his own side. Beth wants it to be anybody. Neither of them wants to be the first to say the name.

**Surface action, in order:**
1. Dane sends Ned home along the shore toward the village and says why in one line, to Ned and Beth together (B2): "Go home along the shore, Ned. I won't leave her to walk you in, and the only other man on this island with a gun is on my list." Ned picks up his lantern and goes. Do **not** use the "Ned looks back" motif here (it pays in Ch 12 and Ch 16).
2. Beth gets them out the other way, the shore she knows: up the rocks at the far end into the edge of the trees, away from the beach road. She goes ahead of him and doesn't speak. Dane keeps his gun out and between her and the woods.
3. At the tree line just above the rocks' end, where the man had moved to, they find **blood on the snow** and a dragged line going off into the woods toward the east side. That's the heel, said once, in new words. Dane says it plainly: "I hit him." He doesn't follow the trail, and in one clause he thinks why: he won't leave her.
4. At the split rock, out of the wind, he checks his phone: no signal, so no report. Then the argument (F7), face to face and short:
   - Beth (plainer and bossier now): anybody could guess the north beach; it's the only sand on this end of the island.
   - Dane: the man knew three o'clock. The wind was down by one, and a boat could have come at one, and he waited in those trees for three.
   - Then Dane is fair about his own side, which is the competence the reader has been missing: the Marine Patrol had three o'clock, and his boss had it from them. One callback is allowed: in his head, "And me," his boss had said.
   - Dane: **"So it's one of your three, or it's one of mine."**
5. Neither says Ned's name. Show it in what they do, not in narration. She looks back down the shore where the lantern went, then away. He sees her do it and says nothing. One line can restate the clock inside the action: no boat now until this storm blows through, and Monday is six days off.

**Subtext:** Both of them are thinking of the man who just confessed to two years of Cole's cash and then waded into the sea for her. Dane keeps his boss on the list because he won't protect his side at her expense, and because he has no proof yet. Beth's silence about Ned is the last thing she can do for him.

**Tension source:** interpersonal and internal. The truth has to be said fairly by two people who both want it to point somewhere else. Behind it, situational: the storm, her wet clothes, a wounded man with a gun somewhere in the woods.

**Texture budget:** (1) The blood and the dragged line in the snow. It shows that Dane's shot landed, and that it was the kid. (2) The phone with no signal. It shows the boss will hear about the beach from the Marine Patrol before she hears from Dane, which sets up Ch 10's call from the Dyer house window.

**Ends on:** *Dialogue, a question.* Beth: **"Which one?"** He doesn't answer, and the chapter stops on her line. At most, one physical beat under ten words may follow (the snow coming in harder). No interior answer, no theme.

**Word target:** 370.
