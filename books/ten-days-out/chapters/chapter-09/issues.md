## Issues

**Issue 1**
- Problem: Day label ambiguity. Ned's line 4 says "Friday night" for the Day 1 call. The scene is Friday night (Day 8) after midnight, and Ned says "tonight" twice elsewhere. A listener hears the money call as tonight's, which also makes "You told me home, and I told you me and God" read as tonight. The label is factually right; the page can't carry it.
- Location: "Friday night he said they wanted to talk to you about the money," Ned said. "You told me home, and I told you me and God."
- Suggested fix: Change the opener to "The night the bridge came down he said they wanted to talk to you about the money." Dane's s3 already uses that marker, so it is set on the page. Leave "Wednesday" as is.

**Issue 2**
- Problem: The Day 0 line says "that first Thursday, when the unit came aboard dark and you'd gone up the hill." The unit came aboard at twenty to nine with Beth aboard (Ch 01 s1); she went home after the Pruett return, and Arsenault came at about 11:15 (CASE FACTS). The sentence merges the two and puts her on the hill when the unit loaded.
- Location: "That first Thursday, when the unit came aboard dark and you'd gone up the hill, Lyle Arsenault came aboard and told me what was under the lobsters."
- Suggested fix: "That first Thursday, after you'd gone up the hill, Lyle Arsenault came aboard and told me what was under the lobsters."

**Issue 3**
- Problem: The gasoline channel can't be followed, and Arsenault's whereabouts contradict themselves. Ned says Arsenault "said send the boy with the gas" and then "I let myself in the back and keyed nine," which reads as Ned on the phone with Arsenault. The next clause has Arsenault in the store hitting Tess ("Lyle put her into the counter for it"), and Ned keyed nine at the store's set at 11:02 with, apparently, Arsenault in the room. Bible World rule 11 requires the page to show one end of each channel. CASE FACTS has Ned on the back step hearing Arsenault send Doucette.
- Location: "Then he said send the boy with the gas. So I let myself in the back and keyed nine. Lyle put her into the counter for it."
- Suggested fix: Rewrite as: "Then I heard Lyle on his own phone out front say send the boy with the gas. So I let myself in the back and keyed nine, and when Tess came through the curtain Lyle put her into the counter." That keeps the back step, names who was where, and keeps Ned's "I heard"/"He said" register.

**Issue 4**
- Problem: "At dusk" for the skiff in s4 contradicts s1. The s1 skiff came after the bait call (an hour and more past dark, about half past five; sunset is about four). It idled at the open stern with its engine running.
- Location: "At dusk the skiff had come with its engine running, so she would hear it."
- Suggested fix: "Earlier the skiff had come with its engine running, so she would hear it."

**Issue 5**
- Problem: New Ned tells beyond the beats. The beats say no new tell, only a voice in s4 and the store in s6, and he looks at nobody's hands. The draft adds a gaze-away before he speaks and a breath before "Sober."
- Location: "Ned looked at the dark window first." / "He breathed out. \"Sober.\""
- Suggested fix: Cut both. "Dark window" is also wrong: the next paragraph says the front window shows Russ's truck with its dome light on. Both cuts count toward the trim.

**Issue 6**
- Problem: Dane's geography in s5 is inconsistent with s3. He sits at the bait truck's rear wheel in the lane between the trucks. In s5 he fouls "the chain stowed along the bulwark" and then says he is "by the bait truck's back wheel." The chain bins are along the bulwarks, not in the lane. The man pours "by the propane truck," a body's length from Dane's left shoulder, which is closer to Dane than the draft treats it.
- Location: "He had gone toward the pouring by its sound and fouled the chain stowed along the bulwark, and she heard him go down on the steel and the breath go out of him when the bad arm took it."
- Suggested fix: "fouled a binder chain left across the lane" (binders and D-rings are planted on the page in Ch 01), so he goes down at the bait truck's wheel and his own report is true.

**Issue 7**
- Problem: A narrator-explained callback inside the and-chain. The gut clause says "as she had kept clear of the gut since she was six" in narration. The banned list covers "the way she'd" callbacks and a narrator explaining a callback. The clause also says "kept clear of," which loses the Ch 05 physical rule (a stride inside the lip). "The gut" is a Ch 05 bar term and means nothing on a car deck unless explained.
- Location: "and she kept a stride inside where it dropped, as she had kept clear of the gut since she was six, and the lighter scratched again, nearer."
- Suggested fix: Keep the body's rule and cut the comparison: "and she kept a stride inside where it dropped, and the lighter scratched again, nearer." That pays Ch 05's "stride inside" physically; the orchestrator should log the gut plant as paid by this body rule (beats Flag 5's alternative), or accept the clause as the one allowed callback and rule it. There is no tide conflict either way: the stern edge has no tidal depth, and no depth word appears.

**Issue 8**
- Problem: Narrator nudge directly after a line of dialogue. It explains what "Get Tess" cost, restating s4. Banned: narrator explaining a callback. Writer-notes flag 8 already asks for the cut if it reads as a nudge, and it does.
- Location: "Her cop was halfway up the pier road with Tess's name in his ears."
- Suggested fix: Delete the sentence.

**Issue 9**
- Problem: The last sentence spends the Ch 10 line "Take her across tomorrow" in narration ("to take her across"). The beats list that phrase under Ch 10 and say the sentence should keep the boat with no master. It also says "nobody on the island to take her across", which is false against the cast: Kenny is on his way, and the inspector hasn't cleared her.
- Location: "while down the hill the *North Reach* lay dark at the stone pier with no master aboard her, and nobody on the island to take her across but the woman standing across from him."
- Suggested fix: "while down at the pier the *North Reach* lay dark with no master aboard her and nobody on the island who held a master's ticket but the woman standing across from him." (Beats flag: "master's ticket" is not the word "license.")

**Issue 10**

**Issue 12**

**Issue 13**

**Issue 16**

The chain of "socks" count then reads s3, s4, s5, which breaks the cap (two); so say "in her stocking feet" once in s4, or leave it as boots on and add "She pulled her boots back on" in s3's last paragraph. **Issue 17**
