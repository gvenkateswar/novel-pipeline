# Chapter 09 — Issues (continuity check on draft.md, 2,224 words)

**Issue 1**
- Problem: Dane says his boss got the hour from the Marine Patrol. In Ch 08 Dane gave his boss the hour himself ("He called his boss back himself. 'Three o'clock, the north beach...'"), and the boss said "And me." The boss had the hour from Dane, not from the Marine Patrol. This breaks the fair-play list of who knew the hour and contradicts the callback on the next line.
- Location: "The Marine Patrol had three o'clock too," Dane said, "and my boss had it from them."
- Suggested fix: Change to "The Marine Patrol had three o'clock too," Dane said, "and my boss had it from me, and she's the one who gave it to them." Then the callback ("And me, his boss had said") agrees with the Ch 08 call.

**Issue 2**
- Problem: Boats are called "she" or "her" three times: twice in dialogue and once in narration. Dane's own narration should use "it" or "the boat".
- Location: "Somebody'll need to push that boat off them with a pole, or she'll come in and stay." / "He's put one through her side! We're taking water!" / "the boat had a hole in her"
- Suggested fix: Ned: "...or it'll come in and stay." Officer: "He's put one through the side! We're taking water!" Narration: "the boat had a hole in it".

**Issue 3**
- Problem: Dane's reason for sending Ned home alone is wrong and unclear on the page. The kid is also a man on the island with a gun, and he is wounded in the woods nearby. The sentence also implies Russ is the danger, but the stated worry is about walking Ned in. A listener cannot follow the logic. Ned (on Dane's list) is also sent off alone with no reason given.
- Location: "I won't leave her to walk you in, and the only other man on this island with a gun is on my list."
- Suggested fix: "Go home along the shore, Ned. I won't leave her to walk you in, and I won't take you with us." Or say the list plainly: "...and you're on my list with the rest of them."

**Issue 4**
- Problem: Ned says the night runs happened "when there was nobody aboard but me". But Beth is the deckhand and was aboard on the Thursday night crossing in Ch 01 (Cole's six years of Thursdays). Her only reaction is "You knew." She would ask how he got a truck across her deck without her seeing it.
- Location: "He said he had lobster for restaurants that had to cross at night, when there was nobody aboard but me."
- Suggested fix: Make the runs extra crossings after her shift: "when I took her over alone after you'd gone home." Or have Beth answer in four words: "I worked those Thursdays."

**Issue 5**
- Problem: The beach is placed in two incompatible ways. Section 1 says Dane put Beth on "the one stretch of sand the trees couldn't see". Ch 08's terms, and Scene 2 itself, put her behind the rocks at the far end ("behind the biggest rock at the end"; "You don't set foot on the sand"). The shooter's flash then comes from the trees "right above the rocks," so the trees can see that end. A listener loses where she stands.
- Location: "from up there he had found the one stretch of sand the trees couldn't see. He had brought her down to it along the shore rocks."
- Suggested fix: "from up there he had found the one place on the beach the trees couldn't see, behind the rocks at the far end. He had brought her down to it along the shore."

**Issue 6**
- Problem: Head-hop. The POV is Dane. "She had wanted it for three years" is Beth's interior and a fact Dane was never told. Ch 08 gave "eleven years" and "never asked", not three years.
- Location: "She had wanted it for three years, and now she had it, and for a second she didn't know what to do with her hands."
- Suggested fix: "Beth's face went red under the wind-burn, and for a second she didn't seem to know what to do with her hands."

**Issue 7**
- Problem: The lantern is said to have gone out "in the wet", but Ned set it down on the rock before he ever went near the water. It was never wet. It also was never picked up on the page between the setting-down and Ned's exit.
- Location: "Ned picked up his lantern and went. It had gone out in the wet, and he carried it anyway."
- Suggested fix: "Ned picked up his lantern, which had blown out on the rock, and went."

**Issue 8**
- Problem: Dane explains to Beth what both of them already know: no signal, Tess's radio, and the Monday grand jury. Ch 08 settled all three on the tower, with Beth arguing for Tess herself. This is the "character explains something both know" pattern. The restated facts do not change her choice.
- Location: "There's no signal on this beach," he said, low, to her face, "and the only radio is Tess's, at the top of the road past those trees. If I send this boat away, there won't be another one before this next storm blows out, and the grand jury has to hear you Monday morning."
- Suggested fix: Cut the signal and radio clauses. Keep only the new fact, as a flat refusal: "If I send this boat away there won't be another before the next storm blows out." The helicopter line in his head already carries the rest.

**Issue 9**
- Problem: Superlative tic, which the beat sheet banned ("never in her life" family). Marisol makes "ever been for anyone" contradict Dane's backstory. The plain naming of fear is allowed, but the superlative is not true to him.
- Location: "He had been more afraid for her for the length of that one minute than he had ever been for anyone, and now she was shaking under him, alive"
- Suggested fix: "He had never been so afraid in one minute, and now she was shaking under him, alive" is still a superlative. Better: "He had been afraid for her for the length of that one minute, and now she was shaking under him, alive, and the relief was so big he shut his eyes against her hair before he opened them again on the trees."

**Issue 10**
- Problem: Redundant stillness beats in a row. Beth "stood very still" and then "went still" two paragraphs later, and the second adds that she says nothing more (already shown by silence). It also doubles her reaction to the same line.
- Location: "Beth stood very still. Then, quietly:" / "Then she went still, and she said nothing more."
- Suggested fix: Cut "Then she went still, and she said nothing more." The fists and Ned not stepping back are the end of the beat; her silence in the next scene shows it.

**Issue 11**
- Problem: Motif cap. "Your marshal" is capped at 3 for the whole book. It has been used in Ch 05 (Tess) and is used here. That leaves one use for Ch 10–18. This is not a violation yet, but it is the budget. Ned's bible voice uses it as his name for Dane, so it will be wanted again.
- Location: "Your marshal's got that thing on me, Bethie."
- Suggested fix: Keep it (the bible assigns it to Ned), but note in the continuity log that one use remains. Do not add a second in this chapter.
