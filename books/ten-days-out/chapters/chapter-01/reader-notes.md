# Reader notes: Chapter 1, "Night Freight"

**Draft read:** `chapters/chapter-01/final.md` (v2 rebuild, Oct 29, 2026). I read nothing else.

**Pull score: 7 / 10.** I'd keep going. The first scene pulled me straight in. The second scene, with the jump, kept me. My attention sagged in the last scene, the kitchen. By the end I wanted to see the next chapter, but I wasn't hungry for it.

---

## Where I got bored

- **The walk aft (para 25).** "Her hand went along the stern chain across the transom, and she kicked the ice off the ring buoy's bracket on the way by, and the quarter ladder's rungs went past her knee, black and wet. Then the outside ladder... Her shoulder brushed the breaker box on the aft bulkhead..." She has just seen bricks of drugs while two men stare at her. This is the tensest moment in the chapter, and the prose turns into a tour of the deck hardware. I skimmed it. It reads like objects being set out because they'll matter later. As a reader I noticed that, and the noticing put me outside the scene.
- **The kitchen banter.** "My mother would like this kitchen... I'm making no comment about the counter." / "Counter's clean." / "It is. I said nothing." The jokes are pleasant and do nothing. By this point the threat from the night before has gone quiet, and the scene is a man at a window and a woman packing a bag to make a point. This is where I'd have put the book down for the night.
- **The barometer bet.** "Dollar says it runs." / "I don't bet on what I can see." This is a second round of the same "she's right, he's from away" joke the boots gag already did better.

## Where I got confused

- **Ned's "Go home."** He tells her to go home *before* the crate tips open. Does he already know what's in the reefer? I think the book means me to wonder. But then he asks "That crate of his come open, did it?" and seems to know that too, and she answers "Strapped it," and the book drops it. I couldn't tell whether she suspects him. She doesn't seem to think about it at all, and that confused me more than the hint did.
- **Geography after the night run.** "Ned met her at the foot of the outside ladder with both bags... He went up the ladder slow on the bad hip. At the top he turned and lifted the hand with the bags in it." Up the ladder to where, with the bags? Back to the wheelhouse? I couldn't picture it. "Tess's yellow tag bright on the one in the floods": I read this twice and still wasn't sure which bag was which, or why it mattered.
- **"went off the ramp onto her island before Ned brought it up."** I had to stop and work out that the hooded man stayed on Cutter while the ferry left. It's important (the threat is now loose on her island), and it's buried at the end of a sentence about a heel drag.
- **She never tells Reyes about the hooded man.** She told the trooper about the crate and the tape. By the end of the chapter she hasn't told the marshal that a man who saw her see the drugs walked off onto her island with a limp. She is scanning the slip for that heel drag *while standing next to him*. Is she keeping it from him on purpose? If so, why? If the book means something by it, it's too quiet. If not, it's a hole.
- **Names with nothing behind them:** Kenny, Russ and the *Second Wind*, Marr's fuel float. Marr's float "lit, at an hour it never was" is clearly meant to make me suspicious, but I don't know who Marr is, so it landed as nothing.
- **The subpoena moment.** "He'd had it on him the whole time... and he had let her haul him aboard first." I can feel this is meant to mean something about him: that he chose not to serve her on the ramp, or that he didn't use it as leverage. I'm not sure which. And what does a subpoena get him that the car didn't? "I can't put you in the car. This I can do." I took that to mean he's making her a witness so she's officially his to look after, but I was guessing.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Speed.** The plate is called in past midnight. The driver is in a Portland cell before dawn. By 7:40 a federal marshal is on the ramp in Pruett with a typed subpoena carrying her full legal name. That's a very fast overnight for a court. I went with it, but I felt the machinery working.
- **The dark reefer.** Smugglers carrying live lobsters switch the cooling unit off, so the one tell anybody on the boat would notice is sitting right there? I can buy laziness, but the book doesn't give me a reason. It's the first clue, and it's handed over a little too easily.
- **Reyes's patter.** "That's a good point about the certificate, and I'm going to walk around it. Okay. Here's how this goes." "Your captain's toasting me. I'm choosing to take that as support." "For the record, I jumped. I'd like that on the record." He's charming, but it's a familiar kind of charm: the wry, self-mocking TV lawman. I've met him before. He comes alive when he stops performing ("I want a boat I can't be called off of").
- **"The last witness who knew her own town better than I did is buried in it."** It's a good line, and that's the trouble. It sounds like it was written for a trailer. And the narration props it up: "every word set down like a box he didn't want to drop." I didn't need to be told it was heavy.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Every scene ends on a neat button.** "You're welcome." / "I didn't say anything." / "I noticed." Then "...in her bib pocket, where it would get wet." Then "There goes my boat." / "It was never your boat." Three scenes in a row close on a tidy line, two of them as two-line comebacks. I saw the pattern by the third.
- **The romance is spelled out right after it was shown.** The wrist paragraph (125) already did the work. Then: "She had him, and she'd had him for a while now. Somewhere in there it had turned into two people on a cold deck holding each other by the clothes, and she couldn't have said which of them was supposed to move first." That's the narrator explaining the moment I'd just felt.
- **Watching hands, over and over.** He looks at her hands before her face, watches the pouch leave her hands, "He was watching her hands again," "every so often he turned his head to watch her hands." It's a deliberate motif, and by the fourth time I could see the deliberateness.
- **The phone-turned-away beat happens twice,** written almost the same way both times ("turned the screen away from her with his thumb" / "turned the screen from her"). The second time felt copy-pasted, not like a development.
- **Some similes and aphorisms reach for effect:** "smelled like a decision somebody had made about herring in August," "the way he billed, generously and with gaps," "as quiet as a hearse." The first two made me smile. Together with the rest, the voice sometimes sounds like it's showing off how good it is.
- **Tess, Reyes and Beth all speak in clipped, dry one-liners.** Tess's "Then you'll be the first man off that boat who did" could have been said by Beth. Reyes is wordier, but all three have the same dry snap.

## What I want to know next

- Is Ned in on it? He sent her home, he knew about the crate, he gave the blast that left the gap. That's my strongest pull.
- Who on Cutter drags a left heel? It's someone she'll pass at the store, and I want to see her recognize him.
- What's on Reyes's phone that he keeps hiding? Is he being pulled off, or warned?
- What happened to the witness who's "buried in it"? Was that his fault?
- Who is Marr, and why was the float lit?
- The ferry's cancelled, so the marshal is stuck on the island with her and the hooded man. The setup is clear, but the chapter ends on a quip, not a threat, so I'm curious more than worried.

## Best moment

> "Hundred and ninety. The place on Route One that sells fudge."
>
> He looked down at his feet as if they'd told on him, and said nothing at all.

A close second, and the emotional high point:

> Somebody had sat at a desk in a warm room and spelled her right. She folded it once along the crease he'd made, and put it in her bib pocket, where it would get wet.

That line echoes the man in a good coat who "wrote her father down" in blue pen, and it hit me without being explained.

## Prediction section

What I expect, as a reader, from the page alone:

1. Ned is involved, or at least knows and is protecting her by keeping her out of it. I'd guess the second, and that the book reveals the first.
2. The hooded man with the dragged left heel is an islander she knows. She'll spot him in the next couple of chapters.
3. The storm (the barometer, "storm Friday," the cancelled boat) strands Reyes on Cutter for days. The title suggests ten.
4. Marr's fuel float is a second link in the smuggling chain.
5. Reyes's hidden messages are about him being taken off the case, or about the earlier dead witness. I'd bet on a reprimand.
6. The boat thread pays off at the end: he buys a working boat, maybe her father's or one like it, and stays.
7. Her father's lost boat and the bank are connected to the smugglers somehow.

If most of these come true in that order, the book will feel predictable. Numbers 1, 3 and 6 feel almost guaranteed to me already.

## Listener pass

How this would play read aloud (audiobook or text-to-speech):

- **The long "and... and... and" sentences** are a mixed bag. The wrist paragraph (125) works as one breathless run aloud; it could be the best-sounding passage in the chapter. The walk-aft paragraph (25) is the same structure filled with gear, and aloud it would be where I zoned out.
- **The ferry jargon blurs by ear.** Binders, D-ring, reefer, dolphin, transom, quarter ladder, "one-six and one-three." On the page I can skim past it. Heard, "a gust set them toward the dolphin" means nothing to a non-boater and costs a beat.
- **Passing names vanish by ear.** Kenny, Russ, the *Second Wind*, Marr's, Tess's yellow tag. A listener can't glance back, so these become noise.
- **Who's speaking** holds up in the two-person scenes. The short lines ("To who?" / "To me.") are clear. In the three-way slip scene, "Fair. I'll have exact change" lands as Reyes only because of the context. A narrator would need to make the voices distinct.
- **The VHF call in italics** would sound good aloud. It's a real change of voice, and the chapter needs more moments like it.
- **"Bethie."** The nickname on the first line of dialogue reads warm, and by ear it does a lot of quick work on the Ned relationship.

## Romance check

- **Is there attraction on the page?** Yes. The catch on the ramp is a real meet. It's physical and a little funny, and the wrist detail carries it. Her picturing him at the rail of a lobster boat "getting his hands right on it," and then making "the next coil too tight," is the best longing beat. It's specific and wants something without saying so.
- **Is his interest visible?** Mostly through watching her, and it's handled by repetition, not escalation. He doesn't do anything for her except hand her the subpoena. That handover is the one moment where his care shows, and it's good.
- **Does it feel earned for a first chapter?** The pull between them, yes. The narration naming it ("two people on a cold deck holding each other by the clothes") jumps ahead of what I'd been given.
- **Heat level so far:** clean. Hands, wrists, breath on her knuckles. Nothing past that, and nothing that needs to be.
- **Will I root for them?** I like her a lot. I like him when he's quiet and less when he's performing. Right now I'm rooting for her more than for them as a couple.
- **Is there friction that isn't just the plot?** Some: island versus "from away," her father, the boat he wants. It's thin so far. Their disagreement in this chapter is about the case, not about who they are.
