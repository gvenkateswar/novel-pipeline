# Judge: p3, Craft persona, Opus

Files read: blind/p3-A.md, blind/p3-B.md, docs/calibration.md.

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A's narration surprises me sentence by sentence. The plumber "chained the way he billed, generously and with gaps", the bait truck smells "like a decision somebody had made about herring in August", Ned once sent her "for a bucket of steam", and she hauls Reyes in "the way you'd have a fender or a dog gone overboard, by the scruff". B tells the same story more cleanly and more plainly. B does have the better back-and-forth ("Your captain's toasting me," "He's freight. Sign here."), but that isn't enough to make up the gap.

3. **Where A lost me:** about 37%: "Other people's voices were a thing she did, alone, at the sink, on the dark end of the night run. Tess on the storm net. Russ saying 'somebody.'" The action stops so I can be told about a character trait, and it reads like a note from the bible pasted into the scene. A smaller slip comes later at about 74% in the boat-census paragraph ("Everybody she knew sat somewhere in a boat. A man with no boat was from away, and from away was weather..."). That one is good, but it runs a beat too long.

4. **Where B lost me:** about 80%: "She could check a binder with her hands, and a bow line, and the weather. She had no way to check paper." Here the book explains its own metaphor to me. A trusts the subpoena to land with nothing but "All of it, the Ann too." B also sags at about 73% in the same boat paragraph, which ends with "to see if he'd fit. / He didn't." That's tidy, and it's flatter than A's coil set down "harder than it needed."

5. **Confidence: medium.** These are two drafts of one skeleton. B's Reyes sounds more distinct from Beth than A's does ("I have an app"; "on a recorded line, which I listened to in the car on the way up, twice"), so a reader who cares mostly about dialogue could go the other way. Neither one reads like A1. There are no codes or ledgers, and the couple is physically together and fighting by the second scene.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. It's the same beats, the same lines ("Those boots are for a different ocean", "It was never your boat") and the same cadence, with A pushing the figurative voice harder.
