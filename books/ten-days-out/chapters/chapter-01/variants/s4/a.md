Tess was on the slip with the store's handcart, and she looked at the marshal's boots before his face.

"Calloway. Heard you had a man on the eight. Ferry's quicker than the internet."

"He's freight. Sign here."

Tess signed. "Coffee's free to islanders, Marshal. Two dollars to you." She wheeled her freight off up the store dock.

Beth watched every man on the slip walk. Not one of them dragged a heel.

"Who are we looking for?"

"Nobody." She shoved the line bag at him. "Take that."

She had given the trooper the crate and the tape and the plate and left the man out, and saying so now meant saying it to a man who wrote things down.

At Marr's dock one set of prints went down the planks through the slush to the fuel float, a line scored beside every left one, and stopped at the float's edge where a boat had lain alongside.

"Man off the reefer. Hood. Drags his left heel."

"He went back with the truck."

"Walked off before the ramp came up."

He looked at the line, then at her. "That wasn't in your call."

"No."

He didn't ask why. He came round to her water side, between her and the harbor, and started talking, low and level. "Okay. He's not on the dock. Somebody took him off again, or dropped him and went. There's nobody on the road behind us, which I'd like to keep true. Which boat?"

She looked over the moorings, the lobster boats, the dragger in off the weather.

"Any of them."

Up the road his phone buzzed. He read it and typed back with both thumbs as he walked, and she watched him put the limp down for somebody she would never see.

She took him in by the front door.

In the kitchen she pinned the subpoena under the barometer and tapped the glass. The needle dropped.

"It won't run. Pack if you want. You'll be unpacking by two."

"Dollar says it runs." He wanted to win that one.

"I don't bet on what I can see."

He went to the window. Below, a skiff lay off the fuel float on its painter, one man in it, hood up, oars shipped, not fueling, not fishing. He sat facing the hill. Sitting, he had no walk to show her.

"I see him," Dane said. "He's not doing anything, which is what I'd do if I wanted to know which house was yours." He put one arm back and moved her a step from the glass, then took it away. "Stay behind me."

She stayed. He went on talking, low, and she answered none of it.

At a quarter past one the handheld on the counter, which she'd turned to sixteen, cleared its throat.

*"All stations, all stations, this is the ferry North Reach. The fourteen hundred departure is cancelled. Next departure to be announced. North Reach standing by one-six and one-three."*

"There goes my boat." He said it low, to the window. Nobody answered.

She looked past his shoulder. The skiff lay tied to the float, empty. Nobody on the store dock.

He was already going, past her and through the house toward the back.
