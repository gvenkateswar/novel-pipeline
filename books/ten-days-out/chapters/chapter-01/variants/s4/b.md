Tess was on the slip with the store's handcart, waiting on her freight. She looked at the marshal's boots before she looked at his face.

"Calloway. Heard you had a man on the eight. Ferry's quicker than the internet."

"He's freight. Sign here."

Tess signed. "Coffee's on the urn, Marshal. Free to islanders. Two dollars to you." She wheeled the cart off up the store dock.

Beth watched every man on the slip walk. Not one of them dragged a heel.

The Harbor Road ran past the head of Marr's dock. In the slush on the planks one set of prints went down to the fuel float, and beside every left one ran a line where the heel hadn't lifted. They stopped at the float's edge, where a boat had lain alongside.

Beth stopped too.

"Man off the reefer. Hood. Drags his left heel. Walked off before the ramp came up."

Reyes looked at the planks. "That wasn't in your call."

"No."

He didn't ask why. He came round her and stopped on the harbor side of her, so the water was at his back. "Okay. One man, down the dock and not back up, which means a boat. Which boat?"

She looked out over the moorings, the lobster boats laid over to the east wind. "Any of them."

His phone buzzed. He read it under the open sky and didn't put it away. His thumbs went quick, and she watched her own words go in, the hood and the heel, for somebody she would never see. She'd said them on her own road, and they'd be in a stranger's inbox, in his spelling, before she had her coat off. She said nothing. She took him up the hill and in by the front door.

In the kitchen she pinned the subpoena under the barometer and tapped the glass. The needle dropped.

"It won't run. Pack if you want. You'll be unpacking by two."

"Dollar says it runs." He said it like a man who'd pay the dollar to be wrong.

"I don't bet on what I can see."

He went to the window and stood a step back from it. Below, off the fuel float, a skiff lay to its painter with one man in it, hood up, oars shipped, facing the hill.

"I see him," Reyes said, low and even. "He's sitting. He's not doing anything, which is what I'd do. Step back. Little more. Good." His arm came across and moved her, and touched her nowhere else.

Beth said nothing. They watched a long while.

At a quarter past one the handheld on the counter, turned to sixteen, spoke in the voice Ned kept for the air.

*"All stations, all stations, this is the ferry North Reach. The fourteen hundred departure is cancelled. Next departure to be announced. North Reach standing by one-six and one-three."*

"There goes my boat," Reyes said to the window, low.

Nobody answered it. Beth looked past his shoulder. The skiff lay tied to the float, empty, and there was nobody on the store dock.

He was already moving, past her and through the house toward the back, his right hand gone to his hip.
