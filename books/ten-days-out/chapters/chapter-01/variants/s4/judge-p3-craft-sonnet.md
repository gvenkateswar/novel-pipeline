1. Winner: A

2. Why: A's last scene gives Beth something to hide (she stepped over the heel track and left the man out of her call to the trooper), so she is a person with a secret in the room with a marshal. B has her hand him the heel track and the hood at once, which turns the scene into two professionals sorting out which boat.

3. Where A lost me: "Under the open sky his phone buzzed. He read it and didn't put it away." (~92%) The sentence "She took him in by the front door" comes right after it and is abrupt. The line "She'd given the trooper the box and the tape and the plate and left out the man, and saying so now meant saying it to a man who wrote things down." is a little explained. I didn't drift in either case.

4. Where B lost me: "'Okay. One man, down the dock and not back up, which means a boat. Which boat?'" (~90%) Reyes narrates the deduction and Beth answers "Any of them." This is two people doing business, close to the calibration A1 mode. Then "'I see him,' Reyes said, low and even. 'He's sitting. He's not doing anything, which is what I'd do. Step back. Little more. Good.'" is stock marshal talk. The closing "his right hand gone to his hip" is a cliche hook.

5. Confidence: medium. The scenes are about 85% identical in beats. A's edge comes from the withheld-information beat, the bodies-in-the-window texture (the barometer dollar, "He took the window. She fed the stove"), and "Sitting down, he had no limp to show her." B's cleaner ending hook is real but cheaper.

6. Same-author check: Yes. The two scenes share the same prose and the same dry, nautical, deflecting voice, and the difference is only in what the scene withholds.
