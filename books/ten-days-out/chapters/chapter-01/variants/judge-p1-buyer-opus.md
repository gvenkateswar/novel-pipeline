# Judge: Buyer persona (opus), p1 A vs B

1. **Winner: A.**

2. **Why:** In A she can't stop noticing him. She likes it when he takes the fudge-shop jab, she makes the line "do a thing it didn't need to," she lets him catch her looking in the kitchen and folds the sweater twice. That's what I download a sample for. B's marshal is funnier, but B keeps her colder, and the kitchen at the end goes flat.

3. **Where A lost me:** "She read the binders by feel. Handle down. Chain hard. The D-ring packed with slush, which she knocked clear with her thumb. The propane truck, then the co-op's bait truck, stinking of herring even in this, then the plumber's van." (~2%). That's a woman alone with chains, in a list, and my thumb moved. The dark reefer pulled me back in a paragraph later. I skimmed again at the walk aft, "a hand down the stern chain across the transom to feel it bar-tight, a kick at the ice on the ring buoy's bracket..." (~12%). After he comes down the ramp I didn't skim anything.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Twice he started to say something and stopped. The wind leaned on the house. She knew how it would go, Ned up there with his coffee going cold and the seas building white on the ledges, and she didn't need the radio to tell her, and she turned it on anyway." (~93%). The two of them are alone in her kitchen and nothing passes between them. He looks at the harbor and she thinks about Ned. A little earlier "He'd been going to look at one in a yard in Pruett this afternoon, he said." (~75%) told me what he said instead of letting me hear it, right where I wanted his voice. The boat daydream ending "He didn't." shut a door I wanted left open.

5. **Confidence: medium.** B has the better lines in places: "I have an app," the deputy stuck "behind a plow on Route 1," Tess's "Because you asked," and the wrist paragraph. A reader who likes banter could go the other way. I'd still put my money on the one where the heroine's warming up, and that's A.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes, same lines, same ending. They read like two drafts of one chapter.
