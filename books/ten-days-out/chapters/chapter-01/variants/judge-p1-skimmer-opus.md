# Judge: Skimmer (opus), p1 A vs B

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** Both have the same bones, but A keeps going back to her watching him and him watching her (the "I'll try to look at you more" line, the subpoena he held back so she could haul him aboard first, the sweater folded twice), and B fills those same spots with him explaining how the logistics work, which is exactly where I start skimming.

3. **Where A lost me:** about 12%: "Then she went aft the way her feet always went: a hand down the stern chain across the transom to feel it bar-tight, a kick at the ice on the ring buoy's bracket, past the quarter ladder with its rungs gone white, and up the outside ladder to the wheelhouse..." That's a checklist of boat parts, and my thumb sped up until the limping man showed up. I also slid a little over the opening "Handle down. Chain hard." run (~2%), but it's short and the dark reefer turns up fast. After the ramp it held me all the way to the radio call.

4. **Where B lost me:** about 42%: "No, because the two o'clock is printed on a sign at the top of this ramp, and on a website, and in the window of the coffee place, and a printed schedule is the easiest thing in the world to wait for." Just before it came "There was supposed to be a second deputy here for the friendly part. He's behind a plow on Route 1." It's a man laying out procedure on a ramp, and I skimmed down to "buried in it." B also had the same aft-walk list at ~13% ("checking the stern chain across the transom, knocking the ice off the ring buoy's bracket..."), and the last kitchen paragraph is her alone with the weather instead of the two of them.

5. **Confidence: medium.** B's banter is sharper in spots ("I have an app," "Because you asked," the run-on wrist sentence), and on another night that could flip it.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Same scenes, same lines, often word for word. They read like two drafts of one chapter.
