## Issues

**Issue 1**
- Problem: Pike's text shows Pike already knowing about the arm, but Dane has sent nothing since the shot and nothing on the page tells Pike. This breaks World rule 11 (information travels only by set-up channels). The text's first sentence also repeats what Beth and Dane just watched (the lifeboat turning), which both of them already know.
- Location: "Pike: Lifeboat turned back. Not again today. First light Saturday. LifeFlight for the arm?"
- Suggested fix: Cut "Lifeboat turned back." and "LifeFlight for the arm?". Make the text "Pike: Not again today. First light Saturday. Status?" Dane's one-word thumbed reply then answers the status question and reports the arm.

**Issue 2**
- Problem: The road-lights clause is delivered twice (Beth's line, then the narrator restating it). The first mention of "the box" has no antecedent: the only enclosure named so far is "the one panel", so "the box" and "the panel" read as two objects. "The shed behind the landing" also competes with "the bait shed" (bible: the co-op's bait shed is at the ramp head).
- Location: "The co-op's generator in the shed behind the landing fed the harbor road lights and the float lights off the one panel. The road was lit all the way to the store. Below, the float lay black on the water. Somebody had been in the box."
- Suggested fix: Cut the narrator's first sentence. Keep Beth's "Same set runs the road and the float," then "The road was lit all the way to the store. Below, the float lay black on the water." Replace the next sentence with "Somebody had been in the timer box at the ramp head." Name the generator once, as "the co-op's set behind the bait shed", if it must appear.

**Issue 3**
- Problem: The shot has no sound and is never named. Beth's register is sound-first, and the arm jump is the only sign that Dane was hit. A reader can't tell whether this is a shot or a signal flash.
- Location: "The flash came from out past the float, small and yellow. The road lights behind them had made the two of them one shape on the ramp head. Dane's left arm jumped."
- Suggested fix: Add the report as one short sentence after the flash and before the arm jump, for example "The crack came across the water a half second behind it." Put it in the order Beth hears it.

**Issue 4**
- Problem: The payoff of "I'll know before you do" is out of order. Beth says "Turn round" before any sound, and "She heard it first" comes after Dane has already turned them. The oar is placed "beneath the float", but the flash is "out past the float", so the sound and the light come from two places.
- Location: "She heard it first. His hand on her back hadn't changed." (with the opening "Turn round." and "somewhere beneath the float, where nothing ought to be at this tide, an oar touched aluminum. Once.")
- Suggested fix: Move the oar knock ahead of "Turn round." Beth hears it, then says it. Put the oar "out past the float" so it matches the flash.

**Issue 5**
- Problem: Dane's accusation has no on-page link. Nothing in s1 shows him connecting "Ned set that timer" to a tide question asked below decks while he was in the wheelhouse. A first-time reader meets "You asked Haskell about the float" with no ground for it.
- Location: "Ms. Calloway. You asked Haskell about the float. Below decks, where I couldn't hear it. Tell me what you said, exactly, the way you'd tell a man who's going to write it down."
- Suggested fix: Add one clause before the quote: "You went below with Haskell yesterday while I watched the pier, and the first thing you said at the ramp was his name."

**Issue 6**
- Problem: "Blue to the wrist" is capped at 1 and was spent in Ch 06 ("blue to the wrist with other people's gallons"). This is the second use. It is also "Tess's hand", which is a retired detail for this chapter. In this memory the envelope is bagged Thursday, before the hand is blue.
- Location: "She saw Tess's hands, blue to the wrist, folding the flap under."
- Suggested fix: "She saw the store counter and the bag open on it, the flap going under."

**Issue 7**

- Location: "Dane had stood on the ramp in his wrong boots, watching the pouch go and nothing else."
- Suggested fix: "Dane had stood on the ramp, watching the pouch go and nothing else."

**Issue 8**
- Problem: The s5 opener summarizes travel and intent in one long and-chain (four "and"s). That makes a second "and...and" in the chapter, and s5's real peak is the one allowed. The beats gave "two lines of travel at most", and this opens the scene by summarizing.
- Location: "She came along under the bank at the low with the snow in her face and nobody on the ledges but her, and went up the bank by the Union Church and in at its side door to wait for dark and the night low."
- Suggested fix: Open in the church: "The church had no heat in it. Her breath showed inside a building." Fold the route and the waiting into one clause later ("She had come up from the low by the side door and meant to wait for dark").

**Issue 9**
- Problem: s6 opens by back-filling the hours between (the beats said "with no account of the hours between"). It also leaves two gaps: she went in to her hips at early afternoon, and her clothes are still wet at two the next morning after a wait since the night low. Nothing says where she was between early afternoon and ten.
- Location: "She had come along the riprap below the road at the night low, out of the store's sight, and waited here since." and "Her wet clothes went stiff as they warmed, the jeans first, then the cuffs of her coat."
- Suggested fix: Cut the first sentence and begin "At two a flashlight came along the deck, held low." Change the clothes line to "The salt in her jeans began to soften as they warmed."

**Issue 10**
