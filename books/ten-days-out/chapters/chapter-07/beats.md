# Chapter 07 — His Handwriting: Beat Sheet

**Heading for the prose:** Day 7 to Day 8 — Thursday to Friday
**Span:** Day 7 late afternoon (Thursday, November 19) to about 2:30 a.m. Day 8 (Friday, November 20)
**POV:** Beth only (close third). No Dane POV in this chapter.
**Target:** about 3,000 words in 6 scenes
**Purpose:** Suspense turn 2. The dark night. She reads the notebook, believes it, and leaves him.
**Chapter promise (outline):** Is she any safer with Ned?

---

## Flags before the beats (conflicts found and how this sheet resolves them)

1. **The requested ending vs. the outline's POV plan.** The brief asks for the chapter to end with "Dane awake in the fish house finding the notebook open." The outline gives Ch 07 to Beth only, and makes Dane waking at 4 a.m. to the open notebook the **opening of Ch 08 in his POV**. The outline's POV table exists so that Dane is never in his own head while the secret is still live. A Dane-POV coda here would either clear him (if he thinks about the order) or cheat (if he thinks around it). It would also use up Ch 08's opening. **Resolution:** the final beat stays in Beth's POV. From the locked wheelhouse she pictures what he will wake to, using only what she set up herself: the lantern turned down to a bead, the stove left to die, the book open at the deposit page. That gives the requested image without breaking the POV rule, and Ch 08 shows the real waking. If the orchestrator wants an actual Dane-POV coda, that changes the approved outline's POV structure and goes to the author.
2. **The hooded man stays unnamed.** The outline's Ch 07 entry says "Doucette," but the continuity log says the hooded man has not been named on the page, and Beth does not know his name. In this chapter he is "the sternman" or "the heel" in her head. Do not write "Doucette."
3. **"Every location" vs. World Rule 7.** The notebook holds the cottage, the Ashby place, Lookout Hill and the town landing. The fish house appears only as "secondary location, B's family property, not disclosed." That line is the Ch 08 clue. Beth's eye passes over it with the same weight as the lines around it. No comment, no italics, no extra beat. Narration must not claim the book holds "every place they had been."
4. **"Deputy" once, like a slap (Beth's entry in the bible) vs. leaving without confronting him.** She says it once, under her breath, to the sleeping man at the door (Scene 4). He doesn't hear it. That meets the bible note and keeps the no-confrontation rule.
5. **Tide.** Bible Research note 36 implies a different Day 7 tide than Ch 06's canon (0600 Day 7 was "three hours past low," so low water was about 3:00 a.m. Day 7). Ch 06 is canon. So low water Day 8 is about **3:50 a.m.** At 12:30–1:30 a.m. the harbor is on the ebb and the shore ledges below the Harbor Road are coming dry. That is what makes the shore route smart.
6. **Table vs. workbench.** Ch 08's outline entry says "open on the table." The fish house has a workbench (Key Locations). She leaves the book on the **workbench**. Ch 08 should say workbench.
7. **New continuity for Ch 08.** She switches off Dane's 2300 watch alarm (Scene 3), so he misses his nightly call to Pike. She does not feed the stove before she leaves, so it is cold by 4 a.m. (Ch 08's "cold stove"). The lantern is turned down and burns out on its own.
8. **Ned's coffee.** The bible gives Ned three coffees for Beth in the book. This chapter uses one. The continuity checker should confirm the count from Ch 01–06.
9. **Morning wound, evening dressing.** Ch 06 has pressure only on the wound. The strips go on in the late afternoon because butterfly strips will not hold on a wound that is still weeping. She kept pressure on it until it stopped. One line covers this. It is a competence beat, not an explanation.

## Notebook contents (exact text for the prose-writer)

Dane's handwriting: small, slanted, quick, all lowercase except names. Set it against her father's square capitals on the paint cans in the same room. That contrast is the chapter title. Use one comparison only, once.

Rite in the Rain, yellow cover. The running detail log runs **front to back**. The review runs **from the back cover**, with the notebook turned upside down, the way people keep two books in one. Beth reads in this order:

**A. The page it falls open at (Day 2, the money):**
> D2 Sat 0710 — Quist. $9,000 cash deposited subject's acct, D1 a.m., w/ paycheck, via store bank bag. Hundreds. Teller flagged.

**B. Back to the front (the locations), running forward:**
> D1 — subject's res., Harbor Rd. 2340 back door tried. Unk. subject fled on foot.
> D2 — moved: Ashby place, north pt. Key via Haskell.
> D3 0640 — Lookout Hill w/ subject, woods path. Subj. ID'd sternman by gait. Photos to Quist.
> D4 2240 — Ashby compromised (fire). Moved: secondary location, B's family property, not disclosed.

(Beth's eye goes over the D4 line to the next one. No pause.)

**C. The last written page (the extraction):**
> D6 — Extraction 0600 D7 town landing, CG MLB, conf. Quist.
> D7 0604 — abort. Shots fired from water. Graze L arm. Subj. unhurt.

**D. Turned over, from the back (assess):**
> ASSESS — per Pike (Quist req.). Daily. Do not disclose to subject.
> D2 — no contact outside detail.
> D3 — financial questions. No indication.
> D4 — no change.
> D5 — no change.
> D6 — [one line struck through hard, ink pressed into the paper, unreadable]

The struck line is optional, but recommended. It leaves room for "the entry he is ashamed of" in Ch 08 without deciding now what it said. Beth cannot read it and does not try for long.

Do not add "coordinated with local emergency management" to the notebook. Readers are already about 75% on Tess. The bank bag is enough plant for this chapter.

### Scene 1

**Setting:** The fish house on Calloway Cove, Day 7, about 3:30 p.m. The kerosene lantern is lit because the one small window on the water side has gone the color of slate. The first bands of the second system are slapping rain on the shingles and leaning on the landward door. The oil-drum stove ticks. Sensory anchor: the old adhesive smell when she peels the yellowed paper backing off a butterfly strip.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane

**Surface action:** Open in the middle of the work (no recap of the ambush). Her father's red Pelican boat kit is open on the workbench. She has kept pressure on the graze since morning because strips won't hold on a wound that is still weeping; now it has quit. Dane sits on the cot with his left elbow braced on his knee and his right hand clamped round his left wrist, holding his own arm still for her. She has him take his watch off the left wrist first, because the arm will swell (the watch goes on the bench beside the cot, and it matters in Scene 3). She cleans the skin with water boiled on the stove and cooled in a coffee can, dries the edges, and lays the strips across the graze starting from the middle, pulling the edges together with her thumb before each one sticks. Two of her father's strips are too old to hold. She checks the date on the box and goes on with the rest. Then gauze, then tape. Partway through, her free palm goes flat on his chest to keep him from leaning into her. It is her reading habit and she does it without thinking; under her hand there is only a steady heart. He says one dry thing about the kit (not a callback to "very disappointing kit"; something new about her father's taste in tape). She tells him what the strips will and won't do: no lifting that arm over his shoulder, no hard grip with that hand. He repeats it back in fewer words. He reports what the ledge call gave him: the second system is in by dark, nothing moves on the water until it blows through, the ferry is still Day 10. He asks her to wake him at eleven for the night call to Pike. She says the watch can wake him. He sets it, leaves it on the bench, and lies back.

**Subtext:** This is the closest they have been since the ambush, and both are holding back from saying anything about this morning. She is keeping an exit again (the end of Ch 06) and dressing a wound is something she can do instead of deciding anything. He has something he still isn't telling her. Nothing on the page shows that except one ordinary thing: he lies down with his jacket still on the floor where he dropped it. It is in plain view and means nothing yet.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal and physical. Her hands are on his skin for the first time since the night before, with blood in it now. The storm is building. Their way out has gone. **Optional "not asking" beat (the one allowed this chapter):** she does not ask what else Pike said.

**Ends on:** Not a button. He is asleep within minutes, the way people sleep after blood loss and a day that began at four. She sweeps the strip papers off the bench into the stove, sits on the bench across from him where she can see the door, and is still sitting there when the window goes fully dark.

**Word count target:** 550–600

**Romance beat (bible rule):** her palm on his chest, his breath on her wrist while she works, his eyes on her hands. One or two lines. No stated feeling.

### Scene 2

**Setting:** The fish house, about 10:40 p.m. The storm is fully in: wind in the shingles, rain driven against the landward door. The stove is low. The lantern is turned down. Sensory anchor: wet soft-shell steaming the moment it touches the heat off the drum.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane (asleep)

**Surface action:** She gets up to feed the stove and steps on his jacket. It has been on the plank floor since his last ledge call, soaked through. She picks it up to hang it on the stove nail, where it hung last night. It is heavier on one side. As she gets it onto the nail, the notebook slides out of the inside pocket and lands on the floor face-down and splayed. She picks it up to put it back. It is open. Her own name is at the top of the left-hand page in a hand that isn't hers or her father's, and under it is a dollar figure. She does not put it back. Show this as a choice: she holds it to the lantern on purpose, because her name is on it. She reads Entry A in full on the page.

How the money reads to her: she has never seen nine thousand dollars in one place in her life. She thinks of her balance as she last saw it (a real, small number). Somebody put that money in her account to make her look bought, and **he has known since Saturday.** Her mind goes straight to the Ashby generator shed on Sunday, fuel on her hands, "Anything come in lately?" She slides past "via store bank bag" to "D1" and to him. The words "store bank bag" are on the page in his print and the narration does not touch them again. That is the plant the reader can catch.

**Subtext:** For a week she has read everything about him by touch and by what he does. This is the first thing she has been able to check with her own eyes, and it is written in his hand.

**Tension source:** Internal and situational. He is asleep six feet away, armed, and his watch is set for eleven, twenty minutes off.

**Ends on:** She turns back to the first page. (Action, not a line.)

**Word count target:** 450–500 (short by design; the find should be quick)

### Scene 3

**Setting:** The fish house, 10:45 to about 11:00 p.m. She stands at the workbench under the lantern, her back to the stove, with the cot at the edge of her sight. Sensory anchor: the Rite in the Rain paper, slick and slightly waxy, nothing like her father's tide-table pages. Her father's square capitals on the paint cans above the bench.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane (asleep)

**Surface action:** She reads B, then C, then D, in that order (text above).

- **The locations (B).** Her house, with the time the latch lifted. The Ashby place, with "Key via Haskell." Lookout Hill, with her name, his "photos to Quist," and the walk she picked out. Every place in this list that someone found is written here in the same quick hand. Her eye goes over the D4 line without stopping.
- **The extraction (C).** "Extraction 0600 D7 town landing, CG MLB, conf. Quist." The bait shed wall, the hole a foot behind her head. Then his own account of this morning, in four short pieces: "Subj. unhurt." She is "Subj." She has been "Subj." all week.
- **Assess (D).** She turns the book over to see if there's more and finds the pages written from the back. "Do not disclose to subject." "Daily." The Saturday phone call she heard from under the eave ("No. She doesn't know. ... Daily. Understood.") turns over in her head and lands the right way up: the "she" was her. "D3 — financial questions. No indication." The shed was an interview. She tries to read the struck D6 line, can't, and remembers the night of Day 6: he opened his mouth, closed it, and kissed her. She took that for tenderness.
- **The swear.** Here, in her head, at the notebook: the single sharpest swear in the book (Language Rule 4). One word, italic or free indirect. It is the chapter's one narration swear. No other strong swears in this chapter.
- **The alarm.** It is 10:58 on his watch on the bench. She presses the button that kills the 2300 alarm. Her hands do it steadily. (She knows this watch; she turned its face down on Day 6.)

The Dane reading must feel real, so let her lay out the case in the order she would, in short declaratives, as a list she could write on the paint-can lid. The porch call twenty minutes before the latch, Day 1. "She doesn't know," Day 2. "I'll ask her," Day 3. He picked the Ashby place, and it burned. He set the landing time, and someone was waiting with a gun. And every one of those things is in this book, written down every night for people she has never met. She does not have to decide whether it is him or the people he writes to. Either way, the book goes out, and men come after it.

**Subtext:** He has been gentle with her all week, and now that gentleness reads to her as method: the money questions, the kettle, his hand stopping. What she finds unbearable is not that he lied but that she can't tell which parts weren't lies. She is her father, believing the bank.

**Tension source:** Internal, with a clock: the alarm, and how lightly he sleeps.

**Ends on:** She lays the book open on the workbench at the Day 2 page, the money, squared to the edge of the bench, the way you leave a manifest for the next shift. Not a one-line button: end on the physical placement.

**Word count target:** 600–650

**Rules for this scene:** Do not touch the gun. She does not look in his other pockets. Nothing Dane does in this scene (he only sleeps) clears him. "No indication" may read to a careful reader as clearing *her*. It must never read as clearing *him*.

### Scene 4

**Setting:** The fish house, about 11:05 to 11:20 p.m. The landward door is shut against the wind. The lantern is turned down to a bead. The stove is left to die. Sensory anchor: the cold coming off the door planks against her face before she opens it.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane (asleep)

**Surface action:** She reasons out leaving, in actions, with the reasons in her head as clipped statements:
- **Why she doesn't confront him.** He is armed and trained, and he reads people for a living. If he is the leak, telling him she knows is the worst thing she can do. If he isn't, he will explain, and she cannot check an explanation. She can check a binder; she can't check a man's answer. A fight she can't win tells him one thing only: that she knows.
- **Why now, in the dark.** The storm covers sound and keeps people in. He is asleep and the alarm is off. By daylight he would see it on her face.
- **Where.** The ferry. Steel, lit, a fixed VHF, a wheelhouse door that locks, and Ned, who has known her since before she could walk the deck. **Not the store:** it's public, the whole island is in and out, and anything that came after her there would come at Tess. (Pure irony for the reader. Beth thinks it once, fondly, and moves on. Do not underline it.)
- **What she takes.** Her own go-bag. The ferry-issued Icom handheld (hers, in its freezer bag) is her only link to anyone. She checks with her thumb that it is **switched off**. She leaves the sat phone, the gun and his kit: none of it is hers.
- **What she leaves.** The book open at the money, so he knows she left on her own and why. If she just vanished, he would think she'd been taken and come out after her with a gun in the dark. The open book is also the one honest thing she can do. It tells the truth for her.

At the door he shifts on the cot and says her name, still asleep. She stands without moving until his breathing evens out again. Then she says "Deputy" under her breath, the only time in the book, to a man who doesn't hear it. She lifts the door on its hinges the way her father taught her so it won't scrape, and goes out.

**Subtext:** One or two lines of the romance: his hand open on the blanket, the stubble, the strips she put on six hours ago showing white at the edge of the gauze. She doesn't touch him.

**Tension source:** Will he wake? Situational, with the pressure of the decision underneath it.

**Ends on:** **Button 1 of 2.** One short line closing the door behind her, on the book left open. Keep it plain and concrete, not an aphorism (cold readers flagged "neat closing lines").

**Word count target:** 400–450 (short; momentum)

### Scene 5

**Setting:** Outdoors, about 11:30 p.m. to 1:30 a.m. First the grown-over path from the cove to the cove road, then the cove road, then a short stretch of the Harbor Road by the Union Church, the churchyard, and the lean-to woodshed behind it. Then down the bank to the harbor shore. Rain driven sideways on a northeast wind. No moon. She uses no light. Sensory anchors: split oak and snow-wet bark in the woodshed; the bell rope knocking inside the church on every gust; then rockweed and barnacled ledge under her boots on the shore.

**Characters present:** Beth, the sternman (unnamed; seen only as a flashlight)

**Surface action:** She walks the path blind by feel and memory, a path she has walked since she was a child. On the Harbor Road, short of the church, she sees a flashlight coming the other way. Its rhythm is uneven: a long stride and a short one, the beam dipping on the short one. She knows that walk from Lookout Hill. She is off the road and into the churchyard before she has finished the thought, flat behind the woodshed, the cord wood between her and the road. The light goes slowly along the road, into a dooryard, out again. A handheld breaks squelch on him: a burst, a low voice she can't make out, the hiss tail. He moves on only after each burst, so someone is talking him along. **Counting use 1 of 2 (the number matters):** she counts the gap between bursts. It is steady, the rhythm of somebody reading off a list. Her own Icom is off in its bag against her ribs. She thinks of the squelch tail that would have given her away the same way, and keeps her hand on the bag. The light passes the church, close enough that she sees rain in the beam, and goes on toward the store end of the road. She waits until the bell rope has knocked a good while and the light is gone. She does not go back to the road. She drops down the bank behind the church to the shore. The tide is on the ebb toward low water at about ten to four, and the ledges below the Harbor Road are coming dry. That is a shore route nobody would take in this weather and nobody watches. She works along it toward the stone pier, keeping the store's lit windows above her and to one side. She doesn't climb to them.

Do not let her reason about why they are sweeping the village and not the cove. No line about the fish house. If the reader makes that connection, it is theirs.

**Subtext:** Her body is ahead of her mind. Show fear in her hands, her breath and her knees on the frozen bark, not in named emotions. The one person she would have woken to go to the door is the one she just left.

**Tension source:** Situational and immediate: an armed man fifteen yards off, being guided by radio.

**Ends on:** An image, not a button. She is crouched on the wet ledge under the stone pier with the iron ladder above her. Over the pier's lip the *North Reach* shows her deck lights, sodium orange through the rain.

**Word count target:** 500–550

**Danger-beat check (Danger beats table, Ch 07):** she goes to ground and lets him pass, then takes the shore route and not the road. She never moves toward the light. She never uses a light. The radio stays off.

### Scene 6

**Setting:** The M/V *North Reach*, moored starboard side to the stone pier with the bow ramp raised, about 1:45 to 2:30 a.m. First the pier at the gangway (the aluminum brow to the starboard gate amidships, the gate chained). Then the crew mess. Then the wheelhouse, which locks. Sensory anchors: the generator's thrum through the deck plates; the mess heater's burnt-dust smell; in the wheelhouse, burnt coffee, wet wool and electronics, the fixed VHF hissing on 16; the two storm-bound trucks chained below in the floods.

**Characters present:** Beth, Ned

**Surface action:**
- **Boarding.** She climbs the pier ladder and waits in the shadow of the wrecked slip's tower, out of the floods. She does not hail him on the radio; the whole harbor would hear it. She knows Ned's storm habit: pump rounds every two hours, flashlight along the car deck. When the light comes along the deck at two, she steps into the gangway light. His hearing aid is off at night, so she slaps the steel of the gate twice with her palm. The light swings onto her and holds. Ned looks at her for a long moment (he told her in Ch 03 to stay off his boat until this was done). Then he unhooks the chain. He looks past her at the empty pier before he does.
- **The crew mess (the breakdown, all observable).** He switches on the mess heater and leaves her there. She can't get the float coat's zipper down; her fingers won't do it. She sits on the bench. She puts her forehead on her forearms on the table, and the sound she makes goes into the wet sleeve. She hasn't cried since her father's funeral, and the narration doesn't say so. Keep it short and physical.
- **"Where's your marshal?"** Ned in the doorway with two mugs of coffee (this is one of his three coffees). He sets hers down by her hand, not in it. He says, "Where's your marshal?" She says, "Asleep." It is true, and it is all she gives him.
- **The wheelhouse.** She asks for it. His eyes go to the Icom on the table. He works the wheelhouse key off his ring (keys are how Ned gives things: the cage key on its orange float, the Ashby brass) and holds it out. They climb the inside companionway. He wipes the forward windows with the rag that makes them worse. Then he asks the question: "Anybody know you came here?" She says, "You." It is the Ch 02 exchange with the roles swapped. **It is not a scene ending.** It sits in the middle and the scene goes on. Ned looks at her for a while. Then he puts one finger to the hearing aid and turns it off (his tell that a conversation is over). He goes down the ladder.
- **Locked in.** She turns the key. She sets the handheld on the console beside the fixed set and leaves them both on 16. Through the aft windows, over the lighting panel on the aft bulkhead, she watches his flashlight go along the car deck between the two chained trucks to the gangway. It stops there, then goes out. He is sitting in the dark at the only way aboard from the land. She cannot tell if he is guarding the gangway or waiting at it. (This sets up the Ch 08 beat where Ned blocks the gangway against Dane, and keeps the doubt about Ned that the chapter promise needs.) Small cold doubt, by fact only: Ned knew the Ashby place too, and Ned lied about the reefer.
- **The final image (Flag 1).** In Beth's POV, without seeing it, she pictures what is waiting for him, built only from what she set up herself: the lantern burning down to its bead and out, the stove gone cold, the book squared on the workbench at the page with her name and the money. He will wake all at once, the way she saw him wake on the night of Day 1, and read his own handwriting. Place it as the closing paragraph, in concrete images, no reflection and no question in narration (a banned cliché). It should not be a one-line button.

**Subtext:** She has gone where she is good: on steel she knows by feel, behind a door that locks, beside a radio. It turns out not to be enough, because the one man left is someone she has already caught in a lie. In the wheelhouse she keeps an exit in her head again: the inside companionway, the outside ladder, the door she just locked.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal: Ned's silence, his question, the hearing aid, the flashlight going dark. Underneath it, grief that the scene never names.

**Ends on:** The fish house picture: the lantern out, the book open at her name. This closes the chapter on the Dane image the brief asks for. The paragraph just before it (Ned's light going out at the gangway) carries the chapter promise, "Is she any safer with Ned?"

**Word count target:** 650–750 (the longest scene; it carries the arrival, the breakdown, Ned and the handoff)

- **Every scene advances plot or deepens character.** Scene 1 is the lightest on plot. It earns its place with the romance beat, the watch (set up for Scene 3), the jacket on the floor (set up for Scene 2) and Dane's report (no way off). Keep it under 600 words.
- **Buttons:** at most two. Planned: Scene 4 only. "You." in Scene 6 falls mid-scene. Every other scene ends on an action or an image.
- **Counting:** at most twice. Planned: once (Scene 5, the gap between squelch bursts). The tide time in Scene 5 is a fact she knows, not a count. Do not add step counts.
- **"Not asking":** at most once. Optional in Scene 1 only.
- **Retired tics (do not use):** "put it where she put things"; "longer than X needed" / "one breath longer than it needed to"; "the way she'd [boat task]" (the "squared like a manifest" image in Scene 3 must not be written in that construction; write it as a plain action); "right side clear"; neat closing lines; "wet/hard"; "let go of everything." No simile used for Beth in an earlier chapter may come back.
- **Swearing:** one strong swear, Beth's, in her head at the notebook (Scene 3). Dane: none (his two are Ch 06 and Ch 09). Ned: "Jesus" or "damn" at most. Nothing on the VHF. Mild words under eight in total.
- **Heat:** no sex. The intimacy is the wound dressing and one or two lines at the cot in Scene 4. No stated emotion anywhere.
- **Danger choices (Beth's entry, "never make her"):** she leaves for a destination with reasons the reader can defend. She never touches the gun, never uses a light, never keys the radio near the road, goes to ground rather than toward the threat, and takes the route nobody watches on a tide she has worked out. This is not "sneaking off alone to think" (a banned cliché): she has a destination, a radio and a reason.
- **Doubt about Dane stays alive.** On the page he only works, sleeps, and says her name in his sleep. Nothing he does clears him. "No indication" clears her, not him.
- **Fair-play plants:** "via store bank bag" in his print (Scene 2), with Beth sliding past it. The D4 "not disclosed" line (Scene 3), given no weight. The sweep of the village road rather than the cove (Scene 5), with no reasoning.
- **No recap** at the top of the chapter or of any scene.
- **Chapter promise:** "Is she any safer with Ned?" It is set up by "Anybody know you came here?" / "You.", the hearing aid going off, and his light going dark at the gangway.
