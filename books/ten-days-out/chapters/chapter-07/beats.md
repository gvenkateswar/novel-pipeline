## Scene 1 · The sea

**Event (outline):** Dane goes into the November sea, and she hauls him out. The man's light stays on the water where he went in for a long time, then goes away.

**Setting:** The north shore rocks below the burning summer house, Sunday, full dark falling (straight on from Ch 06's last line). Iced granite, black water coming up white over the low rocks. Up behind them the house is still burning and puts an orange edge on the snow along the top of the bank. The flashlight is at the top of the bank, stopped. **Sensory anchor:** the sound of the sea pulling back off the rocks before it comes, a long dragging roar and then nothing. Then the cold. For the first second it's only his chest locking shut.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth. (The man appears only as a light at the top of the bank.)

**Want:** Dane wants to do exactly what she does and not be the thing that kills her. Beth wants both of them flat on this rock before the sea gets here. In the way: iced rock, his wrong boots, a sea bigger than he can see.

**Surface action (in order):**
1. Open on the outline line: "Dane Reyes did exactly what she did, right up until the wave took him off the rock."
2. One step back to show what she did: she drops flat on a big rounded rock, jams her free arm into a crack on the high side, and puts her face down. Her one line, before it hits: "Down. Arm in the crack. Face in." He copies her. His crack is glazed with ice.
3. The sea comes over them, all of it, black and then white, and its weight shoves him flat. He holds. Then it goes back out, and the pull going out is worse than the push coming in. His arm comes out of the iced crack, and the rock goes out from under his chest.
4. **His choice, on the page, in his head, in one plain sentence:** her hand is still in his, the sea is taking him, and he opens his hand so it won't take her too. (This is his wound, deciding for her, done out of love. The reader should see him choose it.)
5. In the water: the cold. Follow the bible's water facts at true size and in plain words. He gasps, and the gasp is sea. His breath goes and won't come back. He tries to say her name, and nothing comes out. *The man who talks through everything has no voice.* His coat fills, and his boots fill. A rock hits his hip under the water.
6. Two hands in his coat collar. Beth has come down off her rock into the water to her thighs, on the next rock down, and she has his coat in both fists. The next swell lifts him, and she uses the lift. He feels it in her: she waits for the sea to give him to her and then hauls, shoulders and back, the way she hauls a ferry rope. He comes up the rock on his stomach.
7. She drags him along the waterline into the black under the overhang of the bank, behind a big rock (never "ledge"). He's on his side, shaking so hard his teeth knock. She puts her hand over his mouth, gently, to stop the sound of his teeth.
8. **The light.** It comes down the top of the bank and finds the white water where he went in. It stays there. Give it room, a long time, so the reader waits with them. Dane's interior runs long here because his mouth can't: he's counting, because he counts everything, and he loses count. In the same run of thought, one plain line with feeling in it restates Ch 06's fact: four people had known about that house, and it burned, and the only place nobody had known about, nobody had come. Then he notices his hands have stopped closing.
9. The light sweeps once along the rocks, short of them, and goes back to the water. Then it goes away up the bank toward the fire, and it doesn't come back.
10. Beth, her mouth at his ear, the first thing she has said since the wave: "He thinks we drowned."

**Subtext:** He let go of her to save her and doesn't regret it. She came in after him anyway and hasn't decided yet how angry she is. Both of them understand that being dead is the best thing that has happened to them since Thursday.

**Tension source:** Situational and physical. The sea, the cold that takes his voice and then his hands, and the light that will decide whether they're found. The beat of fear before it lands (World rule 8): the sea pulling back, the roar she can hear and he can't see.

**Texture budget:** (1) The cold-water facts: the gasp, the voice gone in the first minute, the hands going. What it shows: the talker struck mute, and how close it came. (2) Beth using the swell's lift to haul him, in one sentence. What it shows: her body knows the sea the way his knows a room. No other technical detail.

**Point of view:** Dane. His interior keeps its long "and" sentences while his body fails. In the light passage the sentences may break apart as the cold takes him, and come back short.

**Ends on:** *Consequence.* "He thinks we drowned." The light is gone, and it's dark all the way to the water.

**Word target:** 700. No floor. (The writer should not compress the wait for the light. That's where the slowdown goes.)

## Scene 2 · The mile, the window, the bed

**Event (outline):** she half carries him a mile to the old lighthouse, gets him in through the window that doesn't shut right, strips him and gets him warm, skin to skin, under every blanket in the house. When his voice comes back, the first thing he says is that he fell asleep on watch.

**Setting:** First the shore, going north in the dark, about a mile of rocks and snow. Then the old lighthouse on the north point, its light turning far above them and lighting nothing near the ground. Beside it the keeper's house, where the lighthouse keeper used to live (gloss it once), a summer rental shut for the winter. Inside: cold plaster, mothballs, total dark, one bed. **Sensory anchor:** mothballs and cold sheets. Then, much later, the first heat that isn't hers, coming back into his own chest.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth.

**Want:** Beth wants him warm, and she gets there with her hands, a list and no softness. Dane wants his voice back so he can say the thing that has been sitting in his chest since the fire. In the way: his body, which has stopped taking orders.

**Surface action (in order):**
1. Open in motion on the shore: Beth with his arm over her shoulders, his weight half on her, his right knee stiff from the cold and the rock. No recap of the sea.
2. **Beth talks.** She is the one who goes quiet in danger, and he can't talk, so she does it the way he does, and badly. Give her his job in her own syntax: short whole sentences, all orders. "Left foot. Flat one. Now up." "Round the point. Then the lighthouse." "Every kid on this island has been in that window." When he starts to sag, she tells the one story she has, flat, with no smile to signal the joke: "I tried one of those apps once. It found me nine men inside fifty miles, and two of them were my cousins." He laughs with no sound. She feels it in his ribs and keeps walking. Dane's interior hears her doing his thing and knows why: she's talking so she knows where he is. **Do not** write "it cost her." Show it in how short her sentences are, and in his name, said twice when he stumbles.
3. The keeper's house. The window that doesn't shut right: she lifts it on its frame and it comes up. She gets him over the sill and follows.
4. Inside, in the dark, she works by feel and talks less now. One line, as an order: "No fire, no light. Smoke shows white from the whole north end." She tries a light switch or a pull cord, and nothing happens. (Plant: the dead generator, Ch 08. One sentence only.)
5. She strips him. His fingers can't do buttons. Hers, raw from the ice, just can. The coat, the sweater, the boots heavy with sea, which she empties onto the floor. His gun: he won't let her put it out of reach, and he gets that across with one hand, no words. She sets it on the floor by the bed where his hand can find it. The tape on his arm is gone, and the cut under it is white and closed from the salt (injury closed).
6. "This is medical." She says it flat as she strips off her own wet jeans and shirt, because she's soaked to the waist and shaking too. **He can't answer.** He has the answer ready and no voice to say it with. Let his interior hold it.
7. Every blanket in the house: she makes trips in the dark to the closets and the other rooms, and they land on the bed in loads. Then she gets in, chest to chest. **Texture:** she doesn't rub his arms, the way he rubbed hers in the snow the first night. She warms his middle first, his chest against hers, and keeps his arms in against his own body. One line from her: "You warm the middle first." Then Beth's tender line, word for word: "Come here. You're shaking. Let me be the warm one for once."
8. Time, without a clock. The shaking comes and goes in long rounds and slows (keep the word "wave" for the sea). His hands hurt as they come back, which means they're still his (the Ch 01 echo is allowed in his head, not quoted). Her heart against his chest. **The wanting here is honest and comic:** every part of him that could want her is busy staying alive, and he knows it, and he thinks it, and it's the most naked he has ever been with anyone.
9. His voice comes back rough. **First words, word for word:** "I fell asleep on watch, and you nearly burned for it."
10. The fight, face to face, in the dark. Both are partly right:
    - Beth: "You'd been up three nights. I heard you go under, and I let you."
    - Dane: he let her let him. He says so in his own words, one line.
    - Beth: "You let go of my hand."
    - Dane: "It was going to take you with me."
    - Beth: "I was holding the rock. You don't get to decide what I can hold."
    - Dane: "Fair."
    - (Stop there. She doesn't state a condition, and he doesn't promise. Ch 13–15 pay it.)
11. A pause in the dark. Then, twenty minutes after she said it, the first moment he can: "Best medical I've ever had."
12. She laughs out loud. In his head: two.

**Subtext:** She's furious that he let go, and she's holding his whole body against hers. He's ashamed of sleeping, and he's lying in the arms of the person who let him. Each of them has saved the other once tonight, and neither will put it that way.

**Tension source:** Physical first (will he get warm), then between the two of them (who gets to decide in the water). It tips into flirting on the late joke.

**Texture budget:** (1) Warm the middle first, and don't rub the arms. What it shows: Beth knows cold the way islanders do, and it quietly corrects the first night. (2) No fire and no light, because smoke shows white. What it shows: being dead is something they have to keep up. Nothing else.

**Point of view:** Dane. His voice is gone until beat 9, so his interior carries the first half, and it should run long. While he's mute, the contrast with Beth talking is the scene.

**Ends on:** *Dialogue.* "Best medical I've ever had." Her laugh out loud, and two in his head. Nothing after.

**Word target:** 600. No floor. (The mile gets about 150 of these words at most. Nobody follows them, so it's a hard walk and nothing more.)

## Scene 3 · The ask

**Event (outline):** heat #2, level 4, full. The condoms come from her coat. She asks out loud: "I want you. I'm saying it out loud so you can't pretend I didn't." In Dane's POV he hears her ask and knows it's the first time she has asked anyone for anything.

**Setting:** The keeper's house bedroom, later the same night, in total dark under every blanket in the house. Wind in the tower next door, and the light far up turning over nothing. Her wet coat over a chair, his gun on the floor by the bed. **Sensory anchor:** salt dried on her skin. He can taste it, and he can't see her at all.

**Characters present:** Dane, Beth.

**Want:** Beth wants him, and this time she wants to say so out loud. She has always taken things for herself, and asking is the new part. Dane wants her more than he has ever wanted anything, and he has no window to watch, no door, no jury. He can't see her, so he has to learn her from what she tells him. In the way: nothing, for the first time. That's the scene.

**Surface action (in order):**

*Approach (about 300 words):*
1. Open on the ordinary pleasure: dry wool socks. He wakes (he slept, and she let him again) and finds she has pulled a summer person's wool socks from a drawer in the dark and put them on his feet. Give it three or four sentences: wool, dry, his own feet back. He tells her it's the best thing that has happened to him all day, in one of his long sentences ending on a short undercut. Beth: "Nothing out there but wind. I've been listening." He believes her, and he notices that he believes her.
2. **His competence, short (texture):** he takes his gun apart on the blanket by feel, dries each piece on a pillowcase, and puts it back together blind, the way the Army made him learn it. His phone lights up in the dark with no signal. For the second night running his boss has heard nothing from him at six, and there's nothing he can do about it from this end of the island. One sentence. That's the boss beat.
3. **The reprieve, his read, in their voices:**
   - Dane: "He watched the water a long time, and then he went to tell somebody it was done."
   - Beth: "So I'm dead."
   - Dane: "As of tonight. Nobody on this island knows different." Then: "So if anybody comes for you now, somebody had to tell them you're alive."
   - Beth, her flat verdict, and her only answer to that: "Then he can take my picture down."
   - **The September line** (Dane's interior, one sentence): somebody had pinned her face to that wall in September, two months before she'd seen anything, and he still couldn't make it add up.
4. **The hour, owed:** Beth: "You said off that hill you'd kiss me for an hour." Dane: "I did." Beth: "We've been off it a long time." (The owing flips: the man who would rather be owed is the one who owes.)
5. **The ask, word for word, separate and spoken:** "I want you. I'm saying it out loud so you can't pretend I didn't."
6. **Dane hears it (the longing):** his interior, in one long sentence. The woman who wouldn't let Tess forgive her a penny, who carried her own bag down the steps before he could offer, who said "I don't owe anybody" with her hand on his taped arm, has just asked him for something, out loud, in the dark, and he knows it's the first time she has asked anyone for anything. Then his mouth goes quiet. All he says: "Beth."

*The intimate action (at least 600 words, real time, from the first plainly sexual touch to the first line after climax):*
7. **Protection, in one line, with a laugh inside it:** she gets out of bed into the cold for her coat's inside pocket. Beth, coming back: "The box is soup. These are fine." (The foil kept them dry.)
8. **A different build from Ch 04,** which was fast and lit: his hands, her coming first, the net, the heater. Ch 07 is slow, in total dark, face to face on their sides. They're the same height, so in the dark her mouth is level with his without either of them looking for it. He can't see her, so:
   - Dane: "I can't see you."
   - Beth: "Then I'll tell you."
   - And she does. Her register: short orders in plain words ("Slower." "That." "Come here."). Never Ch 04's "There. Right there." or "Don't stop." Dane's register: quiet out loud (her name, a question), with long sentences in his head.
9. **Bodies, specific and new to this scene:** the bruises her knuckles left on his collarbone when she hauled him by the coat; the strength in her shoulders and back that pulled him out of the sea, which he can feel now under his hands; her palms, skinned by the ice, so she touches him with the backs of her fingers and her forearms, and he notices why; salt dried in her hair and on her skin; his bad right knee. **Leave out** the palm scar, cold feet on his shins, stubble and the taped arm. Ch 04 used those.
10. **Humor inside the scene:** his right knee cracks loud in the dark as he moves over her. Beth: "You creak like an old boat." He takes it as the nicest thing anyone has ever said to him and doesn't say why. (Ch 08 pays the boat. Don't explain it here.)
11. **The check, then her yes, in their voices:** before he's inside her, his hand waits. Dane: "Tell me." Beth: "Yes. Now, Dane." Then inside her, slow. Keep to the choreography limit: never more than two sentences in a row about who's touching what, then a thought, a sound or a line.
12. **Plot touches, at least two, inside the act:** (a) His jury habit fails. In his head he reaches for twelve people from Maine and finds nothing, because nobody can see this, and as of tonight nobody even knows they're alive. (b) The people she'd die for: once, he thinks that she has three people on this island she'd die for and one of them might be selling her, and he puts the thought down because she says his name. Keep it general: no Ned, no names (World rule 12).
13. **His want, built differently from the earlier chapters' "He wanted X. He didn't do X."** Use these shapes: there's nothing to watch, no window and no door, and for the first time in nine years of this job the only thing he wants to watch is her, and he can't see her. And he stops counting, and notices he has stopped. Say the plain wanting in words at least once, in his head ("He wanted her."). Then let the body carry it.
14. Both come, her first, and she says something short. Then him, with her name and nothing else. Use frank words sparingly and exactly, from the HEAT RULES list. None of the banned euphemisms, and no "came apart" or "over the edge."

*After (about 120 words at most, back to the relationship):*
15. Her head on his shoulder, the blankets, the wind in the tower. In his head: Monday, Portland, her island, his motel room on loan from Boston. She will never leave this island, and he has known that since the oar.
16. **His line, word for word:** "I'm going to get you off this island alive, and then I'm going to lose you, aren't I?"
17. She doesn't answer. What we see: she finds his hand under the blankets and holds it hard, the way she held it on the rock before the sea, and she doesn't let go. **No line after that.**

**Subtext:** She's asking for him because she nearly lost him an hour ago and she's done not asking. He's hearing a woman who never asks, and that scares him more than the sea did, because now he has something to lose on Monday. Her hand at the end means both things: she's holding on, and she isn't answering.

**Tension source:** Inside each of them and between them; the plot stays outside the door. Can she ask? Can he stop keeping watch? Under it all is the question he finally says out loud: what happens after Monday.

**Texture budget:** (1) The gun stripped and dried by feel. What it shows: he's very good at something, and his hands work again. (2) The condoms dry in their foil while the box is ruined. What it shows: the joke from Tess's counter comes home, and the sea didn't get everything. Nothing else technical.

**Point of view:** Dane. His interior runs long the whole way through, and his mouth goes quiet once she asks. Beth gives the orders out loud. If both of them go quiet in the act, the scene has lost a character: she talks, and he listens.

**Ends on:** *Question.* "I'm going to get you off this island alive, and then I'm going to lose you, aren't I?" No answer. Her hand closing on his. This sets up the outline's chapter promise: will this outlast the ten days? It's a new question for the book, the first time either of them has asked what happens to *them* after Monday, and Ch 08 opens on his answer to it ("I'm in love with you").

**Word target:** 1,000. **Word floor:** 800 (heat). At least 600 words go to the intimate action itself, in real time. About 300 for the approach and about 120 after.
