## Where I got bored

- **The second half, once they're warm.** The wave is the best action in the book. After it, the chapter does no plot at all. Nobody comes. Nobody looks for them. Nobody learns anything new. It's a shelter scene, then a sock joke, then the gun gets cleaned, then a long sex scene. My attention went at "He took the gun apart on the blanket by feel, dried each piece on a pillowcase and put it back together blind." It's a competence detail with nothing riding on it. I started skimming toward the next thing that could happen, and nothing happened.
- **I've read this chapter's shape before, in Chapter 4.** Shot at in the dark. Run along the shore rocks. She leads, he slips. Hide from a flashlight above. Break into a shut building. She makes it warm. He's hurt and she tends him. They have sex. Somebody makes a joke about what's missing (in Ch 4 the "disappointing kit", here "The box is soup"). Then the chapter ends on a line about the case or the future. Ch 6 was a third lap of the chase part. By now I can feel the pattern: night, chase, shelter, sex. The danger is starting to read as the thing that gets them into bed.
- **Dane's thoughts while the flashlight sits on the water.** "Four people had known about that house, and it had burned. Only Russ had found the loft, and nobody had come to the loft. He couldn't stop hearing that." That's Chapter 6's reasoning almost word for word, and Beth already did it there. Same with "Somebody had taken her face off Tess's board in September... and he still couldn't make it add up." I know it doesn't add up. You told me last chapter. Rehashing it is where I drifted.

## Where I got confused

- **"Every kid on this island has been in that window."** It shows up from nowhere. One line she's telling the dating-app joke, the next she's talking about a window I haven't seen yet, and only after that do we get the keeper's house. The whole mile of walking got skipped, and I had to read it twice to find where we were.
- **"Monday was Portland."** I read that as tomorrow. Earlier the grand jury was "Monday the twenty-third", and on Saturday Beth said "nine more nights". So it's Sunday night and Portland is more than a week away? Or is it tomorrow? I can't tell, and it matters, because the last line ("then I'm going to lose you") lands very differently if the clock says one day versus eight.
- **Boston.** "He would go back to Boston." This is the first I've heard of Boston. He was in a motel near the harbor, his father drove a bus in Lowell, and the pickup was to Portland. It isn't wrong, it just felt dropped in.
- **The boss.** Dane has to call his boss at six every morning and night. He's had no signal since the fire, so he's missed at least one call. Does the boss think Beth is dead now? The boss is one of the four suspects, so that's the most interesting thing in the chapter's situation, and the chapter never raises it. I don't know whether that's a deliberate setup or an oversight.
- **Where is everyone?** A summer house burned down on an island this small, with fire visible from the north shore. Tess runs the one fire truck. Russ is the constable. Ned lives there. Nobody comes to the fire, and nobody is out on the shore calling for them. Either the island thinks they're dead or nobody is looking, and the page doesn't tell me which.
- **"It was the nicest thing anyone had said to him in years. He didn't tell her why."** He didn't tell me either. I don't know what "you creak like an old boat" means to him. It reads like a hook for something, and it's too coy for a moment that's supposed to be intimate.

## Where I stopped believing

- **The sex right after near-drowning.** Hours, maybe, after he couldn't make a fist or speak, with his knee locked and his hip hit by a rock. I'll give the genre some room, and the "Hours ago" line tries to cover the gap. But the time jump between "Two laughs now" and "Dane Reyes woke with dry feet" is doing all the work, and I didn't feel it. He goes from hypothermic to "moved hard" too smoothly.
- **The dating-app joke.** "It found me nine men inside fifty miles, and two of them were my cousins." That's a stand-up line about small towns, not something Beth would say. She talks in orders and dry one-liners about what's in front of her (the boots, the coffee, "You fell toward the boat"). This is a canned bit she's pulling in from outside, while dragging a drowned man a mile. It sounds like the writer, not her.
- **The foil.** The box is "soup", but the packets are fine? She went into the sea to her thighs. The coat was hanging on the chair, so maybe it stayed drier. I believed it, but I noticed the book needing it to work.
- **The flashlight man walking off.** He kept his light on the water for ninety-plus seconds, then swept along the rocks and stopped "a body-length short". Then he gives up for good and goes to "tell somebody it was done". He's walked up to windows to look in before firing. This time, with two people possibly alive in the surf below him, he doesn't climb down a few feet? It's convenient. It works once. It's the third time in the book the light has stopped just short of them (Ch 4 under the boathouse, Ch 6 at the boulder, here).

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Callbacks everywhere.** Nearly every beat points back to an earlier chapter: the coat in the snow on the first night ("The first night, in the snow, he had rubbed her arms"), "which meant they were still his" (from "Hurting means they're still yours"), "the way she hauled a ferry rope", the twelve people from Maine, the penny, "she didn't owe anybody", "quiet was how she got when she was afraid" (nearly verbatim from Ch 3), "like the shore rocks", the hour he owed her, "Fair." Any one of these would be lovely. All of them together feel like the book is reading back its own notes to me.
- **Feelings explained outright.**
  - "Counting was what he did with fear." Ch 3 already showed this ("He counted. He counted everything."). Now it's a label.
  - "it was the most naked he had ever been in front of anyone." That says the point of the scene instead of letting it land.
  - "The woman who wouldn't let Tess forgive her a penny, who said she didn't owe anybody with her hand on his taped arm, had just asked him for something for herself... He knew it was the first time she had done that with anyone." This is the worst one. It's a summary of her character arc, written by Dane as an essay. It also repeats Ch 4's "She had never told a man what she liked. She told this one", so it's the same "first time" beat twice.
- **Counting tics.** "That's one laugh" (Ch 4), "Two laughs now, he thought" (here). The witness count, the seconds, the jury, now counting to ninety. "Fair" again. "Polite and slow" was in Ch 4 and Ch 5. It's turning into a checklist of character traits.
- **Neat scene endings.** "He thinks we drowned." / "Two laughs now, he thought." / "and then I'm going to lose you, aren't I?" All three scenes end on a button. The last one in particular is a line nobody says out loud. It's the chapter's theme spoken as dialogue. It would hit harder unsaid, or said more clumsily.
- **"He forgot every plan he had ever made."** Stock romance phrasing.
- **One-word orders in the sex scene.** "Deeper." "Again." "Yes." It's a device, and "Each one changed how she breathed" tells me the device is working rather than letting me feel it. "Both at once" lands. The rest is fine but generic.
- **Both leads joke the same way.** "which tells you about my day", "Best medical I've ever had", "Best I can do with these hands", "You creak like an old boat". Their deadpan has the same rhythm. I couldn't always tell whose quip it was without the tag.

## What I want to know next

- **Who told?** I'm still on the hook. My guess, from the page, is Ned. He was afraid on the ferry in Ch 1, said "you didn't see it", knew the north-side house, and the book keeps reminding me he can't swim and won't wear a life jacket. That feels like it's being loaded for something. Russ is being cleared too loudly (the loft line, twice). Tess's phone call and the board are almost too obvious for her to be the one. The boss is the wild card.
- **Is being dead useful?** "If anybody comes for you now, somebody had to tell them you're alive." That's the best new idea in the chapter: a trap, or a way to smoke out the leak. I want them to use it. If the next chapter just walks them back into town, I'll be let down.
- **What happens to the boss call they missed?**
- **What did the September picture mean?** It's the strongest mystery thread, and the chapter only says it "doesn't add up". I need it to start adding up soon.
- **The romance question** ("then I'm going to lose you") is weaker pull for me than the mystery, because the book has told me "she would never leave this island" without showing me why that's true for him rather than just assumed. I'm not worried about it yet.

So yes, I'd turn the page, but for the mystery, not the couple. **I'd keep reading, but** this chapter is the first one where the plot stood still for half its length, and if Ch 8 opens on another shelter and another warm-up, I'd likely put it down.

## Best moment

The release in the wave:

> "Her hand was still in his. The sea had him now, and it would take whatever he was holding. He opened his hand so it wouldn't take her too."

And it pays off in the dark, in what is the best exchange between them in the book:

> "You let go of my hand."
> "It was going to take you with me."
> "I was holding the rock." An inch from his face, in the dark. "You don't get to decide what I can hold."

It's plain, it's physical, it's a real disagreement between two people who want different things, and nobody explains it.

Close second: "He was shaking so hard his teeth knocked, loud as dice in a cup." Then she puts her hand over his mouth to stop the sound. Fear that's also tenderness, and practical.

## Pull score and scene read

- Scene 1, the wave and the flashlight: **5/5**. The best action in the book. It drops only for the rehash in Dane's head mid-count.
- Scene 2, the walk and the keeper's house: **3/5**. Good stripping-and-warming detail, the gun-hand moment works, and "You don't get to decide what I can hold" is strong. The app joke and the "most naked" line drag it down.
- Scene 3, waking, the "dead" idea, sex, the last line: **3/5**. The "dead" idea is the hook. The sex is competent and does shift things (it's in the dark, she gives the orders, he follows the way he followed her on the rocks). But it's the third intimacy scene in four chapters, and the ending is a speech.
- **Chapter pull: 6/10.** The wave carries it. The second half coasts.

## Prediction

Ned is the leak, or knows who is, and he goes into the water before the end. Being "dead" gets used to flush someone out. The boss turns out clean. Beth ends up at the wheel of the ferry, finally using the captain's license.

## Listener pass (heard, not read)

- The counting ("He got to forty... Sixty... Ninety") works well read aloud.
- "Deeper." "Again." "Yes." will sound awkward in text-to-speech: three clipped one-word lines in a row, read flat.
- Lines like "said, flat" and "she said, flat" repeat close together. You hear it.
- The jump from "two of them were my cousins" to "Every kid on this island has been in that window" is even more confusing by ear. With no visual break, it sounds like she's still on the cousins.
