# Chapter 03 — The Check-In Log: Beat Sheet

**Heading:** Day 2 — Saturday
**POV:** Beth only (close third). No Dane section this chapter. The Pike call reaches the reader only as fragments Beth overhears.
**Target:** about 2,800 words across 5 scenes.
**Chapter promise (outline):** What doesn't she know about Dane, and will the new place hold?

## Pre-flight: continuity and outline conflicts (resolved here, flagged for the orchestrator)

None of these change the plot. Each one is a small conflict between the outline entry and the bible or log, and the default chosen here is noted.

1. **How the prowler gets reported.** World Rule 5 says landlines die about 6 a.m. Day 2, so Beth can't phone Russ. **Default:** Beth tries the kitchen phone at first light and gets no dial tone (this puts the rule on the page). Russ's truck comes down the Harbor Road on his storm rounds, and Beth walks out and flags him down over Dane's objection. That is still "Beth insisted on reporting."
2. **Sunset.** The outline says "after the 4:15 sunset." Bible Research note 37 corrects this to "after dark, a little past four." The bible wins.
3. **The generator "pull cord."** The bible's Ashby place has a 20-kW diesel Generac standby, and a unit that size has no pull cord: it cranks electrically from its own battery. The outline's "her hands on the pull cord" (and the bible's "pull cord's rasp") would be wrong for a heroine who names the specific thing. **Default:** her hands are on the **hand primer lever of the lift pump**, stroking fuel up after weeks of standing, and then on the control panel's crank switch, with Ned's warning that the battery is low. The physical beat (her hands working, his light on them, his breath at her temple) is unchanged. If the orchestrator prefers the literal pull cord, the bible's generator should become a smaller pull-start unit, and Ch 04's fuel-line bleed still works either way. **Flag for Ch 04:** the bleed lesson should be a different job (air in the line after the fuel filter, bled at the injector pump screw) so it doesn't repeat this scene.
4. **"Nobody else has signal."** World Rule 5 says any phone inside the store or within about 50 feet of it works off Tess's booster. **Default:** Dane's own phone picks up a bar inside the store and he notices. Tess explains the booster and jokes about it. The clue still works, since the store is the only place on the island with signal. Dane doesn't use his phone there: the room is public, and the sat phone is his reporting line.
5. **Who chose the Ashby place.** The outline's Ch 03 has Ned hand the key to Beth. The leak table and the Dane red-herring list (Ch 05) say "Dane chose the move and carried the key." **Default:** Beth names three of Ned's empty summer houses (scene 1). Dane picks the Ashby place for his own reasons (one road in, water on three sides, no neighbor with a view). Beth gets the key from Ned (scene 4) and hands it straight to Dane, who carries it. Both versions hold.
6. **"Learns the *Margaret Ann* is in."** Beth already saw the dragger on the guest mooring on Day 1. **Default:** at dawn on Day 2 the guest mooring is empty, and with a quarter mile of visibility in driven rain she can't see where the boat went. At the store she finds it doubled up at the store dock: storm-bound, crew ashore, tied to Tess's float.
7. **First naming of the skipper.** Arsenault has been unnamed so far. Tess calls him "Lyle" once, and Beth's head supplies "Lyle Arsenault, the *Margaret Ann*'s skipper," because he bought her a coffee two summers ago. **Doucette stays unnamed and off the page.** Nobody in a hooded jacket appears; Ch 04 owns that sighting.
8. **Ned's lie and the Thursday runs.** Ch 01 established that Kenny has worked the Thursday night runs since last winter. So Beth hasn't seen that reefer in about a year, and Ned has seen it every week. That fits the bible (Ned has known the truck was wrong for a year). Beth may notice the gap in one clause of narration, but she must not reason from it. She explains Ned away (scene 3).
9. **Ned's "goddamn."** The bible ties it "to the bridge collapse," and the log holds it for Ch 03 "telling the bridge story." These agree: it comes in the retelling at the store. It is Ned's only one in the book.
10. **Dane's notebook.** Ch 04 is where Beth first sees him close it. Here it may appear only as "the inside pocket," where he puts the Ashby key. It is not opened or described.

### Scene 1
**Setting:** The back step off the cottage's mudroom, about 7:15 a.m. Gray daylight, wind still northeast and hard, sleet turned to rain. Under the eave the slush has held a boot print since 11:40 last night. Sensory anchor: rain coming off the eave in a sheet, and the mudroom door knocking against its latch behind her (the sound from Style Sample 2, back again because nobody is holding it now).
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:**
- Enter late: Beth is already crouched on the step in her XtraTufs, her own boot set beside the print, her printed tide card weighted under a beach rock beside it for scale. She photographs both with her phone. The phone works as a camera, and the screen says No Service.
- She reads the print: longer than hers, a lug sole, and the right print pressed deeper than the left at the heel. Whoever it was carries his weight on the right. She files it. She doesn't say "limp" yet, and the reader may remember the Ch 01 walk on their own.
- One line: at 6:20 she lifted the kitchen receiver and got nothing, not even the jammed tone from last night. The landlines are gone.
- Dane comes out with two mugs and stands where the eave covers him and he can see down the side yard. She states her conclusion before he asks: "He's never been in this house, and he'll come back to it." Then the reasons, only because he asks.
- The harbor through the rain from the step: the guest mooring ball is bare, bobbing in the slop. The dragger is gone from it. A quarter mile of visibility, and she can't see where it went.
- Beth counts the days: eight left on Ned's ten, if the state finds a barge.
- They decide quickly and plainly. Dane: they move tonight. Beth names three of Ned's shut summer houses. Dane asks two questions about each (how many roads in, who can see the door) and picks the Ashby place on the north point: one gravel drive, water on three sides, no neighbor with a window on it. She notices he chose it the way she'd choose a mooring.
- Beth: "Hiding gets noticed out here. Being seen is normal. I'm going to the store." She needs to see who is storm-bound, and they need water, food and fuel for a house with its water shut off. Dane objects once, with his reason (a crowded room he can't control) and his condition: daylight, before noon, she stays where he can see her, and she tells him who in the room is from away. She agrees, because those are good terms.
**Subtext:** Both of them are working the same problem as professionals, and both notice the other doing it well. Beth is also deciding, without saying so, that she won't hand over her island. She will walk into it.
**Tension source:** Situational (the print, the dead phone, the missing boat) and a light interpersonal pull over the store: his control against her knowledge, settled by terms rather than a fight.
**Ends on:** An image, not a line. Beth lifts the rock off the tide card. Rain off the eave is already running into the print and softening its edges, and by the time she has the card in her pocket the heel is gone.
**Word count target:** ~400 (short. It sets up the decisions and leaves).

### Scene 2
**Setting:** The cottage, about 10:00 a.m. Beth is in the mudroom, stepping into her Grundéns bibs and dry socks. Dane is outside on the sheltered side of the house under open sky, which is the only way the Iridium works. The mudroom wall is one layer of board and shingle. Sensory anchor: the wind's rhythm against the north gable, and the lulls between gusts when sound comes through the wall.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Russ Whitcomb. Pike and Quist are on the call; Beth hears only Dane.
**Surface action:**
- Dane told her at breakfast that he calls Portland at ten. Beth isn't listening for it. She is pulling up her bibs when a lull drops the wind and his voice is suddenly just outside the boards.
- Fragments only, with gusts between them. "Go ahead." A long silence. "When?" Silence. **"No. She doesn't know."** A gust eats something. **"... Daily. Understood."** Nothing that names money, a deposit or Quist. Do not put "nine thousand" or any number in his mouth.
- Beth stands still with one strap over her shoulder and counts the length of the lull, eleven seconds, because counting is what her body does when it goes quiet.
- She decides on the spot not to ask. Asking would tell him she listened, and she doesn't yet know who "she" is. It could be her, or someone in Portland, or nobody she knows. She files it.
- Dane comes in. He is exactly as he was: wet shoulders, level voice. One small tell she can read but not decode: he sits at the kitchen table in the chair against the wall with the window on his left, the one he has taken every time, and she takes the other chair without being asked. She notices that she did. He looks at her hands on the mug a moment longer than the mug needs.
- Russ's truck comes down the Harbor Road at a crawl on his storm rounds, wipers going. Beth is up and into her float coat. Dane: "The fewer people who know someone tried that door —" Beth: an unreported prowler gets talked about on this island more than a reported one. Somebody saw the tracks, or saw a man from away on her porch at midnight, and by supper it will be a story with no constable in it. Reported, it's Russ's business and nobody's gossip. Dane hears the logic and lets her go. He follows to the dooryard and stands where he can see the road both ways.
- Russ at the truck window, cap on, leaning on the door frame as if standing were optional. Beth gives him the facts: 11:40, the latch, one man, the print (gone now, but she has the photo). Russ is wry and competent. He takes it seriously and says so in a joke. He looks Dane over and asks nothing about him. Then, as he puts the truck in gear: "Where you sleeping tonight? Not asking for me. Asking because somebody will."
- Dane answers before Beth can: "She's covered," or something equally flat that gives Russ nothing. Russ takes his cap off, puts it back on, and smiles as if that answers his question too.
**Subtext:** Beth has just learned there is something Dane is keeping, and she does what she always does with something she can't check: she sets it aside and watches. Taking the other chair is the first sign she is learning him, and it happens in the same minute she starts to doubt him. Russ's question is either neighborliness or reconnaissance, and Beth can't tell which. (It is neighborliness, see the bible, but nothing on the page says so.)
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and internal. The overheard secret sits in the room unspoken. Russ's question lands on a nerve because it is the exact question someone dangerous would ask.
**Ends on:** Russ's line, "Asking because somebody will," then the truck pulling away. **This is the chapter's one button ending.**
**Word count target:** ~600.

### Scene 3
**Setting:** Marr's General Store, about 11:15 a.m. The bell on the door. Coffee and kerosene. The floor creaks where the sills are rotten. The woodstove is held at 58°F, with Tess's soup pot for Mrs. Dyer on its back corner. Out back, the store generator's flat roar, the only engine running on Harbor Road. Through the front window, a red hull doubled up at the store dock with deck lights on at midday and a radio playing country. Sensory anchor: the roar of the generator under every conversation, and the light in the store being the only electric light Beth has seen since last night.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Tess Marr, Ned Haskell, Lyle Arsenault. (The room may hold background islanders for texture, but none of them speaks or is named.)
**Surface action (in this order):**
1. **Enter.** Dane takes the spot by the stove with his back to the post office wall and his right side clear. Beth goes to the counter. Tess comes around it: a hand on Beth's arm, a tug on her braid, "Bethie." She says something warm and something about money (what generator gas costs this week, what she's carrying on credit for people who can't pay).
2. **The bridge story.** Ned is on the stool by the stove in his green Johnson Woolen jacket, telling the room about the slip as if it were weather: the surge, the lift cables parting "like a **goddamn** rifle shot," the bridge going down at the seaward end and twisting. Short and slow, with long pauses. Tess calls him "Captain" to tease him off it.
3. **The barge.** Ned: "District says they've got a crane barge —" Tess, ringing up Beth's water jugs without looking up: "— out of Rockland Wednesday, if it lays down." Ned: "I'll put it out at noon." Beth registers that it hasn't been on the radio. Her explanation comes in one clause: he called the district from Tess's back office, the only phone on the island, and he tells Tess everything, which the whole island knows.
4. **The log.** While Tess bags groceries (water, canned goods, a tub of coffee, batteries, enough for a house with no water), Beth reads the spiral check-in log upside down on the counter. Friday, 1800 net, in Tess's hand: **Calloway — home (phone).** Saturday's 0800 line carries a tick beside her name. Tess sees her looking and squares the log to the counter edge. Beth thinks only: off the air, the way she asked. The prose must not hint that the line is dangerous. The reader does that work.
5. **The phone.** Tess's phone, face-down by the register, buzzes. Tess picks it up already smiling, says "Hold that thought, hon," and goes behind the curtain into the back office. Dane comes to the counter for coffee. His phone, in his hand to check, shows one bar. When Tess comes back he says something mild about it. Tess: "Booster's on the generator. Only signal on the island, so don't you tell anybody or I'll have the whole town in here charging." This is Dane and Tess's one counter conversation: she is friendly, he is polite. Beth thinks: the booster, of course. That explains it.
6. **Arsenault.** The bell. A tall man in a good Musto jacket, clean-shaven, which no one on a dragger is. "Pack of Golds, Tess. And whatever the gentleman's having." Dane declines, with thanks. Arsenault: "Rough night to be from away." He looks at Dane once, the length of a handshake. Tess rings him up wrong, voids it and rings again. Beth reads it as Tess hating a cash drawer with a man waiting. Beth's head supplies Lyle Arsenault, the *Margaret Ann*'s skipper, a coffee two summers ago, and that is all she feels. She looks past him through the window at the red hull at Tess's dock and counts its lines: four, doubled. The boat she lost at dawn has been here all morning. He goes out. Nobody in a hood comes in with him.
7. **The reefer.** Dane, back at the stove, asks Ned quietly whether the dealer's reefer was running when they loaded Thursday. Ned's answer is over-detailed and doesn't answer the question: "He always runs his unit. Always has. Thermo King, it's a good one, they run all night on those." Dane repeats it back in fewer words. Beth stands four feet away with her palm still cold from that dark box. She knows the unit wasn't running. She knows Ned's tell, more detail than anyone asked for, the way she knows the boat's engine note, and has since she was 22. For a single clause of narration she notes that Kenny has had the Thursdays since last winter and Ned has seen that truck every week she hasn't. Then she explains it away in her own terms: Ned lies about the boat's business, the weights and manifests and the freight contract, and he always has, to keep it running. He doesn't lie about people. She says nothing in front of the room.
**Subtext:** Beth came to read the island and she is reading it accurately, except where it is hers. Every clue to Tess passes through her hands and comes out as Tess being Tess. "She doesn't know" surfaces in her head once, when Arsenault looks at Dane and Dane doesn't look away: one line, no reasoning. Dane, for his part, is watching Ned's hands and Arsenault's jacket, and Beth sees him watching without knowing what he concludes.
**Tension source:** Situational (a skipper from away in the room, his boat at the dock, a stranger beside her whom everyone in the room has seen) and internal (catching Ned in a lie and choosing loyalty).
**Ends on:** A gesture. Ned reaches up with one finger and switches off his hearing aid, then turns on the stool to look out the window at the rain. The conversation is over. Beth picks up the bag of jugs and says, to no one in particular, that she needs a tank filled.
**Word count target:** ~850. This is the longest scene and the one to cut first if the chapter runs long: trim items 1 and 2 (texture, background, prices) before any plant in items 3–7.

### Scene 4
**Setting:** The propane cage behind the store, about noon. Chain-link around the cylinders, rain dripping off the shed roof, and the store generator ten feet away, roaring loud enough to cover anything said at the cage. Sensory anchor: the generator's exhaust heat on one side of her face and the cold on the other, and the rust on the cage padlock leaving orange on her fingers.
**Characters present:** Beth, Ned, Dane (watching from the store's back doorway, out of earshot).
**Surface action:**
- Ned comes out after her without being asked, with the cage key on its float. He fills a 20-lb cylinder from the bulk tank because it is something to do with his hands.
- Dane stands in the back doorway where he can see her and the lane. Beth stands with her back to the generator so the roar is behind her words.
- She asks for the Ashby place: not why, only for how long. Ned doesn't ask either. He works the key off his ring, a brass Schlage with a paper tag he tears off and puts in his own pocket so nothing on it says Ashby. One practical warning: the generator hasn't run since the power went, the battery is down, so prime it before she cranks. Then: "Nobody but me and God."
- His hand stays on hers around the key for a beat. "You stay off my boat till this is done." That line is protective. Read later, it is guilty. Beth hears only the protection.
- She doesn't mention the reefer, and he doesn't either. She notes that they both don't.
**Subtext:** Two people who love each other and have each just caught the other keeping something. Beth's choice to trust him is the flaw the bible names: it is a mistake about a person, not about the water. It looks reasonable on the page, and it is how the location will leak (Ned tells Tess on Day 3).
**Tension source:** Interpersonal, all unspoken: the lie at the stove ten minutes ago between them, with the key handed over under it.
**Ends on:** An action. Beth walks the cylinder to the back door and hands Dane the key without a word. He puts it in the inside pocket of his jacket, the same pocket his hand went to on the porch last night, and zips it. She watches the zipper close.
**Word count target:** ~300 (deliberately short. It is one exchange).

### Scene 5 — the romance beat
**Setting:** The Ashby place on the north point, after dark, a little past four, then the evening. They walk out the north road in the dark with packs and jugs. Beth's pickup stays in her own dooryard so the cottage looks lived-in (her call: a known truck parked at the Ashby drive would give them away to anyone passing). The house: mothballs, cold wallpaper, furniture under sheets. The generator shed: concrete pad, diesel smell, the green Generac housing, a gap barely a body wide between the unit and the shed wall. Sensory anchor: total dark inside the shed except Dane's flashlight, and the cold coming up off the concrete.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:**
- Enter late: they are already inside the shed. (No walk montage. A sentence or two of the road at most, in her count of the driveway's length in steps.)
- Beth opens the housing. Dane holds the light. She finds the lift pump and works the hand primer lever, counting strokes under her breath, until the resistance changes and fuel is up. Her hands are cold and quick, smelling of diesel. His light stays exactly on her hands, steady, and she notices he hasn't moved it once.
- To reach the control panel she has to come back through the gap. He doesn't step out of it in time, or the shed doesn't give him room. She is turned half into him with her shoulder against his chest, his breath at her temple, the light now pointed at the floor between their boots. Neither of them moves. She can feel him breathing through her float coat. This is the one held moment in the chapter: two or three sentences, physical and specific. No kiss and no hands where they shouldn't be.
- She reaches past him and holds the crank switch. The starter grinds on the low battery, once, twice, and the diesel catches. The single bulb in the shed ceiling comes on, and they are suddenly lit, closer than either of them would have let the dark make them.
- **He steps back first.** The bible requires a visible tell that his restraint is care: his free hand starts toward her, stops, and goes to the shed door instead, which he holds open against the wind for her, and he takes the heavier jug from beside the pad without being asked.
- Crossing the dark yard to the house, the generator roaring behind them, she asks him the direct question, since she has decided to: why is he out here alone? He answers plainly, a little longer than she would: a one-day job out of Portland, the office short that month, a second deputy meant to meet them at Pruett, a storm that came twelve hours early. One dry line at his own expense (he was supposed to be back at the motel by supper; his boots are wrong for every surface on this island and he knew it when he packed them). It is true as far as it goes, and she can hear that it is true.
- Inside: they keep one room lit at the back, shades down. Dane checks the sightline from the drive. Beth picks the sheeted room off the kitchen and counts the steps from the couch to the back door, nine, before she sits.
- Later (a short fade, no summary): around nine she hears the porch door and his boots, then the low voice under the generator, too far and too low for words. The nightly call. She doesn't get up to listen. In her head "Daily. Understood." sits in his voice beside the voice from the shed, and she doesn't try to make them agree.
**Subtext:** She wanted the moment in the shed, and so did he. He chose to step back, and she saw him choose. His candor on the walk makes the morning's "She doesn't know" both harder to believe and harder to ignore. The house is safe as far as she can count it, and she counts it anyway.
**Tension source:** Physical proximity in the dark (the romance), set against the unspoken overheard call (suspicion). Situational: a new house she doesn't know as well as her own, with one road in.
**Ends on:** An action, not a line or a question. Beth gets up off the couch, sets her XtraTufs by the back door with the toes facing out, the way her father left his boots at sea, and lies down again in her clothes with the porch voice still going. That sets up both halves of the promise (what she doesn't know about him; whether the place will hold) without asking either in narration.
**Word count target:** ~650. This scene has to carry the chapter's feeling. If the draft runs over, take words from scene 3's texture, never from here.
