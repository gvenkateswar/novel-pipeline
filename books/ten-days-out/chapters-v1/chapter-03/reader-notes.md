# Reader Notes — Chapter 3: The Check-In Log

**Verdict:** I'm still reading and this is the strongest chapter so far, but I'm 30% into a book sold as open-door and all I've had is one accidental lean in a generator shed. And the plot just had the hero do something no marshal would do: he let the safe house go through the one man we watched lie.

---

## Where I was gripped

- **The overheard call at ten.** "No. She doesn't know." / "—daily. Understood." It lands because Beth hears it through one board and a course of shingle with her bibs half on, so there's no way she can ask about it. That's a good trap for a character to be in. The wind eating the middle of the call is a cheap device, but it worked on me.
- **Ned and the reefer.** "He always runs his unit. Always has. Thermo King, it's a good one, they run all night on those." Then Dane repeats it flat: "He always runs it." My stomach dropped with hers. In Chapter 1 we were told Ned's tell is saying more than anyone asked for. Here it pays off without anyone pointing at it. The hearing aid going off again afterwards is a nice echo of Chapter 1.
- **Arsenault at the counter.** The clean-shaven detail ("No one on a dragger was clean-shaven"), Tess ringing the Golds wrong, and her fingers slowing on the drawer with him standing over it. The red hull Beth "lost at dawn" turns out to have been tied at Tess's float all morning, with four lines, all doubled. That's a real chill.
- **The generator shed.** This is the first time I felt the romance as a romance, not as two competent people respecting each other. See Best moment.

## Where I got bored or skimmed

- **Ned retelling the bridge collapse to the room.** "Sea came in under her. Lifted the whole boat." We saw this happen at the end of Chapter 2. Tess's "We've got the picture. Some of us were in it" is funny, but it's funny because the scene is redundant, and I was skimming before she said it.
- **The three caretaker houses.** "Lathams' has two. Hallorans' has the road and the church lot..." It's a menu read out as Q&A. I skimmed to the answer, which I could see coming as soon as "water on three sides" came up.
- **Tess's grocery list and the barge logistics** ("out of Rockland Wednesday, if it lays down"). That's texture, and there's a lot of it in the store scene before the scene gets going. My attention only came back when the bell rang for Arsenault.
- **The counting.** Primer strokes "Twelve. Twenty. Thirty-one" to thirty-eight. Three hundred and forty steps. Nine steps to the back door. Eleven seconds of lull. Nine boats. Four lines. In Chapter 1 the counting was character. By Chapter 3 it's a tic I've started to notice, and once I notice it I stop believing it's her and start seeing the author.

## Where I got confused

- **Italic *She doesn't know.* when Arsenault looks at Dane.** Why does Beth's mind go there at that moment? Does she think Arsenault and Dane know each other? That Arsenault is the "she" being kept in the dark? Or is it just floating anxiety? I couldn't tell what I was supposed to infer, and it felt like it was meant to be a clue.
- **Phones.** In Chapter 2 Beth calls Tess on the landline and Tess has no signal. Now the landline is dead and Tess's back office is "the only phone on the island now", and then it turns out Tess also has the only cell signal, through a booster. "The booster. Of course." I had to stop and sort out who can call whom. The "Of course" sounds like Beth just worked something out, but I don't know what. That Tess has a private line out? That Tess took a call right as Dane walked in? Spell it out or don't wink.
- **"Mrs. Dyer's. Tess carried it up the hill at one."** Odd tense. Has she already done it? Will she? It's one line, but it snagged.
- **The shore path to Ashby's "at low water. Nobody comes in that way."** Then why mention it? It's either a Chekhov's gun or Beth is wrong, and I can't tell which she thinks.
- **The boot print.** The right heel is deeper and the hooded man in Chapter 1 favored his left leg. I think that's consistent (he takes the weight off the bad leg), but I had to flip back and work it out. Dane "looked at the heel longest" and said nothing, so I know it matters. I just wasn't sure I'd read it correctly.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Going to Ned for the Ashby key.** This is the big one. Two minutes after Beth and Dane both catch Ned lying about the reefer, she asks him for the safe house, by name, "till the barge." Dane, who heard the lie himself and has just picked the house because "nobody" can see its door, stands in the doorway and lets it happen. "Nobody but me and God" is a lovely line, but the marshal has just told the one suspect he has where his witness sleeps. Beth's reasoning ("he didn't lie about people") is her own, and I can believe it from her. I can't believe it from Dane. Unless this turns out to be a deliberate trap, it reads as the plot needing them at the Ashby place.
- **No extraction after an attempted break-in.** In Chapter 2 we were told LifeFlight lands on the ballfield. Someone tried the witness's door at midnight, and the next day it's just rain, not a storm. Why isn't Portland moving heaven and earth for a helicopter or a Coast Guard boat? Dane's "one-day job" explanation covers why he's alone. It doesn't cover why he's still alone.
- **Russ's "Where you sleeping tonight? Not asking for me. Asking because somebody will."** It's a good line, but he says it twice and then smiles "as if that answered his question too." It's too pointed. It felt planted, so I mentally filed Russ as a red herring instead of a person.

## Is the attraction registering? Do I want them together?

It's registering now, a little. The details work: he looks at her hands on the mug "a second longer than the mug needed"; she takes the second chair "before she thought about it. Then she thought about it."; in Chapter 2 she held out the towel before deciding to. The shed is the first real charge. His hand coming up and stopping, then going to the door instead, is good restraint.

But for an open-door book this is slow. At roughly a third of the way in I've had one accidental body contact and no conversation where either of them says anything personal, except his boots-for-a-parking-lot answer. Do I want them together? I respect them both. I don't want them yet, because I know almost nothing about Dane as a man. The "A wife." line is the most interesting romantic beat in the chapter, and it's a suspicion, not a desire. Right now I'm reading for the leak, not for the couple.

## Do the leads sound different?

Not enough. In Chapter 2 they're set up as mirrors ("She'd counted him the way he had counted the slip"), and that's fine as a concept. On the page, though, both talk in the same short declaratives, both read terrain in lists, and both keep their reasons to themselves. Dane gets the slightly drier lines ("I'm the waiting room," "I'm going to stop arguing with you about water," "I packed these boots for a parking lot"), and those are the only times he sounds like a separate person. Beth has no humor at all. The secondary characters sound far more distinct than the leads: Tess ("Christ on a bike," "Hell's bells," "That's why I like it"), Ned's pauses, Russ's cap.

## Where it sounded like AI

- "He heard it. She watched him hear it."
- "It was true. She could hear that it was true. It went as far as it went."
- "Those were good terms."
- "Neither of them said *reefer*. They both noticed." Too neat. The scene had already shown me this.
- "She didn't try to make them agree." The chapter ends on a tidy thematic line. Chapters 1 and 2 also end on a crafted beat (the light switched off, "She didn't step out."). Three out of three chapters close on the same kind of quiet final image.
- The filing phrase is on its third outing: "put it where she put the things she'd look at later" (Ch 1), "She put it where she put things" (Ch 2), "put it where she put things" plus "She filed it." (Ch 3). It's now a verbal tic.
- The rhythm of fragment after fragment after fragment ("Coffee and kerosene." "The bell." "Lug sole, deep cleats, a chevron at the instep.") is relentless. It's controlled and it's good, but it never changes gear.

## Who I suspect of leaking her whereabouts

1. **Tess** is my main suspect. On the night of the drop, the fuel float was lit four hours past her 6 o'clock closing for the *Margaret Ann*. She has an $1,800-a-month loan "off her neck." Beth told her "I'm home" at 5:40 and the latch was tried at 11:40. Her laugh "came late." She has the island's only cell signal and took a call in the back office the moment the marshal came in. She went slow on the drawer with Arsenault standing over it. She runs the check-in net and logs every name. That's almost too many clues, which makes me half-suspect she's the red herring. But she's the only person who knew Beth was home that night, apart from Dane.
2. **Dane's side: Pike or someone in Portland.** The book clearly wants me to suspect Dane himself (the 11:20 call, the latch at 11:40, "She doesn't know," "daily"). It's a romance, so I don't believe it's him. I do now suspect whoever is on the other end of that sat phone.
3. **Ned** is complicit in the smuggling, I'd bet: the lie, the hearing aid, giving Kenny the Thursdays, never looking at the lit float. But I don't think he'd sell Beth. He's going to be the heartbreak, not the leak. And now he knows about the Ashby place.
4. **Russ** looks like a red herring from that one over-pointed question.

Arsenault is the muscle and the money, not the leak. I'm watching for whoever limps on the left.

## What I want to know next

- What did Pike say that Dane is keeping from her? Who is "she," and what is "daily"?
- Does anyone come to the Ashby place tonight, and if so, did it come through Ned?
- Is Ned running the drugs, or just looking the other way for freight money?
- Who's the hooded man with the left-leg hitch, and is he still on the island?
- Will the ferry be back by Sunday the 22nd for the Monday grand jury? (The ticking clock works.)
- When does the romance actually start?

That's plenty pulling me forward. The suspense hooks are strong. The romance hook is weak.

## Would I keep reading?

Yes, mostly for the mystery and the island. If Chapter 4 doesn't give me a real scene between them (talk or touch, not another near-miss), I'd start to feel I bought the wrong book.

## Best moment

> She came back through the gap. He hadn't stepped out of it, or the shed hadn't left him room to. She came up turned half into him with her shoulder against his chest, and the light fell to the floor between their boots. His breath was at her temple, warm and slow, and then not slow. She could feel him breathing through her float coat. Neither of them moved.
>
> She reached past him and held the crank switch over.
>
> The starter ground once on the low battery. Twice. The diesel caught and came up to a roar, and the bulb in the ceiling came on, and there they were, lit, closer than the dark had let them be.

"And then not slow" and "closer than the dark had let them be" are the best romance writing in the book so far. Having the generator kick on so the light catches them is exactly the right move.

## Cross-chapter observations

- **Chapter 1** is excellent. It's the hook: the sprung lid, Beth pressing it shut and saying "You'll lose your count," the four brake lights. I'd have bought the book on that scene.
- **Chapter 2** carries the barometer readings too far. Every scene ends with a tapped glass and a number, and I started counting the taps, which I suspect is not the intended effect. Chapter 3 drops them, which is a relief.
- **The POV switch** in Chapter 2 (Dane for one section, then Beth) is the only time we've been in Dane's head. Chapter 3 is all Beth. If the book is dual POV, Dane has gone missing. If it's Beth-only, that Chapter 2 opening is an anomaly. In either case I know Dane only from outside, and that's part of why the romance isn't landing.
- **Night-call structure.** Each chapter closes at night with a call or a sound and Beth lying awake in her clothes. That's three in a row now. It's a good pattern, but it's becoming the pattern.
- **Setting** is the book's real strength: the Thermo King, the binders, the propane cage, the check-in net, the canvas money bag on the ferry. I believe in Cutter Island completely.

## Three chapters in

A tight, well-observed island suspense with a heroine I trust. The hook is the best thing in the book and the setting is sharp. The leak mystery is seeded well, maybe over-seeded around Tess. The weak spots: the hero is mostly a silhouette, the two leads talk alike, the counting and filing tics are wearing thin, every chapter ends on the same kind of crafted line, and it's very slow on heat for a book sold as open-door. The Ashby-key scene is the first plot choice I didn't buy.
