# Reader Notes — Chapter 9 (Car Deck, No Lights)

Read: chapters 1–9, final.md only.

**One-line verdict:** A tense climax that pays off nearly every plant from Chapter 1. It's let down by the main villain being arrested offstage in one line, and by a hero whose big moment is getting tangled in a chain.

---

## Was it tense?

Yes, mostly. The tension comes from the two sections in the middle: Dane in the alley listening to the paddle come closer ("the drip arriving before the next stroke instead of after"), then Beth in her socks on the plates. The radio call to Tess is the best-built suspense in the book. The open carrier, "a woman in a back office with the mic up at her mouth and nothing coming out," followed by "Okay, Bethie," made my stomach drop.

The tension falls off as soon as Doucette is in the water. Everything after the cuffs (the Russ report, the Arsenault walk, the Ned broadcast) reads like a tidy-up checklist.

## Could I follow the fight in the dark?

Mostly. I always knew where Beth was, because the book has spent nine chapters teaching me this deck: the bulwark cap, the bin lip, the ring buoy bracket, the gatepost, the thigh-high gate chain with water four feet below. The ring buoy and the chain across the transom from Chapter 1 paying off here felt earned, and I enjoyed noticing them.

What I had to reread:
- **"Somebody was fighting the chain one-handed and losing, a sleeve fouled in the binder run."** I worked out it was Dane, but only because of "one-handed." For a second I didn't know which man was down and which was getting up. "A man was getting up off the plates. Only one." That line is deliberately ambiguous, and it worked as a scare, but I lost the thread for a beat.
- **After the shot, how does Doucette track her?** She's silent, in socks, moving. "The heel came after her, fast and uneven." Is he following sound? Did she make noise on purpose? He has a gun and has just fired at her voice, so why does he close to touching distance instead of firing again? It reads as if he's coming to her so she can do the shoulder move.
- **How he ends up in the water:** clear enough. Shoulder into the hip, the chain at thigh height, he goes over. "It held. He didn't." is a good line. It also felt a little easy: one hip-check from a deckhand ends the climax. The bad left leg makes it believable, since the setup says his weight is coming onto that leg. I bought it, but just barely.

## Did the channel 9 call make sense? Did I see how it would reach the boat?

Yes. Beth to Tess on 9, Tess passes it on to whoever is standing over her (Arsenault, it turns out), and Doucette comes in from the dragger by outboard and then paddle. The lies are clever and I liked catching them: Ned "gone up to Russ's" while Russ is standing at the gate, and Dane "down with a fever." "She'd left out two things, and she ran them once to be sure" is clear without being spelled out too much.

One itch I couldn't scratch. Chapter 1 made a big point that "every boat in the bay listened to that radio, and every kitchen with a scanner." Now Beth broadcasts on the fixed set, the one that "carried," and says "keep it off the air" while on the air. I accept that 9 is "their old channel," but I wondered who else heard her say she was alone on the boat. The book doesn't address it.

## Did Tess's "water side" land? Did the Beth–Tess moment work?

"Water side—" followed by a clatter landed hard. It's the turn of the book: the leak warns the target. I didn't need it explained, but Beth explains it to herself anyway ("Not the pier. The stern, and the port quarter ladder..."). Then "What Tess had just done would get her killed tonight" says what I already felt.

The gangway moment is the best thing in the chapter. Twelve feet of brow, the red band on Tess's wrist, and the old braid-tug reach stopping in the air and coming back down. Nobody says anything. Ned walking beside her with "a foot of cold air" between their sleeves did a lot with almost nothing. Tess never speaks in person in this chapter, and that's right for now, but I'm owed her voice in Chapter 10.

## Did Dane get enough to do?

Not really. His POV section is good on mood and gives him one strong choice: "he would not fire into a dark she was somewhere in." Then his actual action is a tackle that ends with him tangled in chain and losing, and afterwards he frees himself (using the binder Beth showed him, which is a nice touch), helps haul, and cuffs. The heroine owning the climax suits this book. But a male lead with a gun who spends the fight fouled in a tie-down reads as sidelined, not supporting. His kiss afterwards ("kissed her once and let her go") is nice but small. I wanted one beat where his training matters as much as her deck sense.

He also goes over Marisol again ("Four years ago a woman had asked him for ten minutes"). By now it reads as a repeated ritual more than something he's feeling.

## Too easy / too convenient

- **Arsenault's arrest happens offstage.** The man running the whole thing is taken by a part-time constable, offscreen, summed up as "He had her by the wrist and the radio by its cord. I asked him for both." It's a good line of dialogue, but it's a letdown for the villain. Then he walks down the pier "like a man going to look at a boat he might buy" and says nothing. Why was the boss personally in the store holding the radio? Convenient.
- Beth sending Russ away leaves a one-armed man and a woman in socks to hold the boat. She justifies it, but it's reckless, and the book doesn't let anyone push back.
- Doucette dropping the gun as he goes over the chain ("Still aboard") removes the biggest problem in one sentence.
- "The sea already allowed it." The weather clearing right on cue has been on the clock all book, so I didn't mind it much.
- The other crewman "still aboard her with the lights on... Marine Patrol can have him." And who fired from the gray skiff at the landing? Russ's briefing says "Nobody saw the shooter." Was that Doucette? The other hand? I want that closed.

## Confusing

- The opening paragraph does a lot of staging at once (floods on, sodiums off, two trucks named, Ned's duffel "on his good side"). "Good side" has meant Dane's arm all book, so for a second I thought this was Dane.
- The section stamps "eleven o'clock" (Dane) and then "ten past eleven" (Beth) are fine, but Beth throws the breakers in the eleven o'clock radio section, and Dane hears the floods go out after the outboard cuts. I had to line up the clocks to check this matched. It does, but barely.
- Cross-chapter: in Chapter 8, "Then she had split the round" means a kindling round. In a book with gunshots, I read it as a bullet for a second.

## Where it sounded like AI

- Russ's cap ritual again: "took off his cap, looked into it a while, and put it back on." That's at least the fifth time. It was charming in Chapter 3. Now it's a tic the writer reaches for.
- "She knew the walk." This exact line is in Chapters 7, 8 and 9.
- Clipped fragment pairs that tell me the significance: "Not where. Never where." / "Her father's call. The bank had the boat. The words were still hers." / "*Keep it off the air.* The Day 1 words. Tess had carried them once already. She'd carry them again." That last one explains a callback I had already caught.
- Russ's opening briefing ("The shot at the landing came off a gray skiff from away... Quist's people tie that skiff to Arsenault...") and Dane's "Shane Doucette, twenty-nine, Arsenault's sternman, with a bad left leg" both recap things I already know.
- Every section ends on a neat closing image or line. The chapter ends with Ned's radio call, which echoes Chapter 2 exactly. It's a good echo, but by now I can predict the shape of every chapter ending.
- Across the book: "right side clear," "put it where she put things," "Walk me through it," counting steps, "Christ on a bike." Each worked at first. Together they make the characters feel assembled from tags.

## What I want to know next (Chapter 10 has to deliver)

- **Tess, in her own voice.** Why? The tank loan, fear, Arsenault's leverage? How long? Did she know the shed would burn, or about the gun at the landing? And what does Beth say to her? A silent reach at the gangway is the right move for Chapter 9 and the wrong one for the ending. I need the conversation.
- **Ned.** A year of looking the other way for the subsidy. Does he face charges? Does he lose the boat? And the master's license in Beth's drawer, "Asking Ned for the wheel is asking him to be old," is the most obvious unfired gun in the book. If Beth doesn't take the wheel, I'll feel cheated.
- **The testimony.** Monday in Portland. The $9,000 has to be cleared, and the defense will use the relationship. I want to see her say "I chose it. In daylight" under oath, or close to it, since the book has promised that twice.
- **The romance.** Dane is off the case, facing internal affairs, from Lowell, and lives on the mainland. I need a concrete answer to how this works, not just a look across the brow. The emotional repair from the notebook happened in Chapter 8, so Chapter 10 has to be about the future, not forgiveness again.
- Who fired from the gray skiff, and what happens to the other crewman.

## Would I read a book 2 on this island?

Probably, for the island. Cutter is the real star: the check-in net, the bank bag on the ferry, the fuel float, scanners next to the bread. I'd come back for Russ, who is the most likeable person in the book, and I'd buy a Russ book on the strength of "I asked him for both." I'd also come back for a Tess-aftermath story. I would not come back for Dane and Beth again. Their arc feels finished, and her deck-and-weather cleverness would be hard to repeat without it turning into a formula.

## Best moment

> The speaker hissed. Somebody at the store had keyed and was holding it. The carrier ran open, a dry empty rush, the sound of a woman in a back office with the mic up at her mouth and nothing coming out.
>
> Beth wiped the grille of the mic on her sleeve.
>
> "Okay, Bethie."

Close second: Tess's hand coming up toward the braid at the foot of the gangway and stopping in the air.

## Cross-chapter observations (1–9)

- **Strongest stretch:** Chapters 1, 2 and 7. Chapter 1's freight-deck discovery is a great opener. The Chapter 7 notebook reveal and the "*She doesn't know*" payoff ("The she was her") is the best twist in the book. I didn't see it coming, and once I saw it, it was fair.
- **Where I'd most likely have drifted:** the middle of Chapter 3 (the Russ stop, then the store, then the propane cage) and the Day 5 store scene in Chapter 6. Both are a lot of island texture with little forward motion.
- **The heat:** the scenes in 5, 6 and 8 each change something. Chapter 5 is consent plus the "who knew" reckoning, Chapter 6 is losing track of the door, and Chapter 8 is "in daylight, in the record." The trauma-kit joke is the warmest moment between them in the book. Chapter 8's bunk scene is the best of the three because of the arm-on-the-rail business.
- **The leak whodunit:** it works, but the solution reaches Beth in Chapter 8 through a dense block of inner deduction (ledger, emergency management, bank bag, the braid not being tugged), not through a scene. The "Tess stopped touching me" clue is lovely and I only felt it in hindsight.
- **Voice sameness:** Beth, Dane and Ned all talk in the same short, flat, declarative way. Tess and Russ are the only voices I could pick out with my eyes closed.
