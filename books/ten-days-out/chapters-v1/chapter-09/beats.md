# Chapter 09 — Car Deck, No Lights: Beat Sheet

**Heading:** Day 8 to Day 9 — Friday to Saturday
**Target:** about 3,400 words across six scenes
**POV:** Beth (Scenes 1–3, 5–6); Dane (Scene 4, about 600 words, his third share)
**Chapter promise to set up (from outline):** What happens to Tess, and to them?

---

## Conflicts flagged before the beats

1. **Outline vs. World Rule 7 (the fish house).** The outline's Key event has Beth tell Tess that Dane is "feverish at the fish house." World Rule 7 says the fish house is never mentioned to anyone else, and the Ch 08 payoff depends on it never leaking. Naming it on channel 9 would also send the network to Dane instead of to the boat. **Resolution in these beats:** Beth says Dane is down with the arm and running a fever, and that she "left him where he's warm." She does not name the place. The plot is unchanged.
2. **Outline vs. the cold-read rule (counting).** The Dane section has him hear the outboard cut "and count." The bible says Beth counts and Dane does not. **Resolution:** Dane judges the distance and the closing by sound (strokes getting nearer, the drip off a paddle blade, the hull touching steel). He never counts.
3. **Outline order (opening image).** The outline opens on Beth's thumb on the mic. Russ has to be aboard and in position before Tess hears the lie, and he can't be seen walking down the pier after the call (the lie puts Ned at Russ's house). **Resolution:** Scene 1 is Russ's recruitment and the positions, at dusk. Scene 2 opens on the thumb-on-the-mic image. Only the order changes.
4. **"Dane cuffs him at the ramp."** The bow ramp is up and the starboard side lies against the pier, so nobody pulled from the water can be brought to the ramp. **Resolution:** Doucette is hauled up the recessed port quarter ladder and over the stern gate. Dane cuffs him on the deck plates at the stern under a blanket.
5. **Ned's third coffee.** One is left (bible: three in the book). These beats **do not spend it**. Save it for Ch 10 (the first boat, Day 10).
6. **The *Margaret Ann*'s second hand** ("Crew (2)" on the paint-can lid). Not resolved on the page before now. Optional: one line from Russ in Scene 6 saying the dragger's other hand is still aboard on the mooring with her deck lights on, and Marine Patrol can have him. Ch 10 should close it.

No unresolvable conflicts with the bible.

## Chapter-wide constraints (for prose-writer and voice-editor)

- **The bait argument is settled.** It must not be re-argued, not even in one line. Dane asks to be walked through the deck. He does not object to the plan.
- **No recap** at the top of any scene. Enter late.
- **Counting:** at most twice. Count 1 is the 34 steps from the foot of the wheelhouse ladder to the stern gate, used in Scene 5. A second count is optional and should be avoided if possible. Dane never counts.
- **"Not asking":** at most once. The beats don't need it.
- **Button endings:** at most two. Planned: Scene 2 ("Okay, Bethie.") and Scene 6 (Ned's radio call). Every other scene ends mid-action or on an image paragraph.
- **Retired tics, banned:** "put it where she put things"; "longer than X needed"; "the way she'd [boat task]"; "right side clear"; "wet/hard"; "let go of everything"; neat closing lines; no simile already used for these characters.
- **Language:** Dane's second and last swear of the book happens in the Scene 5 fight, under fire. At most one swear in Beth's thought. **No profanity on VHF**, and every radio exchange is formal and short. At most four strong swears in the chapter.
- **Heat:** no sexual content. At most one line of kiss (Scene 5, after the water). Romance comes through hands, the arm, the positions she gives him and the one he keeps.
- **Naming:** "Doucette" goes on the page for the first time, said by Dane (Scene 1, briefing Russ), then used in Dane's POV (Scene 4). Beth uses "Shane" once, in the dark (Scene 5).
- **"Fentanyl"** must not appear. Use "the pills" if needed.
- **Radios:** the wheelhouse fixed set is on 9 for Tess. The Icom stays on 16 in Beth's float coat (the Ch 08 flag). Russ carries his own handheld and reports on 16. The storm net runs at 1800 on 68.
- **Weather:** northwest wind, clearing since mid-afternoon. A clear, cold night, the sea going down, sound carrying far over flat water. No barometer endings.
- **Doucette is pulled out alive** (bible: he lives). The threat is ended. Arsenault is taken by Russ in the store, off-page, and reported.

### Scene 1
**Setting:** The *North Reach*, crew mess, then the open car deck. About 4:40 to 5:15 pm on Day 8, full dark since a little past four. The mess smells of burnt coffee and wet wool. On deck the sodium floods are on as usual ("Ned's boat looks like Ned's boat"), the northwest wind is cold, and the two storm-held trucks stand chained in the orange light: the fire company's propane truck and the store's box truck.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Russ, Ned (briefly, leaving)
**Surface action:** Ned brings Russ down the pier and aboard by the gangway. Then Ned goes back up the pier alone with his duffel, walking his bad hip up the Harbor Road toward Russ's house, so anyone watching sees the captain leave. In the mess, Dane gives Russ the federal facts in a few level sentences: the case, the Day 7 shot, and the name, "Shane Doucette. Sternman. Quist ran the crew list." This is the first time the name is on the page. Beth tells him the rest: Tess. Russ takes his cap off, holds it, puts it back on. He doesn't argue about Tess. He goes to the cousin link himself. Jolene's cousin's boat, family trouble, and he means to be there this time instead of last to know. He wants in, and he says so in a deadpan that sounds like a joke and isn't. Dane asks one practical question (his training, his weapon). Russ answers it plainly (the M&P, part-time officer certification, arrest powers on the island). Beth goes up on deck and sets the positions. Russ goes inside the starboard gate at the gangway, in the shadow of the deckhouse door, where the pier is the only way in from land. Dane goes in the dark alley between the propane truck and the box truck, with a sightline aft to the stern gate and across to the deckhouse door. Beth goes up in the wheelhouse, lit, where anyone on the water or the pier can see her. She gives them one agreed signal. If the floods go dark, it's the water, and that means the stern, because the bow ramp is up and the starboard side is on the pier. Dane says, "Walk me through it," and she puts his good hand on the box truck's rear binder, then the D-ring, then the chain bin lip, so he knows what is under his feet in the dark.
**Subtext:** Beth is trusting Russ on thirty years of knowing him and because he stayed off the boat when the feds told him to. That is the same kind of trust she gave Tess, and she knows it. Russ wants to be taken seriously as a lawman, and he wants it to have been family that saved this, not family that broke it. Dane accepts a position from a civilian without a word of objection. That is his whole arc in one gesture, and Beth notices what it costs him (his left hand stays tucked in his jacket front, and he takes the binder with the right).
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Federal and local size each other up, and Dane is wary of an islander with a family link to the boat. Situational: three people, one of them one-armed, to hold a steel boat in the dark.
**Ends on:** Mid-action, not a button. Beth climbing the outside ladder to the wheelhouse with the floods throwing her shadow up the deckhouse ahead of her, and below, Dane stepping back between the trucks until the dark takes him.
**Word count target:** 650. The romance beat for the chapter starts here, in the hand-on-the-binder moment. One touch that lasts, nothing more.

### Scene 2
**Setting:** The wheelhouse, about 5:30 pm. All the wheelhouse lights are on, so the windows are lit from the harbor. The wheelhouse smells of electronics and old coffee, and Ned's rag is on the console. The fixed VHF's channel display reads 16, and the Icom is in her float coat, also on 16.
**Characters present:** Beth; Tess (voice only, on channel 9)
**Surface action:** It opens on Beth's thumb on the fixed set's channel knob, turning it to 9, their old channel. She keys the mic and calls Tess the way she did at fifteen from her father's boat. Tess comes back fast and bright. Beth asks to be counted for the six o'clock net and to keep it off the air on 68. She says she's on the boat tonight by herself. Ned has gone up to Russ's with his hip ("You know him. Won't sleep on that settee another night"). Dane is down with the arm and running a fever, and she "left him where he's warm." She'll be up in the wheelhouse till the pumps cycle at midnight, and Ned left the deck lights on, "you know Ned." Then she signs off the way she did as a girl: "*Ruthie C*, clear." There is a pause on Tess's end, long enough for the reader to hear the open carrier. Then: "Okay, Bethie." At six Beth listens to Tess run the net on 68 in her warm net-control voice, and she does not say Calloway on the air.
**Subtext:** Show the words being chosen. Beth's thought should run as short, working decisions, not reflection. "Keep it off the air" is the exact phrase from Day 1. That is the version Tess has passed on once already, so it is the version Tess will believe and pass again. Every fact is built to travel through Tess to Arsenault: she is alone, the armed man is down, the captain is gone, the deck is lit, and there is a window till midnight. Two things are left out on purpose: the place where Dane is, and Russ. The sign-off is not for Arsenault. It is for Tess, and Beth uses it knowing it is the one thing that will make Tess hear complete trust. Beth is using her oldest friend's love as a tool, and it is the right call. Show it in her body: her thumb stays on the key a moment after she releases it, and she wipes the mic grille on her sleeve.
**Tension source:** Internal. She is lying in her own voice to the woman who drove her to the mainland the day her mother left. Situational: one wrong note and Tess hears it.
**Ends on:** BUTTON 1. Tess, after the pause: "Okay, Bethie." (The six o'clock net can fold in just before it, or just after as a short paragraph. If it comes after, the scene ends on the net, Tess's ordinary voice counting the island and leaving Beth out. Then this is not a button. Prose-writer's choice. Either way, Scene 6 must stay the only other button.)
**Word count target:** 450

### Scene 3
**Setting:** The wheelhouse, 6 pm to 11 pm. Lit like a lantern. Outside is the clear cold, the harbor going flat, the *Margaret Ann*'s deck lights on her mooring off the fish pier, the store's single lit window up the hill (the generator), and the stars coming out hard behind the northwest wind.
**Characters present:** Beth; Russ and Dane on deck (seen from above, not heard); Tess (voice, on 9)
**Surface action:** The wait. Keep it short and physical. Beth does real work so that she looks like a woman alone on watch: she wipes the windows with Ned's rag, which makes them worse, checks the bilge pump panel, and sits in Ned's chair. From the wheelhouse she can see a sliver of Russ's cap brim at the gangway and nothing of Dane, which is correct. She does not go down to them. At 11:00 the fixed set opens on 9: "Bethie. Water side." Then a clatter and dead carrier, as if someone took the radio. Beth understands both halves at once. "Water side" means not the pier. It means the stern and the port quarter ladder, the only way aboard from the water. The cut-off means Arsenault is in the store with Tess, and he has just heard her warn Beth. She goes out the wheelhouse door and speaks down to Russ at the gangway, not on the radio: Tess warned us, he's in the store with her, go now, the water's ours, call on 16 when it's done. Russ goes up the pier at a run without arguing. Her reasoning, in two lines: Tess will be killed for what she just did, and two can hold a boat whose only open door now is the stern. Beth goes back in, opens the aft-bulkhead lighting panel and puts her hand on the car-deck flood breakers.
**Subtext:** The warning confirms Tess in Tess's own voice (Leak row 4). It is also Tess's one act for her. Beth doesn't get to feel it. She has to use it. Give it one physical beat: her palm flat on the console, the way she reads things, before she moves. Sending her only fresh armed man away from the boat is the smart choice, and the reader must be able to defend it: the land side is no longer the threat, and the person most likely to die tonight is now Tess.
**Tension source:** Situational. The wait, then the warning cut off mid-word, which tells her where they are coming and that her friend is in danger.
**Ends on:** Mid-action. Her fingers on the breakers. She throws them, the deck below goes black, and she kills the wheelhouse lights with the same hand. The scene cuts before her eyes adjust.
**Word count target:** 500

### Scene 4 (Dane POV)
**Setting:** The car deck, in the alley between the propane truck and the store box truck, from shortly before 11 pm. His back is against the box truck's cold aluminum side, the deck plates are under his boots (wrong boots, and he knows it), and there is a smell of salt, hydraulic oil and propane-tank paint. At first the floods turn the alley mouth into a lit frame and the alley itself dark. Then everything goes dark.
**Characters present:** Dane (Beth and Doucette heard, not seen)
**Surface action:** Dane holds where she put him. His left arm is useless above the shoulder and has no grip, so he holds it in the front of his soft-shell. The Glock is in his right hand. He judges distances in his own terms: the alley mouth to the stern gate, the deckhouse door, the outside ladder, where a man coming over the port quarter would have to pass. He hears Russ go up the pier at a run and doesn't know why. He hears an outboard out on the flat water, well off, then it cuts. Then paddle strokes, and the drip off the blade, closing. He reads the distance by how the sound changes, and he does not count. Then the floods die overhead, the agreed signal, and he knows she's been warned and that she is coming down. For a few seconds he is blind. He doesn't move. He thinks once of Marisol's ten minutes and sees the difference: this isn't a rule he bent, it's a position she gave him. He knows the name from the case, Shane Doucette, twenty-nine, the sternman with the left leg. Aluminum touches steel at the port quarter.
**Subtext:** Dane's arc lands here: he is following her plan, not overriding it, and his restraint is care (his finger is off the trigger until he has a shape, and he refuses to fire into the dark where she will be). He wants to go to her, and he won't. His voice must differ from Beth's: longer sentences, judgment of people, sightlines, no counting.
**Tension source:** Situational. Wounded, blind, alone in the dark on a steel deck he doesn't know by feel, while the woman he loves is somewhere above him moving toward the danger by design.
**Ends on:** Mid-action. A boot on the quarter-ladder rung, then on the deck plate, heavy on one side, the heel ringing on the steel. Dane doesn't move.
**Word count target:** 600

### Scene 5
**Setting:** The car deck in full dark, about 11:10 pm, from the foot of the outside wheelhouse ladder aft along the port bulwark to the stern gate. Beth has steel under her bare palms, and the binders ring when they're touched. The ring buoy is in its bracket on the port bulwark aft, with 90 feet of line, and the stern gate is waist-low with its chain across. The water is 48°F. The only light is stars, which show the sky and none of the deck.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Doucette
**Surface action:** Beth comes down the inside companionway, out the deckhouse door, and to the foot of the outside ladder, gloves off. From the ladder foot she knows it is 34 steps to the stern gate (**count 1**). She tracks Doucette by his heavy right heel ringing on the plates as he comes forward along the port side toward the ladder, toward where she said she'd be. He has a handgun. Dane steps out of the alley mouth to take him, and they collide and go down among the box truck's tie-down chains. Dane's bad arm and sleeve are fouled in the chain run between the binder and the D-ring, he can't pull free one-handed, and the Glock is pinned under him. Doucette gets up over him. Beth, unarmed, makes her call: she draws him off the downed man and onto the part of the deck she knows and he doesn't. From the dark aft she says, "Shane," and moves the instant she has said it. He fires at the voice. The muzzle flash shows her the deck for a blink. Dane's swear, his last in the book, comes here, with her name after it. Doucette comes aft after her, limping on the left leg on wet plate, and she backs along the bulwark cap by feel, past the ring buoy bracket, to the gatepost. When his bad leg takes his weight at the gate, she drives her shoulder into his hip and he goes over the gate chain into the water. His gun hits the deck plates and skids (evidence preserved on deck). Then the cold-shock gasp from the water, silence while he is under, and a second gasp as he surfaces. Within that first minute she has the buoy out of the bracket and thrown at the sound, the line running through her bare hands. She talks him in with short commands: arm through it, both arms, kick, keep your face up. She hauls him hand over hand to the recessed port quarter ladder while he can still grip, well inside the ten minutes. Dane has freed himself by working the binder's ratchet release with his good hand. He learned it from watching her all week, and that callback should be there in one line. He reaches the stern, and the two of them get Doucette up the ladder and over the gate. Doucette can't stand. Dane cuffs him on the plates. Beth brings the wool blanket from the crew cabin. The Icom in her float coat opens on 16: Russ, grimly formal. "*North Reach*, Whitcomb. One in custody at the store. Tess Marr is unhurt. Over." Beth answers formally. Then the one kiss line: Dane's good hand at her jaw, cold, brief, and back to work. She goes to the ramp-light switch by the deckhouse door and the sodium lamps come up slowly, pink and then orange, over a deck she has been holding blind.
**Subtext:** Beth wins with the boat, not with a weapon, which is World Rule 11. She knows the deck through her hands, and that knowledge has been planted since Ch 01. Throwing the buoy is who she is: he is evidence, and she is not that kind of person. Make it action, not reflection. There is no line about mercy. She just has the buoy in the water before she thinks. Partnership under fire: he held, she ended it, and he freed himself from her binder.
**Tension source:** Physical danger in the dark: an armed attacker, the armed protector fouled, Beth unarmed and using her voice as bait for the second time tonight. Then the 1-10-1 clock on a man in 48°F water.
**Ends on:** An image paragraph, not a button. The sodium light coming up orange over the deck: Doucette shaking under the blanket at the stern gate, the gun on the plates where it stopped, the chain still ringing faintly where Dane tore loose.
**Word count target:** 800. This is the climax and gets the most words. Keep the fight sentences short. Never more than two sentences of who-grabbed-what in a row.

### Scene 6
**Setting:** The stone pier and the starboard gangway, about 1 am on Day 9, then the wheelhouse at first gray, about 6:15 am (sunrise is about 6:30). The floods are back on. Inside the passenger cabin the stove is lit, and Doucette sits in the crew mess under the blanket by the heater. Dawn comes clear, with the northwest wind dropping and the harbor flat.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Russ, Tess, Arsenault, Ned. Doucette is present but silent.
**Surface action:** Russ walks Arsenault down the pier in cuffs: clean-shaven, the good Musto jacket, saying nothing. Tess walks beside them, with Ned on her other side not touching her. Russ brings them to the ferry because it has heat, light, steel, a lock and a federal officer, and the town office has no power. He tells the arrest in two flat lines: Arsenault had Tess by the wrist and the radio by its cord, and Russ asked him for both. Optional: the dragger's other hand is still aboard on the mooring and can wait for Marine Patrol. Arsenault goes to the crew mess with Doucette, under Dane. Russ says Tess isn't under arrest: "I'm not sure what she is." **Beth and Tess face to face, briefly:** Tess at the foot of the gangway in her red vest with a red mark around her wrist, Beth at the top. Tess's hand starts up toward Beth's braid and stops in the air, calling back the tic she dropped in Ch 06. Beth doesn't step back and doesn't step closer. She answers with a task: "Russ, put her in the passenger cabin. The stove's lit." **Do not use the Ch 10 lines** ("They said they'd only scare you." / "You warned me."). Beth says nothing about the warning. Dane reports to Pike from the open deck, and relief is coming the first hour the sea allows, which is now. At first gray Ned climbs to the wheelhouse. He does not bring coffee. Through the wheelhouse glass Beth can see Tess sitting upright in the passenger cabin window. Ned takes the mic, turns the fixed set from 9 back to 16, and makes his formal all-stations call: first departure Sunday at first light, passengers and light freight, *North Reach* standing by one-six and one-three.
**Subtext:** The threat is over, and what's left is what it cost. Tess hears the boat's voice and the island's ordinary morning going on without her. Ned and Tess side by side on the pier, not touching, is the price of "Tess doesn't count," and it should get one image, no dialogue between them. Beth turning the radio off 9 is not shown as Beth's action. Ned does it, and she lets him. Their channel is closed.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and unspoken: Beth and Tess with twelve feet of gangway between them, and Ned beside a woman he has told everything to for twelve years.
**Ends on:** BUTTON 2. Ned's formal call, ending "*North Reach* standing by one-six and one-three," with Tess's face in the cabin window below. This sets up the chapter promise (what happens to Tess, and to them) and puts the Day 10 first boat on the record. Do not end on a question in narration.
**Word count target:** 400
