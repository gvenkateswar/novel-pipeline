# Chapter 05 — Half Tide: Beat Sheet

**Heading for the chapter:** Day 4 — Monday
**POV:** Beth, close third, throughout. No Dane section.
**Target:** ~3,300 words across five scenes. Sex scene (Scene 4, from the stove catching to the end of the scene) 850–900 words.
**Chapter promise (outline):** Who on Cutter Island is passing her location to the *Margaret Ann*?

---

## Flags before drafting

1. **Sex-scene length.** Bible Heat Rule 2 sets scene 1 at 800–1,000 words. The orchestrator's brief says 700–900. **Use 850–900.** That satisfies both.
2. **The shore path and Russ.** The continuity log has Beth ruling the shore path out as a way IN (Ch 03), and Russ walking it on Day 2 (Ch 04). These don't conflict with the escape, but the prose has to show why nobody watches it at night: Russ walked it at low water in daylight. At 10 p.m. on a young flood with a storm swell, nobody from away would think it was passable, and the man waiting is at the end of the driveway. Scene 1 puts this on the page so the escape reads as a smart choice and not a lucky one.
3. **Dane's sat call and the fish house.** World Rule 7 says the fish house is never disclosed, and Dane logs it as "secondary location, B's family property, not disclosed." Beth hears him say "moved, not where" (Scene 5). That plants the Ch 08 deduction fairly, as the outline intends ("in plain view from Ch 05 on"). In Ch 07 she will be looking at the extraction time and miss it, so this doesn't spoil the dark moment. Keep his line to one sentence and don't have Beth remark on it.
4. **Tess.** Dane "never suspects her until the Day 8 call" (bible). In Scene 5 Tess is the obvious person who knew about the check-in. Beth explains her away aloud, and Dane, who has met Tess once, lets it go. This is Beth's fourth on-page excuse for Tess (the bible asks for at least three, so a fourth is fine). Make the excuse sound smart. The irony is the reader's to notice, not the narration's.
5. **Pacing flag from the outline.** The outline allows the Ch 06 Russ conversation to move into Ch 05's closing beats if Ch 06 runs long. **Not done here.** Ch 05 is at its target, and the midpoint turn needs the last scene to itself.
6. **Tic budget for this chapter (continuity log):**
   - Counting: **two uses, both assigned.** (a) Scene 1, steps across the bar and minutes to the flood, as one passage. (b) Scene 3, the seconds of the wade. Beth counts nothing else. Dane never counts.
   - "Not asking": **at most once.** Assigned to Scene 5 (the notebook in the drying jacket). Optional.
   - "Put it where she put things": banned.
   - Barometer: none in this chapter. The Ashby place has no barometer on the page, and the fish house has none.
   - Button endings: **at most two.** Scene 4 ends on a button. The other four scenes end on images or actions, as noted on each one.
7. **No recaps.** Scene 1 opens on the bar, not on Ch 04's kiss. The kiss shows up only as behavior: who reaches first, who steps back.

### Scene 1
**Setting:** The shore path below the Ashby place, then the gravel bar, around 7:00 a.m., which is dead low water (low is at 7:10). It is a little past sunrise: gray light, wind still northeast but easing, and the swell running long into the cove even at low water. The sensory anchor is the bar gravel crunching dry under her XtraTufs, then going soft and sucking where the first trickle of flood will cross. Rockweed is draped over the ledge, and the alders stand at the top of the path.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:** Beth walks from the ledge foot across the bar and back, hood down so she can hear the water. **Counting use 1:** she paces the bar's low spot and works out the minutes until it floods, in one passage. Then she stops counting. Dane has followed her down from the house and stands at the top of the ledge watching. He stays where he can see the alders and the house at once. When she comes back he asks her to walk him through it ("Slower than you want to" is already his; give him a fresh line in the same mode). She explains: the bar dries about two hours either side of low. On the young flood it is a knee-deep wade of about ninety seconds at the low spot, longer if a sea is running. By half flood a man from away would call it the ocean. He repeats it back in fewer words. She points out that the driveway is the only road out and runs 340 steps to the north road. Don't recount the steps, just state the number she already knows: "If I wanted someone in this house, I'd wait at the end of that." Dane brings up Russ: "Whitcomb walked this Saturday." Beth answers that he walked it at low water in daylight, and that anybody can. She points across the cove to the shingled shed at its head, on granite blocks with buoys under the eave: her father's fish house. She gives it one line. Nobody has been in it in years. Coming back up the ledge, he offers his hand at the steep pitch and she takes it. Neither of them lets go at the top as soon as they could. He lets go first.
**Subtext:** She is building an exit because Day 1 taught her a house can be found. She is also showing him that his chosen location (he picked the Ashby place out of three) has a back door only she can open. Taking his hand after yesterday's stop is a test of whether "not yet" still holds in daylight. He lets go first, and she reads it the same way she read the shed: care that costs him.
**Tension source:** Situational and interpersonal. The flood coming in under her feet is the clock she is learning. Dane's question about Russ puts a name next to the path: someone besides her knows this way. Under that runs the unfinished kiss.
**Ends on:** Image, not a button. Halfway up the ledge Beth looks back. The first thread of flood crosses the low point of the bar where she was standing, and the gravel she walked on goes dark.
**Word count target:** 550.
**Advances:** plot (it plants the escape and the smartness of the choice, and introduces the fish house as a destination) and character (her competence, and the romance beat for this chapter).

### Scene 2
**Setting:** The Ashby place, the kitchen and the seaward mudroom, late afternoon going to dark, sunset a little past four. Sheet-covered shapes, mothballs and cold wallpaper, the propane furnace ticking on. The generator hums steadily from the shed across the yard. That is the sound the next scene takes away.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:** Beth packs a bag and stands it by the seaward mudroom door: dry socks in a bread bag, strike-anywhere matches in an old pill bottle, a headlamp with a red lens, a wool watch cap, a granola bar she has been saving. She sets her boots beside it with the tops open. Dane watches her do it, says nothing, then puts his own things by the same door: the spare magazine, the trauma kit, and the sat phone in a zip bag. That is his yes to her plan, given without talk. They eat standing at the counter (canned chowder heated on the propane range). He sits at the sheeted dining table with his back to the wall and his right side clear, and she takes the other chair without being asked. Give the chapter's daytime romance here in one or two lines: the near-miss of passing in the narrow mudroom, or his hand stopping on the bag strap when hers is already there. He goes out on the porch at dusk with the notebook. She watches through the gap in the window sheet. The pen moves, and he closes the notebook when he turns.
**Subtext:** His bag next to hers is the first time he has arranged himself around her plan instead of his own. She sees that, and it matters more to her than the kiss did. The notebook stays a closed question. Don't spend the "not asking" beat here; Scene 5 has it.
**Tension source:** Waiting. Both expect something, and the house is too big and too quiet. The notebook is a low hum under the scene.
**Ends on:** Action, not a button. Beth checks the seaward door's latch twice, then leaves it on the hook instead of bolted, so it will open with one hand.
**Word count target:** 450. Keep it tight. If the chapter runs long, cut here first, but keep the two bags by the same door.
**Advances:** plot (it makes the escape fast and believable) and character (the partnership forming, and the notebook kept alive).

### Scene 3
**Setting:** The Ashby place, 10:00 p.m., then the ledge and the bar, about 10:05 to 10:15 p.m., on the young flood (half flood is not until 10:40). Rain is back and coming in gusts. It is black dark except for the fire.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane. Walt Grindle and the fire company are heard, not seen, at the end. Doucette is never identified: he is a shape and a windshield.
**Surface action:**
- Beth is in the dark front room. The generator's hum stops: it chokes, stutters, and quits. Then there is a smell she knows from the ferry engine room, diesel burning where it shouldn't be, with shingle smoke under it. Orange light grows at the edge of the window sheet.
- Dane is up with his hand at his hip before she says a word. He goes to the front corner where he can see up the drive without showing himself. The firelight from the shed catches a windshield where the driveway meets the north road. No lights, no engine.
- Beth says it as a conclusion first: "Not the drive." Then the reason in one line: the fire is meant to push them up the drive toward whoever is waiting. Then the plan: "The bar. Now, while it's knees."
- Dane follows without arguing. Give him one beat of visible cost: he looks once more at the windshield, the way a man looks at the thing he would rather go and handle, and then he turns his back on it. That is the outline's "on her word alone."
- They take the bags from the mudroom door and go out the seaward side with no light. Beth leads down the ledge by feel and by the line of the rock against the glow behind them. Dane comes behind with the gun out and low, watching uphill.
- **The wade.** The water is 48°F, and it hurts at the shins first, then at the knees, then nothing. Beth puts herself on the seaward side and takes a fist of Dane's belt (bible). She tells him where to step in four-word sentences. A sea comes in surging and lifts the water to mid-thigh. Gravel slides out from under one of them. The fist on the belt holds. **Counting use 2:** she counts the seconds of the crossing under her breath. It runs past ninety, because the swell set them back.
- The point the prose must make: the danger is falling in the surge and taking the cold-shock gasp under water, not hypothermia (Research note 38). She knows that, and that is why she holds the belt.
- They come out onto the far gravel soaked to the thighs and wet everywhere else from the rain. Behind them, over the point, the shed roof goes up in a column of sparks. From the north road comes the fire company's tanker horn: Walt Grindle, who leans on it the whole way. Beth knows it's him by the horn.
- One flash of loss, shown through an object, not named as a feeling: the shed where his light was on her hands, and where she showed him the bleed screw, is the thing burning.
**Subtext:** Someone knew about the Ashby place. Only two people besides themselves could have known. That thought comes later, so here it is only in her body. He is trusting her with both their lives on water he can't read, and she knows exactly what that costs a man like him.
**Tension source:** Situational. Fire behind them, a watcher at the drive, the flood rising, the cold, and the dark.
**Ends on:** Action, not a button. At the head of the cove, her hands are shaking too hard for the first try. She finds her father's key on the nail under the eave where he always left it. The door has swollen, and she puts her shoulder to it.
**Word count target:** 750. This scene warrants the extra room; the wade is the chapter's main danger beat.
**Advances:** plot (the second attack and the escape) and character (her competence, and his deferral to her on water).

### Scene 4 — SEX SCENE 1 (heat 3)
**Setting:** The fish house on Calloway Cove, about 10:30 p.m. One room on granite blocks. The oil-drum woodstove, the cot under the gear-loft hatch, the workbench, the kerosene lantern, the red Pelican first-aid case on the shelf (plant it; it comes back in Ch 07), coils of pot warp, buoys in Hollis's yellow over black, paint cans with her father's handwriting on the lids. Smells of old bait, pine tar, then woodsmoke. Wind in the shingles. The cove slapping on the ledge. One door, landward, and a small window on the water side.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:**
- **Opening: follow Style Reference Sample 4 closely in beat and register (don't copy it word for word).** She lights the stove with shaking hands and breaks three matches. The heat comes off it in a wall she feels on her shins through wet canvas. Dane stands by the door, dripping, and hasn't spoken since the bar. She tells him to take it off. He takes off the jacket and the fleece and stops at the T-shirt. She crosses the room and takes it off him. She puts her palms flat on his chest (her tic for reading a man), and feels his heart going hard and his breath stop.
- **Before the breach line,** she drops the iron bar across the landward door herself, or wedges it. One clipped line from her calls back Ch 04: the door is shut behind him. (Don't quote his Ch 04 line back in full; a three- or four-word echo is enough.) **She starts it.**
- **The breach and the answer, in the bible's words:** Dane: "When this is over, I report this." Use the bible's full version if it fits ("I want you to hear that before, not after"). Beth: "I know what you are. I'm choosing anyway." She asks whether he is too, and he says yes. That is the explicit verbal yes.
- **The protection line (Heat Rule 2 and Rule 10), in one exchange, intimate and funny, with his dry humor at his own expense:** neither of them has anything. Her father kept a lot in this room, and not that. He has a trauma kit, and it isn't that kind of kit. They laugh, and the laugh is what makes it intimate. This settles that it will be hands and mouths only, and they both say so without negotiating.
- **The body of the scene (heat 3, about 450 to 550 words):** they move to the cot, his jacket hanging on the nail by the stove and steaming. **Note that the notebook is in that jacket's inside pocket.** Give it one glance at most, no comment; it pays off in Ch 07, when she hangs the same jacket by the same stove. Temperature does the work: cold skin against the stove side, his cold hands warming on her, stubble, calluses on both their palms, wet wool on the floor. Use frank words sparingly and precisely (Heat Rule 4): one frank noun lands harder than three. Use hands and mouths for both of them, but never more than two sentences in a row about who is touching what. Her orgasm stays in her body and in what she is thinking about him, not in banned metaphor (no "came apart" or "shattered"). His is brief, seen from her side.
- **The consent check (Rule 6):** one moment where a hand waits. Write a fresh exchange in their voices; don't reuse the bible's examples word for word.
- **Plot inside the scene (Rule 8, at least two references):** (1) the breach line, (2) his gun on the workbench within reach, where he set it before anything else and where she watches him look at it once, (3) the burning shed in her head: someone knew. That makes at least three.
- **The return to the suspense question (Rule 7, within 150 words of the climax):** under the wool blanket on the cot, the stove ticking, he says what both of them have been holding: they knew the Ashby place. She says only Ned knew. He corrects her: "And me." Then Beth decides, and the decision has to be visible as a decision. She tells him what she never told him on Day 1. How she checked in: the landline at 5:40, Tess, "home, don't send anybody, keep it off the air," and Tess writing it in the spiral log that sits open on the store counter where anyone buying cigarettes can read it.
**Subtext:** For her, sex is the first thing in four days she has chosen with no exit counted. Telling him about the check-in is the second. It is the real intimacy, because it gives up the fact that has let her suspect him since Day 1. He names the cost before anything happens because he has been through a review once, and he won't let her find out afterwards what this costs him. The reader knows (and she doesn't) that the notebook drying on the nail holds the order he hasn't told her about. That double edge is what keeps the scene from being liftable.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and internal. Fear just behind them, and the sense that wanting him might be the exact mistake her father made. The breach line puts the professional cost on the table before anything happens.
**Ends on:** **Button 1 of 2.** Dane, after a silence, quietly: "Then I wasn't the only one who knew."
**Word count target:** 850–900 (the sex scene proper). This is the chapter's longest scene by design.
**Advances:** plot (this is the information that turns the midpoint) and character (trust up; the romance's first open-door beat).

### Scene 5
**Setting:** The fish house, past midnight. The lantern is turned low. Their clothes are steaming on the pot warp coils and the nail. Rain is easing. Through the water-side window the glow over the point has gone to a dull red smear, and the tanker's work lights can be seen up on the north point.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:**
- Dane goes out the landward door with the sat phone, because it needs open sky. Beth watches through the gap in the door, not the window, so she can hear. He is out under two minutes. She hears one line of it: Pike gets that they moved, a secondary location, "not on this phone, not where." He comes in and says the same to her in fewer words. She files it and says nothing. Don't comment on it; this is the fair plant for Ch 08.
- The reasoning, done as a dialogue that works the problem the way she would work a load:
  - **The check-in log circle.** Everyone who stood at that counter since Friday could have read "Calloway — home (phone)." Dane names Arsenault first, because he stood at that counter on Day 2 and looked at Dane. Beth adds the six islanders she named in Ch 03. Russ comes up, from his questions and the woods road, and Beth puts him in without liking it.
  - **Tess (Flag 4).** Dane says, neutrally, "And the woman who wrote it down." Beth's excuse, and it should sound smart: if Tess were selling her, she would have kept it in her head instead of writing it in a book anyone could read. Dane has met Tess once. He lets it go.
  - **The Ashby circle.** Only Ned and Dane knew. Dane: "Or someone Ned told." Beth: "Ned doesn't talk." (Both lines are from the outline. Keep them.)
  - **The red herring at Dane (outline: "He chose the Ashby place"):** in her head only, one or two lines. He picked it from Ned's three. His calls go out every night. Her palm on his chest an hour ago told her nothing she could check. His jacket on the nail, with the notebook's square shape in the pocket. **The "not asking" beat goes here, once:** she doesn't ask what's in it. Then she sets it down beside the fact that he followed her into the water.
- **The turn (the midpoint reversal).** Beth, conclusion first: they are done hiding. Then the reasons, each one specific. Hiding has been found twice in four days. By morning Walt's crew will have told the whole island the Ashby furnace was warm, so the island knows where they were and doesn't know where they are. Someone on her island is passing on what they know. She knows all 240 people, every boat by its exhaust note, and who buys what at the store, and none of that is any use from inside a shut house. The man in the driveway expected them on the road. They aren't the ones who don't know the island.
- Dane's response has to read as care (bible). He states the cost and the rule (Pike will say sit tight; a witness who goes looking is a witness who gets seen), and then gives her something she can do instead. This is not a refusal. He ends on his mode of yes: "Walk me through it." She lays out tomorrow: the store at opening, in daylight and in the open, with him, because being seen is normal and hiding gets noticed. He asks what her reason is for being in the store. She says she'll think of one. Keep it light; it quietly sets up the Ch 06 condom purchase without naming it.
**Subtext:** This is where the emotional question turns active. She has trusted him with her body and with the check-in, and she has decided to trust her own reading of her island over his rulebook. She hasn't seen that the trust she won't question, Tess, is the hole. He agrees because the shore path proved her right about the island the same way the 2 p.m. boat proved her right about the weather.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal: his rules against her reading. Internal: the notebook against the water he walked into for her. Plot: the suspect pool has just widened from one man to a whole store counter.
**Ends on:** Image and action, **not a button, not a barometer.** Beth takes her father's carpenter pencil from the workbench and turns over the lid of a paint can, with Hollis's handwriting on the other side ("*Ruthie C* — trim, 2 coats"). By lantern light she starts writing the names of everyone who stood at Tess's counter since Friday. She starts with the ones from away, then the island, then Ned. She stops, because that's everybody. **Tess's name is not on the lid.** The narration must not point this out. A careful reader will see it; the outline's fairness test depends on it. Dane leans on the bench beside her and reads it upside down. The stove ticks. That sets up the chapter promise: who on Cutter Island is passing her location to the *Margaret Ann*?
**Word count target:** 700.
**Advances:** plot (the midpoint reversal from hiding to hunting, the widened suspect pool, and the fish-house fairness plant) and character (Beth's decision; the misplaced trust in Tess shown on the page by omission).
