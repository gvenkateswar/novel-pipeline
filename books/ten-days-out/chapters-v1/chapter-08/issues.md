# Chapter 08 — Issues

**Issue 1**
- Problem: Beth's whoopie-pie memory contradicts Ch 04, which is canon. Ch 04 has Tess drive Beth down to the boat in a borrowed truck, buy her a hot chocolate in Pruett Harbor, and let her hold it till it went cold. Ch 04 adds that neither has mentioned it since. The draft changes the truck, the treat, the crossing and the "whole way home".
- Location: "The day her mother left, Tess drove the store truck onto the boat with Beth in the passenger seat, nine years old, and at Pruett bought her a whoopie pie and didn't make her talk the whole way home."
- Suggested fix: Use the Ch 04 facts. Borrowed truck, hot chocolate bought in Pruett Harbor, and "let her hold it till it went cold". Drop "onto the boat", "whoopie pie" and "the whole way home".

**Issue 2**
- Problem: Beth's recollection of Ch 06 is wrong. In Ch 06 she did think of Tess as the local EMD ("The island's version of that was Tess with a clipboard, and nobody in Portland phoned Tess"). She dismissed it. The draft says she pictured only a county desk in Pruett. That erases the fair-play beat where she explains the clue away.
- Location: "*Local emergency management.* She had split kindling and pictured a county desk in Pruett. On Cutter, local emergency management was a woman who'd been slow putting her hand down at town meeting and laughed about it at the register."
- Suggested fix: Rewrite so she remembers thinking of Tess with a clipboard while splitting kindling, and deciding that nobody in Portland phones Tess.

**Issue 3**
- Problem: The bank-bag payday contradicts Ch 01. Beth handed Tess her paycheck on Thursday (Day 0) at 4 pm for the morning bag. She does not hand it over "every other Friday". The "green marker" for the TM tag is new; Ch 01 and Ch 02 say only "initials on the tag".
- Location: "Her paycheck in it every other Friday with the island's deposit slips, and the seal tag through the zip pull with *TM* on it in green marker."
- Suggested fix: Change to "Thursday, at four, for the morning boat" and cut "in green marker", or log the color.

**Issue 4**
- Problem: Dane's POV scene pre-empts Beth's recognition. He knows Tess by name from Ch 03 and Ch 06, yet thinks of her only as "the woman", which is a coy POV dodge. He states that Beth's Friday check-in "had gone to the woman". He then says he "could not place Beth with the woman", which is illogical because the woman is her closest friend. This puts Tess's three plot facts (bank bag, check-in, landing contact) in the reader's lap before Scene 3.
- Location: "Two pairs of hands. Beth's Friday check-in had gone to the woman. The Ashby key had come from the captain, and on Saturday the captain had lied to his face about the reefer, with more detail than anyone had asked for. / He could not place Beth with the woman. He could place her with the man."
- Suggested fix: Cut "Beth's Friday check-in had gone to the woman" and "He could not place Beth with the woman." Keep the bag crossing the rail (the Ch 02 plant) and the captain's lie. Let "where she'd go" rest on Ned alone.

**Issue 5**
- Problem: Dane volunteers that "She doesn't know" meant Beth. He has no way to know she overheard that call. The notebook does not contain the phrase, and Beth has not told him she heard it. This is a knowledge leak.
- Location: "Quist asked. Pike ordered. Saturday." He didn't look away. "*She doesn't know* was you."
- Suggested fix: Have Beth supply the phrase ("'She doesn't know.' Saturday, under the eave.") and Dane confirm it. Or cut his line.

**Issue 6**
- Problem: Dane cannot know the shooter is "on this island with a radio". Beth saw Doucette being guided by radio near the church in Ch 07, and she never tells Dane on the page. He also has no basis to say the man shot him, since the shooter was never seen (Ch 06).
- Location: "The man who shot me is on this island with a radio," Dane said. "He knows your face."
- Suggested fix: Have Beth give Dane the church-road sighting in one line before Ned offers, or trim Dane to "Someone shot at us from a skiff yesterday and he's still ashore."

**Issue 7**
- Problem: Ned calls Beth "Bethie". That is Tess's nickname and no one else uses it (bible). Ned says "Beth" in Ch 01 and Ch 07. Here it also steals the sting from Tess's voice in the scene's one sympathy moment.
- Location: "She's scared, Bethie. Since the storm she's scared all the time. I thought it was the loan."
- Suggested fix: Change to "She's scared, Beth."

**Issue 8**
- Problem: Ned's account breaks the "ten lines at most, no speech" rule. After the confession he runs about sixteen sentences in five consecutive speeches about Tess. He also over-explains what the reader has just heard ("I thought it was the loan" is said twice).
- Location: "She gets everything. Always has. Supper, then I go home." / "She's been running that pump after dark. For the loan. I knew. Her business." / "Sleeps down the store now. Back office, in her chair by that booster, light on. Every night this week, sitting by that phone." His voice went lower. "I thought she was waiting on you to call. Morning net, she ticks your line. Every day since the lines went, and you've not called once."
- Suggested fix: Cut the duplicate "I thought it was the loan" and fold the pump line into the first speech. Keep the back-office chair/booster and the ticked line as Ned's whole Tess account, about four sentences.

**Issue 9**
- Problem: Scene 3 is a catalogue of earlier clues, one short paragraph per object, each re-quoting its earlier excuse. It ends on an explanatory aphorism, which the beat sheet and the retired-tics list forbid. Beth does nothing physical until she sits down, so it reads as the narration explaining the reveal back to the reader.
- Location: "It had never been the book. It was the counter, and Ned's supper, and the one phone that rang."
- Suggested fix: Cut that closing pair. Collapse the list to three or four items tied to something her hands do (the ledger page, the bag's tag). Cut the repeated "That had been true / true too" echoes.

**Issue 10**
- Problem: Lock and key. In Ch 07 Ned worked the wheelhouse key off his ring and gave it to Beth, and she locked herself in. In Ch 08 Ned opens the door with "his key" with no spare accounted for.
- Location: "His key went into the lock."
- Suggested fix: Have Beth unlock it herself from inside after "Come in", or add a half-line that Ned has the district's spare.

**Issue 11**
- Problem: The sex scene reuses images and lines from Ch 05 and Ch 06. The chapter instruction is "no image or line" from those scenes. Ch 06: "He followed, breath stopped against her ear". Ch 05: "she said his name, once, like a complaint" and "He said her name into her hair". The Ch 08 climax repeats the same three beats.
- Location: "'Dane,' she said, once, into his hair." and "he followed her, his breath stopped against her throat."
- Suggested fix: Give him a different physical tell at climax (his good hand going slack on her back, the bunk rail ringing). Have her say his name mid-sentence in a plain line, not as a "once" beat.

**Issue 12**
- Problem: Heat Rule 5 (no step-by-step choreography, never more than two touch sentences in a row) is broken across the middle of the scene. Physical continuity also has gaps: her bibs, jeans and boots never come off, and she lowers herself onto him with no lower clothing removed.
- Location: "She undid his jeans. He lifted his hips for her, careful of the rail. Light from the slot of sky lay across his chest, gray and moving, and she could see all of him and he could see her, and neither of them looked away." / "She rolled the condom onto his cock and lowered herself onto him slowly."
- Suggested fix: Add one clause on her trousers and boots coming off (the cold on her legs). Break the run of who-touches-what with her interior or a plot line (the record, the night).

**Issue 13**
- Problem: Scene 5 is over length by my paragraph count (I could not run wc). It is about 880 words against the ~800 target and 850 hard cap. The lead-in runs about 155 words to "I'll tell you after" and about 265 to the box, against a ≤100-word lead-in. The post-climax sky, wind and tide passage is about 150 words before the plan line.
- Location: "She woke in the lower bunk to gray light moving on the overhead. Two hours." (opening of Scene 5) through "By morning the sea would lie down. When it did, the *Margaret Ann* could sail."
- Suggested fix: Cut the lead-in to about 90 words (drop the porthole/granite sentence and the "legs out, the go-bag beside him" detail). Cut about 60 words of post-climax weather. Target about 800.

**Issue 14**
- Problem: Emotion and reasoning are stated rather than shown, and they land as aphorisms. Two of the three short-sentence closers are narrator verdicts.
- Location: "That was its own danger. She knew it, and kept on." and "His name at seven matched hers at twenty to. He'd paid before she could ask the price."
- Suggested fix: Delete both. The beat is already carried by her sliding the notebook back across the table and by "Tonight there would be the dark again".

**Issue 15**
- Problem: Object contradiction in Scene 4. She slides the notebook back to him, then it lies "between them" and neither picks it up. The closing line also works as a third button ending (Scenes 2, 4 and 5 all end on a short line). The beat sheet allows two.
- Location: "Beth took her hand off the notebook and slid it back across the steel to him." and "The notebook lay on the table between them. Neither of them picked it up."
- Suggested fix: End Scene 4 on Ned's boots leaving the brow and the ringing stopping, as the beat sheet says. Or put the notebook on his side of the table and let Dane leave it there.

**Issue 16**
- Problem: Dane's Marisol line is a non sequitur as an answer to "You could have told me Saturday". Beth asked nothing against the plan. He then repeats the same line almost word for word in Scene 6, which dulls both uses.
- Location: "The last time a woman asked me for something off the plan, I gave it to her. She died in a parking lot." and "The last woman who asked me for something against the plan got it."
- Suggested fix: Keep one. The Scene 4 line should explain the silence (rules were the only thing that kept the last one honest). Cut the Scene 6 echo and let the "lock this door" reason stand alone.

**Issue 17**
- Problem: Pronoun ambiguity. "Then go tell her" follows two lines about Tess and reads as Dane being sent to tell Tess. It means Pike.
- Location: "Pike hears it from me first," he said. "Before anything happens. Not after." / "Then go tell her."
- Suggested fix: Replace with "Then go call Pike."

**Issue 18**
- Problem: The costs are incomplete against the bible and outline. Dane states the referral and the relationship on the record, but "the case" is reduced to "off this detail". Nothing says he loses the prosecution or his place in it.
- Location: "I'm off this detail when relief lands, and there'll be a referral to internal affairs."
- Suggested fix: Add a clause that he is off the case, and that Quist will have to say why in front of Moreau.

**Issue 19**
- Problem: Pike's reaction. Dane missed a nightly 2300 check-in on a live protective detail and reports five hours late. Pike answers on the first ring and never mentions it, which is out of character for a "terse, tired, protective" supervisor.
- Location: "Pike answered on the first ring. Quist came on a minute after."
- Suggested fix: Add one flat Pike line ("You missed eleven.") before she goes to relief, or have her say she had been trying the sat phone.

**Issue 20**
- Problem: Weather timing departs from the bible. The bible has the second system clearing with a northwest wind on the evening of Day 8. The draft has the wind in the northwest, the sky "hard, washed blue" and the harbor flattening by about 1:30 pm.
- Location: "The wind had gone into the northwest, off the land, and was flattening the harbor behind the pier."
- Suggested fix: Make it the first backing and a thinning of the sky at one, with the full clear by the 3:30 scene. Or log the earlier clearing as a canon change.
