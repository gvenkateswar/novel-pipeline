# Chapter 08 — What Ned Told: Beat Sheet

**Heading:** Day 8 — Friday
**Target:** ~3,600 words, six scenes. Scene 5 is sex scene 3, about 800 words.
**POV:** Dane (Scene 1 only, about 750 words, his ~10% share), then Beth (Scenes 2–6).
**Weather arc:** the second storm is still blowing hard at 4 a.m. It eases through the morning, and the wind goes into the northwest in the afternoon. Cold, hard light by Scene 6. High water is about 4:15 a.m., low about 10:30 a.m., and sunset is a little past four.
**Chapter promise (outline):** Can they use the leak against the people she has been feeding?

## Pre-flight: conflicts and decisions (read before drafting)

1. **Sex scene length.** The brief asks for 800–1,000 words. Heat Rule 2 (scene 3) says 700–800, and the outline's pacing flag says ~700–800 for Ch 08. **Resolution:** target **800 words**, which fits both. Do not go over 850. The chapter's pacing flag (two revelations plus a sex scene) is the reason.
2. **Location of scene 3.** The brief says "the wheelhouse or Ned's cabin." The bible (Heat Rule 2) and the outline both say **the North Reach's crew cabin, lower bunk, steel bulkhead.** **Resolution:** use the two-bunk crew cabin aft of the mess. Make it the deckhands' cabin, not Ned's bed. Ned has been sleeping on the mess settee by the gangway door, where he sat dark in Ch 07. It is Beth's space: eleven years of naps between runs, and her spare oilskins on the hook. This is new ground after the fish house cot, and it does not put the scene in an old man's bunk.
3. **The bait argument moves into Ch 08.** The outline's Danger Beats table puts "Dane objects, and her reasoning wins" in the Ch 09 row. The brief ends Ch 08 on that argument, and Heat Rule 7 needs scene 3 to "end with a decision." **Resolution:** Ch 08 ends with the bait plan agreed and Dane's objection lost. **Ch 09 must not rerun the argument.** Ch 09 still holds the channel 9 call, recruiting Russ, and Beth setting the positions (Dane on the car deck, Russ at the gangway). Ch 08 names the ground (the boat, tonight, channel 9). It does not assign anyone's position.
4. **When Dane learns the EMD's name.** The outline has Dane learn "Teresa Marr" inside his own POV section. Inside his head he would either think the answer (and settle the leak before Beth's recognition, which the brief wants to carry the weight) or think around it (the cheating the outline warns against). **Resolution, honest in both POVs:** at 4 a.m. Pike does not have the name to hand ("Ops made the calls. I'll have it for you at seven"). Dane leaves with two-thirds of the answer, a bank bag that passed through **two** pairs of hands (the red vest's and Ned's), and real fear that Beth has gone to the one who carried it and lied to him about the reefer. That keeps Ned live into Scene 2. The callback comes in Scene 4 on the pier, in Beth's POV, and his answer matches what she has already reached. The plot is unchanged; only the staging moves. Dane still reaches Tess through the EMD notification.
5. **Dane's section runs ~750 words, not ~1,000.** The brief says "short," and the budget needs the room.
6. **The notebook is on the WORKBENCH,** squared to its edge (Ch 07 canon), not "on the table" as the outline has it.
7. **The condoms.** It was never established who has the box after Ch 07. **Resolution:** Beth tucked the box into the lid pocket of Dane's go-bag on Day 6 (one line; nobody has contradicted it). Dane comes to the ferry with the go-bag on his good shoulder. Protection gets one line in Scene 5 (Heat Rule 10).
8. **No conflict found** with the continuity log's state at the end of Ch 07: Beth is aboard and locked in the wheelhouse with both radios on 16, Ned is dark at the gangway, and Dane is asleep in the fish house with the book open.

## Chapter-wide constraints (for prose-writer and voice-editor)

- **Tess does not appear on the page,** not even on the radio. She is present through Ned's account, Dane's callback and Beth's memory. The reader gets **one** sympathy moment for her, in Scene 2 (marked).
- **Ned's coffee:** he brings it **once** this chapter, in Scene 2, as the apology after the confession. That is **2 of 3**, leaving one for the book.
- **Ned:** confession of ten lines at most, short, angry, ashamed, no speech. No "goddamn" (spent). Only "Jesus" or "damn," and at most one.
- **Swears:** Dane has **none** (his two are Ch 06 and Ch 09). Beth may have at most one mild word, and none in narration. Keep the chapter well under the ceiling.
- **Beth's counting, at most twice, assigned:** (1) Scene 3, the seconds the Icom's knob sits on 9 before she turns it back. (2) Scene 6, the steps from the wheelhouse ladder to the stern gate. Nowhere else. **Dane does not count**: his scene runs in distances, doors and what hands did.
- **"Not asking," once, assigned:** Scene 4. She does not ask what the call cost him, and he tells her anyway. This makes him state the costs unprompted.
- **Button endings, at most two, assigned:** Scene 2 and Scene 5 may end on a short line. Scenes 1, 3, 4 and 6 end on action or image.
- **Retired and banned:** "put it where she put things"; "longer than X needed/needed to"; "the way she'd [boat task]"; "right side clear"; "wet/hard" as a pairing; "let go of everything"; neat aphoristic closers. Everything in FORBIDDEN PHRASES. No "trust me," "I've got you," "you're safe now" from Dane. No "We need to talk," no "It's not what it looks like."
- **No recap at the top of any scene.** Scene 1 opens on the cold stove, not on what Beth did.
- **"Fentanyl" must not appear.** Use "the pills" if needed.
- **The leak reveal is recognition, not news.** Readers are at 85% on Tess. The weight comes from what it costs Beth (specific memories, her body, the grief), not from surprise. No narration question like "Could it be Tess?" The scene does not explain the reveal back to the reader.
- **Romance registers in every Beth scene** (bible cold-read rule), even in the confrontation: one held look, one touch.

### Scene 1
**Setting:** The fish house on Calloway Cove, about 4 a.m., high water, the second storm still on the shingles. Sensory anchor: the oil-drum stove gone **cold to the touch** (the first thing his hand finds), the lantern burnt dry and smelling of kerosene wick, and the cove slapping the ledge close below the water-side window at the top of the tide.
**Characters present:** Dane (POV). Pike and Quist on the Iridium.
**Surface action:**
- Dane wakes cold on the cot. He reads the room before he moves: one landward door, barred from inside? No, the bar is lifted and leaned against the frame. The window is on the water side. The loft hatch is shut. Her float coat is gone from the nail. The stove is cold. In the dark, his left arm is stiff under the strips.
- His watch: the 2300 alarm is off. He set it in front of her, so she switched it off. He has missed the check-in with Pike by five hours.
- A dead lantern, then his flashlight. The sat phone is on the bench where he can reach it. His gun is on his hip. The red Pelican kit is closed and latched. The Icom is gone. On the workbench, the notebook lies **open at the CALLOWAY, E. page with the dollar sign, squared to the bench edge.** Dane notes the squaring. Someone who leaves in a rage doesn't do that. Someone who wants to be understood does.
- He reads his own page as she read it. Short, judging sentences, his register: what the page proves to someone who doesn't know what he knows. Every location. The landing time. "ASSESS — do not disclose to subject." He sees the D6 line he struck out and knows what she couldn't read under it.
- He does not write the morning's entry. He closes the book and puts it in his inside pocket. What he has to say goes by voice.
- Outside on the ledge, in the open with spray coming over and the arm held against his ribs, the sat phone to Pike. Quist joins. In plain words he reports: (a) the witness has read the credibility log and has left the detail of her own accord, armed with nothing, carrying a handheld radio, about 2300; (b) he has been in a relationship with the protectee since Day 4 night; he named it to her then and is naming it now; (c) he asks to be relieved and removed from the detail at the first boat that can reach the island.
- Pike takes it without comment. Quist says one dry, procedural line: she will have to disclose all of it, and she knows he knows that. Pike: **"I'll have relief out there the first hour the sea allows. Until then you're still the detail. Act like it."**
- Dane asks the one question: who outside the Marshals had the landing time? Pike: the Coast Guard station, and Ops called "the local emergency management contact." She doesn't have the name in front of her. "I'll have it for you at seven."
- Walking back in, he lays out what he has in sightlines and hands. The $9,000 came in the bank bag. He saw the bag cross the rail on Day 1: a short woman in a red vest handed it up, and a captain who would not look at him took it. Two pairs of hands. Beth's Friday check-in went to the woman in the red vest. The Ashby key came from the captain. The captain lied to him about the reefer.
- Where she'd go: not the store (public), not her cottage (watched). **"She'll go where she's good."** Steel, a radio, a door that locks: the ferry. And Ned.
- He shoulders the go-bag on the good side and goes out into the weather by the grown-over path to the cove road, gun drawn and held low.
**Subtext:** He understands her completely, and that is the punishment. Reporting the relationship is the promise from scene 1 kept on the page (Heat Rule 11), and it is kept before he knows whether she will ever speak to him again, which proves he isn't doing it to win her back. The fear underneath is Marisol: a woman who went somewhere he didn't plan, and a parking lot.
**Tension source:** Situational and internal. She is out in the dark with Doucette on the roads (he doesn't know about the woodshed). The one person he can place her with is a man who carried the bag and lied to him. And the career he has protected for four years ends on this call.
**Ends on:** Action, not a line. Dane at the top of the cove path in first gray, the harbor below him and the ferry's work lights on at the stone pier, already moving toward them.
**Word count target:** ~750.
**Voice notes:** Dane's sentences run a little longer than Beth's and judge people. No counting. Rooms arrive as doors and distances. He does not swear. No backstory monologue: Marisol appears as one image at most (a parking lot, ten minutes).

### Scene 2
**Setting:** The North Reach's wheelhouse at the stone pier, first light (about 6:15). Sensory anchor: wet wool and burnt coffee. The fixed VHF's coiled mic cord stretched to its full length. Both doors: the inside companionway door Ned comes through, and the **outside door to the steel ladder, which Beth has unlatched and propped open** behind her, with the wind pushing cold into the room.
**Characters present:** Beth, Ned.
**Surface action:**
- Ned knocks on the companionway door and unlocks it from outside. Before she lets him in, Beth has set herself up: outside door open behind her, the fixed set's mic off its clip in her right hand, **thumb off the key**, the set on 16, and the bagged Icom zipped in her float coat. If he comes at her, she keys 16 and the Coast Guard and half the bay hear it. That is her witness. This is the Danger Beats row: mic in her hand, door open, no Dane.
- Ned sees the mic and reads it. He stops just inside the door. His finger goes to the hearing aid. Beth: "Leave it on." He lowers his hand.
- She names **two lies**, plainly, as statements, the way she reads weather. One: the dealer "always runs his unit." Two: the Ashby fire, the Generac backfeed story, which had too many parts (his tell is over-detail).
- **Ned's confession (≤10 lines, pulled out of him by her questions):** he has known about the reefer for a year: unit off, wrong weights, a sternman from away loading it. He took no money. He looked away because that dealer's contract is the night freight, the night freight is the winter subsidy, and without the subsidy the boat stops and the island goes with it. He tried to send her home that night ("I'll work it short"). He's angry at himself and shows it in his body (a hand flat on the chart table, the rag wiped across the window, making it worse), not in a speech. He does not say sorry.
- Beth: the Ashby place. Who did you tell?
- Ned, offended: **"I told nobody where you were. Nobody but Tess. Tess doesn't count."**
- Beth doesn't answer. Ned fills the silence, which he never does. He tells Tess everything; he always has. Supper every night, separate houses. He knew she ran the pump after hours for cash, the tank loan, and called it her business. "She's scared, Bethie. Since the storm she's scared all the time. I thought it was the loan."
- **Tess sympathy moment (the one in the chapter):** Ned's specific account. Every night this week Tess has slept in the store's back office in her chair beside the booster with the light on, "sitting by that phone." He thought she was waiting for Beth to call. And at the 0800 net, she has ticked Beth's line every morning since the lines went dead. Played straight. It is love and guilt at once, and Ned doesn't know which he is describing.
- Ned goes below without being asked and comes back with coffee (2 of 3), sets it by her left hand on the chart table, and steps back to the door. She doesn't pick it up yet. She clips the mic back onto its hook, and that is how he knows she believes him.
**Subtext:** Ned is confessing the smaller crime to avoid looking at the bigger one he has just described without knowing it. Beth is holding a weapon on the man who taught her the boat, and it costs them both. He came up to the wheelhouse hoping she'd let him make it right with coffee. She wants him to be the leak, because the alternative is worse, and she can feel it arriving.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and situational. Ned is big, and she is cornered on purpose with an exit. The real tension is that the reader hears "Tess doesn't count" a second before Beth lets herself.
**Ends on:** Permitted button. The coffee steaming by her hand, untouched, and Ned in the doorway with his finger halfway to the hearing aid. He leaves it on.
**Word count target:** ~650.

### Scene 3
**Setting:** The wheelhouse, alone, about 6:40. Ned has gone down to check the bilge pumps (she hears the pump cycle start under her feet). Sensory anchor: the windows Ned's rag has smeared, and through them, across the gray harbor, **the store, the only lit building on the island**, its generator's flat roar carried on the wind, and the fuel float's pole light dark.
**Characters present:** Beth.
**Surface action:**
- Beth takes the Icom out of its freezer bag and switches it on. She turns the knob off 16 and onto **9, their old channel**, and holds it there with nobody talking. *(Count 1: she counts the seconds the knob sits on 9 before she turns it back to 16.)* She does not key it.
- The recognition, staged through objects and her hands, not a list in her head. Each piece arrives as a physical memory with one concrete detail, and each one undoes the excuse she made on the page at the time:
  - The phone that rang when no one else's could. *("The booster's on the generator.")*
  - The spiral log on the counter: *Calloway — home (phone)*, in Tess's hand, the first name the back door came for.
  - The green fuel ledger with nothing after four on Day 0, and the float lit at nine. *("Cash after hours isn't a crime. She needs the money.")*
  - "Local emergency management." Beth thought it meant the county office in Pruett. On Cutter, local emergency management is a woman who was slow putting her hand down at town meeting.
  - The bank bag, the one Beth missed in the notebook: her paycheck in it, the zip, and the seal tag Tess initials in green marker. Only one pair of hands closes that bag.
  - Tess stopped touching her. On Day 5, hands on the register, the bag, the ledger, never on Beth's arm or braid. Beth notices it now, for the first time (bible tic 3 payoff).
- **The fish house, hard (the cold-read flag's main lever):** she remembers the notebook line she read past, *secondary location, B's family property, not disclosed*. He kept it out of his own book. Every place that went up the pipe came back as a man with a gun. The one place that went nowhere has had nothing come to it for four days. If it were Dane, the fish house would have burned first. The pipe was never the notebook. It was the counter, Ned's supper, and the EMD's phone.
- **The weight:** one memory, under a line, never explained: Tess's truck on the mainland ferry the day Beth's mother left, Beth at nine in the passenger seat, and Tess buying her a whoopie pie at Pruett and not making her talk. Then Beth's body: she sits down on the helm chair because her legs decide it, and she drinks Ned's coffee, now cold.
- She doesn't say the name in narration as a reveal line. She has known it for a page.
- Through the smeared glass she sees a figure come down the pier road with one arm held against his ribs and a bag on the other shoulder, walking the way he walks into any space: the line of the pier wall first, then the gangway.
**Subtext:** Clearing Dane of the leak gives her nothing, because the notebook was never only about the leak. He was still assessing her. She loses Tess and gets back a man she is still furious with, both in the same five minutes, and has no task to put her hands to.
**Tension source:** Internal. The reader is ahead of her, so the tension is how much it hurts, not whether. Also situational: the store is lit across the water, and Tess is in it with whoever is in it with her.
**Ends on:** Image, not a line. Dane at the head of the gangway, and Ned stepping off the mess-deck door onto the aluminum brow to stand in his way.
**Word count target:** ~400. Short on purpose. Don't pad: the brevity is the weight.

### Scene 4
**Setting:** The gangway and then the crew mess, about 7:00 to 7:40. Sensory anchor: the aluminum brow ringing and shifting under boots as the ferry works against the pier fenders. Then the mess: a steel table with fiddle rails, the bench, the settee where Ned has been sleeping with his green wool jacket balled as a pillow, and wet gear steaming on the heater.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Ned. Pike on the Iridium (Dane's side only).
**Surface action:**
- From the top of the wheelhouse ladder, Beth watches Ned stand on the brow with his arms folded, saying nothing. Dane stops at the pier end. He does not push past, and his gun is holstered. Restraint that reads as care: he lifts his good hand, open, and waits for Beth, not for Ned.
- The Iridium goes off in Dane's jacket. It's seven, out on the open pier. He takes the call there in the wind, eyes on Beth the whole time. She can't hear the words. She sees him turn his head and look across the harbor at the lit store, then back at her, and she knows what name he has just been given because she already has it.
- Beth to Ned: "Let him on." Ned steps aside and stays on deck. He gives them the mess.
- **The clearing, dramatized as a confrontation.** Beth asks short questions and Dane answers each one straight. She holds out her hand for the notebook, he gives it to her, and she keeps it under her palm on the table while he talks. Keep his answers short. Don't let it become an explanation block.
  - **The order:** Day 2. Quist asked and Pike ordered: log her, report nightly, don't tell her. "She doesn't know" was about her. He obeyed because telling her could be framed as coaching, and coaching would end her testimony.
  - **The $9,000:** Pike's ops gave the EMD's name at seven: Teresa Marr. The bag: "Her hands, then Ned's, then the credit union. The teller found it under your slip, under her tag." Tess planted it. He tells her he wrote "no indication" on Day 3 and believed her from then on.
  - **The entry he is ashamed of:** D3, the generator shed. He asked her about money while she taught him the bleed screw because Quist wanted it asked, and he wanted to know her anyway. "Both were true. One went in the book."
  - **The struck D6 line:** she makes him say what it was. It began *Subject and I*, and he struck it because he would not put her in Quist's book that way. What he reported this morning, he reported by voice, to Pike.
  - **Marisol (brief, in context, not a monologue):** why he obeyed the order instead of telling Beth. The last time he bent a rule for a woman because she asked, she died in a parking lot. One or two lines.
- **"Not asking" beat (the chapter's one):** Beth does not ask what the call cost him. He tells her anyway, plainly, as facts.
  - He has reported that she read the log and reported the relationship, both by name.
  - He is off the case at the first relief. There will be a referral to internal affairs (say "internal affairs" or "OPR" once).
  - The relationship is now in the record. The government has to hand it to the defense, and a lawyer can put it to her under oath at trial.
  - **"I'd rather you get asked than have it found."**
- Beth believes him, and the prose shows why through action. It cost him, he told her before she could ask, and his seven o'clock name matches the one she reached at 6:40. Her tell: she slides the notebook back across the table to him instead of keeping it. The romance beat: her scarred left palm stays flat on the table between them, and his good hand stops an inch from it and waits.
- Ned in the mess doorway: someone has to bring Russ, and he's the one who can walk up the Harbor Road without being looked at. Dane names a reason Ned can't go (Doucette on the roads). Ned's answer is island logic: nobody shoots the ferry captain in daylight on his own road. Beth backs Ned. Ned goes ashore in his green jacket.
**Subtext:** He is asking to be believed and knows asking isn't enough, so he pays first. She is deciding whether a man who did the right thing the wrong way is someone she can stand next to tonight. Both know the notebook betrayal and the leak are now separate things, and only one is solved.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Her anger is earned and doesn't evaporate. Situational: Tess's name is now in the federal record, and Ned is about to walk past her store.
**Ends on:** Action. Ned's boots on the brow going ashore, the brow ringing, and the two of them alone on the boat with the notebook on the table between them, neither one picking it up.
**Word count target:** ~650.

### Scene 5: Sex scene 3 (heat 3–4)
**Setting:** The deckhands' crew cabin aft of the mess, about 1 p.m. The wind is easing. **Daylight**, for the first time between them (both earlier scenes were at night by stove and lantern). Sensory anchor: the porthole on the outboard side looks straight at the **wet granite face of the pier** a few feet off, with a slot of hard-washed sky above it, so the light comes in gray and moving. The steel bulkhead is cold through a shirt. The fenders squeal against stone as the boat works. The bilge pump starts and stops under the deck plates. Her spare oilskins hang on the hook. The lower bunk has a thin vinyl mattress and a navy ferry-district blanket.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:**
- **Lead-in (≤100 words, no recap):** she has slept two hours in the lower bunk, and he has been sitting on the deck plates outside the cabin door with his back to the bulkhead. She opens the door, and he looks up.
- **She leads, and he lets her** (Heat Rule 2). She brings him in, shuts the door, sits him on the bunk's edge. **The arm:** she lays his left arm along the bunk rail herself, and that is where it stays. Consent, in their voices. A check from her about the arm, and an answer from him that won't give her the answer (new wording; don't reuse the bible's sample lines word for word). And an explicit yes from him that is about more than the arm: he asks whether she wants this knowing it's on the record. Her answer is a yes with plot in it. Something like: then let them ask her; she'll say she chose it in daylight.
- Protection: **one line.** The box from the go-bag's lid pocket, where she tucked it on Day 6.
- **New images (do not reuse Ch 05 or Ch 06):** daylight on skin, seeing each other plainly. His stubble gone to most of a beard. The gray strips on his arm. She looks at him the way she would look at a chart she'll have to run in the dark, learning it now. The cold steel at her back, then at his. The narrow bunk forcing it slow and close. The slap and squeal of the boat moving under them. Her braid coming loose and his good hand in it.
- **BANNED here (used in Ch 05/06):** palms flat on his chest, fingers on his wrist like a pulse, stove heat, his cold mouth, keeping or losing an exit, "wet/hard."
- Frank vocabulary, sparingly (Heat Rule 4); one plain noun lands harder than three. No more than two sentences in a row on who touches what. Intercourse, her on top because of his arm. That is a practical reason and also the point: she has the controls.
- **Plot touches inside the scene (≥2, Heat Rule 8):** the record, the arm, and the night coming. She says his name once (not "Reyes," not "Deputy").
- **Within 150 words of climax, back to the suspense question (Heat Rule 7):** lying on the narrow bunk against the steel, she watches the slot of sky in the porthole go from gray to hard blue. The wind has gone northwest. When the sea lies down, the *Margaret Ann* can sail. The decision forms in her before she says it. **No afterglow ending.**
**Subtext:** Trust rebuilt with full knowledge. This is the bible's Need paid off: she chooses him having checked everything, at the exact moment trusting him gets most dangerous, because tonight she will put her life where he says he'll stand. He lets her lead because he has given up the right to steer anything between them, and she knows it.
**Tension source:** Internal. Whether she can want a man who wrote her down, and doing it in daylight, with nothing to hide behind. Situational: the clock, the clearing wind, Ned and Russ somewhere ashore.
**Ends on:** Permitted button. Beth, still against him and looking at the sky in the porthole, says the opening line of the plan, something that announces it without explaining it (e.g. that she needs the boat, tonight, and her own voice). He goes still under her hand.
**Word count target:** ~800 (inside the sex scene's 800–850; see Pre-flight 1).

### Scene 6
**Setting:** The wheelhouse, about 3:30 p.m., the light going. The northwest wind is clearing the sky and laying the harbor's chop flat in streaks, and the glass is rising (she taps it once; **do not end on it**). Sensory anchor: the cold coming off the windows now that the wind has backed off them. Across the harbor, the store's lit windows and the *Margaret Ann* on her mooring with deck lights on.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:**
- **Beth's plan, as statements, then reasons when he asks for them** (her voice rule):
  - The network has to reach her before the first boat runs on Sunday. Once relief lands tomorrow, they lose their chance and they know it.
  - When the sea lies down, Arsenault can sail, and the island side of it walks off with him.
  - Tess will pass on whatever she hears.
  - **Only Beth's own voice on channel 9 will make Tess believe it.** Not Ned's, and not a marshal's.
  - The story: she's alone on the ferry tonight. (Keep the exact lie for Ch 09; here just "alone, on the boat.")
  - The ferry is steel. She knows it blind in the dark, and they don't.
  - The ground is hers to choose instead of theirs.
- *(Count 2:)* she walks it out loud: the steps from the foot of the wheelhouse ladder to the stern gate, the number she's walked in the dark for eleven years.
- **Dane objects, with a reason and an alternative** (his voice rule): she's the witness, and Pike said he is still the detail. The alternative: lock this door, both of them behind it, until relief comes at noon. He doesn't raise his voice. One plain line on the last woman who asked him for something against the plan, without saying Marisol's name again.
- **Her reasoning wins, and the winning reason is humane:** if they sit tight and Arsenault sails, Tess is the only witness left against him, and she is in that store with him tonight. Waiting kills Tess. Beth says "Tess" aloud for the first time in the chapter here. Second reason: this way the truth about the leak comes from Tess's own mouth, not from Ned or a phone log.
- Dane concedes, with one condition true to him: he reports it to Pike **before, not after** (an echo of "I want you to hear that before, not after," not a quote).
- Not yet: no positions assigned and no Russ on the page (Ch 09). Ned and Russ can be seen far off, coming down the pier road together in the last light, small figures. That's all.
**Subtext:** She is making herself bait partly to save the woman who sold her. That is the most Beth thing in the book, and Dane sees it. He is letting a protectee take a risk against the plan, the exact thing he swore off, and doing it by the book: he reports it first.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal (his duty against her judgment). Situational: the light is going, and the store is lit.
**Ends on:** Image and action, not a line. Dane goes out onto the wheelhouse wing into the northwest wind with the Iridium, face turned up to the clearing sky, reporting. Beth, inside the glass, takes the Icom out of its freezer bag and sets it on the chart table beside the fixed set, the knob still on 16. **Ch 09 opens on her thumb switching to 9. Don't switch it here.**
**Word count target:** ~400.
**Chapter promise set up:** Yes. The leak is known, and the plan is to use it against the people Tess has been feeding.
