# Chapter 04 — Glassing the Harbor: Beat Sheet

**Heading:** Day 3 — Sunday
**POV:** Beth, close third, all five scenes
**Target:** about 2,900 words across five scenes (600 + 450 + 450 + 850 + 550)
**Chapter promise (outline):** When will they come again, and what is in that notebook?

---

## Pre-flight: continuity checks and flags

No conflict blocks the outline entry. These are the points where the sources disagree or a rule limits staging. The prose-writer should follow the resolution given.

1. **Where the money talk happens.** The outline puts Dane's money questions "at the Ashby place that afternoon", followed by the fuel-line lesson and the kiss. Style Sample 3 (Dane POV, later) remembers her telling it "in the generator shed with fuel on her hands." **Resolution:** the money talk, Dane's history and the kiss all happen in one generator-shed scene while she bleeds the line. That satisfies both sources.
2. **Generator mechanics.** The bible's Ashby sensory line mentions "the pull cord's rasp". Ch 03 is canon: a 20 kW diesel standby with a **hand primer and no pull cord**. Use the primer lever and the bleed screw. No pull cord.
3. **The limp.** In Ch 03 Beth kept the limp to herself after seeing the heel print. In this chapter she tells Dane for the first time, after she has checked it with her own eyes. That fits her character (she reports only what she has checked). Dane should register that she held it back for a day. That is a small echo of his own withholding, and he does not comment on it.
4. **The hooded man stays unnamed.** Nobody says "Doucette." Quist's records give Dane only "two crew listed." Beth calls him "the sternman" or "him."
5. **Radio range.** Per Research note 14, the handheld does not reach across Lookout Hill to the north point. So the 0800 net is heard **on the hilltop** (line of sight to the store), never at the Ashby place. Beth brings the ferry-issued Icom handheld this morning. Ch 07 needs her to have it at hand later.
6. **Sat phone.** Open sky only (World Rule 5). Dane's 0800 call is made standing on the bare granite, away from her. The 11:20 call is made on the Ashby porch.
7. **"Woods path" and "woods road."** They go up and down by the **woods path** (islanders only). Near the bottom the path crosses the **old camp road** (the woods road) that serves the summer camps on the back of the hill. Russ is there with his truck.
8. **Palm-flat-on-his-chest** is used in Style Sample 4 (Ch 05). Don't spend it on the kiss here. Use a different touch.
9. **Names.** Beth thinks of him and addresses him as "Reyes" until the kiss. The first "Dane" comes right after he stops it. He has called her "Beth" since the back door.
10. **Tess's likeable scene is on the radio, not in person.** Beth and Dane have no in-story reason to go to the store on Day 3, and Tess must not learn the Ashby move before Ned tells her at supper tonight. The 0800 storm net on channel 68, with Tess as net control, is her scene. Keep it **clue-free**. The Ch 03 cold read said she is over-seeded, so this scene is only warmth.
11. **Language budget.** Dane: no swears (his two are reserved for Ch 06 and Ch 09). Russ: none. Beth: at most one mild word, at the weather or gear. No strong swears are needed in this chapter.
12. **Buttons.** At most two button endings. Only Scene 3 and Scene 5 may end on a short closing line or image. Scenes 1, 2 and 4 end mid-motion or on a longer beat. No barometer endings.
13. **Two voices (cold-read rule).** Beth counts: moorings, steps, minutes, seconds of primer strokes. Dane never counts. He reads hands, distances and people, and passes judgment on them in slightly longer sentences. He uses "Walk me through", repeats back in fewer words, and aims his humor at himself.

### Scene 1

**Setting:** Bare granite on top of Lookout Hill, first light (about 6:30 a.m.). The wind has gone round to the north as the low passes, and spray still carries up 160 feet. Wet lichen soaks through Beth's bibs at the knees and hips. The harbor lies below like a chart: moorings, the store dock with the red-hulled *Margaret Ann* storm-bound alongside, the fish co-op pier, the *North Reach* starboard-to the stone pier beside the twisted transfer bridge, and Beth's own gray Cape on the Harbor Road.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane. Seen through the glasses only: the hooded man, Ned.

**Surface action:**
- Open on Beth flat on the granite with Dane's binoculars, counting moorings through the spray. The count is aloud or under her breath and specific: twenty-two boats where there should be twenty-four, and she names the two missing ones (hauled for the winter). No recap of the climb. At most one line places the woods path behind them: spruce, dark, chosen because the north road is the only road and anyone looking would watch it.
- Dane lies beside her, a little higher, where he can see the path mouth behind them. His right side is clear. One line of his humor at his own expense: his hiking boots have gone through the granite's wet layer, "sold to me as waterproof."
- At about 6:50 the *Margaret Ann*'s aluminum skiff comes out from the dragger's side. Hood up, one man rowing (no outboard; too much noise on a Sunday). Beth tracks him. Then **the Ned beat:** the skiff passes under the ferry's stern, where Ned is on deck with a pump hose. The rower lifts one hand off the oar. Ned turns his back and keeps working the hose. Beth can't tell whether that was refusal or an answer. She says nothing about it to Dane yet and files it.
- The man ties up at the fish pier ladder, climbs, and walks up the pier. **The walk:** head down, favoring the left leg, the right heel coming down hard. Beth's body registers it before she speaks: her free hand flattens on the granite.
- She tells Dane, conclusion first: "That's him." Then the reasons, because he asks. The walk from Day 0. The heel print on her back step yesterday, heavy on the right. She admits she saw the print and kept it until she could check it against the walk. Dane repeats it back in fewer words ("Left leg. Right heel. You waited to be sure"). He doesn't comment on her holding it back. She sees him note it.
- Dane photographs the man by pressing his phone lens to the binocular eyepiece while Beth holds the glasses steady on the rock. Close quarters, his cheek near hers, the cold metal of the phone against her knuckles. This is the one romance-awareness line for the scene.
- **The threat beat:** the man doesn't go to the store. He walks the Harbor Road to Beth's driveway, goes up it, and disappears round the back of her cottage. Beth counts the seconds he is out of sight (somewhere near ninety). He comes back round the front, stands one moment looking at her kitchen window, and walks back toward the pier.

**Subtext:** Beth is proving her worth the only way she trusts: by seeing what no one else can. She also kept the limp from Dane, which is a small private test of him she hasn't admitted to herself. Dane is quietly impressed and is filing everything she says, which he now has to do (the reader doesn't know this yet). Watching a man walk round the back of her own house turns the threat into something physical that she can't think her way past.

**Tension source:** Situational (they are exposed on a bare summit, and the man is at her house) and interpersonal (what she withheld, and Ned's turned back).

**Ends on:** Not a button. End mid-motion. Beth keeps the glasses on the man until he is back in the skiff, then hands them to Dane and tells him exactly where to look ("Third piling from the ladder. He's bailing."). Dane looks, and the scene cuts as he does.

**Word count target:** 600

### Scene 2

**Setting:** The harbor-side edge of the spruce, just below the bare top, 8:00 a.m. They have backed off the skyline into cover that still has line of sight to the store, so the handheld can reach. There is wet spruce duff underfoot and the Union Church below with nobody walking to it. Its bell rope knocks in the wind and nobody rings it.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane. Tess, Mrs. Ida Dyer, and Walt Grindle on the radio.

**Surface action:**
- Dane walks back out onto the open granite with the Iridium because it needs sky. Beth stays in the trees with the Icom on channel 68 at low volume, against her ear.
- **Tess's net, the likeable scene.** Tess opens the 0800 net, fast and warm, and works her list. Sunday: "Reverend says church is cancelled and pray at home. I say pray near your stove." Mrs. Dyer comes on and talks too long about her cat and her chimney. Tess lets her finish and doesn't hurry her, then tells her the chowder is coming at noon with last Thursday's paper, "so you'll know how it all came out." Walt Grindle checks in for the fire company, and Tess teases him about his generator. Then her credit offer to the whole net: anybody short on generator gas, come down, "pay me in April, or pay me in lobsters, I'm not proud." Then, for the people she can't raise: "Everybody I can't reach I'm counting home and dry unless somebody tells me different." Beth hears it as Tess covering her without saying her name.
- Beth gives one or two lines of memory, no more. Tess was the one who drove her to the mainland boat the day her mother left. They have never talked about it. Tess bought her a hot chocolate in Pruett Harbor and let her not drink it. Keep it to an image, not exposition.
- Meanwhile Beth catches pieces of Dane's call when the wind drops: "Sternman. Left leg. She picked him out at six hundred yards." Then "Yes." Then, after a pause, "I'll ask her." This is the reread plant for the afternoon's money questions. Beth hears it as procedure.
- Dane comes back into the trees and reports plainly. Quist ran the boat: Gloucester registry, Arsenault the owner, two crew listed. HSI will take the boat when the sea goes down. Until then nothing. Beth: "He's as stuck as we are." Dane explains the refusal she hasn't asked for, which he always does. He is one deputy with no backup on an island with no way off. If he goes after the man, he has left her alone, and his job is her. Then he offers what he can do instead: "I can make sure he never sees you." He doesn't say "trust me."
- He nods at the radio. "Who's that?" "Tess." "She's good at that." Beth: "She's good at everybody." Play it straight and warm. No irony in the prose.

**Subtext:** Beth is home in Tess's voice, the island holding itself together on a radio net, and she loves it. Dane is carrying an order he can't name. "I'll ask her" is him telling Quist yes. To Beth, refusing the arrest reads as care, and it is care.

**Tension source:** Situational (the man is loose, nothing can be done for days) and interpersonal (what Beth half-hears and doesn't weigh).

**Ends on:** Not a button. Tess closes the net ("Cutter net secured, stay warm, stay off the water, I mean it, Haskell"), and Beth switches the handheld off and clips it inside her float coat as they start down. The scene ends in motion.

**Word count target:** 450 (short scene. It earns its place as Tess's warmth plus the "I'll ask her" plant and Dane's reasoned refusal.)

### Scene 3

**Setting:** The woods path near the bottom of the hill, about 8:30 a.m., where it crosses the old camp road that serves the summer camps on the back side of Lookout Hill. Russ's pickup is idling in the ruts with its exhaust hanging low in the spruce, and a camp's porch shows through the trees with its storm shutters up.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Russ.

**Surface action:**
- Beth hears the truck before she sees it (she knows the engine by its note and names it as Russ's). She chooses not to retreat into the trees. Ducking out of sight of a constable on an island gets talked about more than walking past him. This is the same logic as Ch 03.
- Russ is leaning on his tailgate as if standing were optional, with a pipe wrench and a bucket in the bed. He has been checking camps for frozen pipes: "Somebody's got to make sure the summer people's pipes freeze in an orderly way."
- **Too curious, part one:** "You come down off the top? Nice morning for it." He asks what you can see from up there today, and then, before anyone answers, "Margaret Ann still tied up at Tess's?" He answers his own question: "Course she is. Nobody's going anywhere."
- **Too curious, part two (the reason to look at him):** "Heard a Generac running out on the point last night. Ned doesn't usually start the Ashby one till the power's been out a week." Cap off, cap on.
- Dane steps so his right side is clear and watches Russ's hands, not his face. Dane asks the useful question: "Who else would notice?" Russ, deadpan: "Anybody up the north road with ears. That's four houses. Two of them are deaf." Make it funny and true.
- Russ to Beth, kindly: if she needs anything, he's on 68 or at the town office. He drives off toward the next camp.
- On the walk out, the leads disagree in their own voices. Dane, in a longer sentence that judges a person: a man who tells you he knows where you sleep is either warning you or finding out what your face does when he says it, and he couldn't tell which with Russ. Beth, short, from her flaw (she trusts her island by default): "Russ has known where everybody on this island sleeps since he was twelve."

**Subtext:** Russ really is a lawman doing his job by noticing. Dane's wariness is right in method and wrong about the man. Beth defends her island without checking it, the same reflex that will cost her with Tess. The reader gets a clean reason to look at Russ (the boat, the generator, the north road) and another at Ned (Russ implies Ned started the generator early, which only means something if Ned knows who is out there).

**Tension source:** Interpersonal, between Dane and Russ (federal and local), and between Beth and Dane over whether her island can be trusted.

**Ends on:** **Button 1 of 2.** Dane: "He knows where you are." Beth: "He knows where the generator is. Out here that's the same thing." End on her line. Don't add a reaction beat.

**Word count target:** 450

### Scene 4

**Setting:** The Ashby generator shed, about 2:30 p.m. It has a concrete pad, the 20 kW diesel standby, diesel smell and cold, the door propped open on the driveway for light, and the wind in the spruce. The generator surged twice after lunch and died. Dane topped up the day tank from the jugs this morning and let the line suck air. Beth knows it from the sound before she reaches the shed.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.

**Surface action:**
- **The fuel-line lesson.** Beth teaches it as a sequence, competently and without fuss. She cracks the bleed screw on the fuel filter and works the hand primer, counting strokes, until the diesel comes out clean with no bubbles. Then she closes it, cracks the injector line nuts and has him crank. She makes Dane do it with his own hands, because you don't learn it by watching. He gets diesel on his knuckles. She gets it on hers. Keep the mechanics to a few precise nouns (bleed screw, primer lever, injector line), not a manual.
- Dane faces the open door while he works. Show it in one line early, because it matters at the end of the scene.
- **The money questions (the reread plant for Ch 07).** He starts "Walk me through" on what the ferry pays. Beth answers directly, once she has decided to. She is on winter hours. The bank took the *Ruthie C* on paper eight years ago. She spent four years paying off what the sale didn't cover. She owns the cottage outright and still pays taxes on her father's fish house on the cove, "which nobody's opened in four years." That is the fair plant for Ch 05. **The master's license:** she has a hundred-ton license in a drawer and has never asked for the wheel, "because asking Ned for the wheel is asking him to be old." Then Dane asks one question too many, in different words: anything come in lately? A settlement, insurance, anything? Beth laughs. "Who'd send me money?" She takes it for a man who wants to know her. Play it as intimacy. On a reread it is an interview.
- **Dane becomes a person.** Beth turns it on him: "You ask a lot about money for a man who hasn't said where he's from." He answers in his own longer rhythm, and this is the scene's center:
  - Lowell. His father came from Ponce and drove a city bus for thirty years. He knows every pothole in Lowell and will tell you about each one.
  - His mother was a dispatcher for the Lowell police. He grew up hearing her radio at supper, which is how he knew how to do this work before he knew what it was.
  - Humor at his own expense: his father thinks a marshal is a cowboy and asks every Christmas whether he has a horse yet. Or: his father would have had this generator running in four minutes and talked about it for a year. Use one, not both.
  - His Spanish: good enough with his father, bad on the phone. One line, and no Spanish words are needed.
  - Why he does this work: somebody has to stand between the person who saw it and the people who'd rather they hadn't. His mother did it with a radio.
- **Marisol, hinted only.** Beth asks, "You ever lose one?" Dane wipes his hands on the rag twice, longer than diesel needs. "Once." Then: "She asked for ten minutes. I gave them to her." He says nothing more and gives no name. Beth doesn't push. She answers with a task, which is her way: "Crank it."
- The engine catches and steadies, loud in the small shed, and the scene turns straight into Scene 5 without a break.

**Subtext:** Beth thinks she is being courted, and in a way she is. Dane is doing his job and wants to know her, and he can't separate the two, which is exactly the problem. Telling her about Lowell is the part he can give her honestly, a payment against what he can't say. "Ten minutes" is the first crack in his rule. The reader should feel it carries weight without knowing what it is.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal and internal. Two closed people trade information the way they would trade work, and both of them are aware of how close they are standing.

**Ends on:** Not a button. The diesel settles into its run and Beth puts her palm on the generator housing to feel it, which is her tic, on the machine and not on him. She turns and finds him nearer than she thought, and the action runs straight into Scene 5.

**Word count target:** 850 (warrants more than 700: it carries the lesson, the money plant, Dane's history and the Marisol hint, and it is the scene the Ch 03 cold read asked for)

### Scene 5

**Setting:** The same shed, continuous, with the generator hammering. Then the house: the sheeted front room and the porch, 11:20 p.m., with the north wind easing, the propane furnace ticking, the 340-step gravel drive running pale into the dark, and cold mothballs inside.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.

**Surface action:**
- **The kiss (keep it to about 150 words of contact).** Beth takes the rag from him and wipes the diesel off his right knuckles, over the old knot of scar. Her thumb stays on the scar. She asks what it was. "A door. It won." Mutual: she lifts her face, he comes down, and it is not tentative. Give the sensory detail: diesel and cold, stubble, his hand coming up to the back of her neck under the braid. Use one frank line of desire at most and no choreography.
- **He stops it, and the reason is readable on the page.** His hand stops first (the visible tell the bible requires). He steps back and looks past her at the open door. He has had his back to it for the whole kiss, the one thing he never does. Then the bible line: "I can't keep you alive with my hands on you. That's not a no. It's a not yet I can't afford." He is not curt. The reader must see what it costs him: he doesn't let go of the rag for a moment, and his voice comes slower.
- **She is stung and respects it.** Show the sting in her body and a task. She turns back to the generator and checks the bleed screw she already closed. Show the respect in her answer, plain and short: "Okay." Then, for the first time, his name: "Dane." He hears it and the reader sees him hear it.
- A practical kindness straight after (the bible's restraint-reads-as-care rule): he takes the heavier jug back to the house, the same way he did in Ch 03, and walks ahead of her down the path checking the drive. Bridge to the night quickly, in a line or two. Don't summarize.
- **The 11:20 porch call (the Ch 04 red herring at Dane).** Beth is awake (she has been counting the furnace cycles). Through the gap in a sheeted window she sees him on the porch with the Iridium, a short call. Then he writes in the notebook standing, facing the road, as he does every night (his tic).
- She goes out with her boots unlaced and a blanket round her shoulders. He closes the notebook. He doesn't hurry, and the deliberateness is what she notices. He puts it in his inside pocket. She registers where it lives.
- Short exchange. Beth: "Pike?" Dane: "Pike." "What does she want every night?" "To know you're alive." Beth: "What do you write?" Dane: "Where you were, and when. If something happens to me, somebody has to be able to find you." Every word of that is true. He tells her to lace her boots or go in, giving the reason (the porch boards are iced), not an order.
- One threat line in Beth's head as she looks down the drive, stated as a fact, not a question: the man walked round the back of her empty house this morning, and on an island an empty house is something somebody asks about by supper. This sets up "when will they come again." Tess learns the Ashby move from Ned at supper tonight, off-page, so the line is truer than Beth knows.

**Subtext:** The refusal stings because it was honest. She wanted him and he wanted her, and he chose her life over it. She respects that more than she would a yes. But the notebook closing is the first thing he has done that reads as hiding *from her*, and coming on the same night as the kiss it lands harder. Dane's answer about the notebook is true, and it is not the whole truth.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal (the refused kiss, the closed book) and internal (she can't check him with her hands, which is the book's emotional question).

**Ends on:** **Button 2 of 2, an image, not a line of narration posed as a question.** Beth goes in and shuts the door. Through the gap in the sheeted window she watches him take the notebook back out of his pocket, open it, and write one more line before putting it away. End on his pen moving. This sets up the chapter promise: what is in that notebook.

**Word count target:** 550 (about 250 for the kiss and its stop, about 300 for the porch. Below 700 because it is a continuation of Scene 4's location and should move fast.)
