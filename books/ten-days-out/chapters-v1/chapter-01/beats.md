# Chapter 01 — Night Freight: Beat Sheet

**Heading:** Day 0 to Day 1
**POV:** Beth only (close third, past tense)
**Target:** ~2,800 words across 5 scenes
**Chapter promise (from outline):** Who was the hooded man, and does he know her name?

---

## Flags before drafting

**Flag 1: the reefer has been wrong for a year, yet Beth's tell depends on years of hearing it run.** The bible says Ned has known for about a year that this dealer's Thursday reefer is wrong ("unit off"). Style Sample 1 has Beth hearing that unit "drone across the deck" every Thursday for six years. If the unit had been off every Thursday for a year with Beth on deck, she would have noticed long ago.
**Proposed fix (no plot change; adopted in these beats unless the orchestrator overrules):** Beth hasn't worked a Thursday night since last winter. That is when Ned gave the night runs to Kenny ("Kenny wants the night money"). She knows the unit's continuous drone from the five years before that and from the dealer's Monday day-boat trucks. Tonight she is on only because Kenny is out sick. This is one line in Scene 1, and it makes Ned's "I'll work it short" sharper as a red herring. Continuity-checker should log it.

**Flag 2: Style Sample 1 says "Six Thursdays a month."** A month has four or five Thursdays. Prose-writer must not copy that line. Use "every Thursday."

**Flag 3: why she doesn't phone from the mainland terminal.** There is cell coverage at Pruett Harbor, so a reader will ask why she waits. Scene 3 has to close that gap on the page: one bar, Ned three feet away, Pelletier's cab idling at the head of the ramp, and the deadhead leaving in six minutes. She doesn't yet know who on her own boat she can say it in front of. The beat sheet covers this. Do not cut it.

**Flag 4: the bank bag.** Do not show the bank bag leaving at dawn in Scene 5. In Ch 02 Dane watches Tess hand it to the deckhand (Kenny) on the boat's return run. In this chapter the bag appears only as the paycheck memory in Scene 2.

No conflict with the continuity log. This is the first chapter and the log has no entries.

### Scene 1

**Setting:** Car deck of the M/V *North Reach* at the Cutter Island slip, 8:40 p.m., Thursday. Sleet comes in flat across the open deck from the east. Sodium floods over the ramp turn it orange. The diesel idles at 900 rpm, and Beth feels it through her XtraTufs.

**Characters present:** Beth, Ned (voice on the handheld, then a shape at the wheelhouse window), Wade Pelletier, Shane Doucette (not named; to Beth he is "the hooded man")

**Surface action:**
- Open mid-task, no preamble. Beth goes down the line of vehicles with her gloves in her float-coat pocket, because you can't read a binder through neoprene. She checks the propane truck and the plumber's van. She tests each binder handle by its angle and the tension, takes up a quarter turn on one, and tugs a chain at its D-ring.
- The Icom on her chest clicks. Ned: "I can work it short. Go on home." Beth: "Kenny's in bed with a bucket." One line of narration covers it: she hasn't worked a Thursday night since last winter, when Ned gave the night runs to Kenny (Flag 1).
- The dealer's reefer is nose to the ramp. She puts her palm flat on the box. Give all three cues in sequence: the door seal isn't cold under her hand; the Thermo King's display is dark (switched off, not cycling); there is no drone, where for five years that unit droned across the deck like a second engine. A box of live product runs continuous.
- She goes aft along the trailer to the rear binders. The rear doors are open. A man in a black hood under an oilskin hands the last wax-box cartons up off a hand truck while Pelletier stacks them inside. A top carton catches as Pelletier shoves the stack, and its lid springs. Under a layer of live lobsters in wet rockweed and newspaper she sees plastic, vacuum-sealed and squared like bricks, with blue showing through.
- Both men see her see it. Hold this for one sentence only: Pelletier's hand stops on the carton, and the hooded man's face stays inside the hood.
- Beth, flat and bored: "Lid's sprung. You'll lose your count." She presses the lid down, cinches the load strap over the stack, drops the binder handle on the rear chain and pins it, then raps the trailer twice with her knuckle, the way she would on any truck. She walks forward.
- On the way she passes the stern gate, its chain across, the deck low to the water there, and the ring buoy in its bracket on the port bulwark. She reaches up and resets a spare binder that has fallen out of its bin, because a loose binder rolls. She does not look back.
- At the deckhouse door she passes the ramp-light switch and climbs the outside ladder. The rungs are iced and she counts them.

**Subtext:** She wants to run and does the opposite, at exactly her normal speed. She knows that how fast she moves now is the only thing the two men will judge her by. Being bored is her cover.

**Tension source:** Situational. Two men, one of them a stranger, have seen her. Forty-five minutes on a steel boat lies ahead, and there is no way off it.

**Ends on:** From the wheelhouse window, looking down: the hooded man walks off the boat and up the transfer bridge with his hand truck, against the flow, favoring his left leg. He doesn't look back either. Then the ramp starts coming up.

**Word count target:** 750 (the longest scene; it carries the hook and the Ch 09 geography)

**Advances:** Plot (inciting incident). Deepens character (competence under threat).

### Scene 2

**Setting:** The wheelhouse, 8:55 to 9:05 p.m., as they back off the slip and clear the harbor. It smells of burnt coffee, wet wool and warm electronics. The wipers are losing to the sleet.

**Characters present:** Beth, Ned (Pelletier is visible below in his cab)

**Surface action:**
- Ned is at the wheel, wiping the glass with the rag that makes it worse. Beth goes straight to the lighting panel on the aft bulkhead and throws the car-deck floods down to running lights. It's her job at every departure. Name the breakers by what they control.
- She takes the stool beside the radar instead of going below. Ned glances at her and says nothing.
- Below, Pelletier's dome light is off and his dash glows. His face is turned up toward the wheelhouse windows. She can't see his eyes, but she can see the angle of his head.
- They clear the breakwater. Across the harbor the fuel float at Marr's is lit, and a red-hulled dragger with a white wheelhouse lies alongside it with deck lights burning. She knows the boat: the *Margaret Ann*, from away, Gloucester on her stern.
- Short memory beat, under 150 words, prompted by the light: at four that afternoon, at Tess's counter, Beth slid her paycheck across in its envelope for the morning bank bag. Tess initialed the slip, tugged Beth's braid, and complained about the tank loan: "Eighteen hundred a month, Bethie. For dirt with steel in it." Then Tess turned the pump sign to CLOSED for six o'clock, the way she always has.
- Back in the present, Beth's explanation, in free indirect: cash after hours isn't a crime, and Tess needs every gallon. She files it.
- Ned keeps his eyes on the compass and doesn't look at the float. She registers that he doesn't look.

**Subtext:** She is choosing which odd thing to think about. The float light is a kind of relief, a small and ordinary wrong thing belonging to someone she loves, which she can explain away. The reefer she can't explain away.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal and internal. She is being watched from below, sitting beside a captain who tried to send her home and won't look at the float.

**Ends on:** Ned: "You're quiet." Beth: "Sleet." He reaches up and turns his hearing aid off with one finger.

**Word count target:** 600

**Advances:** Plot (two leak plants and one Ned red herring). Deepens Beth and Tess, and Beth and Ned.

### Scene 3

**Setting:** Mid-bay, then the Pruett Harbor ferry terminal, about 9:45 p.m. Sodium lights hang over the mainland ramp, and the transfer bridge clangs as it takes the ramp.

**Characters present:** Beth, Ned, Pelletier

**Surface action:**
- Mid-bay, kept short (about 150 words): her phone shows no service, which she knew. The VHF sits on 16 and 13, and she looks at the mic and leaves it hanging, because every boat and kitchen in the bay listens to that radio. She counts: crossing minutes left, cartons stacked in that trailer, how many bricks one carton would hold. Normally she makes one deck round mid-crossing. She tells Ned the binders are good and stays. He lets her, which he never does.
- At the terminal she lowers the ramp and works the lines. Then she has to go down and release Pelletier's binders herself, right beside his cab. She keeps to the routine: bare hands, handle up, chain off and laid in the bin. His window stays shut. She hears the cab heater fan and smells his cigarette through the seal.
- She stands in the shadow of the bulwark with her phone low at her hip, where a deckhand checks the time. As the reefer rolls up the ramp, the sodiums light the rear plate and the trailer number, and she takes two frames without lifting the phone. Brake lights come on at the head of the ramp and hold for a count of four, then he goes.
- She now has one bar of signal. Ned is on the wing three feet above her calling for the stern line, and the deadhead leaves in six minutes. She puts the phone in her bib pocket and zips it. Close Flag 3 on the page in one or two sentences of her reasoning: not in front of anyone, not yet, and not with the truck still sitting there.

**Subtext:** For the first time she doesn't know whom she can say this in front of on her own boat. She doesn't let herself finish the thought about Ned.

**Tension source:** Situational (proximity to Pelletier, the photograph) and interpersonal (Ned within earshot).

**Ends on:** The ramp comes up empty under the lights. Ned brings them around for home with no freight aboard, and the car deck behind her is bare steel and chain.

**Word count target:** 500

**Advances:** Plot (the evidence that gets the truck stopped). Character (she distrusts Ned without saying so).

### Scene 4

**Setting:** Beth's kitchen in her father's Cape on the Harbor Road, 12:40 to about 1:20 a.m. The woodstove is banked low. Her father's clock is going in the front room. The wall phone has a 12-foot cord. The barometer hangs by the kitchen door. The window looks down on the whole harbor and the fuel float.

**Characters present:** Beth (and a state police dispatcher or trooper on the phone, unnamed here)

**Surface action:**
- Do not recap the ride back or the tie-up. Enter on her at the table, writing in pencil on the back of the ferry district's tide calendar: the time, the plate, the trailer number, the count of cartons, the blue, and *hood, oilskin, left leg*. She writes it in order, the way she'd log a fuel transfer.
- She taps the barometer with one knuckle on her way past. It has fallen since supper. She notes the number.
- At the window, the fuel float light is still on. While she watches, it goes out. The *Margaret Ann*'s deck lights slide off the float and settle onto a guest mooring.
- She doesn't dial 911, because county dispatch goes out over the scanners half the island keeps on the kitchen counter. She takes the phone book from the drawer under the phone and dials the state police barracks number directly. 1:10 a.m. on the stove clock.
- The call: she gives conclusions first and reasons only when asked, as the bible has her talk. The dispatcher asks her name and she gives it. Asked "Did anyone see you?" she answers: "Two men." She keeps her palm flat on the wall beside the phone the whole time.
- After hanging up: nobody on Cutter locks a door. She hunts for the back-door key and finds it in her father's drawer with his reading glasses and a spark plug. She has to lift and shove the sticking thumb-latch to seat the door before the key will turn. She knows that sound and wouldn't make it by accident.

**Subtext:** She is carrying it alone, the way she carries everything. Reporting it doesn't make her feel safer. It makes it real, and her name is now attached to it.

**Tension source:** Internal. The quiet house, and how big the thing she has just done is.

**Ends on:** She sits in her father's chair facing the mudroom door, still in her bib pants, with the kettle on and the key on the table in front of her.

**Word count target:** 550

**Advances:** Plot (the report). Character, and it plants the cottage's geography for Ch 02: the latch, the barometer, the window over the harbor.

### Scene 5

**Setting:** The same kitchen at dawn on Day 1, about 6:10 a.m. Gray light. The sleet has stopped, but the wind has backed northeast and is working at the north gable. The NOAA weather radio sits on the shelf over the sink.

**Characters present:** Beth, Trooper Dana Ames (phone), Ned (phone)

**Surface action:**
- The phone rings. She has it before the second ring.
- Trooper Ames, plain and procedural: the truck was stopped on Route 1 south of Pruett Harbor a little before three, and the driver is in custody. A federal agent will call her (no name needed yet). "Ma'am, don't go to work." The Marshals are sending someone on the first boat. Beth asks one question, the practical one: "Which boat?" Ames tells her.
- She turns up the weather radio. The forecast has been upgraded: a nor'easter warning from this afternoon, east backing northeast, gale to storm force, coastal flooding at the evening high tide. She taps the barometer. It is down again.
- She has to phone Ned and tell him she won't be on the boat, and she won't tell him why. Ned: "I'll work it short." It is the same line as last night. He doesn't ask why. She notices that he doesn't ask. Then he tells her he's getting Kenny out of bed. This sets up the deckhand Dane sees in Ch 02.
- At the window: the *Margaret Ann* on the guest mooring. Her deck lights are still burning in daylight and a radio is playing faintly. A figure stands on deck, too far off for her to see a face or a hood, turned toward shore. The guest mooring lies square below her kitchen window, so her lit window sits square above it. Everyone on Cutter knows which house is the Calloway place.

**Subtext:** Help is coming, but the person she would normally tell everything to (Ned) is someone she has just chosen not to tell. The arrest closes one threat and turns her into the witness against the rest.

**Tension source:** Situational (the man on the dragger, the storm coming in) and interpersonal (holding back from Ned).

**Ends on:** She reaches over and switches off the kitchen light. End on the action itself, with no question in the narration (the bible bans ending a chapter on one).

**Word count target:** 450

**Advances:** Plot (arrest, protector on the way, storm). This scene sets up the chapter promise: the hooded man walked back onto her island, the dragger lies below her window, and she has to assume he knows which house is hers.

## Notes for prose-writer

- Do not name Doucette, Arsenault or Dane. Beth doesn't know Doucette or Dane, and Arsenault appears only as the *Margaret Ann*. Pelletier can stay unnamed too ("the driver"). Beth wouldn't know his name, and the trooper doesn't need to say it.
- Call the cargo "pills" and "blue." Do not use the word "fentanyl" in this chapter.
- Language budget: Ned may say one "Jesus." Beth may have at most one short swear in her head, at the bricks, and it is optional. There is no profanity on the VHF.
- No sexual or romantic content in this chapter. Dane appears only as "someone" on the first boat.
- Tess is warm and funny in her memory beat (the braid tug, the joke about the loan). She is never sinister.
- Day header: **Day 0 to Day 1**. Do not print the year.
