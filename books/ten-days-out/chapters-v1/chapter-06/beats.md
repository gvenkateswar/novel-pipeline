# Chapter 06 — The Window: Beat Sheet

**Heading:** Day 5 to Day 7
**POV:** Beth, close third, throughout. No Dane POV.
**Target:** about 3,400 words. Sex scene 2: 800–1,000 of them.
**Chapter promise (outline):** How did they know the time?

---

## Flags before drafting (continuity and outline checks)

1. **Russ's family link is stated three ways.** Outline Supporting Cast says Russ is "cousin to the antagonist skipper's ex-wife". The Ch 06 outline entry says "Arsenault is his ex-wife's cousin". The bible says Jolene, Russ's ex-wife, is Lyle Arsenault's cousin. **The bible wins:** Arsenault is Jolene's cousin, so he is Russ's cousin by marriage. The orchestrator brief used the outline's other wording. Nothing in the plot changes, but the continuity-checker should hold the bible version.
2. **Day 5 has a 600-word cap (bible calendar), and the Russ beat doesn't fit inside it.** Ch 05 is finished, so the outline's fallback (moving Russ into Ch 05) is gone. The bible's "what she knows" line puts the Russ link on "Day 5–6", so Russ moves to **Day 6, at the town landing**. That scene also plants the landing's layout before the ambush.
3. **When the *Margaret Ann* moves.** The bible has her at the store dock Days 2–5 and on a mooring "from Day 6". To keep the dawn tell working, she is still at the store dock on Day 5 (Scene 1) and is not looked at on Day 6. She moves on the night of Day 6, after Tess's 7 pm call (off-page). At dawn on Day 7 Beth sees her on a mooring she has never used, with a straight sightline to the landing. Her deck lights, on every night for a week, are **off**. That is a second tell, and it fits the bible's "deck lights on all night" as a change from the usual.
4. **Where the shot comes from.** The outline puts a strange skiff on the float. The bible (World Rule 11) has Doucette firing "from the skiff at about 60 yards". Combined: a man is lying in the bottom of the strange skiff, which Beth doesn't see. When the abort is called, the outboard starts, the skiff runs off the float, and he fires from the water at about sixty yards as they go through the bait shed's back door. Beth must clock the skiff as **not** the *Margaret Ann*'s aluminum 16-footer, which she knows from Ch 04. This one is from away.
5. **The fire company EMT standby (World Rule 9)** is at the firehouse on the radio, not at the landing, because the EMD was asked to keep the landing clear. Walt Grindle is heard on the VHF after the shot. That is his Ch 06 VHF appearance from the bible.
6. **Who told Russ to stay off the boat.** Say it was a call from a woman at Homeland Security in Portland on Friday (Day 1), before the landlines died. That fits World Rule 5. Beth registers it as Quist without the name being needed.
7. **Dane's extraction call** is on the afternoon of Day 6 (bible: "Day 6: arranges the extraction"). That has to come before Tess's 7 pm pass-on. The call is outdoors, because the Iridium needs open sky.
8. **Ch 07 opens with Beth closing the graze with butterfly strips.** So Ch 06 must end before any wound care beyond pressure.

None of these conflict with the approved plot. The sheet below follows them.

## How the sheet fits three days into one chapter (pacing flag)

The three days are covered by choosing six scenes with time gaps between them, not by summarizing. Day 5 is one store scene, hard-capped at 600 words. Day 6 gets three scenes (midday, afternoon, night), and each one carries a required plant or beat:
- Russ and the landing layout
- the overheard line and the extraction time
- sex scene 2 with the withholding

Day 7 is the ambush and the retreat. Each scene opens late, on a concrete image and with a clock time, so the reader always knows where they are without recap. Nothing on the morning of Day 6 is shown. Day 5 evening and night are skipped entirely, with no line explaining the gap, because Scene 2's opening image carries it.

## Tic and ending budget (for prose-writer and voice-editor)

- **Counting, two at most.** Count 1 is Scene 2 (Beth paces the bait shed's back door to the shore lane). Count 2 is optional, in Scene 5. Nowhere else.
- **"Not asking", once.** Scene 4 only: she doesn't ask about the notebook.
- **Button endings, two at most.** Only Scene 3 and Scene 6 may end on a one-line button. Scenes 1, 2, 4 and 5 end on an image or an action in motion. No barometer endings.
- **Retired:** "longer than X needed" in any form; "the way she'd [boat task]"; "right side clear"; "put it where she put things". No reused similes from earlier chapters.
- **Dane swears once in the book here**, in Scene 5, under fire. One word.
- **Tess, bible tic 3:** in this chapter she stops touching Beth, and Beth doesn't notice. Her hands stay on the register, the logs and the bag. Don't comment on it in the narration.
- Don't use "Okay, Bethie". It is saved for Ch 09.

### Scene 1
**Setting:** Marr's General Store, Day 5 (Tuesday, November 17), late morning. The generator is roaring out back, the woodstove is held at 58°F, and the floor creaks over the rotten sill by the counter. Through the front window the *Margaret Ann* is still at the store dock with her country radio going.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Tess, Ned.
**Surface action:**
- Open on Beth at the counter reading the green fuel ledger upside down while Tess rings up two five-gallon cans of generator gas for Ned. Ned buys it on credit, for the summer houses he caretakes.
- Beth reads the Thursday, November 12 page. The last entry is just before 4 pm (an island boat, diesel, gallons, initials) and there is nothing after it. She saw the float lit at nine with the red-hulled dragger alongside.
- She excuses it in her head, plainly: cash after hours isn't a crime, and Tess needs the money. Eighteen hundred a month.
- Tess sees her looking and squares the ledger and the check-in log to the edge of the counter (bible tic 1).
- Ned, low, without looking at her: is she somewhere warm. Beth: "Warm enough." He doesn't press.
- Ned then gives the Ashby generator shed fire far more detail than anyone asked for: the transfer switch, three years of telling the Ashbys, those Generacs. That is his lying tell from the bible. Then he turns his hearing aid off with one finger and takes his cans out. The reader should get a reason to look at Ned: he wanted to know where she is, and he is covering something about the fire.
- Dane is across the store reading the propane price card. They walked in by the Harbor Road from the church end, not the cove road. One clause in Beth's head covers it.
- Beth puts her supplies on the counter: kerosene for the lantern, matches, coffee, D-cells, a box of crackers. Then, openly, a box of condoms off the pharmacy shelf. No hesitation; she is not a woman who hides a purchase.
- Tess's reaction is the warmest exchange between them in the book, and it should be funny. "Christ on a bike, Bethie." Tess turns the box over to check the date before Beth can, then hands it to Beth, who checks it anyway because she checks things. Tess: they're good till next August, "which is more than I can say for the chowder base." She tells Beth what the box cost her wholesale, then charges her full price: "I'm happy for you, I'm not a charity." She throws in a Hershey bar for free. Her eyes go once to Dane's back and come back.
**Subtext:**
- Beth is choosing, in public and in daylight, to be a woman who has decided. It is the same nerve as "being seen is normal".
- Tess's delight is real. So is the fact that her hands never leave the register, the bag and the ledger.
- The fuel log is the first clue Beth holds in her hands and then puts down.
**Tension source:** Situational and interpersonal. The ledger gap sits under Beth's eyes while Tess is at her most lovable. Ned's over-detail sits beside Ned's question.
**Ends on:** Outside, from the store dock, Beth looks back through the window. Tess has the ledger squared to the counter edge and is smoothing the cover flat with both palms. Behind Beth the dragger's radio changes songs. Image, not a button. (Optional, to keep the humor: Dane's one dry line at his own expense, "I'd have asked for a receipt", but then the window image has to come last.)
**Word count target:** 600 (hard cap; bible calendar).

### Scene 2
**Setting:** The town landing and the co-op bait shed, Day 6 (Wednesday, November 18), around noon. It is near low water, so the steel ramp pitches steep down to the float and the float boards are slick with weed. The bait shed reeks of herring. The sea is visibly going down outside the harbor mouth: white water on the ledges, but in sets now. The float lights are on their timer pole, dark in daylight, which is normal.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Russ.
**Surface action:**
- Russ is on the float bailing the skiff he rows out to *Second Wind*.
- Beth goes down the ramp to him. She is hunting now, and asking questions in daylight is normal. Dane stays at the head of the ramp where he can see the shore lane and the water.
- Beth asks Russ about the *Margaret Ann* straight out.
- Russ takes his cap off and puts it back on (bible tic 1). Arsenault is Jolene's cousin, which makes him Russ's cousin "the way a nor'easter's your cousin" (or similar; it should be his own joke, not a reused simile).
- Then, plainly: a woman from Homeland Security in Portland called him Friday, before the lines went, and told him to stay clear of that boat. "So I stayed off that boat. I'm not a complicated man."
- Then he is too curious: "When are they taking you off?" Beth: "When the sea lets them." He looks at the harbor mouth the way she does, reading it. He knows the water as well as she does, and that is a reason to watch him.
- On the way back up, Beth walks the route without explaining why: the front roll-up door of the bait shed at the head of the ramp, through the shed, out the back door to the shore lane. **Count 1:** she paces the back door to the lane, a number she will need. Dane watches her do it and doesn't ask why. He leans in the shed doorway and notes the corrugated wall: it stops weather, not bullets. In his clipped way he says so, and she files it.
- Romance beat: in the dim shed his hand at the small of her back as she ducks the low door frame, and it stays a breath. Keep it to one or two lines.
**Subtext:**
- Beth is testing Russ against thirty years of knowing him and can't make him come out clean or dirty.
- She is also, without saying so, preparing a way out of a place she expects to leave from tomorrow. Some part of her is already planning for the landing to go wrong.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Russ's family tie and his curiosity against his openness. Underneath it, the landing as a place.
**Ends on:** Beth at the back door of the bait shed, her palm flat on the cold corrugated wall, looking down the shore lane toward the cove road. Dane is behind her in the shed's dark. Image, in motion: she pulls the door to and the latch drops.
**Word count target:** 450.

### Scene 3
**Setting:** The ledge above the fish house on Calloway Cove, Day 6, about 3:30 pm. The light is going; sunset is a little past four. The wind has come round and is dropping. Spruce stands at their backs and the cove slaps the ledge. Dane is in the open because the Iridium needs open sky. Beth is at the fish house door, about twenty feet below, splitting kindling.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane (Pike on the sat phone; Quist joins; only Dane's side is heard).
**Surface action:**
- Beth hears Dane's half of the call through the wind and the kindling strokes.
  - "Six feet by midnight. ... Motor lifeboat. ... Zero six hundred, town landing."
  - Then he repeats back what Pike tells him (his bible tic): "Your office coordinated with local emergency management for the landing. Understood." This line must be on the page close to verbatim: "coordinated with local emergency management for the landing."
  - "Conf. Quist", or Quist's name, should be audible.
- Beth reads "local emergency management" as some county office on the mainland with a desk and a radio. That is her excuse, given in one clause in her head, not argued. She does **not** think of Tess's joke title. The narration must not point at it.
- Dane comes down and tells her the plan plainly: 0600 tomorrow, the Coast Guard at the town landing, the sea under six feet offshore, and the second system in by tomorrow evening. If they miss this window there isn't another before the ferry. A deputy meets them at the mainland station, then a safe site until Monday.
- Beth takes it as a task. She tells him how they will come in: the shore lane, the back of the bait shed, a look from the front door before anyone sets foot on the ramp. She states it as a conclusion and gives the reasons only when he asks for them (bible voice). He repeats it back in fewer words. He agrees without argument: "You were right about the water. I'm not arguing about the landing."
- He stands and writes in the notebook (bible tic 2), facing the cove path. She sees the pen move and goes inside with the kindling.
**Subtext:**
- For Dane, tomorrow means handing her to someone else, and possibly never seeing her again on an ordinary day.
- For Beth, tomorrow means getting into someone else's boat, under someone else's plan, leaving the island she has not left since this began. That is her core fear (bible).
- The time is set out loud, by him, in front of her. This is the outline's red herring at Dane.
**Tension source:** Internal. Relief at a way out set against the dread of being carried, and the first shape of a goodbye.
**Ends on:** A one-line button (allowed, 1 of 2). Something exact and practical from Beth that is really about leaving, for example her saying she'll set the alarm on his watch for four-thirty because the fish house has no clock. Or the cleaner choice: the stove door shutting on new kindling.
**Word count target:** 450.

### Scene 4 — Sex scene 2 (heat 4)
**Setting:** The fish house, Day 6 night, after his 11:20 call (keep the habit; he goes out under open sky and comes back in). Sensory anchors: the oil-drum stove ticking hot; wind in the shingles, falling; the kerosene lantern turned low; her father's square capitals on the paint cans; the cot against the bench; the paint-can lid list from Ch 05 still leaning on the bench. Cold air off the water-side window, heat on the stove side.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:**
1. **Before.** Dane comes in from the call with the notebook in his hand and puts it in the inside pocket of his jacket on the nail. Beth sees it, and this is the chapter's single "not asking". She decides she doesn't need to, and the narration should show it as trust, not avoidance: she takes the jacket off the nail and hangs it nearer the stove to dry.
2. **She takes the box out of the store bag** and puts it on the bench between them. One line of dry humor, maybe a callback to Ch 05's "very disappointing kit". It shouldn't be a joke-fest.
3. **Slower than scene 1.** He asks (consent check, in their voices). Say "Tell me to stop" or a fresh equivalent, not a copy of the bible's sample. Her verbal yes is clear and plain.
4. **Two plot refs inside the scene (Heat Rule 8).**
   - (a) The morning boat: one of them says, or she thinks, that this is the last night before the mainland. "A goodbye that doesn't want to be one" has to come through action, not be stated.
   - (b) The breach: his "I report this" from Ch 05 comes back in a line. He hasn't changed his mind and she doesn't want him to.
5. **Protection in one line** (Heat Rule 10), handled by him or by her, without fuss.
6. **The act.** Full intercourse. Close POV, sensory: his callused hands and hers, stubble, the stove's heat on one side of her and the window's cold on the other, the cot's canvas, breath. Frank nouns, used sparingly (Heat Rule 4). Never more than two sentences in a row of who is touching what. No banned words; check the list.
7. **The exit beat (bible tic 3 payoff).** In the middle of it Beth notices she doesn't know where the door is. She has stopped keeping an exit in her head, for the first time since Day 0, and she lets that be true. Shown as a noticed absence, not explained.
8. **Within 150 words of climax, back to the suspense question.** After, she asks who he really is. Not his job. Him.
9. **Marisol, short and in pieces, not a monologue (bible ban).** Marisol Duarte, 24. The sister, the ten minutes, the parking lot. Four or five lines, with silences. Beth puts her palm flat on his chest while he says it, to read it (bible tic 1). His heart is steady. He is telling the truth, and that is the irony: she reads true and stops reading.
10. **The withholding, written exactly as the bible requires.** He opens his mouth as if to say something more, closes it, and kisses her instead. Beth reads it as tenderness and the narration lets her. The jacket with the notebook is in frame on its nail, steaming by the stove. Don't point at it.
**Subtext:**
- Her trust is at its peak. She has given up checking.
- He has a true thing he could tell her tonight, and he weighs her credibility on Monday against her knowing. He chooses the case. The reader who knows what "She doesn't know" means (they don't yet) will see the choice later. The reader now sees only that he chose something.
**Tension source:** Internal for both. Under it, the dramatic irony of the notebook a few feet away.
**Ends on:** Withholding (Heat Rule 7). After the kiss that replaced the sentence, Beth falls asleep with her hand still on his chest and no exit in her head. The final image is outside her awareness: the jacket on the nail. Not afterglow alone; the last line carries the lie. Not a button. Let the paragraph run long and close softly.
**Word count target:** 950 (range 900–1,000 per the bible). It should not run over 1,000.

### Scene 5
**Setting:** The town landing, Day 7 (Thursday, November 19), 5:50 am. Gray before sunrise (about 6:30); the tide is falling, about three and a half hours before low, so the ramp is pitched. Swell is still working into the harbor but the wind is light, the window the Coast Guard promised. They approach the landing by the shore lane and wait inside the bait shed in the herring reek, then look out from the front roll-up door at the head of the ramp.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane. Walt Grindle on the VHF. Doucette is unseen, in the skiff.
**Surface action:**
- At 5:52 the motor lifeboat's lights show off the harbor mouth. Dane has the Icom on 22A after the call on 16.
- Beth's three tells, in the order she reads them:
  1. **The float lights are dark.** It is not their timer hour; Ned set those, and Ned never allows them dark.
  2. **The *Margaret Ann*** is not at the store dock. She is on a mooring Beth has never seen her use, with a straight line of sight to the float, and her deck lights are off for the first time in a week.
  3. **A skiff on the float** that is no island skiff and not the dragger's aluminum sixteen. Its outboard is down in the water, ready, when anyone tying up in a swell at a float would have it tilted up.
- Beth stops at the head of the ramp, before open float. She calls the abort at once, quietly, as a conclusion: "No. Not this. Back." Her reasons come in one breath. **Optional count 2:** the seconds it would take to cross the open float to the boat.
- Dane doesn't argue. He backs her on the radio: abort, on 22A, in short formal radio language with no swearing (FCC). The motor lifeboat acknowledges.
- The retreat follows Beth's route from Scene 2: through the shed, out the back door. Behind them the skiff's outboard starts. The skiff runs off the float. The shot comes from the water at about sixty yards as they reach the back door, and Dane is already between her and the open side.
- Corrugated metal bangs. Dane is grazed across the outside of the **left** upper arm (World Rule 12). His one swear in this chapter comes here: one word, under fire.
- They are out the back door and into the shore lane before a second shot, which hits the shed or doesn't come.
- The Coast Guard boat's lights turn away; it won't come in on a shooter at a damaged landing with no one to land.
- Walt Grindle on the radio from the firehouse, formal and shaken: is that the landing, does anyone need the EMT. Nobody answers him.
**Subtext:**
- Beth's competence saves both of them. Her abort comes from things only she can read: Ned's lights, a boat's mooring habits, how a man leaves an outboard.
- The window that was meant to be the end of this is the trap.
**Tension source:** Situational, at the chapter's peak.
**Ends on:** In motion: the two of them in the shore lane, running, Dane's right hand clamped over his left arm, blood between his fingers. The outboard sound is falling away across the harbor. No button.
**Word count target:** 650. Keep the action in short, physical sentences, per the bible register.

### Scene 6
**Setting:** The cove road path, then the ledge outside the fish house, Day 7, about 6:40 am. Full gray light. The wind is already backing, the second system's front edge, with the first fine rain coming over the spruce. The harbor and the window are behind them.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane (Pike on the sat phone; only Dane's side is heard).
**Surface action:**
- Beth takes them home by the grown-over path from the cove road, not the road. She checks behind them once at the bend where she can see the lane.
- At the fish house she gets him inside and onto the bench, puts his own hand back on the wound with hers on top, and makes him hold it. Just pressure; the strips come in Ch 07.
- Dane says he has to report. He goes back out to the ledge under open sky with the sat phone, bleeding, because it only works outdoors.
- Through the doorway Beth hears his side: "They had the time. ... No. Nobody. ... Only us." His people.
- Now the arithmetic comes, in her head, as fact and not as a question:
  - She heard him set the time.
  - She heard him repeat it back to Portland.
  - The only people she knows of who had 0600 and the town landing are him and the voices he reports to every night.
  - She does not remember "local emergency management". It went by her as a county office and stays gone.
- The count named once, in the bible's phrasing: three days and a storm until the ferry, and no other way off.
**Subtext:**
- Hours after giving up her exits, Beth is putting one back. She feels herself doing it and hates it.
- Dane, wounded and reporting, looks exactly like a man telling someone how it went.
**Tension source:** Internal. Trust at its peak last night against the facts of this morning. Interpersonal, unspoken: she says nothing to him.
**Ends on:** The chapter's second allowed button. Beth inside the fish house watches him through the open door on the ledge, the sat phone to his ear and rain starting on his shoulders. She finds she has put herself on the stove side of the room with the door in view, knowing how many steps it is without counting them. She is keeping an exit again. One plain line, with no question in the narration (bible ban). It sets up the chapter promise, "How did they know the time?", which Ch 07 answers wrongly through the notebook.
**Word count target:** 300.
