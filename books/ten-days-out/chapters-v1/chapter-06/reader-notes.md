# Reader Notes — Chapter 6, "The Window" (with notes on Chapters 1–6)

**Verdict:** The strongest chapter since the first one. The abort and the shooting finally put a body count in play. But I'm a step ahead of Beth on who the leak is, and the last line points her at the man I'm sure is innocent. That cools the cliffhanger.

---

## Chapter 6

### Where I was gripped

- **The abort at the landing.** Dark float lights, the dragger on a new mooring with its lights off, a strange skiff with its leg down. Three clues, each one planted earlier (the lights on their timer at noon, a week of deck lights burning, the aluminum skiff on Sunday). Beth reads them in one breath, and her "No. Not this. Back." is the best line of dialogue she's had. This is the book doing what it promised: a woman who notices things.
- **The shot through the shed.** "Corrugated. Stops weather. Not rounds." pays off one scene later. I liked being told the wall wouldn't save them and then watching it fail to.
- **The condom purchase at Tess's counter.** It's funny, it's warm, and it sneaks in a clue. "I'm happy for you. I'm not a charity." Then the free Hershey bar. Then Tess smoothing the ledger cover flat "with both palms, and then again." I laughed and then went cold, and I think that's what the scene was after.
- **The Thursday Nov 12 ledger page.** There's an entry at 3:52 and then nothing, though the float was lit at nine with the red hull alongside. Quiet and damning.

### Where I got bored or skimmed

- **The noon walk-through of the bait shed on Day 6.** It's clearly setup: fourteen steps, the low frame, the back door. I skimmed the middle of it on first read and only went back after the shooting. "She filed that beside the fourteen" is the third or fourth time in the book she's filed something.
- **The Wednesday dusk call scene.** It's half dialogue taken by the wind and half logistics read out twice: once overheard, then repeated to her in full ("Zero six hundred tomorrow. Coast Guard motor lifeboat at the town landing..."). I got the plan once and had to sit through it again.
- **The "You're still reporting this" exchange in bed.** It's the Chapter 5 conversation again, almost word for word. It added nothing the second time.

### Where I got confused

- **Days.** The headers kept me oriented, so I always knew the day. But "*Day 6 — Wednesday*" appears three times in a row and "*Day 7 — Thursday*" twice. That reads like formatting, not storytelling, and the repetition made me look for a mistake.
- **The palm on the corrugated wall.** At the end of the noon scene she puts her palm flat on the wall, looks down the lane, then keeps "that hand shut in her coat pocket all the way to the cove road." I don't know what that means. Fear? A decision? A premonition? It's played as significant, and I couldn't decode it.
- **Landing geography.** Mostly fine: ramp down to the float, bait shed at the head of the ramp, roll-up door facing the harbor, back door to a shore lane under the seawall. But then there's the town landing, the fish pier, the store dock, and Tess's float, and by now I'm holding four piers in my head. I had to stop and work out that the dragger's new mooring was "off the fish pier" with a line to the *town landing* float, not Tess's.
- **Who shot from where.** I got it: the skiff, sixty yards out, broadside, firing at the front of the shed. But this sentence tripped me: "It went by a foot behind her head, where Dane was, between her and the harbor, and he jerked against her." I read it twice to place Dane. And why does the skiff fire blind into a shed? Did they see her at the ramp head? I assume so, but the book doesn't say.
- **"How long you got him for?"** Russ means Dane, I think. For a second I thought he meant Arsenault.
- **"Your office coordinated with local emergency management."** Beth thinks "Some county office on the mainland." Chapter 2 told me Tess is the island's emergency management director, and Tess told Beth that herself on the phone. So either the author forgot, or Beth did. Beth counts binder links and rungs and remembers every barometer reading, so I don't buy her missing this. It reads like the heroine being kept dumb so the reader can be smart.

### Beth's abort: smart and clearly reasoned?

Yes. It's the best-reasoned decision in the book. She gives three observations, each tied to something she knows (Ned's timer, the dragger's habits, how no islander ties up with the outboard leg down). She says it in three sentences and Dane acts on it without arguing. It earns his earlier line "I'm going to stop arguing with you about water." That said, when he says almost the same thing again here ("You were right about the water. I'm not arguing about the landing"), it felt recycled rather than like a callback.

One credibility wobble: the Coast Guard lifeboat hears gunfire at the landing it was about to use, "showed red only, and went away." Nobody answers the fire chief either. I can believe the lifeboat stands off. I struggled to believe it just leaves.

### The sex scene

- **Did it land?** Mostly. The best part isn't the sex itself. It's the moment she loses the door: "Since the night on the freight deck she had known the door in every room she stood in... She reached for it now and didn't have it, and she let that be true." That's the change, and the last line of the chapter (she knows where the door is again) turns it around hard. That structure is the best thing about the chapter.
- **Heat:** 4 out of 5. It's on the page, with penetration and explicit language, but shorter and softer than Chapter 5's anatomy.
- **Different from the first one?** Yes. Chapter 5 was frantic, cold, half-dressed, hands only, with the trauma kit joke ("It's a very disappointing kit." is still the funniest line in the book). Chapter 6 is slow, planned, with a box bought in public, and it's about trust. The contrast works. I slightly preferred Chapter 5, because it had more character in it and less choreography.
- **Did it change anything?** Yes. She stops keeping an exit, and he gives her Marisol. Both are real moves.
- **Mechanical bits:** "though she was wet and he was hard" is stock romance phrasing and sounds like a checklist. "let go of everything at once" is generic. "a man with all night, which was the one thing they didn't have" is a line I've read in a hundred books. "He saw to the condom" is fine, but it's the moment the scene felt like it was ticking boxes. The pulse-reading after ("It was the truth, all of it") is cute, but it does my thinking for me.

### Dane holding something back

Yes, I noticed it, and the book wants me to. "He opened his mouth as if there was more. Then he closed it." Then Beth decides it's "the rest of Marisol." I don't believe her. Here's what I'm carrying from earlier chapters:

- "No. She doesn't know." (Ch 3)
- The money questions: "Anything come in lately? Insurance. A settlement." (Ch 4). Why would a marshal ask that? I think he knows something about money and Beth, maybe that someone is framing her or that someone paid on her.
- The notebook, and "His pen was still moving."

Does it worry me? About Dane's loyalty, no. This is a romance and he took a round for her, so I'm sure he's not the leak. It worries me as a secret that will hurt her when it comes out. The trouble is that the chapter ends with Beth suspecting him, and I don't share her suspicion, so the cliffhanger lands on a doubt I've already ruled out.

### Is the suspense building or flattening?

Building, with gunfire, blood, and the only exit gone. But the pattern is showing: each chapter has an attempt (latch, fire, landing), a narrow escape, a move, and a night together. By Chapter 6 I could feel the next turn coming. The new element, someone shooting, is what kept it from feeling like a repeat.

### Do the two leads sound different?

Not enough. Both talk in short, clipped, declarative sentences. Dane says "Walk me through it" at least three times across the book, and Beth's lines and his could swap in many exchanges. Dane is better when he gets specific: Lowell, his father's horse, "It's a very disappointing kit." In this chapter he's mostly procedure and "Yes." The side characters are more distinct: Tess ("Christ on a bike"), Russ (dry, the cap), Ned (the hearing aid).

### Where it sounded like AI

- **Repeated tics, now on their fifth or sixth use.** Ned switching off his hearing aid (every scene he's in). Russ taking off his cap and looking into it (three times). Tess squaring things to the counter edge. "Palm flat" on everything. "Right side clear." Beth "filed it" or "put it where she put things." Each one was a nice touch the first time. Now they read like a character sheet being worked through.
- **The last line, "She was keeping an exit again."** The paragraph before it already showed this ("she knew exactly where it was"). The extra line explains the image. Every chapter has ended on a neat button line like this, and this is the most over-explained one.
- **"She took it for the rest of Marisol, the part he couldn't say yet, and let him keep it."** This tells me how to read the moment.
- **The repeated day headers.**

### Who I suspect and whether the clues are fair

**Tess. About 75%.** Here's the evidence so far:

- The lit float after hours, the cash diesel, and the $1,800-a-month tank loan (Ch 1, repeated here).
- "The laugh came late." (Ch 2)
- She rang Arsenault's cigarettes wrong, her fingers slow on the drawer.
- She took a phone call behind the curtain on the island's only signal, which she controls.
- She wrote Beth into a check-in log anyone can read (Beth defends this, and her argument is good, which makes me more suspicious, not less).
- She's the emergency management director, and Dane's office "coordinated with local emergency management" for the landing. That's the clincher for me.
- The ledger page with no entry after 3:52, and the guilt-gift Hershey bar.

The clues are fair, maybe too fair. The emergency management line is so pointed that I think the book has tipped its hand. If Tess is the leak, I'll feel I solved it in Chapter 6, and the reveal will need a twist (coerced? the loan? Arsenault holding something over her?) to stay interesting. If it's not Tess, she's a very loud red herring.

**Ned, about 15%, as an accomplice who looks away.** He lied about the reefer, he controls the float-light timer, he defended the shed when nobody asked, and he "tells Tess everything." He's a man who's been paid to look the other way, not the shooter.

**Russ** I now think is a red herring. He volunteered the Homeland call and told her to stay clear, and the lips moving while he times the gaps is a planted fake-out. **Pike/Portland** is the book's decoy for Beth. I don't buy it.

The heel man (left leg, right heel) is the hands. Arsenault's boat is the transport. The question is who on the island is the ears.

### What I want to know next

- Is it Tess, and why? Is it the loan, or does Arsenault have something on her?
- What does Dane know that "she doesn't know"? What's the money question about?
- What's in the notebook?
- How bad is the arm, and can he still shoot?
- With no boat until Sunday and a storm coming back in, how do they survive three days? Does Beth use her master's license? I'm hoping she ends up at a wheel, maybe the *North Reach*.
- Will Beth confront Dane, or hide her suspicion from him?

### Would I keep reading?

Yes, without hesitation. I want the Tess confrontation and I want Beth driving a boat.

### Best moment

> She turned her head on the blanket to find the door.
>
> She didn't know where it was. Behind her head, or past the stove, or off her feet somewhere. Since the night on the freight deck she had known the door in every room she stood in, and which hand for the latch. She reached for it now and didn't have it, and she let that be true, and turned her face back to his.

The runner-up is the condom purchase, from "Christ on a bike, Bethie" to the Hershey bar.

## Cross-chapter observations (Ch 1–6)

- **Opening:** Chapter 1 is excellent. The binder check, the sprung lid, Beth pressing it down and saying "You'll lose your count." I was hooked by page two.
- **Best set piece so far:** the bar crossing in Chapter 5 ("Not the cold. The gasp.").
- **Romance arc:** well paced for a novella. There's the shed near-miss in Ch 3, the stopped kiss in Ch 4 ("I can't keep you alive with my hands on you"), the first time in Ch 5, and trust in Ch 6. Each step is earned.
- **Heat delivery:** right for the label. A reader who bought open door gets two real scenes by the 60% mark. Neither works as standalone erotica, and both are tied to the plot, which is good.
- **Weakest stretch:** the first half of Chapter 3 and the Russ camp-road scene in Chapter 4 felt like the plot circling.
- **Recurring problem:** tics and button endings. Each chapter closes on a crafted last line, and the characters' signature gestures repeat until they're wallpaper.
- **Voice:** the prose is confident and specific (XtraTufs, Thermo King, bleed screws, millibars). It's what keeps this from feeling generic, even when the rhythms repeat.
- **Where I'd most likely stop:** nowhere yet. If I stalled anywhere it would be mid-Chapter 3.

## Verdict at six chapters

A well-made, atmospheric romantic suspense with a heroine I believe and a setting I can smell. The romance and heat are delivering. The mystery risks being solved early by the reader (Tess), and the Chapter 6 cliffhanger relies on Beth suspecting Dane, which no romance reader will share. I'm keeping going. I'd give it four stars so far.
