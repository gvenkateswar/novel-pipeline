## The book in one line

A ferry deckhand sees fentanyl spill out of a lobster crate, and the U.S. Marshal who falls for her on the first night has ten days to keep her alive on a storm-locked island where someone she loves keeps telling the killer where she is.

## The case and the clock (said plainly in Ch 02, again in Ch 03, and once a chapter after that)

- **What the police found.** Beth called at midnight. Troopers stopped Cole's truck on Route 1 at about one, and it was clean. At dawn they searched his cold-storage building on the Pruett wharf and found lobsters and a freshly hosed floor: the load had gone straight from the truck into another vehicle on the wharf and away. All that was left was white powder from the torn brick, ground into the seams of one crate. That's enough to hold him a few days and not enough to convict him. **Beth saw the bricks, saw Cole in the cab, and photographed the plate. Her eyes are the case.** Said in Ch 02 in one line from Dane's boss, and again in Ch 03.
- **The deadline.** On Monday the 23rd a judge decides whether to keep Cole locked up. Before that, the grand jury (a room of ordinary people who decide whether there's enough for a trial) has to hear Beth in person. If they haven't heard her by Monday morning, Cole walks out.
- **After.** Until she testifies, killing her saves Cole. Once she has, killing her only hurts him. Cole's people have done it anyway: two years ago in New Bedford they burned down the house of a woman who'd testified. That's the revenge risk behind the rupture. It's a different case from Marisol's, in a different city.
- **Who warned Cole** is the book's first secret. Answered in Ch 12: Ned.
- *Domain checker, before the bible:* the deadline for a detained federal defendant (preliminary hearing within 14 days unless indicted) and "in person or he walks Monday"; residue plus an eyewitness holding a man a few days; grand jury testimony in person; Marshals protecting a pre-trial witness, and that protection is voluntary; WITSEC's no-contact rule; a Marine Patrol boat bringing a deputy out at night; an island emergency director clearing an emergency landing once the island is declared closed (NB8); a single cell tower giving only "the island" as a location; whether "taken for questioning" is the honest phrase for the lawyer in Ch 17 (NB11). **Fallback clock if "in person or he walks" fails:** Cole's detention hearing is Monday, and without her sworn testimony the prosecutor has no grounds to keep him in. Same date, same stakes.

### 01 · The Last Truck · Beth, then Dane · 2,100 · *(written: `chapters/chapter-01/test-v4.md`, edits 1–3 applied)*
**Purpose:** Hook, inciting incident, the first attack, the leads meet.
**Opens with:** "The last truck onto the ferry that night was the seafood truck, and Beth knew something was wrong with it before it reached the top of the ramp."

- **s1, Beth, the deck.** **Event:** a crate splits open and shows fentanyl, and a scared young man tells her she didn't see it. Cole Varney is at the wheel. Ned: "Then you didn't see it. Not tonight." She has never seen him afraid. At Pruett the kid walks off beside the truck, and she photographs the plate.
- **s2, Beth, her kitchen and the snow.** **Event:** she calls the state police at midnight, and at 2 a.m. a man breaks in, says her name, and chases her to her father's fish shack.
- **s3, Dane, the shack.** **Event:** Dane drives the man off, Beth swings an oar at him, and they spark. "Those boots are for a different ocean." He wants her off the island for the ten days. "No."

**Romance beat (obvious):** Hero falls first: "He was in trouble." Her want shows in the test chapter's own line, where her eyes go to his mouth and stay there, and she isn't sorry (NB6: no second line added).
**Key event:** She has seen the drugs and the man who drives them, and someone came for her.
**End question:** Who told him, and will she leave?
**Say it twice:** fentanyl; Cole driving; the plate; the dragging heel; the boat with no lights, so he lives here; ten days. (Restated in Ch 02 s1.)
**Laugh:** "The government."

**The first night's channel, in order:** about 10:00 p.m. the ferry docks at Pruett. As the truck rolls off, Ned calls Cole from the prepaid phone Cole gave him two years ago for the night runs, kept in the captain's cabin: "She saw it. I know her. She'll call the police tonight." The kid already knew she saw; what he didn't know, and what Ned did, was that she'd call (N8). Cole drives onto the Pruett wharf and moves the load into another vehicle, then sends the kid home in his skiff from the Pruett float, where he always keeps it for the run back, to "make sure she keeps her mouth shut." The ferry carries Beth home at about 11:00. The kid's skiff lands below her house a little after midnight. She calls the police at midnight. Cole is stopped on Route 1 at about 1:00 with a clean truck. At 2:00 the kid breaks in. **He was sent before Beth called,** so the timing Dane notices is a coincidence. On the page: Russ in Ch 02 (the Pruett harbormaster told him on the phone that the kid's skiff left the float at half past ten), and Ned in Ch 12.

### 02 · First Light · Beth · 1,700
**Purpose:** The choice that ends Act 1; Ned saves her; the ferry, the landing and the island's own boats are crippled, which plants the climax and closes the safer boats.
**Opens with:** "Beth Calloway woke wanting the marshal, which was the most inconvenient thing to happen to her in a night that had included a man with a gun."

- **s1 (900), the landing, first light, the police boat's engine running.** **Event:** Beth refuses to board, and Dane chooses to stay. The Marine Patrol officer has ten minutes before the sea closes. The boss on speaker, one line: the truck was clean, the cold storage was hosed down, and her eyes are the case. No second deputy can get out before the storm. Russ, arriving, one line: the Pruett harbormaster says the kid's skiff left the float at half past ten last night. Dane says it plainly, to her face: then the kid was on his way before she ever called, and nobody had to tell anybody anything (N6). Beth's reasons, to his face (N7): she's the only one who saw the kid with the drugs; if she runs, Cole's people learn that shooting at a witness works; and Ned can't get the ferry onto its storm moorings alone. Dane's: protection is voluntary and he can't make her go, and he won't leave her alone on an island with an armed man. He tells his boss he's staying and that he'll report to her alone, because she's the one person in that office he'd trust with his life. The police boat leaves without them. Flirting with an edge: "You're the most stubborn witness I've ever had." / "How many have you had?" / "Forty-one, as of this morning."
- **s2 (800), the pier, the storm's first hour.** **Event:** the ferry breaks loose, smashes the landing float, and Ned pulls Beth out from between the boat and the pier. Ropes parting, the hull swinging in. Ned, slow on land, gets her by the coat collar and hauls her back at the cost of his bad hip. Dane jumps aboard to help with the lines, badly. "I'd like it noted that I jumped." They save the boat, but the steering is broken, and the floating dock where every other boat ties up is splintered (plant, N4): from now on only a boat as big as the ferry can lie against the bare pier in a sea. Across the harbor the lobster boats that weren't hauled out for the winter drag their moorings and go up on the rocks one after another, and Beth watches Russ's go with them (B4). Two trucks are stuck on the car deck, left from the afternoon run when the storm warning came in. Beth teases Ned that he can't swim and still won't wear a life jacket. Dane holds onto her a beat too long. "You can let go." / "I know."

**Romance beat:** Her want, plainly, in her own head from the first line, and the hold on the pier.
**Danger beat:** The ferry breaking loose; Beth nearly crushed.
**Key event:** She stays, he stays, and the island shuts.
**What changes:** No way off. The man is on the island with them. The ferry can't run, the landing float is gone, and the island's lobster boats are on the rocks.
**Emotional beat:** Want and defiance, then muscle and fear.
**End question:** Where does she sleep tonight, with a man on the island who knows her house?
**Say it twice:** the truck was clean, so her eyes are the case; the kid left Pruett before she called; the ferry can't run; the float is gone; the lobster boats are wrecked. (Restated in Ch 03 s1.)

### 03 · The Constable's Kitchen · Dane · 1,700
**Purpose:** The first kiss on the page; his reason, specific to this case; her answer, which wins; the second attack.
**Opens with:** "Dane Reyes lost the argument about Beth's house in under a minute and won the one about Russ Whitcomb's."

- **s1 (900), Russ's kitchen, the power out.** **Event:** the first kiss, his reason, her answer, and both of them knowing it will happen soon. Where to sleep, in under 100 words: her house is out because the kid knows it in the dark. Russ's house is the choice: the constable with a shotgun on the porch, a woodstove, the only working generator on the harbor road. Every house on that road can see the lights and Russ on the porch (N2). Dane reports it to his boss. In the dark kitchen she kisses him, and he kisses her back, hard. He stops and says why: the truck was clean, so she's the whole case, and if Cole's lawyer can tell a jury the marshal was sleeping with the only witness, twelve strangers start wondering what else he told her to say. Beth argues back, and it's flirting: "Then don't lie about it. If they ask, I say yes, and then I tell them what I saw. You think twelve people from Maine never heard of two grown adults in a snowstorm?" And: "You don't stop kissing a woman because of a jury. You stop because she says so. I didn't." He has no answer to that and tells her so. In his head, plainly: she's right about most of it, and he wants her more than he has wanted anything in his life. "Tonight's a bad night." / "Then tomorrow." / "Probably sooner." Both of them know.
- **s2 (800), 1 a.m.** **Event:** Russ is called away, and shots come through the window. Tess on the island's emergency radio: the Dyer house just phoned the store, a tree is through the roof, and old Mrs. Dyer may be trapped. Russ goes. Twenty minutes later the kitchen window blows in. Dane gets Beth to the floor and talks the whole time, low and steady, while she goes silent and listens. He's cut by glass. They go out the back into the storm while the shooter fires at the sound.

**Romance beat:** The kiss, his reason, and her winning answer. No "we can't."
**Danger beat:** Shots through the window.
**Key event:** They kissed, both know it's coming, and the man found her again.
**What changes:** The second place she slept has been hit. Nobody yet knows whether that took a leak: the whole road could see where she was.
**Emotional beat:** Heat, then violence.
**End question:** Beth, in the dark behind the woodpile: "I know a place." *Where, and who called from the Dyer house?*
**Say it twice:** the whole harbor road could see her at Russ's; the call came from the Dyer house. (Restated in Ch 04.)
**Laugh:** "How many witnesses have kissed you?" / "As of tonight? One. I'm keeping count."

## The one-talk sentences (brief item 8)

**The rupture (Ch 13):** If Beth and Dane sat down and told each other the whole truth, this would still be a problem, because Cole's people may want her dead after she testifies, and the only protection Dane's job can give her is a new name in a new state with no way back to the island or to him, which is the one life Beth won't live.

**The kiss and its cost (Ch 03):** If they told each other the whole truth (and they do, out loud), it would still be a cost, because the truck was clean, so she's the whole case, and Cole's lawyer will use the marshal. Beth's answer wins most of the argument: they won't lie about it, and a jury can hear two adults told the truth. They choose each other in Ch 04 with the cost said first, and it comes due on the page in Ch 13 and Ch 17.

**Leaving the island (Ch 02):** If they told each other the whole truth (and they do), it would still be a problem, because protection is voluntary, she's the only one who saw the kid with the drugs, running would teach Cole's people that shooting works, and he won't leave her alone with an armed man.

## The traitor's path (Ned Haskell)

**Motive (Ch 09 and Ch 12):** for two years he took Cole's cash to run his trucks across at night and asked no questions. On the first night he warned Cole that Beth would call the police, which is why the truck was clean and why her eyes became the whole case. The next morning Cole's lawyer called him on the same phone with the warning call and his son. He chose his son.

**Means:** the prepaid phone Cole gave him for the night runs, kept in the captain's cabin. He never used his own, so the records come back clean (Ch 10). The Dyer call was made from the Dyer house's own phone (N1). The kid has a phone on the island. There's one tower.

**Why the reader isn't ahead of Beth (F11):**
- **Ch 01–08:** Ned is a frightened old man. "Never once seen Ned Haskell afraid" reads as fear of the men twenty feet under them. He saves her life on the pier (Ch 02). He makes no statement and no confession, and nobody points him at anyone.
- **The visible lies all belong to others:** Tess relayed the Dyer call, answers about it a little too fast, and hangs up a phone (Ch 03, Ch 05). Russ says "nobody" to Dane's face (Ch 05) and can't account for his nights. The Book 2 spark between them stays hidden inside that lie until Ch 11.
- **His son** is first mentioned by Ned, on the beach (Ch 09).
- **Ch 09 s1 (~47%):** Beth and the reader learn in the same breath that he took Cole's money, and Dane names the risk out loud and acts on it in the same scene. The ambush follows within the chapter. Beth names him out loud in Ch 10 s1 (~52%), and the answer comes in Ch 11 s3 (~62%). The longest the reader can be ahead of Beth is the gap from Ch 09 s1 to Ch 10 s1, about five points, and Beth is thinking it too behind the last rock.
