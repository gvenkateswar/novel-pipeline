## What changed in revision 1 (short)

- **The case:** the truck was clean when the police stopped it. Cole had dumped the load in Pruett because someone warned him. Beth's eyes, her photo of the plate, and the powder in the crate seams are the whole case.
- **The first night's channel:** Ned phoned Cole from the ferry at Pruett. Cole dumped the load and sent the kid back across in his own skiff. The kid was sent before Beth ever called the police, so Dane's guess about his office is wrong for a reason the reader can check.
- **Ned is harder to guess:** he confesses his smaller crime (cash for night runs) on Day 1, saves Beth's life on the pier, and stands in front of her under fire on the beach. Tess and Russ each get a visible lie: they're secretly together, and they cover for each other. The Book 2 spark stays hidden until the trap clears them. Beth tests all three people, Ned included. The reader and Beth first suspect Ned in the same scene, out loud (Ch 10).
- **A real first kiss in Ch 03.** Dane's reason for stopping there is specific to this case and argued out loud: she is the whole case, and a marshal sleeping with the only witness hands Cole's lawyer a way to break her testimony. Marisol is backstory only and never the reason for any refusal.
- **The phone that can't be traced:** Ned uses a prepaid phone Cole gave him for the night runs. Dane pulls all three islanders' phone records over Beth's objection, and they come back clean.
- **Every safer option is closed on the page:** the house on night 1, the hideout search, the rescue's hour, the trap, the Day 8 crossing, the boat search.
- **Fewer repeats:** two fires (Ch 06, Ch 11). The man gets past an armed man three times (Ch 01, Ch 09, Ch 14) and is wounded twice before he's caught.
- **The danger behind the rupture is settled on the page:** the kid gives up names on the wheelhouse floor, and Cole's lawyer is arrested at the courthouse door in front of Beth. Her testimony is on the page.
- **Titles no longer give answers away.**

## The book in one line

A ferry deckhand sees fentanyl spill out of a lobster crate, and the U.S. Marshal who falls for her on the first night has ten days to keep her alive on a storm-locked island where someone she loves keeps telling the killer where she is.

## The case and the clock (said plainly in Ch 02, again in Ch 03, and once a chapter after that)

- **What the police found.** Beth called at midnight. Troopers stopped Cole's truck on Route 1 at about one. It was empty of drugs: Cole had unloaded at his cold-storage building in Pruett. All that was left was white powder from the torn brick, ground into the seams of one crate. That's enough to hold him a few days and not enough to convict him of anything. **Beth saw the bricks, saw Cole in the cab, and photographed the plate. Her eyes are the case.** Said in Ch 02 in one plain line from Dane's boss, and again in Ch 03.
- **The deadline.** On Monday the 23rd a judge decides whether to keep Cole locked up. Before that, the grand jury (a room of ordinary people who decide whether there's enough for a trial) has to hear Beth in person. If they haven't heard her by Monday morning, Cole walks out.
- **After.** Until she testifies, killing her saves Cole. Once she has, killing her only hurts him. Cole's people have done it anyway: two years ago in New Bedford they burned down the house of a woman who'd testified. That revenge risk is behind the rupture. (It's a different case from Marisol's, in a different city, so a listener won't merge them.)
- **Who warned Cole** is the book's first secret. It's answered in Ch 12: Ned.
- *Domain checker, before the bible:* the deadline for a detained federal defendant (preliminary hearing within 14 days unless indicted) and whether "in person or he walks Monday" holds; whether residue in crate seams plus an eyewitness can hold a man a few days; grand jury testimony in person; Marshals protecting a pre-trial witness, and that protection is voluntary; WITSEC's no-contact rule; a Marine Patrol boat bringing a deputy out at night; an island emergency director clearing a landing. **Fallback clock if "in person or he walks" fails:** Cole's detention hearing is Monday, and without her sworn testimony the prosecutor has no grounds to keep him in. Same date, same stakes.

### 01 · The Last Truck · Beth, then Dane · 2,100 · *(written: `chapters/chapter-01/test-v4.md`)*
**Purpose:** Hook, inciting incident, the first attack, the leads meet.
**Opens with:** "The last truck onto the ferry that night was the seafood truck, and Beth knew something was wrong with it before it reached the top of the ramp."

- **s1, Beth, the deck.** **Event:** a crate splits open and shows fentanyl, and a scared young man tells her she didn't see it. Cole Varney is at the wheel of the silent truck (edit 1 below). The bricks, the torn one, the boy she taught to row. "You didn't see this." Ned: "Then you didn't see it. Not tonight." She has never seen Ned afraid (edit 2). At Pruett the kid walks off beside the truck (edit 3), and she photographs the plate in three shaking tries.
- **s2, Beth, her kitchen and the snow.** **Event:** she calls the state police at midnight, and at 2 a.m. a man breaks in, says her name, and chases her to her father's fish shack.
- **s3, Dane, the shack.** **Event:** Dane drives the man off, Beth swings an oar at him, and they spark. "Those boots are for a different ocean." He knows he's in trouble. "Then it was him. The man from the truck." "Then he's from here." He wants her off the island for the ten days to the grand jury. "No." He believes someone in his office talked.

**Romance beat (obvious):** Hero falls first, in his head: "He was in trouble." She leans on him and doesn't notice. "Ask me again in ten days."
**Danger beat:** The break-in and the chase.
**Key event:** She has seen the drugs and the man who drives them, and someone came for her.
**What changes:** A deckhand becomes the witness, with a marshal who wants to take her off her island.
**End question:** Who told him, and will she leave?
**Say it twice:** fentanyl; Cole driving; the plate; the kid drags his left heel and fled by boat, so he lives here; ten days. (Restated in Ch 02 s1.)
**Laugh:** "The government."

**Minimal Ch 01 edits this outline needs** (everything else in the test chapter stands):
1. **Cole in the cab (Finding 1).** After "The driver set his brake and stayed in his cab," add: "It was Cole Varney, who had driven that truck onto her deck every Thursday for six years and tipped her twenty dollars at Christmas."
2. **Soften Ned (Finding 11).** Replace "She looked at him. He looked away first." with "She looked at him. In eleven years she had never once seen Ned Haskell afraid."
3. **Put the kid on the mainland (Finding 2).** After the plate photo, add: "The man in the hoodie walked off the ramp beside the truck and didn't look back." His being back on the island at 2 a.m. becomes a question that Ch 02 answers on the page.
4. No edit to Dane's "Only the state police and his own office in Portland had known she'd called." It stands as his belief, and it's wrong for a reason the reader learns in Ch 02 and Ch 12.

**The first night's channel (Finding 2), in order:** about 10:00 p.m. the ferry docks at Pruett. As the truck rolls off, Ned calls Cole from the prepaid phone Cole gave him two years ago for the night runs, which he keeps in the captain's cabin: "She saw. She'll call it in." Cole drives to his cold-storage building on the Pruett wharf and unloads, and he sends the kid back to Cutter in the kid's own skiff, which is tied at the Pruett float, to "make sure she keeps her mouth shut." The ferry carries Beth home at about 11:00. The kid's skiff crosses in the sleet and lands below her house a little after midnight. She calls the police at midnight. Cole is stopped on Route 1 at about 1:00 with a clean truck. At 2:00 the kid breaks in, and when Dane comes, he leaves the way he came. **The kid was sent before Beth called anyone,** so the timing that makes Dane suspect his office is a coincidence the reader can check. On the page: Russ in Ch 02 (the kid's skiff was tied at the Pruett float at ten and gone by half past), and Ned in Ch 12 (the call).

### 02 · First Light · Beth · 1,700
**Purpose:** The choice that ends Act 1; Ned clears himself on the page; the ferry is crippled, which plants the climax.
**Opens with:** "Beth Calloway stood on the landing with the police boat's engine running and told the marshal, for the third time since dawn, that she wasn't getting in it."

- **s1 (900), the landing, first light, the wind rising.** **Event:** Beth refuses to board, Ned confesses his part in front of Dane, and Dane chooses to stay. The Marine Patrol officer has ten minutes before the sea closes. Dane's boss is on speaker: the truck was clean, Cole dumped the load somewhere in Pruett, and her eyes are the case. No second deputy can get out before the storm. Beth's concrete reasons, to Dane's face: the kid lives here, and she's the only person who can put a face to him. If she leaves, he walks onto the first boat when the storm clears and is gone. And Ned can't get the ferry onto its storm moorings alone. Dane's, to hers: protection is voluntary and he can't make her go, and if someone in his office talked, a hotel they book is the worst place she could be. He tells her, with the boss listening, that he'll report to his boss alone, by phone, because she's the one person in that office he'd trust with his life. Then Beth asks Ned straight out why he told her she didn't see it. Ned, gray: for two years he's taken Cole's cash to run his trucks over at night with no questions asked. He guessed what was in them. He was afraid. He'll tell the marshal all of it, and he does, there on the landing, while Dane writes it down. Russ, arriving: the kid's skiff was tied at the Pruett float at ten last night and gone by half past. The police boat leaves without her.
- **s2 (800), the pier, the storm's first hour.** **Event:** the ferry breaks loose, and Ned pulls Beth out from between the boat and the pier. Ropes parting, the hull swinging in. Ned, slow on land, gets her by the coat collar and hauls her back, and it costs his bad hip. Dane jumps aboard to help with the lines and jumps badly. "I'd like it noted that I jumped." They save the boat, but the steering is broken. Two trucks are stuck on the car deck, left over from the afternoon run when the storm warning came in (plant). Ned: days to fix. Beth teases Ned that he can't swim and still won't wear a life jacket (plant). Dane holds onto her afterward a beat too long. "You can let go." / "I know."

**Romance beat:** The landing fight is flirting with an edge ("You're the most stubborn witness I've ever had." / "How many have you had?" / "Forty-one, as of this morning."). In her head, plainly: she wants him and is furious about it.
**Danger beat:** The ferry breaking loose; Beth nearly crushed.
**Key event:** She stays, he stays, the island shuts, and Ned has told the worst thing a reader suspected about him.
**What changes:** No way off. The man is on the island with them. The ferry can't run. Ned looks clear.
**Emotional beat:** Defiance, then muscle and fear.
**End question:** Where does she sleep tonight, with a man on the island who knows her house?
**Say it twice:** the truck was clean, so her eyes are the case; the kid came back in his own skiff; Ned took Cole's cash for night runs and has told Dane; the ferry can't run. (Restated in Ch 03 s1.)

### 03 · The Constable's Kitchen · Dane · 1,700
**Purpose:** The first kiss on the page; his reason for stopping, specific to this case and argued; the second attack.
**Opens with:** "Dane Reyes lost the argument about Beth's house in under a minute and won the one about Russ Whitcomb's."

- **s1 (900), Russ's kitchen, the power out.** **Event:** the first kiss, and he stops it with a reason they argue out loud. Where to sleep, decided to their faces (Finding 3): her house is out, because the kid has already been in it and knows it in the dark. Russ's house is the choice: the constable with a shotgun on the porch, a woodstove, and the only working generator on the harbor road. Russ asked to watch, so he's on the porch. In front of Dane, Beth calls Ned so the ferry crew knows where she is. Dane reports the house to his boss. In the dark kitchen she kisses him, and he kisses her back, hard. Then he stops, and says why, plainly: the truck was clean, so she's the whole case. If Cole's lawyer can stand up and say the marshal was sleeping with the only witness, twelve strangers start wondering what else the marshal told her to say. Beth: "You think I'd lie in there because you kissed me?" Dane: "No. I think a jury of strangers might." Beth: if anyone asks her under oath, she'll tell the truth, because she's a bad liar. Both are right, and both say so. Dane, in his head, plainly: he has never wanted anyone this much, and it's the worst timing of his life.
- **s2 (800), 1 a.m.** **Event:** Russ is called away, and shots come through the window. Tess on the island's emergency radio: somebody phoned the store, a tree is through the Dyer roof, and old Mrs. Dyer may be trapped. Russ goes, because it's his job. Twenty minutes later the kitchen window blows in. Dane gets Beth to the floor. He talks the whole time, low and steady, and she goes silent and listens. A second volley. He's cut by glass. They go out the back into the storm while the shooter fires at the sound.

**Romance beat:** The kiss, on the page, and an honest stop about a real cost.
**Danger beat:** Shots through the window.
**Key event:** They kissed, and the second place she slept has been hit.
**What changes:** They both know where this is going. Four people knew Russ's house: Russ, Ned, Dane's boss, and Tess, who sent Russ away.
**Emotional beat:** Heat, then violence.
**End question:** Beth, in the dark behind the woodpile: "I know a place." *Where, and who sent Russ away?*
**Say it twice:** who knew Russ's house; somebody phoned the store about the Dyer roof. (Restated in Ch 04.)
**Laugh:** "How many witnesses have kissed you?" / "As of tonight? One. I'm keeping count."

Tess, gossiping, in one line: Ned's boy is in rehab again in Portland (the only mention of Ned's son before Ch 12). Beth won't stay behind, and Dane won't leave her, so she comes. The stove is still warm. Canned food, a phone charger, wet socks drying. His trailer has been empty since Thursday.

## The traitor's path (Ned Haskell)

**Motive, said by Ned in Ch 12:** for two years he took Cole's cash to run his trucks over at night with no questions asked. On the first night, scared of what a search would turn up, he warned Cole so the load would be dumped. That warning is why the truck was clean and why Beth became the whole case. The next day Cole's lawyer had his warning call and his son. He chose his son, and told himself they only meant to scare her.

**Means:** a prepaid phone Cole gave him for the night runs, kept in the ferry's captain's cabin. He never used his own phone, which is why the records come back clean (Ch 10). The kid has a phone of his own on the island. There's signal at the harbor.
