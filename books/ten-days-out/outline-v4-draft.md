## The book in one line

A ferry deckhand sees fentanyl spill out of a lobster crate, and the U.S. Marshal who falls for her on the first night has ten days to keep her alive on a storm-locked island where someone she loves keeps telling the killer where she is.

## What this outline promises the author

| Brief item | How it is met |
|---|---|
| 1. Romance first | Every chapter moves Beth and Dane on the page: flirting, a fight that tips into flirting, wanting said plainly in the viewpoint's head. They meet over an oar in the snow (Ch 01) with obvious spark. |
| 2. Dual POV | Ch 01 alternates by scene (Beth, then Dane). From Ch 02, chapters alternate: Beth even, Dane odd. Every chapter and every POV switch names the viewpoint character in its first line. |
| 3. Exciting Ch 01 | Fentanyl named in the first 400 words. A man breaks in and chases her through the snow. Dane arrives, and she swings an oar at him. |
| 4. Danger acts | Something bad happens on the page in 14 of 18 chapters, and never two chapters in a row without it before the climax. |
| 5. Plain language | Jargon budget below. Every plot fact is said in plain words and said again in the next scene. |
| 6. The mystery is about people | Three suspects close to Beth, plus Dane's office. One plain test: where she was, and who knew. Beth and the reader suspect the same three people from the same moment (Ch 09) and learn the answer from the same line (Ch 11). Six named characters. |
| 7. Heat early | Almost-kiss Ch 03 (~14%). First heat scene Ch 04 (~21–24%). Four scenes, each out of a fight, a fear or a choice. |
| 8. One-talk test, no misunderstandings | Every disagreement between the leads happens to their faces, in the scene where it starts, and both are partly right. Nothing between them is overheard, withheld or mistaken. The rupture survives the whole truth (sentence below). |
| 9. Familiar tropes, their own way | Forced proximity, the protector stuck with the witness, arguing into flirting, the hero falling first, one bed for warmth. The turn each one takes is in the table below. |
| 10. The story moves | Every scene below has a one-line **Event**: an attack, an escape, a discovery, a kiss, a fight, or a choice that can't be undone. No scene goes over known facts, plans logistics, or reports offstage news. No two talk-led scenes in a row. |

## The clock (said plainly in Ch 02, again in Ch 03, and once a chapter after that)

- Cole Varney was arrested on Route 1 at about one in the morning with the truck, because Beth photographed the plate. He's in jail. **On Monday the 23rd, ten days from now, a judge decides whether to keep him there.** Before that, the grand jury (a room of ordinary people who decide whether there's enough for a trial) has to hear Beth tell it in person. If they haven't heard her by Monday morning, Cole walks out.
- Until she testifies, she's the whole case. Once she has, killing her only hurts Cole. Cole's people have done it anyway, to a man in Lowell three years ago. That is the revenge risk behind the rupture.
- *Domain checker:* the federal deadline for a detained defendant (preliminary hearing within 14 days unless indicted); grand jury testimony in person; Marshals protecting a pre-trial witness, and the fact that protection is voluntary; the rule that grand jury testimony can be used if the defendant kills the witness; WITSEC's no-contact rule; a Marine Patrol boat bringing a deputy out at night. The write-arounds in `story-bible-v1.md` may already cover some of these.

## Jargon budget (brief item 5)

- **First 1,000 words of Ch 01:** the test chapter uses plain words throughout ("the deck," "the captain's cabin," "the cooling unit," "chained down every vehicle"). Keep it that way.
- **Whole book, allowed with a plain gloss the first time:** car deck, wheelhouse (or "captain's cabin," pick one and keep it), pier, float (the floating dock), life ring, constable, grand jury, U.S. Marshal, witness protection.
- **Never on the page:** reefer, binders, D-ring, transom, slip, brow, dolphin, gut, bar, channel numbers, tonnage, license classes, radio procedure, tide heights.
- Beth's ear for boats shows in what she does with her hands. It never shows in vocabulary.

## Chapters

### 01 · The Last Truck · Beth, then Dane · 2,100 · *(written: `chapters/chapter-01/test-v4.md`)*
**Purpose:** Hook, inciting incident, the first attack, the leads meet.
**Opens with:** "The last truck onto the ferry that night was the seafood truck, and it was the one that changed her life." The big refrigerated lobster truck comes up the ramp with its cooling unit dark and silent.

- **s1, Beth, the deck.** **Event:** a crate splits and shows fentanyl, and a scared young man tells her she didn't see it. Bricks in gray tape under the lobsters, one torn and blowing white powder. She knows it's fentanyl; two island kids have died of it, one of them a boy she taught to row. The young man in the hoodie: "You didn't see this." She fakes boredom, chains the wheel, walks up to Ned. Ned: "Then you didn't see it. Not tonight." He looks away first. At Pruett she photographs the plate in three shaking tries.
- **s2, Beth, her kitchen and the snow.** **Event:** she calls the state police, and at 2 a.m. a man breaks in, says her name, and chases her. Midnight call; the case goes federal; a Marshal is coming by police boat. At two, the back door. Out the window in her wool socks, down the hill to her father's fish shack. "Beth. Come on. Nobody wants to hurt you." The flashlight finds her footprints.
- **s3, Dane, the shack.** **Event:** Dane drives the man off, Beth swings an oar at him, and they spark. Dragging-heel tracks. "Federal officer!" The man runs to a boat with no lights. The oar. "Badge." "Those boots are for a different ocean." He knows he's in trouble: he has never wanted to make a witness laugh. He catches her when her knees go. "You're an ice cube with opinions." "Then he's from here." He wants her off the island for the ten days to the grand jury. "No."

**Romance beat (obvious):** Hero falls first, plainly, in his head: "He was in trouble." She leans on him and doesn't notice. "Ask me again in ten days."
**Danger beat:** The break-in and the chase.
**Key event:** She has seen the drugs and told the police, and someone came for her within two hours.
**What changes:** A deckhand becomes the witness, with a marshal who wants to take her off her island.
**Emotional beat:** Dread, terror, then a jolt of spark.
**End question:** Only the state police and Dane's Portland office knew she'd called, so who told the man? And will she leave?
**Say it twice:** fentanyl; the plate; the man drags his left heel and fled by boat, so he lives here; ten days to the grand jury. (Restated in Ch 02 s1.)
**Laugh:** "The government."
**Note for the orchestrator:** the test's last line states as narrator's fact ("in an office he trusted, somebody had given that man her name") something this outline makes Dane's mistaken guess. Suggested edit so it reads as his belief: "And unless he was wrong, somebody in an office he trusted had given that man her name." The rest of Ch 01 stands as written.

### 02 · The Easy Job · Beth · 1,700
**Purpose:** The adult argument that ends Act 1; her people on stage; the ferry crippled, which plants the climax.
**Opens with:** "Beth Calloway let the marshal make his whole speech about Portland, and then she said no again, in her own kitchen, with his coat still around her."

- **s1 (600), her kitchen at first light.** **Event:** Dane chooses to stay on the island with her, and tells his boss so on speaker in front of her. The fight, to their faces, both partly right. Dane: someone in his office or the state police leaked her call; he wants her in a hotel in Portland. Beth: the kid saw her on the deck, so nobody had to tell him anything. Dane: then why wait until right after she called? She says out loud that Ned told her to keep quiet, and sets it down with a reason the reader accepts: Ned was scared, with those men twenty feet under them. Dane says protection is voluntary and he can't make her go. Then his reason flips: if his office leaked, a hotel his office books is the worst place she could be. He bends a plan for a witness for the first time in four years, and says so to his boss with Beth listening. Stated plainly in this scene: Cole Varney, the lobster dealer, was driving the truck and was arrested on Route 1 at one in the morning because of her photo. A judge decides on the 23rd. The grand jury has to hear her in person.
- **s2 (400), the landing.** **Event:** the Marine Patrol boat leaves without her, ahead of the storm, and the ferry cancels service. Act 1 ends. Ned tells her to go with them (the second time he tells her to go). He mentions his son in Portland "doing better" (plant). Tess hugs her hard and says the island already knows everything. Russ, the constable, a lobsterman with a badge, says the kid's trailer is empty and nobody knows his name past "the new sternman." He asks where she's sleeping. "Home," she says, to everyone. One line of spark between Russ and Tess.
- **s3 (700), the pier, the storm's first hour.** **Event:** the ferry breaks loose, and Beth, Ned and Dane save it. Ropes parting, the boat slamming the pier, Beth nearly crushed between them. Dane yanks her back and holds on too long. "You can let go." / "I know." He jumps aboard to help and jumps badly. They save the boat, but the steering is broken. Two trucks are stuck aboard on the car deck (plant). Ned: days to fix. Beth teases Ned that he can't swim and still won't wear a life jacket (plant).

**Romance beat:** The fight in the kitchen is flirting with a real edge. At the pier, the hold that lasts. In her head, plainly: she wants him and is furious about it.
**Danger beat:** The ferry breaking loose; Beth nearly crushed.
**Key event:** She stays, he stays, and the island shuts.
**What changes:** No way off. The man is on the island with them. The ferry can't run.
**Emotional beat:** Stubborn pride, then muscle and fear.
**End question:** Home, she said, to everyone on the landing. *Is home safe?*
**Say it twice:** Cole arrested, the 23rd, the grand jury in person; the ferry can't run until the steering's fixed; everyone heard her say "home." (Restated in Ch 03 s1.)
**Laugh:** Dane on the pier, soaked: "I'd like it noted that I jumped."

### 03 · Socks · Dane · 1,700
**Purpose:** The almost-kiss, with the reason for holding back said out loud; the second attack.
**Opens with:** "Dane Reyes was wearing her dead father's wool socks, darned in four colors, when she told him she wanted to kiss him."

- **s1 (800), her house, power out.** **Event:** the almost-kiss, and he says no out loud with his reason. Candles; he has checked every window twice. Too close in the dark kitchen. Beth: "I want to kiss you, and I'm not going to apologize for saying it." Dane, in his head: he wants it more than he has wanted anything in four years. Out loud: if he's thinking about her mouth, he isn't watching the door, and the last time he didn't watch the door, a woman named Marisol died. Beth accepts the reason and hates it. She tells him so.
- **s2 (900), after midnight.** **Event:** the man in the hoodie comes back through the storm, and Dane fights him in the dark kitchen. Beth goes silent and listens; Dane talks, to her and at the man. Glass, a struggle, her father's shotgun, a shot into the ceiling. The man is thrown out into the snow. Dane's forearm is cut. The man has a gun now and fires back at the house from the dark.

**Romance beat:** The almost-kiss and the honest no. Hero POV, plainly wanting.
**Danger beat:** The second break-in, and shots.
**Key event:** He came back to the house everyone knew she'd go home to.
**What changes:** Her house is gone as a refuge, and he's armed.
**Emotional beat:** Warmth and wanting, then violence.
**End question:** Beth, at the back door with the shotgun: "I know a place." *Where?*
**Say it twice:** he came because she said "home" at the landing; he has a gun now. (Restated in Ch 04 s1.)
**Laugh:** "These have been darned in three colors." / "Four."

### 04 · A Disappointing Kit · Beth · 2,000 · Heat #1
**Purpose:** The escape; the first heat scene, out of fear and a fight.
**Opens with:** "Beth Calloway ran Dane Reyes along the shore in the dark with a man shooting at the sound of them."

- **s1 (900), the shore road and the co-op wharf.** **Event:** they escape under fire to the empty net loft over the fishermen's co-op, the long room where the men mend their traps in winter. She tells nobody. The space heater works. She cleans and tapes his arm with her bare hands. The fight, to their faces: he says she should have stayed down; she says he should have let her shoot. Both are right. The fight tips over, and she kisses him.
- **s2 (1,100), the loft.** **Event:** heat #1 (level 3, hands and mouths, consent plain). He has no condoms, only the trauma kit. "It's a very disappointing kit." They laugh and keep going. Beth's POV, plain wanting. After, he says it can't happen again, and neither of them believes him.

**Romance beat:** The first heat scene, and a laugh inside it.
**Danger beat:** Shots as they run.
**Key event:** He has crossed his own line with his witness, knowing what it costs.
**What changes:** They both know where this is going. He knows he's compromised.
**Emotional beat:** Adrenaline into heat, then tenderness.
**End question:** At dawn, Russ's truck on the wharf: "I've been looking for you two all night." He was supposed to be watching her road. *Where was Russ?*
**Say it twice:** nobody knew the loft, and nobody came. (Restated in Ch 05 s1, and again at the midpoint.)

### 05 · Good Till Next August · Dane · 1,500
**Purpose:** The comic chapter after the hot one; seeds for Russ and Tess; she shares the next hiding place; the honest argument about the cost of sleeping together.
**Opens with:** "Dane Reyes stood behind Beth Calloway at the island store at nine in the morning while she bought condoms in front of Tess Marr and two lobstermen."

- **s1 (700), the store.** **Event:** Beth buys the condoms in front of everyone, which tells the island she's sleeping with the marshal, and doesn't care. Russ, red-faced, admits he fell asleep in his truck (suspect seed: broke since the divorce, sold his catch to Cole). Tess, bitter: Cole was the store's best customer and lent her money for the fuel tank, "and you just put my store under, Calloway." Then she hugs her anyway (suspect seed). "Four seventy-nine. Good till next August." Dane: "I'd have asked for a receipt." Russ: a summer house on the east road was broken into overnight (plant: where the man is hiding). Then **the choice, in front of Dane:** Russ offers another summer house he looks after on the north side, Tess will bring food, Ned wants to check on her. Dane says to her face that's three people too many. She says to his that these three are the island. Both partly right. She tells them.
- **s2 (800), the north-side summer house and the beach below it.** **Event:** she outshoots him with her father's shotgun, she kisses him, and he stops it with a reason. He reports their location to his boss in front of her, as he does every night; he trusts his boss and nobody else in that office (plant). The bet: "Dollar says you miss." / "I don't bet on what I can see." She doesn't miss. The kiss. He stops it and says why, plainly: if Cole's lawyer learns he's sleeping with his witness, they'll say he coached her, and Cole could walk. She says that's a reason to be careful and a bad reason to stop. They're both right, and both say so.

**Romance beat:** The counter, out loud. The kiss, the stop, the honest argument.
**Danger beat:** None on the page. Ch 04 and Ch 06 carry it.
**Key event:** Three people and his boss know where she sleeps.
**What changes:** The hiding place is shared.
**Emotional beat:** Comedy, then a charged, honest argument.
**End question:** Dane: "That's four people who know where you are." Beth: "Three of them would die for me." *Would they?*
**Say it twice:** Ned, Tess, Russ and Dane's boss know the summer house. (Restated in Ch 06 s2.)
**Laugh:** "Good till next August."

### 06 · Who Knew · Beth · 1,600
**Purpose:** The second hiding place burns. The question of a leak opens for both of them at once.

### 07 · Cold Water · Dane · 2,000 · Heat #2
**Purpose:** Beth saves Dane; one bed for warmth; she asks for what she wants out loud, and the hero hears her.
**Opens with:** "Dane Reyes did exactly what she did, right up until the wave took him off the rock."

- **s1 (700), the shore.** **Event:** Dane goes into the November sea, and she hauls him out. Her two hands in his coat, his weight, the cold that stops his voice for the first time in the book. The man's light loses them behind the rocks. She half carries him a mile to the old lighthouse on the north end, empty for the winter, and in through the window she knows doesn't shut right. Nobody knows they're here.
- **s2 (400), the keeper's house.** **Event:** she strips him and gets him warm, skin to skin, under every blanket in the house. One bed, for warmth. "This is medical." / "Best medical I've ever had." He gets his voice back. She says she has been more afraid in the last hour than at any time since the truck.
- **s3 (900), later.** **Event:** heat #2 (level 4, full; consent and condoms plain; the condoms were in her coat). She asks: "I want you. I'm saying it out loud so you can't pretend I didn't." Dane's POV, hearing her ask, and knowing it's the first time she has asked anyone for anything. He chooses her over his rule, knowing the cost.

**Romance beat:** The rescue, the one bed, the ask.
**Danger beat:** The sea.
**Key event:** She asks for him, and he says yes knowing what it risks.
**What changes:** They're lovers. She has broken her habit of never asking.
**Emotional beat:** Desperation, then heat, then quiet.
**End question:** In the dark: "I'm going to get you off this island alive, and then I'm going to lose you, aren't I?" She doesn't answer. *Will this outlast the ten days?*
**Say it twice:** he'll tell only his boss about the lighthouse. (Said in Ch 08 s1; restated in Ch 09 s2.)

### 08 · The Tower · Beth · 1,600
**Purpose:** The hero says it first and she hears it; the courtship is visible; the rescue is set.
**Opens with:** "Beth Calloway was elbow-deep in a dead generator when Dane Reyes told her he was in love with her."

- **s1 (800), the keeper's house.** **Event:** Dane tells her he's in love with her, and she doesn't say it back. He says it his way: "I'm in love with you, and I'd like that on the record, since you seem to be the one keeping it." The generator catches under her bare hands. She says, "Ask me in five days." He cooks the one tin of ham like he's feeding a building. The Marisol exchange: Marisol asked him what he'd do if he weren't this; he said he didn't know; she said everybody who says that wants a boat. Beth answers with her own: the captain's job in June, which she has never asked Ned for. He dares her to ask. He tells his boss they're at the lighthouse, with Beth beside him.
- **s2 (800), the lighthouse tower.** **Event:** Dane slips on the icy outside stair, and she catches him by the belt. He climbs for a phone signal; her fist in his belt is all that holds him. On the iron stair, still breathing hard, the boss calls back: the wind drops tomorrow afternoon, and a Marine Patrol boat can come for her at three. It needs a beach only an islander knows (Ned), the island's emergency radio to clear it (Tess), and the one truck that can get there on the snowed road (Russ). They fight it out on the stair, face to face. He wants to tell nobody. She says a stranger's beach will drown the officers. She's right about the water and he's right about the risk. They agree on three hours' notice.

**Romance beat:** Hero first, heard from her side. Her "Ask me in five days" is a yes she isn't ready to say.
**Danger beat:** The fall on the tower.
**Key event:** He says he loves her; she chooses the plan that tells her three people.
**What changes:** Rescue is coming. He is all the way in.

## Pacing flags (where I'm not sure)

1. **Ned's line in Ch 01 is a strong early pointer** ("Then you didn't see it. Not tonight." and "He looked away first"). The test chapter is binding, so the outline answers it: Beth says the line out loud in Ch 02 and gives a fair reason to set it down, and Tess and Russ each get an equally visible reason by Ch 05. If the plain or sample reader still names Ned by Ch 03, soften "He looked away first" in Ch 01. That is the one change I'd recommend to the test text beyond its last line.
2. **The test's last line** states Dane's guess as fact. See the note under Ch 01.
3. **The man gets away four times** (Ch 01, 03, 06, 09) before he's hurt (Ch 14) and caught (Ch 16). Earlier readers punished a villain who "appeared and walked off." Here each escape costs something on the page (a cut arm, a burned house, an officer shot), and he is never just watching. The plot architect should vary how he's seen each time.
4. **Ch 12–13 stack grief on grief:** Ned's betrayal, then the rupture an hour later. That's intended as the worst day, and the Ch 13 boat test gives her a win inside it. If the plain reader calls it too heavy, move the speakerphone call to the start of Ch 13 and end Ch 12 on Ned's cell door.
5. **Ch 17–18 have no danger.** The every-second-chapter rule holds through the climax; after it, Cole in the hallway is tension only. I think the resolution earns this. Flagged for the author.
6. **Ch 05 has no danger,** which is allowed because Ch 04 and Ch 06 both have it. It's the comic chapter, and it needs the condom scene and the shooting lesson to hold. If it reads slow, cut Russ's apology to two lines.
7. **The repair beats are tight** (Ch 14 s3 and Ch 15 s1 carry all four). They land at 77–80%, inside the map's 75–88%, before the climax. The ordinary scene is the ham, and it must stay free of plot until its last line.
8. **The trap (Ch 10)** is the only clue device in the book. It's said in one plain line (church for Tess, school for Russ, nothing for Ned) and restated once. If any reader stumbles, cut the school and test Tess alone.
9. **Domain items** (the clock, the Marshals' role, voluntary protection, in-person testimony, WITSEC rules, the Marine Patrol trips) go to the domain checker before the bible.
10. **POV order** flips from the old plan because Ch 01 alternates by scene. Beth has the even chapters from Ch 02 on. Heat scenes alternate Beth, Dane, Beth, Dane.
