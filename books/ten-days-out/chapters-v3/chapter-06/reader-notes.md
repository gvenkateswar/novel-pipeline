## Where I got bored

- **Scene 3, the whole rock call.** From "The phone went off in his jacket as he topped the rock" to "toward the fish house." I'm told a call happens, I can't hear it, he writes on his knee, glass flashes, he ducks, she asks "Pike?", he says "Pike. Weather." I already knew the dragger had binoculars (Chapter 4 told me), so the flash adds nothing. My eyes slid down the page.
- **The Coast Guard briefing at the start of scene 4.** "They're bringing the inspector over for the ferry, which saves the government a boat, and you go back with them." / "District won't put a helicopter on a non-emergency move, and the one they've got is offshore on a case." This is the book answering a question I hadn't asked. It perks up once the county is in the loop ("The island had one person to ring"), because that's a threat.
- **The ledger in scene 2.** "night lines down the column, each one paid off in hundreds and never rung up as cash." I had to read it twice, and it still felt like the book making sure I'd caught the money-laundering hint. Chapter 2 already showed me Tess's fold of hundreds, and that worked better because it went unexplained.
- **The sex scene, around "She pulled his shirt off over his head. He was warm on the drum side and cold on the other, the same as her."** I drifted here. It wasn't badly written; I had read this scene two days ago in book time. See "Where it sounded like AI."

## Where I got confused

- **Scene 1: where was Dane, and how did he get back?** At the end of Chapter 5 Beth says nobody crosses till the morning low. Now he's gone with the gun, a flashlight at the Ashby end signals "Short. Then long," the skiff answers, and Dane comes back soaked saying he "walked the bar" when "the bar was over a man's head." Did he swim? Lie? Was he the flashlight? I think the book wants me to suspect him, and it worked, but I'm not sure whether this is a deliberate clue or a mistake about the tide. If it's deliberate, Beth's "She didn't ask" is the strongest beat in the scene. If it isn't, it's a hole.
- **"I sent the fire up."** I had to stop on this. Does it mean he reported the fire? Who to, and from where, with no signal past the dock?
- **"Bar" means two things in scene 1.** The oak bar on the door and the sandbar outside appear in alternate sentences: "The oak bar leaned beside it," "the Ashby end of the bar," "found the bar and dropped it," "Walked the bar," "the bar was over a man's head," "set the bar back across the door." I lost track of which one I was in more than once.
- **"Lyle" and "Arsenault" in the store.** Tess says "Lyle," the narration calls him a clean-shaven dragger man, and then partway through he's "Arsenault." It took me a beat to see they're the same man. I'd met him as Arsenault in Chapter 3.
- **"Dollar says she opens with the weather."** Who is she? Pike? I'd assumed Pike was a man, or never thought about it. Then he gets the bet backwards, or does he? "Pike. Weather." suggests he won. I couldn't tell.
- **The start of scene 4.** "Pike's answers had come in on the ledge before light. He gave them to her going down off the rock." Which rock, which day? Scene 3 was Tuesday afternoon on a rock. This is Wednesday dawn, apparently on a rock again. It read like the same scene restarting.
- **The float lights.** The power went out island-wide on Friday in Chapter 2, and nothing has told me it came back. If the whole island is dark, why does "The float lights were dark" mean anything? I felt the dread anyway, but the logic part of me snagged on it.
- **"Six days on this island."** He came over Friday morning, and this is Thursday. By my count that's seven. Small, but Dane is supposed to be the precise one.
- **Marr's price sticker on the impeller box.** "Her eye went over it and on." I know I'm meant to notice it. I don't know what it means. Ned bought a part at Tess's store. So?

## Where I stopped believing

- **Dane leaves her alone in the store with Arsenault.** In Chapter 3 he wouldn't step outside to save his boat deal because it meant leaving her at the counter beside Arsenault. Now he leans on a piling outside while Arsenault walks in and stands close enough for her to smell his soap. Dane just passes him with a nod. Nothing explains the change. I didn't buy it.
- **Arsenault's lines.** "Tess, this store's the heart of the island. I'd hate to see it have a hard winter." and "He turned his smile on her." This is a TV villain. Everyone else on this island talks around things; he's the only one who announces his threat.
- **Beth "didn't ask" where Dane had been.** She has spent five chapters demanding to know what's in his log. I'll accept it as a choice she's making, especially after the fish house, but it pulled at me.
- **The sex scene the night before a dawn extraction.** That night a killer's skiff swept their window with a light, the dragger has moved into position, and the county knows. Genre lets me take it, and the "stopped listening for the door" line half-earns it, but Dane in particular is very relaxed for a man whose last witness died.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Every scene ends on a clipped, loaded line:** "and kept her hand on it a second." / "From here Beth couldn't read it." / "She was already going down off the rock toward the fish house." / "Tomorrow's first." / "She didn't stop it." / "The float lights were dark." Six for six. Each one is fine on its own, but together they make a drumbeat I could set my watch by.
- **The sex scene copies Chapter 5's beat for beat.** Both have the long breathless run-on sentence that ends "and she noticed..." (Ch 5: "she noticed she wasn't steering any of it"; Ch 6: "somewhere in all of it she noticed she had stopped listening for the door"). Both have "She laughed... He kissed her through it." Both have the callus or hard palm, the wool steaming on the wire, the drum ticking, the cot complaining or walking on the planks, and him saying her name as a question. Same furniture, same order.
- **Over-explained feeling:** "Her father had done everything right on the water and lost anyway. She had never wanted to be the one at the wheel when it went wrong." The book has made me work for everything else. Here it hands me Beth's whole psychology in two tidy sentences, right after a strong visual (the license corners "still sharp").
- **"She had said Nobody on the shore, not half an hour back, and meant it. Ned was the boat. A question to the boat told nobody. She knew what she'd promised, and she asked him anyway."** This explains a choice I would have understood from the action alone. The last sentence is fine; the three before it are the author leaning in.
- **Repeated tics:** "Dollar says" (Chapter 1 used it too); "small and exact" for the harbor from above (Chapter 4 used it); the white line on her palm getting a thumb (again); "slow on the bad hip" for Ned (again). The fragment chains ("Soaked. Cold in her bare hand, and stiff with salt.") are starting to sound like a setting rather than a voice.
- **Everyone talks in priced quips:** Tess ("Nine fifty a gallon, and I'm not sorry," "Good till next August"), Dane ("I'd like him boring for a week," "The government reimburses incidentals"), Ned ("Glass is up. So's this."). It's charming, but by Chapter 6 the island has one sense of humor.

## What I want to know next

- **Who dimmed the float lights, and is the dragger's engine turning over for her?** I'm turning the page for this.
- **Where was Dane last night, and how did he get across the bar at high water?** This is the question that matters most to me now. I trust him, and the book has just given me a reason not to.
- **Did Beth just get herself killed by asking Ned "Which tide?"** Ned's phone sits on Tess's shelf. The Coast Guard landing went through the county. Tess wrote something on her hand when Beth left.
- **What did Tess write?**
- **Is Tess the money and not the gun?** I think she's laundering Arsenault's cash and is scared ("I'd hate to see it have a hard winter"), not that she sold Beth out.
- **What did Pike actually say on the rock?** And what is Q's "request"? I've been waiting since Chapter 3.

I have plenty of questions. This is the strongest end-of-chapter pull since Chapter 2.

## Prediction

- The float is a trap. The *Margaret Ann* moved in front of the landing to meet the Coast Guard boat, or to take Beth before it arrives.
- The leak runs from the county to the store to the dragger, through Tess's phone shelf or her office. Tess is compromised by money, not murder, and will make some kind of amends.
- Ned is clean. His confession is the book clearing him, and the "Which tide?" question will turn out to be Beth trusting the right person. (If Ned turns out to be the leak, I'll feel cheated, because the forepeak scene made me love him.)
- Q / Homeland kept Beth "in place" on purpose, as bait for the vessel, and Dane knew more of it than he told her. His night walk had something to do with that.
- Beth will take the wheel of a boat (the *North Reach* with its cracked bow, or Russ's *Second Wind*) at the climax. The hundred-ton license is the loudest plant in the book. I'd bet heavily on it, which also means I'll be a little disappointed if it's the only surprise.
- Ending: she puts her name in for Ned's job, and Dane gets a boat after all, maybe through her.

## Listener pass (how this reads aloud)

- **"Bar"** for both the door and the sandbar, in alternating sentences in scene 1. By ear this is the worst passage in the chapter. A listener cannot tell them apart.
- **Lyle / Arsenault:** two names for one man within a few lines. A listener will hear a third person walk in.
- **"Dollar says she opens with the weather."** The "she" has no one to attach to by ear.
- **"Q"** and **"Pike"** are one-syllable names that vanish in audio. I wasn't sure who was who on the page, and I'd be lost listening.
- **Scene breaks between scenes 3 and 4:** "Tuesday afternoon on a rock" runs straight into "Wednesday dawn coming down off a rock." With no visual break, it sounds like one continuous walk.
- **The fragment chains** ("Short. Then long." / "Slapped." / "Soaked.") sound good aloud, tense and percussive, as long as there aren't too many in a row. Scene 1 is near the limit.
- **The sex-scene run-on** reads well aloud. It has rhythm. The problem is that a listener has heard the same shape in Chapter 5.
- **Ned's forepeak dialogue** is the best-sounding passage in the chapter: plain, rambling, a real man talking. "Thermo King, he runs it continuous for live product, every Thursday since the Fourth of July two years back, you can hear it from the wheelhouse."

## Chapters 04 to 06 together

This is the tightest stretch of the book so far. The mole question (Tess, Ned, Dane's log) works as an engine, and each chapter closes the circle a little more. The romance escalates steadily and each sex scene changes something: Chapter 5 is "I'm choosing anyway," and Chapter 6 ends with her letting him write her plan down.

But the action keeps repeating one move. In Chapter 4 they lie still behind the generator while the hood searches and leaves. In Chapter 5 they stand still in the flood while a flashlight sweeps and leaves. In Chapter 6 she lies under the window while the skiff's light sweeps and leaves. By the third time I knew the light would move on, and the tension had been spent. The man with the dragging heel, the best villain image in the book, has quietly disappeared, and the threat has turned into an outline: Arsenault's smile, a dragger at a mooring. The two sex scenes are built from the same parts. The supporting cast also thins out: Russ gets one line about a thermos, and Tess is reduced to her ledger.

I'm still reading. The ending of Chapter 6 and Ned's confession pulled me through. But if Chapter 7 opens with another hide-and-wait, I'd start to skim.

## Best moment

The forepeak, Ned's confession while they seat the impeller in the water:

> "Crates come open now and again," Ned said. "You know they do. A year back I started seeing too many pieces to the crate for the weight. Figured it was shorts. Boxes rode heavy, and I never gave that a thought. That contract's the winter boat. Lose it, the district runs her three days a week. Somebody gets laid off, and it wouldn't be me. So I never said."
>
> He set the wrench down on the housing. "I thought it was shorts, Bethie."
>
> She took her hand off the impeller and put it on his arm, on the wet green wool, a second. Then she picked up the wrench and gave it back to him.

"Somebody gets laid off, and it wouldn't be me" is the most human line in six chapters. He's protecting her job, not his own, and the guilt sits there unspoken. Then she hands back the wrench instead of saying anything. That gesture does the forgiving, and the book doesn't explain it.

Runner-up: "Four seventy-nine. Good till next August." Tess says something about Beth's whole life in a price and an expiry date, without looking at the box.
