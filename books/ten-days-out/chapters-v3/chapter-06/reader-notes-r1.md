None. I came closest in the Pike call: "They're bringing the inspector over for the ferry, which saves the government a boat, and you go back with them." For a paragraph or two I was reading a travel itinerary.

## Where I got bored

- **The Pike debrief on the walk down off the rock.** "Coast Guard's sending a lifeboat at first light tomorrow, to the town float, because it's the only lee in this swell." Then the inspector, then the helicopter, then "The landing's cleared with the county... Coast Guard won't use a town landing without the town knowing." It's four plot facts in a row, handed over one at a time while two people pick their way down wet rock. The scene wakes up at "The county would ring the island. The island had one person to ring." Everything before that line I could have taken as a summary.
- **Ned's Thermo King speech.** "He always runs his unit. Thermo King, he runs it continuous for live product, every Thursday since the Fourth of July two years back, you can hear it from the wheelhouse." I already know all of this from page one of the book. It sounds like a deposition, and I skimmed it.
- **The middle of the second sex scene.** "The drum's heat lay along one side of her. The cove's cold came up through the planks on the other." Then, two lines later: "He was warm on the drum side and cold on the other, the same as her." It's the same image twice in a few lines. From there to "Now," I was skimming. Ch 05's scene had surprise (the disappointing kit, the jeans that "have taken a side"). This one has "Slower than Monday" and then mostly a replay.

## Where I got confused

- **Two bars.** The oak bar across the door and the sand bar out in the cove both get called "the bar," back to back in scene 1. "She set the oak bar back across the door" comes two lines after "Out there the bar was over a man's head." Later, "The bar was across the door." I had to stop and sort them out more than once.
- **Who had the flashlight in scene 1?** At the Ashby end a light flashes short, then long. The skiff answers it. The light goes out as the skiff leaves, and then Dane knocks from the landward side, soaked to the ankle, and says he walked a bar that's underwater. I honestly can't tell whether the book wants me to suspect Dane of signalling, or whether he went out to call Portland and the timing is a coincidence. If it's deliberate, it's a strong plant. If not, it reads like a hole. Either way I noticed it.
- **"Tess?" as her first question to him.** It took me a beat to realise she meant "did you keep Tess's name out." Out of context it sounds like she's asking whether Tess was the flashlight.
- **Power.** On Thursday "Along the harbor road the lights were on, all the way to the store." The power went out Friday in Ch 02, and I never saw it come back. The store is fifty-eight degrees on Tuesday, which made me think it was still out. So I wasn't sure whether the dark float lights were sabotage or just the outage.
- **Why the skiff left.** They light up the fish house window, slow on it, and go. If they now know where she is, why leave? I assume to come back better prepared, but that's my guess, not something on the page.
- **What did Dane do in the store?** He goes into Marr's first, comes out, nods, and she goes in. I don't know what he checked or why.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Dane leaving her alone at night.** This is the man who wanted "two people awake behind" a locked door, and who said "I watch whatever leaves your hands." He walks out with the gun and leaves her asleep with the door unbarred, while a light is working the bar. Maybe that's the point. On the page it reads as out of character, because no reason is ever given.
- **Ned's confession comes too easily.** She says one sentence, "That unit was dark Thursday," and he hands over the shorts, the contract, the layoffs, everything, unprompted and in order. I believe the man, but I don't believe the speed.
- **Dialogue built to deliver plot.** "Pike asked. 'District won't put a helicopter on a non-emergency move, and the one they've got is offshore on a case.'" Nobody quotes their boss quoting the district out loud like that. That line is there for me, not for Beth.
- **The license, again.** Ch 04 had it "pressing her ribs from her wallet." Now she hands it over: "Hundred-ton. Never used." The corners are still sharp. The moment is fine, but the second showing feels like the book pointing at the ending.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Every scene ends on the same short, withheld beat.** "...and kept her hand on it a second." / "From here Beth couldn't read it." / "Tomorrow's first." / "She didn't stop it." / "The float lights were dark." That's five scenes and five clipped, quiet closers. Over six chapters it has become the book's rhythm, and I can feel each one coming.
- **The "She didn't ask" family.** "She didn't ask." (scene 1), "She didn't stop it." (scene 4), "Beth looked at the hand until he put it away." Beth's silence is a real trait, but on the page it now works as a reflex.
- **The same details keep coming back.** The callus or "hauler's ridge" on his palm (Ch 03, Ch 05, twice here). The white scar on her left palm (Ch 05, twice here). The red tag on his wrist (Ch 05 twice, here). Tess writing on the back of her hand (Ch 02, Ch 05, twice here). "The drum ticked" (Ch 05, several times here). Each worked the first time. Now they read like a checklist.
- **The two sex scenes share a skeleton.** Compare Ch 05, "she heard her own voice asking him for something and couldn't have said what," with Ch 06, "she was asking him for something and then for something else." Compare "Her heel went hard into his back" with "her heel dug into him." Compare "The cot's iron cried out" with "The cot's legs walked an inch." It's the same scene in different clothes.
- **"It had no warmth in it worth the name, and she stayed in it anyway."** It's pretty, generic, and could be in any book.

The voices are still separate, which is a plus. Dane is the joker ("I'd like him boring for a week"; "The government reimburses incidentals"). Tess is fast and prices everything. Arsenault has a smooth menace. Ned's speech, the Thermo King run-on aside, is mostly few words.

## Prediction section

What I think happens next, from the page alone:

1. **The float is a trap.** Somebody knows the hour. The dark float lights and the dragger turning over her engine mean the lifeboat landing is compromised.
2. **The leak:** Beth told Ned the tide on purpose, "She had said Nobody on the shore... She asked him anyway." I read that as a test. I predict Ned is innocent of this one, and the leak runs through the county call to Tess, the island's one phone to ring. Tess has Arsenault on an off-books account, holds the truck keys and the town ring, and has Ned's phone on her shelf. I think Tess isn't the villain but is bought and frightened ("Store like this lives on the boat" is a threat).
3. **Dane's night walk** comes back as a reveal. Either he met Q's people, or the skiff, or he swam. I'd bet he was setting something up with Q (Homeland, "observe only") that uses Beth as bait for the *Margaret Ann*. "Do not disclose to witness" will blow up between them.
4. **Beth drives the *North Reach* out herself** on the unused hundred-ton license, with the crack and the new impeller. That's what the pump repair is for. I'm fairly sure of this, because the book has shown me the license twice.
5. **Arsenault is the money**, and the dragging-heel man is his hand. Pelletier was only the trucker.
6. **Endgame:** Dane gets a boat (the Webbers Cove is gone, so a different one), possibly the *Ruthie C* bought back from the man from away.

## Listener pass (heard, not read)

- **"Bar" is the worst audio problem in the chapter.** By ear, the oak bar, the sand bar, and "out of the fish house" with "the bar across the door" all run together. A listener will lose track in scene 1 and again in scene 4.
- **The nested quote in Pike's message** ("Pike asked. 'District won't...'") can't be followed aloud. You can't hear where Dane stops and Pike starts.
- **Ned's Thermo King sentence** is one long comma chain with no breath in it, and a narrator will run out of air.
- **"'Dane,' he said through the boards."** This works aloud. "Barred it. You were out." is clipped enough that a listener may miss the joke.
- **The day labels** (Tuesday, Wednesday's first sun, Thursday) help a lot by ear. Keep that in mind as something that works.
- **The long sentence in the sex scene** ("He moved, and she moved with him...") reads well aloud. The short fragments around it ("Soaked. Cold in her bare hand, and stiff with salt.") read as choppy once a narrator is voicing them.

> She took her hand off the impeller and put it on his arm, on the wet green wool, a second. Then she picked up the wrench and gave it back to him.
