## Where I got bored

- **The father's boat flashback in the shed.** "The *Ruthie C* was thirty-eight feet and named for her mother..." Two paragraphs of backstory dropped in while Dane is under an engine looking for a pump. The box detail is good (see Best moment), but Ch 01 already gave me the man in the good coat writing her father down "in blue pen." This is the same scene told a second time at greater length, and the scene's clock stops while it plays.
- **The bag Q&A.** "Envelopes from the store. Slip inside. My name on the front in Tess's hand. Flap tucked. Never sealed." I could tell this matters, but it's procedure in clipped answers with no emotion on it, and I didn't know why he was asking *now*. I skimmed until the tag went on his wrist.
- **Ned and the fuel cans.** Once the man on the spine is gone, the scene runs on impellers, funnels and "Bit higher." The "Put my name in" thought is new and interesting, but it shows up from nowhere at the end of a chore.
- **The last scene after she climbs out.** "For a while there was only her body." A full paragraph of heartbeats syncing and the cold waiting to come in. I drifted here. It's the third body-against-body moment in four chapters, and nothing is at stake in it.

## Where I got confused

- **The grand jury date. This one is the biggest problem.** In Ch 02 on Friday, Dane says "the grand jury sits Monday. It's the last sitting before Pelletier walks." Now it's Monday, "a week before the Monday that mattered in Portland." So is the grand jury next week? Did it move? Did they miss it? Nobody in the chapter mentions it. I'd been counting down to this Monday, and the book quietly let the clock go. Either I misread Ch 02 or the stakes just disappeared.
- **"She wants me alive long enough to be useful."** Who is "she"? Q? Pike? I didn't know either one was a woman. And "Both of us": both of whom?
- **Russ at the fork.** "The one to his own house he left standing empty in the snow. Beth knew where that path came out. She turned up toward the north road." I read this three times. I think it means Russ went to Marr's instead of home and Beth noticed. But "she turned up toward the north road" reads as if she's following him, and then she isn't.
- **Why Dane is asking about money.** Is he investigating Tess's bag, or checking whether Beth is part of it? "Who's alone with it?" "The boat." Is she pointing at Ned, or at herself? I couldn't tell whether this was an interrogation or a conversation.
- **"The set."** It took me a beat to work out that "the set" is the generator.
- **The fish house logic.** She says "He's been through mine," meaning her house, and then proposes her family's fish house as the safe place. That's the first building a man looking for a Calloway would check, isn't it? "Nobody looks at it" didn't convince me.
- **"Last night... Was that the end of it?"** It took me a second to place this as the shed kiss on Saturday night, because the chapter opens on Sunday with no bridge.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Russ turning up on the path.** They're hiding on a hilltop, and the one islander who has been asking where she sleeps happens to come up the islanders' path with a thermos "for a look" and falls in beside them. Either it's a coincidence or he's a suspect. The book doesn't let anyone react to it either way. Dane's hand goes to his hip and comes off, and then they all chat.
- **Dane letting Beth wade the gut.** He's a marshal with a witness he's ordered to keep "in place." He lets her go waist-deep into November water alone, and his whole protest is "Let me go first. These are waterproof." The man who said "the last witness who knew her own town better than I did is buried in it" would argue harder than one line.
- **The grand jury going unmentioned** (see above). Nobody acts as though a deadline just passed or moved.
- **The hood showing his face.** "He didn't care who on the island saw that face." Beth flags it, which is good, but then nobody follows it up. Dane doesn't say a word about what it means that a man is fine being seen.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Body tics on repeat.** "Dane's hand went to his hip" shows up again (it's in every chapter, sometimes twice). "Went still all down his length" echoes Ch 03's "went still, the whole length of him" and Ch 02's "the whole length of him across her back." "Hip to hip" appears twice in one scene. "The cold came up" through rock or concrete three times.
- **"Rowing like an islander."** Ch 02 already said "He rowed like somebody who had learned before he could walk."
- **Lines that sound written.** "Either one was true enough to lie down with." "Her hand heard it anyway." "I've been the boat's since I jumped." "That's a not yet. One I can't afford." / "Then save up." Every one of them is a little too finished. Together they make the chapter feel worked over.
- **"She shook. He held on."** Two short sentences made to land like a greeting card.
- **Beth sounding like Dane.** "That's a narrow definition of waterproof" is his kind of joke, dry and wordy. In Ch 01 she spoke in clipped nouns: "Line." "Strapped it." In this chapter the two of them start sounding like one person.
- **Dane is quieter than before.** The man who talked nonstop to calm her in Ch 02 barely talks here. Maybe that's deliberate, but on the page it feels like his voice has gone flat.
- **Every scene ends on a set piece.** "Pull it." Then "And you stay inside where I put you." Both are neat closing lines.

## Is anyone under threat?

Two stretches have nobody under threat:

1. **The shed, from the start of the scene until she spots the man on the spine.** That's the father's boat flashback, the bag questions and the red tag, roughly the first half of the scene. The engine is dead and they're hidden. Nothing is pressing.
2. **From Ned's arrival with the fuel through the end of the chapter.** The man on the spine is gone. Ned's visit is chores. Monday's bar crossing has physical risk (cold, the drop-off), but Beth chooses it, it's a rehearsal, and no antagonist is anywhere near. The chapter ends on a plan, not a danger.

Scene 1 has real threat: a man inside her house who then looks straight up at the hill where they're lying. The spine moment in the shed is the chapter's best stretch of tension. But in both, the threat looks at them and walks away. "Observe only" is the order Dane gets, and it's also how the whole chapter plays.

## Listener pass (heard, not read)

- **Text messages.** "Pike: Q says hands off the vessel. Observe only. Keep witness in place." Read aloud, "Pike colon Q says" is a mouthful, and a listener won't know who Pike or Q is unless they remember Ch 03. "Sold. Sorry." Without italics this will sound like narration.
- **Italic thoughts are lost.** "*North end*, he'd told Portland last night on the porch. *Not the house.*" and "Put my name in. Four words." A listener hears "Put my name in" as narration, and then "Four words" makes no sense. "*Is this going in your log?* was in her mouth" sounds garbled read aloud.
- **Pronoun lag at the opening.** "She heard him first, or her ear did" comes right after a paragraph about Dane. A listener will assume "him" is Dane until the hood shows up.
- **Jargon in a row:** "the set," "injector pump," "Crack it. Quarter turn. Now pump," "the screw spat air," "impellers," "day tank," "the gut," "the sill," "the bar," "the spine." Each is fine alone, but in audio they pile up and I lost the picture in the shed.
- **"A week before the Monday that mattered in Portland."** On audio this slips past before you can think about it, and then you wonder whether you missed a chapter.
- **The Russ fork paragraph** is impossible to follow by ear.
- **Works aloud:** the swearing. "Son of a bitch," she told it. / "steadily and with variety: the water, the cold, the island, Connecticut." / "You're swearing at Connecticut. Which seems fair." That's the only laugh in the chapter, and it lands.

- Who told the hood "north end"? Is it Dane's people?
- Why is a young man with a face nobody knows fine with being seen?
- Is Ned the money?
- What's in Dane's log, and what's the thing he "can't tell" her?

These pull me forward. But I got all of them in Ch 02 and Ch 03, apart from the Ned money angle, which is new and good. This chapter adds almost no new question. It makes me wait on the old ones. The last scene tells me exactly what will happen next ("Ninety seconds, if you don't stop"), and that takes away suspense rather than adding it.

## Best moment

> When it was done he asked her to carry the box out to the man's truck. His hands had started to shake, and he had put them under the table where the man couldn't see. So she carried it. It was heavier than paper ought to be. The corners cut into her forearms all the way down the steps. At the tailgate the man said thank you, dear, and took it from her as if she'd brought him a pie.

"As if she'd brought him a pie" hit me hard. It tells me why she won't be written down and why she hid the man with the heel from the trooper, and it never says either thing. It's the best line in the chapter, even though it's in the scene that bored me.

Close second: the kitchen door at first try. "Then the kitchen door gave at the first try. One lift, then his shoulder." It's an instant chill, because the book spent Ch 02 teaching me that only someone who knows the house opens it like that.
