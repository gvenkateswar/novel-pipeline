## Scene 1 — Lookout Hill

**Setting:** The bare granite top of Lookout Hill, Sunday (Day 3), first light. The storm is easing to a gale. They are lying flat on wet rock among the blueberry stems with Dane's binoculars, and the harbor is laid out below like a chart. Sensory anchor: spray hissing even up here, and cold water soaking through the fronts of their clothes from the granite.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane. Seen through the glasses: the hooded man (unnamed; he never speaks). "Q" and "Pike" appear only as text on the screen.

**Surface action:**
1. **Open (outline):** flat on wet granite with his binoculars. She has the glasses and he lets her keep them, because she knows which roof is hers without looking. One texture detail: the cold coming up through the rock.
2. **The cottage.** A figure comes along the shore below her back steps: hood up, the heel dragging in the slush. He goes up the steep path from the store side and circles the house once, round the front and back to the harbor side.
3. **The latch (Flag 5).** He goes into the mudroom. Through the glasses she sees the kitchen door give at the first try, his shoulder going into it after the lift. **One interior sentence, two readings:** she had taught one person "lift and shove," on her own back step, on Friday; or he'd learned it on her door at twenty to twelve. She doesn't say it aloud. She gives Dane the glasses without a word.
4. **Inside a minute or two.** A shape moves past her kitchen window, the one that looks down on the whole harbor. Then he's out on the back step, and **he pushes the hood back** and turns his face up to the hill. It's a young face. She doesn't know it.
   - **Her habit (once this chapter):** she places men by their boats, and she has no slot for this one. He rows like an islander and has no boat but theirs.
   - Her fear register: she goes still and quiet. Dane, beside her, talks low and steady, transport patter: what's he doing, where are his hands.
   - He looks at the hill a long moment, as if he knows where to look, then puts the hood up.
5. **The consequence.** He goes down to the shore, launches the aluminum skiff from under the store dock, and rows out to the *Margaret Ann*. She takes him aboard, and the skiff is hauled. This is the first time he is tied to a named boat on the page.
6. **The one call (requirement 3b).** Dane types one text where he lies, already outdoors. It's short, and the page doesn't show it. He waits. The reply lands with the other message held in the inbox, and **he turns the screen toward her himself**, the opposite of every screen he has ever angled away. In words on the page (World rule 6):
   - *Pike: Q says hands off the vessel. Observe only. Keep witness in place.*
   - Beth, flat: "In place." The two words land on her.
   - Dane: "She wants me alive long enough to be useful." He means Quist, and he means both of them.
7. **One press on: *Sold. Sorry.*** He doesn't say anything for a second. Then he takes the yard's listing sheet out of the back cover of his notebook, folded in quarters, without opening the pages (Flag 4), and shows her the boat. The page names the Webbers Cove once; no figure.
8. **The laugh that stops.** She laughs, because a deputy carrying a printed lobster boat around in his coat is funny, and then she stops, because he minds. She doesn't apologize. She gives him the sheet back.

**Subtext:** He shows her the screen because she caught him yesterday, and because he can show her this one. Showing her what he's allowed to show is how he asks her to stop asking about the rest. She takes it as the gift it is, and she also hears "keep witness in place." The man on her step looked up at the one place on the island Dane chose this morning.

**Tension source:** Situational: a man in her house while she watches from a hill. Interpersonal under it: the latch she taught one person, and an office in Portland that wants her kept where she is.

**Texture budget:** (1) the cold coming up through the wet granite; (2) the latch giving through the glasses. Nothing about the dragger beyond the skiff being hauled.

**Ending type:** Action, with a prop.

## Scene 2 — The woods road

**Setting:** The islanders' path off the back of Lookout Hill, coming down through spruce into the woods road, a little after sunrise. Wind in the tops, quiet underneath. Sensory anchor: snow coming off the spruce in clumps when the wind gusts, and dropping onto Dane's shoulders.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Russ.

**Surface action:**
1. Russ is coming up the woods road with a thermos, because he saw the skiff go out from his own kitchen, and he knows nobody from away knows this path. He leans on the nearest spruce. He was going out on his boat to ask the *Margaret Ann* a question.
2. **Dane's one prediction (cap):** low, to Beth: "He's going to say 'somebody.' Watch." Russ says "somebody" in his next line. Optional; spend it here or nowhere this chapter.
3. **Russ off the boat.** Dane tells him to stay off the dragger. It's the badge's order, passed on, and Dane doesn't like passing it. Russ: "Feds told me stay off that boat. So I'm staying off that boat. I'm not a complicated man." (laugh)
4. **Russ's offer and Dane's reasons (carried item).** Russ will run her to Pruett on the *Second Wind* the minute it lays down. Dane thanks him and turns it down in one sentence: any boat on this island could be theirs, he can't read this sea, and whoever takes her leaves a wake the whole bay can follow. **Russ takes it without offense**, and that reads two ways.
5. **The cable (carried item; ask-and-answer 1).** Russ says it before anybody asks: "Somebody cut that cable? Somebody'd have to beat the rust to it. That tower's been on the district's list since Labor Day."
6. **Tess (ask-and-answer 2; carried items; the suspicion spread).** Russ, as news, the way the island passes everything:
   - "Who's emergency management on this island? Tess is. Since town meeting. Slow putting her hand down."
   - LifeFlight, one clause, yesterday's news: she had them on the line for an old woman with a bad heart, and the pilot wouldn't lift.
   - The store has the only phone on the island that works, and Tess has been on it all morning.
   - "And she asked me where you're sleeping. Twice." The second time, when he said he didn't know, she wrote it on the back of her hand anyway.
   - Beth gives Russ nothing back. Her interior gets one beat, no more: Tess writes everything on that hand, and always has.

**Subtext:** Russ wants to be the first to know something for once, and he's being kind and nosy in the same breath. Dane is passing on an order he hates to a man who obeys it cheerfully. Beth hears her oldest friend described as the woman with the only phone, the county's ear and a pen.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal and quiet. An islander offering the one thing Dane won't take, and the news about Tess landing on Beth in front of a marshal.

**Texture budget:** (1) the tower's rust (in Russ's line). Nothing else.

**Ending type:** Action seen, which reads two ways.

**Ends on:** Russ goes on down the woods road, and at the fork he takes the path that comes out behind Marr's, not the one to his own house. Beth knows where it comes out. She turns up toward the north road without saying where Russ is going. No line after.

**Word count target:** 450. **Floor:** 380. If long, cut the prediction first, then the thermos.

## Scene 3 — Ashby, under the generator

**Setting:** The Ashby generator shed, Sunday late morning. The tank ran dry in the night and the engine sucked air, so they've put in the last of the fuel can and it has to be bled before it will run. Concrete pad, the shed door propped on its frozen track, gray daylight. Sensory anchor: diesel on cold hands, and the smell of it in the shed.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.

**Surface action:**
1. **The money (outline).** Dane asks about money while he finds the injector pump. It's easy questions, the talky Dane: the bank, who she owes. She owes nobody a dollar, and she says so like a fact about the weather.
2. **The *Ruthie C* (her father's story; cost).** Because he asks, and because he's listening as if he wants her, she tells it. The bank took her father's boat on paper while he did everything right on the water, and she carried the bank man's papers out to his truck because her father asked her to. **This interior passage runs longer than the dialogue around it**, plain and physical. It pays Ch 03's "Don't write me down" without saying so. The picnic boat in one clause.
3. **The bag (outline: envelopes, who seals, who carries).** "Walk me through it" (the book's one use). Beth tells it as routine:
   - Envelopes, a slip inside, her name on the front in Tess's hand, the flap tucked and not sealed.
   - Tess bags them.
   - The boat carries the bag and the fare bag across, and the courier zips both into the pouch.
   - **His questions are a little too exact**: who sees the slip, whether the flap is ever sealed, who's alone with the bag. Beth hears the care in them. The reader may hear an interview (Dane's side).
4. **The tag (requirement 1; the longing).** He asks what keeps a bag shut.
   - She has a strip of spare fare-bag tags in her coat pocket, carried since Thursday, because she seals the *North Reach*'s fare bag after the last run.
   - She pulls one, loops it round his wrist and runs it tight with one pull. The beat is the wrist and the zip of the strap, not her fingers.
   - **The line (verbatim or near it, one line):** "Fare bag's red. That's the boat's. Tess seals hers yellow."
   - **The comic button:** her verdict, "Now you're the boat's." His reply is in his flirting register (a "for the record" line is available).
   - **The tamper-evident joke:** it only comes off with a knife, and "if it's off, I'll know." She has finally found one thing about him she can check.
   - **No mention of Ned, and no second color line.** The tag stays on his wrist.
5. **The unasked question (outline R3-5).** He asks one more about her envelope. She nearly says "Is this going in your log?" It's in her mouth, and she doesn't ask, because she wants the answer to be no. The narration says only that she didn't ask.
6. **Under the generator (generator bleed).** He's on his back on the pad under the set, and she's down beside him on one elbow, hip against his, talking him through the bleed screw: crack it, pump, wait for the air to stop spitting and the fuel to run clean. No hand on his hand (hands rested).
7. **The charge, unbroken (requirement 2).** Lying there, in her desire register (her one question this scene), she asks him whether the shed was the end of it.
   - Dane, quiet: "That's not a no. It's a not yet I can't afford."
   - Beth, flat, a verdict: "Then save up." It's her joke, and it makes the wait hers.
   - **Nothing stops this.** No phone, no log, no one arrives. The work goes on through it and the touch stays where it is.

**Subtext:** She tells him her father because she thinks he wants her. He asks about her envelope because Portland wants it. Both are true, and the tag on his wrist is the only thing in the shed either of them can check.

**Tension source:** Internal and interpersonal: intimacy taken as an interview, and an interview that is also intimacy. The reader knows more than Beth about why a deputy wants to know who's alone with a bag.

**Texture budget:** (1) the seal tag; (2) the bleed screw spitting air, then running clean. Nothing else.

**Ending type:** Cut mid-action.

**Ends on:** The fuel runs clean from the screw with no air in it. She says "Close it," and he does. The red tag shows on his wrist on the wrench. Cut there. No line after, no engine catching (Ch 03 has the catching).

**Word count target:** 800. **Floor:** 700. If long, trim item 1 to two exchanges. Never cut items 2, 4, 5 or 7.

## Scene 4 — Sunday dusk, the diesel

**Setting:** The foot of the long Ashby drive and the shed, Sunday dusk, the gale dropping. Ned has come up the north road with two cans of diesel from the *North Reach*'s day tank. Sensory anchor: the glug of diesel into the tank in the cold, and a flashlight beam going yellow in the dusk.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Ned.

**Surface action:**
1. Ned pours. Dane holds the light. Ned, without looking up: "You hold a light like nobody ever handed you one." (laugh; Dane is Ned's straight man)
2. **"Bethie"** (once). Ned answers with weather first, then the news: the pump on the *North Reach* is eating impellers, so he's going back aboard and staying aboard till the barge comes. That's his alibi, said plainly, and it reads in his favor. Dane hears it.
3. **No reefer lie** (Flag 1). If the ruling keeps it, it goes here as one over-detailed sentence, unremarked.
4. **The wheel, not asked (outline).** Ned says something about the *North Reach* taking water slow ("she'll keep"), and the words Beth has carried for a year are right there: put my name in. Her hundred-ton license is in her wallet inside her coat, its corner against her (**the license plant**). She doesn't say it. It's one interior beat, about her, not him.
5. Ned picks up the empty cans and goes down the drive.

**Subtext:** Beth wants something from the one man she never checks, and she won't ask for it, because asking means saying he's old. The reader sees an old man going back to his boat and a woman not asking. Nothing here argues for Ned or against him.

**Tension source:** Internal: Beth's want, unspoken. A small comic friction between Ned and Dane.

**Texture budget:** (1) the flashlight. Nothing else (no pump parts, no impeller detail).

**Ending type:** Image, with the plant.

## Scene 5 — Dead low, the gut

**Setting:** The gravel bar from the Ashby shore toward Calloway Cove, Monday (Day 4), dead low, early light between squalls. The bar is dry and firm, hers end to end. At its seaward end the gut, a dark trench where the bottom drops away. Sensory anchor: cobbles knocking under the wash, and the suck as it draws back.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.

**Surface action:**
1. **Why they're here, said once, in action:** Ashby has one road. She wants a back door: the bar, the cove and the fish house, and the only bad part is the gut. She has to find where it drops, now, while she can see the bottom.
2. **His rule against hers.** Dane walks every place a protectee goes before she goes there (bible), so he starts in. "These are waterproof."
   - **Beth, already going in: "That's a narrow definition of waterproof."** They kept Saturday's sea in till supper. (Primary laugh; boots 2 of 3; pays the soaked knee.)
   - "Stand there." He stands where she puts him at the edge of the dry bar, and doesn't walk it first. His rule broken, without a word.
3. **The wade.** She goes into the gut at its seaward end, feeling for the edge with her feet. She goes in to the waist, and swears, steadily and with variety, at the water, the cold and the island. The cold climbs (bible: painful, not dangerous at this exposure). She finds the lip where it drops and paces it back to a stride inside.
4. **Dane talks the whole time (his fear register):** low and continuous, so he knows where she is. She tells him to shut up so she can hear the water, and he does. It's their friction (talk against silence), and he gives way to hers.
5. **Out, and the charge (requirement 2; the longing).** She comes up out of the gut soaked to the ribs and shaking.
   - He opens his jacket and she walks into it. Her wet front against him, her face against Day 3's stubble, rough where she watched the razor go two days ago.
   - The red tag on his wrist is at the edge of her eye, at her back (the color seen once more, no comment).
   - **Nothing interrupts it: no phone, no log, no third person.** It lasts. Her interior runs physical and plain, and longer than anything said: the cold, then him.
6. **The plan.** Dane, into her hair: "What's the plan?" (He asks for the plan first; she always looks at it when she gets there. This time she has looked at it.)

**Subtext:** She builds the back door herself, because she trusts the bar and not the road, and maybe not the people who know the road. He lets her go where he can't, and holds her when she comes out. For the first time she hands him a plan.

**Tension source:** Situational (the gut, the cold). Interpersonal under it: his rule against hers, settled her way.

**Texture budget:** (1) the gut's lip, found by foot; (2) the boots. Nothing else.

**Ending type:** **Consequence (outline).** The plan exists now, and the next chapter has to use it.

**Ends on:** Beth, inside his jacket, still shaking, giving him the plan: **"Ninety seconds, if you don't stop. And you stay inside where I put you."** (verbatim; outline). That is the time along the gut at half tide in the dark, knee-deep. Then they go up the shore toward the house together, his jacket round both of them. No line after, and no stove; Ch 05 opens on the stove.

**Word count target:** 470. **Floor:** 400.
