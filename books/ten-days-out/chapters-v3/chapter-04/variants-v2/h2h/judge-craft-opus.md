Winner: B

**Persona:** Craft. **Model:** opus. **Read:** `chapters/chapter-04/variants-v2/h2h/A.md` and `B.md`, plus `docs/judging.md` and `docs/calibration.md`.

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B the people sound like different people. Dane talks in long, dry running commentary ("There's probably a form"; "from people who were annoyed with me"), Russ counts reasons, and Ned answers in four words. Beth also gets a want of her own, the license pressing on her ribs while she holds the funnel. In A everyone talks in the same clipped bark, so even its best sentences feel like one voice taking dictation.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Envelopes from the store. Slip inside. My name on the front in Tess's hand. Flap tucked. Never sealed." (~45%). That's a manifest, not a woman talking. It comes right after the *Ruthie C* paragraph, the best paragraph in either version, and the bag Q&A that follows ("Who's alone with it?" / "The boat." / "What keeps a bag shut?") is the couple doing business in code. A gets me back with the man on the spine and the stalled generator, which is the tensest scene of the two chapters. But A's Dane never gets a sentence longer than his breath, and by the Ned scene ("Nobody did.") I couldn't hear him over anyone else.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Who's emergency management on this island? Tess is. Since town meeting. Slow putting her hand down." (~35%). It's in quotes with no speaker, it answers its own question, and I had to go back and work out that this was Russ still talking. Then the bag interrogation sags the same way A's does, only longer: "Who sees the slip?" / "Tess. Me. The teller." / "Flap's never sealed? Not once?" / "Never." (~52%). Dane's jokes and the hip-to-hip pumping carry me through it, but it sags.

5. **Confidence: medium.** A has the better sentence-level rhythm ("Either one was true enough to lie down with. Neither one would settle.") and the generator standoff, which B cut. B wins on voice, humor and Beth's interior want, which is what this persona came for.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Whole lines are identical ("Good hull," "I'm not a complicated man," "Son of a bitch, she told it," the stubble since her father's razor). It's one writer cutting to two different lengths.
