1. **Winner: A.**

2. **Why:** In A, the two of them lie hip to hip on cold concrete, not breathing, while a man with binoculars stands up the drive. That's the moment I put the phone down and kept it in my hand. B gives that same scene a pump and a pulse in his forearm, and I liked it, but I wasn't scared for them.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Envelopes from the store. Slip inside. My name on the front in Tess's hand. Flap tucked. Never sealed." (~45%). For four lines it's an inventory and my thumb sped up. Then she ties the red tag on his bare wrist and his pulse goes "slow, then less slow" under her fingers, and I was back. It's a short dip and the tag moment pays for it.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Who's emergency management on this island? Tess is. Since town meeting. Slow putting her hand down." (~37%). I couldn't tell who was talking, so I read it twice and then skimmed the rest of Russ. Right after that came the bag ledger, which is longer here: "Who sees the slip?" / "Tess. Me. The teller." / "Flap's never sealed? Not once?" (~50%). That's two people doing paperwork. Dane is funnier in B ("You've tagged a federal employee. There's probably a form."), and the "Put my name in" ache with Ned is lovely. But the middle of B is where I'd check how much of the sample was left.

5. **Confidence: medium.** B's banter and the Ned longing nearly evened it out. A's embrace on the bar is fuller, though, and its generator scene has a real threat in it, and those two things are what a reader my age stays up for.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. They share whole lines ("Sold. Sorry.", "Good hull," "steadily and with variety"), so this is one writer working the same material twice.
