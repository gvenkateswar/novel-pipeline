Why: A's people sound like different people (Dane's "mostly at night, from people who were annoyed with me," Russ's "I'm not a complicated man"), and it keeps the geography physical, with the dark trench and the ledge she paces off. B sands some of that down and then pads the ending with a generic body-and-heartbeat paragraph.

2. Where A lost me: Dane's answer to Russ, "Thank you. No," Dane said, "because any boat on this island could be theirs, because I can't read this sea, and because whoever runs you across leaves a wake the whole bay can follow." (about 30%). It is a speech in a scene that wants a beat, and it sounds like a briefing. The second spot is the sentence that explains Calloway Cove at the start of the bar scene, "the cove on the east shore that carried her family's name, a long way from her house on the harbor" (about 82%). It stops the scene to give directions.

3. Where B lost me: "For a while there was only her body. Her legs had gone past aching into a far-off burning, as if they belonged to somebody standing a little way down the beach. Her heart went hard at her ribs. Against his, by slow degrees, it came down to his pace." (about 92%). It is the standard hypothermia-and-hug paragraph, and it repeats what "She shook. He held on." already did. B also says "She shook" twice, with the paragraph in between. Earlier, Dane's reply to Russ is trimmed to a clipped sentence and the "Nobody did" exchange loses its joke, so Dane starts to sound like Ned.

4. Confidence: medium. B has the best single line in either text ("thank you, dear, and took it from her as if she'd brought him a pie"), and its bank scene is slightly better written. A wins on voice, texture, and not over-explaining the ending.

5. Same-author check: Yes. It is the same chapter with the same beats, the same lines (Connecticut, "not yet," "Good hull"), and the same habit of repeating a good detail.
