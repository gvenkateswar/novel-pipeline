1. Why: A gave me the father handing over the box ("as if she'd brought him a pie") and a long, warm hug on the bar that I felt in my own legs. B trims both and leaves me with banter and mechanics.

2. Where A lost me: the diesel and Ned stretch at about 70-75%. "Glass is coming up. Be down to a breeze by morning, squalls in it. Pump on the *North Reach* is eating impellers." That's weather and boat-repair talk, and my thumb sped up until "Pull it." The hug paragraph at about 95% ("For a while there was only her body...") runs long, but I was still with them.

3. Where B lost me: about 55-60%. "One more stroke and the screw would run clean, and one pull after that the set would catch." It's the same fuel-line procedure, and the father story before it was cut short. I also felt the thin hug ("Her jeans weighed what the water weighed") as a step down. The bar scene is over quickly, and the closing plan line comes before I'd had time to feel anything.

4. Confidence: medium. B has the funnier lines ("That's three. I'd have taken any one of them." and "hit his head on the frame"), and a reader who wants quips could go the other way. I wanted the couple pressed together and the ache about her father, so I'd keep reading A.
