1. **Winner: A.**

2. **Why:** In A the two of them are pressed together on a cold slab behind the generator while the man stands a foot away with his bare hand on the steel, and I couldn't have put the phone down if I'd wanted to. Then the handprint in the frost at the end sent me straight to the next chapter. In B the man stays a dot up on the road, and the closest they get is in the middle of a conversation about envelopes.

3. **Where A lost me:** It nearly didn't. The thumb sped up once, at about 70%: "He asked the next thing the way he'd have asked for the wrench, with her still lying on him. 'Who's alone with it?'" A man just had his hand a foot above her head, and now we're auditing the fare bag. She's lying on him, though, so I stayed. The bank-man paragraph right after it ("So he asked Beth to carry the box of papers out...") is squeezed so tight I had to read it twice.

4. **Where B lost me:** At about 39%: "'Walk me through it,' he said. 'The bag.'" Then "Envelopes from the store. Slip inside. My name on the front in Tess's hand. Flap tucked. Never sealed." That's the couple doing business with a wrench in hand, and I skimmed it. The man on the spine shows up after that, but he never comes any closer than the road, so I didn't get scared. The wading scene and the embrace in his jacket won me back ("You're swearing at Connecticut"), and that's the best romance on either page. But it came after I'd already been skimming.

5. **Confidence: medium-high.** B's last scene is warmer than anything in A, but A's shed scene is the one I'd tell my sister about.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same hill, same lines, same "Then save up." It reads like one writer's two drafts of the same chapter.
