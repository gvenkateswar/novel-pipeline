Winner: A

1. Why: A puts a man's bare hand a foot above her head while they lie behind a cold generator and then pays it off with the handprint in the frost, so the sentences work for a reason. B has the better jokes ("swearing at Connecticut") but spends its middle on a quiz about the bag while the danger stands far up the drive.

2. Where A lost me: "Minutes ago a man's hand had been a foot from her head, and Dane wanted her envelope." (~58%). The who-sees-the-slip catechism ("Who's alone with it?" / "The boat.") runs straight after the scare and reads like procedure. Beth's own aside saves it, but my eye sped up for about six lines. A smaller snag is "Leather between teeth." (~45%), which is a fragment that doesn't say whose teeth.

3. Where B lost me: "Walk me through it," he said. "The bag." (~33%). The Q&A about the envelope, slip and seal runs through the middle of a scene with a man on the ridge. Even with "Pump's right there" as a dodge, it is the couple doing business. Later, "For a while there was only her body." (~92%) slows into a long paragraph of sensation, and the chapter stops on "you stay inside where I put you" with no landing.

4. Confidence: medium. A is the tighter and more physical chapter. B's third scene has real voice, and a reader who likes banter could go the other way.

5. Same-author check: Yes. Both share the same islander diction, the left heel dragging, the father's box carried to the truck, and the "not yet" exchange.
