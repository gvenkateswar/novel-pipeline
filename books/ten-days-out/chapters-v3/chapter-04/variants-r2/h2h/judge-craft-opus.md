2. **Why:** A puts both of them flat behind a dead engine with a stranger's bare hand a foot above her head and her fingers on Dane's mouth, and the sentences tighten to fit that. B gets its best voice in the wading scene and the Ruthie C memory, but it spends its middle scene going through how the fare bag works while the man stays far off up the drive.

3. **Where A lost me:** "She went down the list. 'Mine he's been through. The boat's under the dragger's nose. Fish house at the head of Calloway Cove's had the cove over the sill since Friday...'" (~60%). Right after the best stretch in either text she starts reading out a property inventory, and the Q&A that follows ("Who's alone with it?" / "Who sees the slip?" / "Who do you owe?") runs like a form while she's lying on him. The squeezed Ruthie C paragraph ("She gave him the *Ruthie C*. ... So he asked Beth to carry the box") reads like a summary of a better scene. I slowed down but I didn't stop. The frost handprint at the end got me back.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Envelopes from the store. Slip inside. My name on the front in Tess's hand. Flap tucked. Never sealed." (~40%). That's a ledger written as dialogue, with "Walk me through it. The bag." right in front of it. This is where my eyes started moving faster. The man on the spine is a long way off and nobody's in danger, so the scene is mostly diesel bleeding and procedure. The Connecticut swearing pulled me back in. Then it ends on another procedure: "Half tide, in the dark, the bar's knee-deep... Ninety seconds."

5. **Confidence:** medium. Some of B's lines are better than anything in A ("heavier than paper ought to be," "That's a narrow definition of waterproof," the legs "belonged to somebody standing a little way down the beach"), and its Beth sounds more like a person. But A's chapter holds together as one tightening scene with a payoff image. B's is three pieces, and the one in the middle is the kind of thing the author called boring.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Whole sentences are word for word the same in both ("the way a buyer walks a boat," "as if she'd brought him a pie," "Now you're the boat's").
