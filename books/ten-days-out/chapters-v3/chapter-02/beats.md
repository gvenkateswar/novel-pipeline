## Flags (read before drafting)

1. **Act 1 must end at or under 20%.** Ch 01 ran 3,113 words. This chapter is set at **2,850**, 50 under the outline. The s1 target drops from 450 to 400. If the draft runs over, cut s1 first (outline pacing flag).
2. **The skiff from Ch 01 is still live.** Ch 01 ends with the skiff tied empty at the fuel float, the man ashore, and Dane heading for the back of the house with his hand on his hip. **s1 opens on that threat and pays it off: the back of the house, not a deck chore.**
   - s1 is the back step. The skiff is gone from the float, and Beth finds it pulling out through the moorings until a squall takes it.
   - The threat doesn't go quiet in any scene. Every scene has a sign of him or of the dragger.
3. **Pike learns about the limping man in s1.** In Ch 01, Dane typed before Beth told him at the window. The s1 call (seen, not heard) is his report, and Pike's answer is Monday.
4. **The afterdeck figure (s5) builds on the skiff man.** Beth knows the way he sat, facing her hill. Standing on the dragger's afterdeck in the deck lights, he shows her no limp, and she can't tell whether it's him.
   - Don't confirm it. Ch 4 s1 gives his face and the row out to the dragger.
   - She may name the dragger as a Gloucester boat in for the weather. "*Margaret Ann*" is allowed once if Beth needs it. Arsenault stays off the page.
5. **R3-2 and R4-4, the watch staging, are binding.**
   - The truck comes **"a little after eleven"**, not "just before eleven." It has to come after Tess's lights are out (bible Open question 3, R4-2).
   - Dane goes out **the front, to the road side**, to report it.
   - The mudroom's outer door goes quiet **while he's out**: the man is inside the outer door, holding it, waiting for the house to settle.
   - Beth gets him in by the front, with no light. Both are behind the chimney.
   - Dane's one line on why he won't open an inner door on a man in the dark: **"He comes to us. Not the other way."**
   - At **11:40** the latch lifts, sticks, lifts. That is the only clock time in the chapter (World Rule 6).
6. **The "why they stay" line drops "a crippled boat everyone saw them leave."** They leave the boat by the shore in the dark specifically so nobody sees them, and the net has Beth "aboard." Use: the boat is crippled and open astern; the storm; the truck may be waiting up the road; the chimney and the gun are the best she has. One line.
7. **R4-2 on the page: Tess goes home.** In s5, house by house, Beth watches the store go dark and a flashlight go east along the Harbor Road to Tess's, then Tess's window lit and then dark, before the truck. This gives Tess's dooryard without naming the truck. The truck is **"a pickup with no lights."** No make, no lettering, no plow frame. Beth must not recognize it; Ch 5 is where she does.
8. **Dane must be out of earshot when Beth tells Ned "Home"** (s4). Otherwise his "Who knew you were here?" isn't a real question.
9. **Don't add weight to Ned (orchestrator instruction).**
   - The "me and God" exchange is played plain and warm, with no lingering and no narrated weight. Ned's report at the store, his phone and the back step all stay off the page.
   - There is no light at the store's back step in s5.
   - The outline's "She doesn't count Ned" **stays off the page as narration**. Her answer is "You.", and the last beat is the glow of his phone in the front yard. The reader does the counting.
10. **Boat thread: none in this chapter.** No Webbers Cove, no viewing, no yard, no "my boat," no "thirty-one." "Boat" means the *North Reach* only.
11. **Reader lessons from Ch 01, applied in every scene.**
    - No scene ends on a quip. The ending types vary; see the ending audit.
    - No gear-list paragraphs. The set piece (s3) puts each detail inside an action, never three items in one sentence.
    - Dane's charm comes from specific lines and from his silences. No "Here's how this goes," no "for the record," and "okay" twice at most in the chapter.
    - "Watching her hands" stays rested: Dane doesn't look at her hands in this chapter.
    - No narration that explains a moment the reader has just felt. Ch 01's "two people on a cold deck holding each other by the clothes" is the model of what not to do.
12. **The two storm-bound trucks** (the fire company's propane delivery truck and the co-op's bait truck) were loaded for the two o'clock and are left chained aboard when it's cancelled. One clause in s3 places them, with one lane of steel between them (→ Ch 9).
13. **Private boats:** Beth closes them with the sea in s1, in one line ("Nothing's crossing in this."). Dane's fuller reasons (any island boat could be theirs; the trail of who took her) wait for Ch 3, where bible note 34 allows them.
14. **Boat jargon is losing the buyer** (sample-read-ch01-r2: binders, D-rings, the dolphin, "master and one deck"). **Rule for this chapter:** the first time any trade term appears, the action shows what it means. Show the thing working or failing before, or instead of, naming it:
    - **The transfer bridge:** "the steel bridge the cars drive across from the shore onto the bow." Show it as a road that has just become wreckage.
    - **The forepeak:** "the space right up in the bow, below the car deck." Ned goes down into it and the water is coming in.
    - **The brow:** "the aluminum gangway." Show it as the plank they walk off on, grinding.
    - **The stone pier:** a granite pier beside the slip. Show it as where she throws the lines.
    - **"Sector":** don't use the word in this chapter. Ned says "the Coast Guard."
    - **No** binders, D-rings, dolphin, transom, quarter ladder or certificate language in this chapter. The stern is "the stern," "low and open to the water."
    - **Budget:** at most one trade term per scene that isn't plain English. No VHF channel numbers other than "nine," and that one is Ned's ("You need me, nine"), which explains itself as a radio channel by context. Add one clause if needed: her handheld lives on nine.
15. **Fairness: Tess has to carry equal weight soon** (sample-read-ch01-r2: after Ch 01 the buyer reads Ned as complicit). This chapter gives:
    - **One beat for Tess that reads as guilty on its own (s2):** the fold of hundreds. CASE FACTS: Tess sold night fuel for cash to the dragger on Day 0. Hold that true, and show nothing else.
    - **One beat for Ned that reads as innocent or protective on its own (s3 and s4):** he goes down into the flooding forepeak himself, and Beth knows he can't swim. At the brow he hands her his gloves without a word, as he does every winter.
    - **Label neither.** No interior line weighs either beat. Beth doesn't think "suspicious" or "kind." She just sees them.

### Scene 1 — Lift and shove
**Setting:** The cottage's back step, on the harbor side, just after the cancellation, early afternoon. The steep path drops to the Harbor Road and the store. The wind is making from the east, and the first of the snow is in it. Sensory anchor: the latch's iron clack.
**Characters:** Beth, Dane.
**Want:**
- Dane wants to know every way into this house before dark, and where the man went. In the way: the back door won't open for him.
- Beth wants the man in the skiff found, by her eyes, on her harbor. In the way: weather.
- The reader learns Dane's want when he tries the door a second time instead of asking her.
**Tension source:** situational. The man is ashore somewhere below them. Under it, interpersonal: she has just admitted she stepped over his track, and Dane hasn't said a word about it.
**Surface action:**
1. **Open on the threat paying off, mid-motion.** Dane is outside on the back step with his hand at his hip, working the thumb-latch, which won't open. He went straight here because a man is ashore. The first sentence is about him and the door, not about chores. Beth, inside, lifts the latch and shoves, and the door opens on him. "Lift and shove." He does it himself, badly, then right, then a third time without a sound. One line from Beth, the plant: nobody who has been in this house makes the noise he made the first time.
2. **The skiff (interior, longer than the dialogue).** From the step, the store and the fuel float below. The skiff is gone from the float. She finds it out among the moored boats, one man at the oars, pulling for the outer harbor, and a squall comes down across the water and takes it before it reaches any boat. She keeps looking at where it was. Dane says nothing; he has followed her eyes.
3. **The call, seen and not heard.** He makes it there on the step, under open sky, with the harbor behind him. She goes in, and through the kitchen glass she watches his mouth and can't hear him, which she minds.
4. He comes in with snow on his shoulders. Monday, one line and plain: the grand jury's last sitting before Pelletier's deadline is Monday, and they want her in front of it. Beth glances once at the subpoena under the barometer and doesn't reread it.
5. Private boats, closed in her line: "Nothing's crossing in this." Dane: tonight, then, somewhere with one way in from the land, a locked door and somebody awake. He doesn't name it. She does.

**Subtext:** He reported the man she left out, and both of them know it without saying so. She'd rather sleep on the boat than in her father's house with the marshal guessing at its doors.
**Texture budget:** (1) The latch, the one detail of the house given room, because s5 turns on it. (2) Monday, a single line. No tide talk, no gear, no boat terms.
**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** **Consequence, in one word.** Beth, decided at the edge of it: "Boat." Played flat, as a decision she made by looking at the harbor. No reply from him, and no reaction beat.
**Word target:** 400.

### Scene 2 — For the net
**Setting:** The store step at dusk, with the bell over the door and the store generator's flat roar out back. The harbor going dark below. Sensory anchor: the generator's roar, felt through the step.
**Characters:** Beth, Dane, Tess.
**Want:**
- Tess wants every islander's whereabouts for the evening storm net (the island's radio roll call, which she runs), Beth's first.
- Beth wants to get past the store to the boat without being asked anything.
- In the way: Tess is the one who runs the net, and nobody gets past Tess.
- The reader learns Tess's want with her question.
**Tension source:** small and social, with danger at the edge. Where Beth sleeps is about to become island knowledge. Out on the harbor the dragger's deck lights come on at dusk, though nobody is on her deck. And Tess has something in her pocket she doesn't want seen.
**Surface action:**
1. Tess on the step with her pencil through her glasses, in headlines: the net's at six, so who's where, starting with Beth. "Where you sleeping, Calloway?" / "On the boat."
2. Tess writes it on the back of her left hand in ballpoint, *CALLOWAY — BOAT*, and blows on the ink. Beth watches her name go down on somebody's skin. One beat.
3. **Tess's guilty-on-its-own beat (Flag 15).** Tess digs in her red vest for the pen cap, and a fold of bills comes up with it, hundreds on the outside, more than the store takes in a week in November. She has it back in the pocket before the cap is on. Then she gets brighter and busier, her stress register: a price, a headline, a door held open.
   - Beth sees it, and so does Dane. Neither says anything. No interior verdict.
   - Don't tie it to the fuel float or the dragger in the text. The reader has Ch 01's float, lit late, and does the arithmetic.
4. One Tess line to Dane, in her own register (a price or a title), not a Beth-style verdict. Something only Tess would say, e.g. on the net he's "the marshal, aboard," which costs nothing to say and two dollars to hear.
5. Out on the harbor, a dragger on the visitors' mooring, a Gloucester boat in for the weather, has her deck lights come on. Dane looks at it and not at Tess. "That one?" / "In for the weather." No more than that.

**Subtext:** Being on the net is how the island keeps you safe, and tonight it's a cover. She doesn't yet see that it's also a list, kept by a woman with a pocket full of somebody's hundreds.
**Texture budget:** (1) The net, explained in a clause by its use, and Tess's hand as its log. (2) The fold of hundreds. Nothing else.
**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** **Cut.** Tess going in with her inked hand held out to dry, the bell swinging behind her. (Prop ending, 1 of 2.)
**Word target:** 300. The cash beat adds about 40; take them from the Dane/Tess line, which may shrink to a clause.

### Scene 3 — The bridge (set piece)
**Setting:** The *North Reach* in the island slip, about two hours before the top of a surge tide, full dark, the storm on. Sensory anchor: the steel bridge from the shore grinding on its pilings at every sea.
**Characters:** Beth, Dane, Ned.
**Want:**
- Beth wants the lines doubled before the top of the tide, and she wants it done herself.
- Dane wants her inside. She won't go, so he stays on the bow with her, in the wrong boots.
- Ned wants his boat off the slip before the bridge eats her.
- The reader learns Beth's want when she walks forward past the two trucks.
**Tension source:** situational, high. The sea and the steel. Then the shared danger turns into the longing.
**Surface action (each detail inside an action; trade terms shown before or instead of named, Flag 14):**
1. **Open in motion.** She goes forward down the one lane of steel between the two trucks left chained for the two o'clock that never ran (one clause). The surge is lifting the whole boat against the steel bridge the cars cross on from the shore, and the bridge is grinding on its pilings.
2. A cable that holds the bridge up parts with a shriek and whips across the bow. Dane takes her down flat under the steel rail, her fist in his collar. In order: steel against her cheek, his weight, and his voice in her ear going on low the whole time, telling her where the cable went and where he is. Her dialogue: none. Her interior turns to sound, in the order it arrives.
3. **The longing (one or two sentences).** After the cable is still, neither of them moves for the length of a sea, and her fist is still in his collar. Don't name it.
4. The bridge comes down on the bow, twisted, half in the water: the road off the island, wreckage on her boat. Beth is up first. The boat has to come off the slip before the bridge grinds through her, so she throws the lines to the granite pier beside the slip. Dane on a line, pulling the wrong way: "Other way. Unless you want to go with it." Ned works her off and alongside the pier.
5. **The island goes dark.** The power goes in the same surge. The lights along the Harbor Road go out, and the generators start up one by one along the shore. Out on her mooring the dragger's deck lights don't even blink.
6. **Ned's innocent-on-its-own beat (Flag 15), and the laugh.**
   - Ned goes down the hatch into the space up in the bow under the car deck himself, with a flashlight, because the water's coming in there.
   - One clause in Beth's head: Ned can't swim, and only she knows it.
   - Beth goes down after him. Ned is up to his shins, the pumps going, and he's looking at the cracked plating like it's weather.
   - "Well. That's the bridge." / Dane, from the hatch: "Does it do that a lot?" / "Just the once."
   - Dane asks when she'll run. Ned: "She's not carrying anybody till somebody from the Coast Guard has his hand on that." He'll call it in to the Coast Guard tonight, one clause. "Ten days, if the state gets a barge out."
   - Dane, at the hull: at least she's steel. Ned: "Steel doesn't burn. Everything in it does."

**Subtext:** For one second, when the bridge came down, she was glad she didn't have to leave (bible: what she fears he'll find out). It stays in her body: one physical beat, never stated. Dane has been under a rail with a protectee before, and he talks the way he does because of it.
**Texture budget (set piece; cap six; each item in motion, never in a list):**
- (1) The bridge grinding, then the cable.
- (2) The lines to the granite pier.
- (3) The power and the generators, with the dragger lit through it.
- (4) The water in the bow.
- (5) Ned's Coast Guard line.
- The two trucks are a plant clause, not texture. No pump specs, no knots, no tonnage, no "forepeak" or "transfer bridge" unless the meaning is already on the page.
**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** **Consequence, an image.** Beth climbs out of the bow onto the dark deck. The whole island is black except for generators coming on one window at a time, and the dragger lit end to end on her mooring as if nothing had happened. **Don't end on Ned's line.** "Steel doesn't burn" sits mid-scene, followed by at least two sentences of action.
**Word target:** 900. **Word floor:** 800 (protective: a set piece and the chapter's turn).

### Scene 4 — Me and God
**Setting:** The stern of the *North Reach*, then the aluminum gangway from her side to the granite pier, at about twenty past ten (no clock). Then the shore path in the dark, then the iced back steps on the harbor side. Sensory anchor: the gangway's wheels grinding on the stone each time the boat surges.
**Characters:** Beth, Dane, Ned.
**Want:**
- Dane wants her off a crippled boat with an open stern and into a house the island thinks is empty, unseen.
- Beth wants Ned to know where she is. She always has, and she never thinks of it as telling.
- The reader learns Dane's want with "Anyone with a skiff."
**Tension source:** situational: the open stern; the walk in the dark with the man somewhere ashore. Interpersonal, quietly: Dane overrules Ned on Ned's own boat.
**Surface action:**
1. At the stern, which sits low and open to the black water behind a chain. Dane: "Anyone with a skiff." / Ned: "Nobody's coming over that stern in this." Dane gives his reason politely: the island has her on this boat, so the dark house is the place nobody's looking. ### Scene 5 — The watch
**Setting:** Beth's dark kitchen, from about a quarter to eleven to a quarter to midnight (only 11:40 as a clock time). No lamp, no stove. The island must think the house empty. **Want:**

   - who listens on a scanner;
   - who'll have the two of them married by Tuesday;

   - **R4-2, in the same run:** the store goes dark, then a flashlight goes east along the Harbor Road to Tess's, then Tess's window is lit, then dark. "Tess. Bed. Late, for her."

   Her interior runs longer than his lines here. The door knocks.
3. **The laugh and the longing.** He says he knows which house talks about her most. She waits. "You. Since this morning." She tries not to laugh and fails without a sound, her face in the blanket. His knee against hers. One beat, not explained.
4. **The truck.** A little after eleven, a pickup climbs the Harbor Road with no lights, from the village end, idles under the house on the road side, and goes on. Neither breathes. Dane's voice is the only thing moving: very low, very little. This is the one stretch in the chapter where he goes nearly silent, and Beth notices.
5. **Why they stay (one line):** the boat crippled and open astern; the storm; the truck maybe waiting up the road; the chimney and the gun are the best she has.
6. **The yard call, seen.** He goes out the front to the road side to report it: his boss has to hear, and the phone needs open sky. Through the front-room glass she sees only the faint glow under his chin. His back is to the road the truck took, and the harbor side of the house is behind him.
7. **The door goes quiet (interior, longer than anything said).** The mudroom door, which has knocked in every gust all night, stops. She knows what it means: somebody is inside the outer door, holding it, waiting for the house to settle. Fear register: she listens, and the sounds arrive in order (her father's clock, the wind on the clapboards, nothing from the mudroom).
8. She goes to the front door without a light and gets him in by the sleeve. At his ear: "Don't talk. Door's gone quiet."
9. **Behind the chimney.** His hand goes to the gun. He talks now, low and continuous, in the nouns of her house (where he is, where she is, the brick at her back, her father's clock), and once: "He comes to us. Not the other way."
10. **11:40.** The latch lifts, sticks, lifts: the sound nobody who has been in this house makes. Dane goes to it. The outer door bangs. Someone runs down the back path toward the harbor. Dark scuffs on the white of the iced steps, seen by sky-light. (No heel detail; that is Ch 3's opening.)
11. Dane comes back from the door. "Who knew you were here?" / "You."

**Subtext:** She has been happy for an hour, in the dark, with him, and that is exactly why the answer comes out of her so fast. He doesn't defend himself: a hand that stops on the blanket's edge and then goes back to the gun. That silence is his tell and his care at once.
**Texture budget:** (1) The mudroom door's knock and then its quiet. (2) The latch. The truck and the deck lights are events, not texture.
**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** **Question (unease), on an image, after the exchange.** After "You.", one beat only: she sees again the glow under his chin in the front yard, a few minutes before the latch lifted. Stop there. No narrated question, no thought about anyone else, no reply from Dane.
**Word target:** 900. **Word floor:** 800 (the end of Act 1; the first attack, in real time).
