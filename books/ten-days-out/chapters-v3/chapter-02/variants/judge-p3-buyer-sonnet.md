# Judge: Buyer (sonnet), p3-A vs p3-B

1. Winner: B

2. Why: B lets the two of them be people in a room and in a cold wind (the gray at his temples "at sixty... somebody would get to see it", "You're very good at lying down", "Like the dog"), where A keeps handing me tactical staging with the warmth held back.

3. Where A lost me: "Then tonight I want one way in from the land, a door that locks, and somebody awake behind it." followed by "One way aboard from the shore. A wheelhouse door that locked. Ned, who never slept in weather." (~15%). It's a security checklist, and I started going faster. The net-control roll call that follows ("Roll call by house. Calloway.") is a second stretch of logistics (~22%), though the Margaret Ann click saved it a little.

4. Where B lost me: "This boat's got a hole in the front and no back door. Anyone with a skiff." (~50%). It's the same security reasoning the chapter already did in the kitchen, so I'd heard it. Tess's "D batteries are up a quarter till the boat runs" (~25%) is store-inventory chatter that slowed me, but the money in her vest pulled me back.

5. Confidence: medium. The second half is nearly word for word the same in both. The call comes down to the first half and the cable monologue.

6. Same-author check: Yes. The prose is the same underneath, with the same mudroom door, the same Allegedly bit and the same ending.
