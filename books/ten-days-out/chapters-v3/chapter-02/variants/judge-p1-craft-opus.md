# Judge: Craft persona, opus. p1-A vs p1-B (Ch 02, Ten Days Out)

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** In A the people sound more like themselves. Tess gets "Like the dog," Dane gets "I'd like to be wrong about it from a distance" and "You're very good at lying down. Best thing you've done all day." B tells me what A trusts me to catch: Dane says the hundreds out loud, and Beth's relief is explained after the bridge falls.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Below her the ferry lay in the slip with the bow shoved up under the bridge, the steel ramp the cars drove aboard on, hung from two towers at its seaward end." (~20%) This is a diagram of how a ferry ramp works, and it sits right where scene two should be picking up speed. A smaller snag later: "Dane said it politely, to the chain" (~55%). It's the second "politely" in two scenes, and it's starting to become a habit of his.

4. **Where B lost me:** "One way down to the boat from the land. A wheelhouse door that locked. Ned awake aboard in every blow since Beth was a girl." (~17%) She ticks off his conditions one by one, like a checklist, where A let the decision run through her father's chair and the door that took three tries. B then loses me again with "'That was hundreds,' Dane said to the glass. 'Where does a store on an island take in hundreds in November?'" (~25%), which says out loud the clue I had already seen. And after the bridge: "as if the thing she had been braced for had come, and fallen, and was done" (~45%). That's the narrator explaining a feeling A shows in five words: "Her shoulders came down."

5. **Confidence: medium.** From the blanket on the floor to the end, the two are nearly word for word the same, so the whole choice rests on the first 45%. B does have real advantages. Tess's storm-price rant ("when the boat runs or when I die, whichever's first") is great. "I'm sorry it's a brick" beats A's "I'm sorry about the floor." And B's longing line, about touching the gray in his hair, is more physical than A's "at sixty" thought. But A's people are funnier and its sentences are tighter.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** The second halves are the same text, and both have the same "lift and shove" sentence music.
