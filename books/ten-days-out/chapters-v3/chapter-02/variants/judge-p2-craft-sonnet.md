2. Why: A sets up Tess's radio voice on the air (the "Allegedly" and the two clicks from the dragger), so Beth's later impression of her pays off. B only reports the net and puts Tess on the doorstep with a clue.

3. Where A lost me: "Your rope's under my hand. Line. Your line's under my hand, and it's staying there." (~38%). Dane's talking-her-through-it speech stumbles here, with the self-correction and the repeat. It's the one place the rhythm goes wrong. I still read on.

4. Where B lost me: "a fold of bills came up with it, hundreds on the outside. It was back in the pocket before the cap was on." (~18%), followed by Dane saying to the glass, "Where does a store on an island take in hundreds in November?" The author is pointing at the clue, and Dane saying it out loud is a detective in a book talking. A smaller one is "she wanted to put her fingers there and find out whether it was as soft as it looked." (~8%), which states the want outright. A's version has her looking at the gray and "She stood there finding it." That shows it and is better.

5. Confidence: medium. The chapters are about 85% the same text, and the contest is mostly the Tess scene and a few lines. A's choices are the more distinctive ones. They are the radio net, "She," Beth said, the dragger's two clicks and Ned laying the boat alongside "as if it were June." B is clean but flatter.

6. Same-author check: Yes. They share the same chapter skeleton and the same lines, such as "Steel doesn't burn" and the knocking door. They read as variants of one draft. Neither is the clipped, procedural A1 style.
