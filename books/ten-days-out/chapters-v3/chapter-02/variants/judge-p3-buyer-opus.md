# Judge: Buyer persona, opus — p3-A vs p3-B (Ch 02, Ten Days Out)

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B I get Tess on the pier in person, with the names inked on her hand, the roll of hundreds and "Like the dog," and I get Beth thinking he'll look like that at sixty. A gives me the same island over a radio, call signs and all, so B is the one that kept me in bed with the phone.

3. **Where A lost me:** "He was still watching when the handheld on Beth's coat opened up, and she thumbed it off nine to the net. / *'Cutter storm net, this is Marr's, net control. Roll call by house. Calloway.'*" (~22%). It's radio procedure, keying and channels, and the two of them are doing business. I skimmed from there to the cable. The "Margaret Ann" clicks were the one thing that pulled me back. A smaller wobble came earlier, in his speech under the cable: "Your rope's under my hand. Line. Your line's under my hand" (~38%). He sounds like he's reading out a checklist, not like a man lying on top of her.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Dane looked at the chain. 'This boat's got a hole in the front and no back door. Anyone with a skiff.'" (~52%). After the bridge falls, the walk aft to look at the stern chain is security logistics, and my thumb moved faster until they were walking home by the shore. It's short, though, and the kitchen floor won me straight back.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The last 45% is close to word for word the same in both, and it's the best part of either. The whole call comes down to the middle: Tess in the flesh beats Tess on the radio, and B's patter under the cable ("You're very good at lying down. Best thing you've done all day.") beats A's checklist.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Whole paragraphs match sentence for sentence. The differences are one writer's second draft.
