## Flags (read before drafting)

### Needs a ruling (staging change, no plot change; nothing in the outline was edited)

1. **The Tess–Beth fight puts outline s1 in the store instead of the Ashby drive.** Two of the required lines only work at Tess's counter: "I can see your steps from right here, Beth" (the counter looks up at Beth's back steps) and the cot blankets going back under the counter. So **S2 is at Marr's at about half past one**, and Tess's two o'clock trip to Ashby happens because she drives Beth and Dane back up the north road with the two pots. The outline's facts all stay true:
   - Tess sees the Ashby smoke at noon on her rounds. Beth sees the truck stop on the spine (S1).
   - Two pots, one for them and one for Ned. "He hasn't come off that boat since he brought you the diesel."
   - The truck is in the Ashby drive at two with chowder, so Beth knows its shape at ten.
   - Ned's noon visit is off the page, and finished before Beth and Dane reach town.

   **Cost:** Beth goes to town by the north road in daylight on Day 4. S2 frames it as hiding. She goes to ask Tess to keep Ashby off the air. That keeps the s4 reversal ("Tomorrow I go to the store and buy what I need, and I watch who watches me buy it") working: today she went to ask to stay hidden, and tomorrow she goes to be seen.
   **Fallback if the ruling is no:** the fight happens in the Ashby drive at two with the pots and blankets on the truck seat. Then "I can see your steps from right here" becomes "I could see your steps from my counter," and the blankets go back behind the seat. Both required lines would change, so I haven't drafted it that way.

Dane waits **out front**, on the store dock, where Beth can see him through the front window. ## Scene 1 — Ashby, the stove

**Setting:** The Ashby kitchen, Monday (Day 4), from the morning after the gut to noon. The woodstove Dane lit is going hard. Squalls come and go past the sheeted windows, and between them the air goes dead still. Sensory anchor: her jeans and socks steaming on the stove rail, with the smell of wet wool cooking.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.

**Want:**
- Beth wants to be warm, and then she wants to be unseen. The stove gives her the first and costs her the second.
- Dane wants her to stop shaking.
- The reader learns Beth's want when she says "Smoke."

**Surface action:**
1. **Open (outline):** her clothes steaming on the rail. She's in his fleece, sleeves past her hands, feet on the oven door. He feeds the stove in a summer family's kitchen: one split, then another. Her interior runs physical and plain, longer than anything said: the heat coming back into her legs from the outside in, and him crouched at the firebox door.
2. Between squalls, through a gap in the window sheet, she sees the smoke go straight up off the chimney in the dead air, one gray stalk the whole island can read.
3. Beth: "Smoke." Dane, his line (whole sentence, warm): she was blue an hour ago, so the stove stays lit till she isn't. Then he puts on another split. **This is the Dane-side clue.** She sees it as care. The page doesn't comment.
4. **Noon.** From the window she watches a truck come over the spine on the north road, with the propane rack catching the light. It stops at the top and sits there with the engine running, facing Ashby. Then it goes on down the east-shore fork, on its rounds.
5. One interior beat: she knows that truck, she knows who's driving it and what she'll bring when she's done her rounds, and she knows where Tess writes things down.

**Subtext:** He's taking care of her in the most visible way there is. She lets him, because she likes it, and that's the mistake she sees too late.

**Tension source:** Situational. A signal fire for the whole island, lit by the man paid to hide her.

**Texture budget:** (1) the smoke straight up in dead air. Nothing else.

**Point of view:** Beth.

**Ends on:** **Consequence, seen.** The truck goes on down the fork, and the smoke still stands straight up over the roof behind her. Beth takes her jeans off the rail, still damp, and starts putting them on. No line after.

**Word target:** 180. (Cut to 120 first if the pre-scene total runs over.)

## Scene 2 — Marr's: the two pots

**Setting:** Marr's General Store, Monday (Day 4), about half past one. Tess is back from her rounds, and the store is empty: the gale is down to squalls and everybody is home feeding a stove. The store generator roars out back, coming through the office curtain. The storm log lies open on the counter. **Two cot blankets are folded on the counter by the till** before anyone says a word. Through the front window Dane stands on the store dock, where Beth told him to wait, out of earshot under the generator. Sensory anchor: Tess ladling chowder out of the big pot on the woodstove into two smaller pots, steam on the cold front window, and the store's cold, which she keeps under 58.

**Characters present:** Beth, Tess. Dane is outside, seen through the glass; he has no lines until the last beat, and isn't in the room.

**Want:**
- **Beth** wants Tess to keep Ashby off the air, off her hand and out of her book. In the way: Tess already knows, and she's hurt.
- **Tess** wants Beth under her roof tonight, where she can see her. In the way: Beth hid from her.
- The reader learns Tess's want before she speaks (the blankets) and has it confirmed at "You'll sleep at mine."
- **The single cause of Tess's anger, said on the page by Tess:** "I kept you off the air, and you let me find you by your smoke." Wording is the writer's, but the content and the plainness are binding. Every later line of the fight comes back to it.

## Scene 4 — The fish house (intimate scene 1)

**Setting:** Alton Calloway's fish house at the head of Calloway Cove, Monday night after eleven. The oil-drum stove on the first match. The kerosene lantern turned low.
