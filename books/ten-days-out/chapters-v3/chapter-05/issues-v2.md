**Issue 1**
- Problem: Tess says "he brought you the diesel," but nothing on the page gives her a way to know Ned took diesel to Ashby. The bible has Ned holding Ashby and Tess "Not Ashby" until the smoke. The line also undercuts her own cause, "you let me find you by your smoke." If she knew Ned had been out to Beth, she did not find Ashby by smoke. The bible's CASE FACTS carry this exact line, so the conflict is built in.
- Location: "He hasn't come off that boat since he brought you the diesel. I carry it down." and, a few lines on, "Sat on it since Friday, Calloway. And you let me find you by your smoke."
- Suggested fix: Make the clause something Tess can know without being told, for example "since Sunday, when I saw him hauling cans up the north road." Or keep the line verbatim and flag it to the plot auditor as an open who-knew question for Ch 09.

**Issue 2**
- Problem: The day word is missing. The beat sheet requires "Monday" once in 5.1, and the continuity log's Master flag says to keep weekday names where a scene opens. 5.1 never says Monday. The first "Monday" in the chapter is Dane's "Monday's the twenty-third," in 5.4, where it means the following Monday. A reader has to infer that it is still Day 4 from "Yesterday a man had stood on the spine."
- Location: "Between squalls the air had gone dead. She put her cheek to a gap in the sheet over the window."
- Suggested fix: Add "Monday" to that sentence, for example "Between squalls the Monday air had gone dead."

**Issue 3**

- Location: "Yesterday a man had stood on the spine with binoculars on this point. Neither of them said so." and "Beth said nothing. On the corner Dane held his place."
- Suggested fix: Replace each with an action. For the first, put Beth's eyes on the spine road. For the second, give her hand a task, for example her thumb going to the pot's towel.

**Issue 4**

- Location: "A shape on it, lights off, black against the glow. The plow frame. The rack. A dark cab." (5.2), against "Beth kept the shape of it as it went: high and square at the back, the rack, the cab sitting up on its springs." (5.1)
- Suggested fix: In 5.1, when the truck comes down the drive, add the plow frame on its nose, for example "the plow frame on its nose."

**Issue 5**

The continuity log's Master flag asked Ch 05 to "make the rule plain in a clause." The reader gets that Tess's phone works and Dane's does not, but not why or where. The bible says the charger is under the hook in the office, not on a shelf.
- Location: "His phone lives on my shelf, only place on this island a cell phone's a phone, and does he come up for it?"
- Suggested fix: Add a place to Tess's line, for example "on my shelf at the store," or add one clause of Beth's interior after "Dane's phone talked to the sky and nothing else" tying it to the store's booster.

**Issue 6**
- Problem: A run of touch sentences breaks the heat rule "never more than two sentences in a row about who is touching what." The hem and wrist paragraph has six sentences about hands in a row. Later, one dialogue line is the only break in a four-sentence run.
- Location: "His hand came to the hem of her father's sweater and stopped there. It waited. She could feel it waiting, the warm backs of his fingers against her hip through the wool. She let it wait. Then she took his wrist and moved his hand under, onto the bare skin over her ribs. She held it there until his fingers spread." Also: "He came up the cot to her. She pulled him down alongside her and got her hand round him again." followed by "\"Show me,\" she said. He put his hand over hers and showed her. Then he took it away and let her."
- Suggested fix: Put one non-touch beat inside each run. For the first, put the stove or her breathing between "She let it wait" and "Then she took his wrist." For the second, move "Show me" to open its own paragraph with a sound or a thought before his hand moves.

**Issue 9**
- Problem: The fire company arrives about two hours after the fire. The shed goes up "near ten." The scene then runs the crossing, the lighting of the stove, and the whole 5.3, and 5.4 is "near midnight." A volunteer company with one engine on a small island would not take that long to answer a fire it could see from the village. The bible has the island knowing Ashby burned by Ch 06, but the lateness reads as an error.
- Location: "Past it, up on the north point, the fire company's lights were coming up the north road, red and turning."
- Suggested fix: Add a clause explaining the delay, for example the engine stuck at the tower cut, or "the first the island had seen of the glow once the squall cleared." Or move 5.4 earlier by dropping "near midnight."

**Issue 10**
- Problem: The kettle image is unclear as the "too late" ending. The beat asks for the smoke to thin as a consequence, seen. Setting a kettle down in the coals would not cut the smoke. The reader cannot tell whether she is choking the stove, boiling water, or smothering the fire.
- Location: "She lifted the stove lid and set the kettle down in the coals. Over the roof the smoke thinned, leaned, and went to nothing much."
- Suggested fix: Make the action one that visibly chokes the fire, for example she shuts the damper or fills the firebox with ash.
