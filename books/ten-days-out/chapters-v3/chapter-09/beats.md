### Scene 1 — THE BAIT
**Setting:** The *North Reach*'s wheelhouse, Friday (Day 8), an hour and more past dark, the boat starboard side to the stone pier with the brow running up to it. The sky has cleared hard out of the northwest, and the stars are out over the harbor. The car-deck floods are on below: two trucks chained in their light, one lane of steel between them. The wheelhouse is dark, and nobody has lit it all evening. Up the Harbor Road the store's windows are yellow on its generator. Sensory anchor: the cold coming off the wheelhouse windows, and the hiss of the fixed VHF set's carrier on 68.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Tess (her voice on 68).
**Surface action:**
1. Open on the transmission, no lead-in. Beth keys the wheelhouse set on 68, where the storm net and the store listen. **"Tess. It's me. On the boat tonight, by myself. Ned's ashore for supper."** She's a bad liar out loud: it comes out too short, and she lets go of the key and goes straight to a task (she coils the set's cord, or turns the squelch down a hair). No narration about the lie.
2. **Tess's pause (the clue).** Tess answers everything on the net in headlines, at once. This time the carrier opens, hisses, and nothing comes. Render it physically: the open carrier and the length of it. Behind it, faintly, the store door's bell rings once. Then: **"Okay, Calloway."** Not "Bethie" (bible rule 1). Beth hangs the mic up. Don't explain the pause; the reader can put Arsenault at the counter or not.
3. **Dane at the chart table, in his anger register: very polite, slow, sentences longer than hers.** He was there for every word of it. "Ms. Calloway" (the one use in the chapter). He says this once: Pike's order this morning was to hold her in the most defensible place he has, and she has just told everyone with a radio that the most defensible place has nobody in it but her. One sentence, his shape, a dry turn at the end.
4. **Beth's reason, one line, conclusion first:** they catch a man coming for her, and the nine thousand dollars is theirs, and everyone in Portland can see it. Keep it short. (Outline: "a man caught coming for her explains the $9,000.") Don't say "nine thousand" here; s6 has the one use. "The money" is enough.
5. **The danger, on the page.** Across the harbor the *Margaret Ann*'s skiff comes off her side, an outboard and no running light, and runs down the harbor toward the pier. Beth sees it first, by its wake catching the store's light. It slows astern of the ferry and idles a boat's length off the open stern, low on the black water, one hooded shape at the tiller facing the boat.
6. **She acts.** She's out the wheelhouse door and down the outside ladder (boots on, rungs iced) and aft along the car deck into the full light of the floods, alone, exactly as she said on the air. She works the stern chain across the transom, takes up its slack, checks the hook on its eye, her back to the skiff. **Interior passage 1:** plain, physical, the light hot on her face in the cold, the skiff's idle in her spine, and yesterday's shot on the ramp head that found the two of them as one shape. Her mind runs to the chain and the hook. Dane stays above in the dark wheelhouse with the gun, because the lie says she's alone. She doesn't look up at him, and he can't talk to her across that distance. Keep that silence physical: the one sound is the outboard.
**Subtext:** She has made herself the bait, and she proves it with her body before she's sure it will work. Dane can't hold her this time: he's above, in the dark, by her plan, watching her stand where he was shot. The man at the tiller sees a woman alone at an open stern. He's seeing what Ned has already told them is a trap (the reader learns that in s6).
**Tension source:** Situational. An armed boat a boat's length away, and a woman with her back to it in the light. Interpersonal underneath: she has overruled Pike and Dane both.
**Ends on:** Consequence, seen. A long sentence, at least 25 words. The skiff's engine comes up and it goes on toward the store dock, and its wake runs in under the stern and lifts the deck under her and slaps hollow against the transom, and the chain in her hands goes taut with it and slack again. Don't end on a look, a line or a thought.
**Who knew:** On the air, everyone: Beth alone aboard, Ned ashore. Tess heard it at the counter, with (unseen) Arsenault there. Tess knows only the bait (CASE FACTS who-knew, Day 8).
**Texture (2):** the open carrier on 68; the stern chain's hook on its eye.
**Word count target:** 420 (floor 400).

### Scene 2 — POSITIONS
**Setting:** The head of the brow, Friday night, a little before nine, on the ebb toward the night low. With the water going, the boat has dropped and the brow runs steep and iced from the pier down to the gate (Ned's "Rail'll freeze hard by nine" is true). The pier's single light, the bollards capped with ice. Sensory anchor: the brow's wheels grinding on the granite, and the rail's ice under her glove.
**Characters present:** Beth, Russ, Dane.
**Surface action:**
1. Russ comes down along the riprap and up onto the pier, then down the steep brow, slow, his hand on the iced rail. His brass badge is on the outside of his coat where the pier light finds it, as she asked (Ch 08 s6). He takes his cap off and puts it back on (his buying-time habit, once).
2. **The laugh.** Russ: **"Ned says you need a constable. I said I'd look for one."** He looks round the car deck, at Dane, at her, and answers himself: **"Found one."** Tag both lines.
3. **Russ's Ch 03 question, paid.** Russ: "Where you sleeping tonight?" Beth: "Not sleeping." (First time she answers him. Two words, tagged.) Russ takes it with a slow nod. He doesn't add "because somebody will."
4. **Positions, given by Beth, conclusion first. Dane holds them; it's her boat.** Russ on the pier at the foot of the brow by the bollard, badge in the light, so anybody looking from the land sees a constable on it. Dane on the car deck in the dark lane between the two trucks, watching the open stern. Beth in the dark wheelhouse with the handheld on nine, and the outside ladder is her way down. One added rule: **"Floods go, it's me."** (She'll tell them nothing more.) Dane takes his position with one fuller sentence that is his own, about where he'll be and what he'll do if the stern moves. Give him a "because" in it, and a dry turn at the end of the sentence.
5. **Russ at the ladder (the second laugh, small).** Beth glances at the iced outside ladder, and Russ follows the glance. He asks whether he looks like a man who goes up ladders, then answers that he doesn't: he'll take the bollard. Then he starts on another question, something about how long a man is meant to stand on a bollard in November, with its answer ready.
**Subtext:** She's setting a trap shaped like her boat. The land side looks guarded and lit, so whoever comes will come from the water, where she knows every fitting in the dark. Dane, the man who wants the plan before the door, is taking a plan from her and standing where she put him. Russ is the badge she wouldn't wave down in Ch 07, used now on purpose.
**Tension source:** Low and comic, with the cold under it. Three people agreeing to a night none of them can see the end of.
**Ends on:** Cut mid-exchange. Russ is halfway through answering his own question about the bollard when the scene stops. No line closes it.
**Who knew:** Russ came because Ned sent him (Ch 08 s6). Ned knows Russ's position the way he knows his own boat. Nobody on the page says so.
**Tide check:** Day 8 low about 10:55 p.m. By about nine it's two-thirds ebbed: the brow is steep, and the riprap is out of the water below the pier face. Nobody goes along the shore under the bank in Ch 09.
**Texture (1–2):** the steep iced brow at the ebb; the badge on the outside of the coat.
**Word count target:** 315 (floor 300).

### Scene 3 — DANE (the chapter's one Dane section; it settles nothing)
**Setting:** The car deck, toward eleven. Dane in the dark lane between the fire company's propane delivery truck and the co-op's bait truck, one lane of steel between them, his back against the bait truck's rear wheel and his right side clear. The floods light the stern beyond him and leave the lane black. Overhead the stars, the sky clear, the harbor gone flat calm under the northwest air. Sensory anchor: the cold of the steel deck coming up through his jeans, and herring on the bait truck in the cold.
**Characters present:** Dane (POV), Beth (comes down and goes back up).
**Surface action and interior (passage 2; Dane's register per the bible):** long sentences about people and hands, a score kept quietly, each paragraph landing on a short clause that undercuts him. **No recap of the plot.** Every thought is about something in front of him or a person, never a summary of the case.
1. **The night.** The water is flat calm under the clearing. One clause on Ned's line from the first night, "Nobody's coming over that stern in this," which was true then, and on the deck tonight there's no *this*. Then Dane's own rule from the first night: **"Let him come to us."** He's sitting in it.
2. **The trucks.** The propane truck's cylinders in their rack a body's length from his shoulder, and the line Haskell gave him on the night the bridge came down: **steel doesn't burn; everything in it does** (3 of 3; render it as his memory of Ned's voice, once).
3. **His one call (the chapter's one screen).** Earlier, under the stars from this lane, he sent Pike one text in his own words: the witness put herself on the air as bait, it was her call, and he's staying with it. 4. **The sorting habit (his father's bus).** Arsenault had exact change for everything, the cigarettes and the coffee and the stranger's coffee. One sentence. 5. **Beth comes down.** She comes down the outside ladder in her socks with her boots left at the top, and a mug in her hand. He hears her before he sees her and knows it's her by the fact that he hardly hears her at all. She puts the mug in his good hand. Steam off it, and Ned's coffee too strong.
6. **His line, aloud, settling nothing:** **"I'd still put Haskell on the list."** (Tag it.) Beth answers with her hands: she closes his fingers round the mug, or straightens his collar against the cold, and goes back up the ladder without a word. Don't write "said nothing." Let the action carry it.

Two or three sentences. One of them notices that she looked at the heater grille in the mess this morning when he said the seal line, and that he filed it and didn't ask. **Subtext:** He believes her and still keeps a list, and both are true. The reader is inside the only head on the boat that has doubted Ned out loud, and that head can't decide either.
**Tension source:** Internal, with the night pressing on it. Waiting is the scene.
**Ends on:** **The chapter's one short withheld line.** His paragraph is running toward a thought about her that would undercut him, and out past the stern, on the water side, a paddle knocks once on aluminum. Land the paragraph on that sound in a short clause in his register. No reaction after it.
**Who knew:** Dane knows nothing he didn't know in Ch 08. Pike has his text.
**Texture (2):** the propane truck's cylinders; exact change.

### Scene 4 — CHANNEL NINE (the turn)
**Setting:** The dark wheelhouse, two minutes past eleven, a few minutes after the night low. Beth has kept it unlit all evening so her eyes would be ready for the dark (one clause; this is why the dark blinds everyone but her). The handheld on nine lies on the chart table. Through the windows: the lit car deck below, the black harbor, the store's windows up the Harbor Road. Sensory anchor: the handheld's speaker opening on nine with a click she has heard every working day for eleven years.
**Characters present:** Beth, Ned (his voice on nine, from the store's set), Russ (on the pier below).
**Surface action:**
1. Open on the handheld. The squelch breaks on nine, the channel that's only hers and Ned's. **"Bethie. Water side."** In Ned's voice. (Tag it in her recognition, not with "said": she knows it before the second word.)
2. **The form is the reveal (pays "Ned's formal radio voice," Ch 01).** Ned on the air is always formal, a different man: call signs, "standing by". This voice uses her name on the air, a private word on an open channel, which nobody on this island does and Ned never has. Beth puts nothing private on the air, and neither does he. **One interior beat, physical, no list:** her hand on the chart table, and the store's lit windows up the road, which is where the store's set is, and the store is where he is. No string of clues, no list of who knew, and no seal (Flag 1).
3. **Water side.** She looks to port, away from the pier, out over the dark water. Under the port quarter, out of the floods' throw, something low lies alongside where the quarter ladder is.
4. **Her first choice after the reveal.** She's out the wheelhouse door to the rail on the pier side and calls down to Russ at the bollard, not on the radio: **"Russ. Store. Get Tess."** (Tag it. Russ has one line or none. "Going," or just the sound of his boots on the pier, and he's gone up the pier road toward the path behind Marr's.) She has just sent away the land guard to protect the woman she named that afternoon. No narration about what that means.
5. **The floods.** At the top of the outside ladder, the breaker box on the aft bulkhead, where her shoulder brushed it the first night. She finds the floods' breakers by feel and throws them. The car deck goes black.
**Subtext:** The one man she never checks has just told her he's the leak, by warning her. He does it with her name, in her father's channel, and he says it on the air where it can be heard. She doesn't get to feel it yet. She has a deck to cross.
**Tension source:** Situational and personal at once. The voice and the skiff in the same minute.
**Ends on:** Cut mid-motion. The deck goes dark under her hand, and she's already over the top of the ladder in her socks (boots left by the wheelhouse door), going down into it. The sentence is still moving when the scene stops.
**Who knew:** Ned gave the network the whole boat at the store's back step after supper: Beth aboard, Dane aboard and wounded, Russ on the pier. He told them it was a trap, so they came from the water, with fire. Then he heard Arsenault send the boy with gasoline, and he went into the store with his own key and keyed nine (CASE FACTS Day 8). The page shows the end of the channel in s6 ("from my back step").
**Texture (2):** channel nine on the handheld; the breaker box.
**Word count target:** 390 (floor 370).

### Scene 5 — THE BLACK DECK (set piece; texture up to six)
**Setting:** The car deck of the *North Reach* in the dark, just after two minutes past eleven. The floods are out. Starlight, the store's distant windows, nothing else. The two trucks are black shapes with one lane of steel between them. The open stern is behind its low transom gate with the chain across it. The quarter ladder is on the port quarter, and the ring buoy is in its bracket on the port bulwark aft. Sensory anchor: gasoline, sweet and wrong in the cold, and the deck plates through wet wool socks.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, the hooded man (Doucette; unnamed in Beth's POV, see Flag 3).
**Surface action (real time; every beat on the page, no summary):**
1. **Sound first, in the order it arrives** (Beth's fear register: she stops talking and listens). The glug of a can being poured. A wet slap along the port side by the propane truck. The hooded man's walk on steel: one boot sets down clean and the other scrapes the deck plates every second step. Render it fresh. Don't use "step, drag" or "She knew the walk." The smell reaches her through the cold.
2. **Dane goes down.** When the floods died he was blind, because he'd sat in their glare all night. He moved toward the pouring by sound and fouled a chain stowed along the bulwark (binders and D-rings, the bins), and he's gone down on the bad arm. She hears the chain ring and his breath go out. Then **Dane talks**, low and continuous, present tense, his fear register: where he is ("by the bait truck's back wheel"), that he has the gun, that he can't see a thing, and a dry turn at his own expense. He keeps talking so she'll know where he is. (Don't use "Talk to me" or "low and steady.")
3. The hooded man hears him too and comes at the voice. A boot finds Dane in the dark. **His one line, to the deck at large:** "Where's your cop?" (He means the constable he was told about.)
4. **THE TRUST TEST.** Dane has the gun up at the sound in the dark, with gasoline on the steel all round them. Beth speaks, the woman who goes silent: **"Dane. Gas. Stand still. Don't fire."** (It echoes "Sea coming. Stand still.") Then where she is, in two words ("Bait truck."). Tag it as hers. **He holds.** The boot lands on the bad arm while he holds, and he takes it and doesn't fire. Show it by his voice stopping for one breath and starting again. No narration about trust.
5. **The lighter.** Aft by the transom, a lighter's wheel scratches, sparks and doesn't catch, because the man's glove is wet with gasoline (his thumb on the wheel: the chapter's one "thumb"). It scratches again.
