# Reader Notes: Chapter 07, "His Handwriting"

**Draft read:** `chapters/chapter-07/final.md` (3,053 words), read cold after chapters 01–06 `final.md`. I read nothing else.

## Stop line

None. I didn't put the book down. The closest I came was scene 4, at "She stood holding it." That's where I noticed I was waiting for the chapter to move.

## Where I got bored

- **"She stood holding it."** The oak-bar sequence (lift it, hold it, think, shift it into the crook of her arm, walk to the cot, hand over his chest, walk back, stand it against the wall) runs a paragraph and a half past where I'd got the point. The open Pelican case gets described twice.
- **The church opening.** "The church had no heat in it. Her breath showed inside a building." Then the light, the bell rope, the oak, the bark. Atmosphere with no question attached, because I don't know why she chose the church.
- **"On the front steps of the church: a step. A drag. A step."** This isn't boredom with the line. It's boredom with the device. The dragging heel has announced the hood in every chapter since the first. By now the limp is a doorbell, not a threat. I knew he'd turn up before he did.

## Where I got confused

- **"Somebody had been in the timer box at the ramp head."** How does she know? It's stated as fact before anyone looks at the box. The timer box and its two keys never came up before this page. They feel invented for the deduction.
- **Pike's "Not again today."** Again? Did the lifeboat try before? And why does it turn away: the shot, the dark float, the swell? I couldn't tell whether the Coast Guard heard a gunshot.
- **"The ring hung on its nail by Tess's safe."** It's dropped in right after the joke about Tess. I read it as Beth's thought, but it sits there like a stray line.
- **The money and the seals.** This is the big one. Chapter 4 said the fare bag is red and belongs to the boat, and Tess's bag is yellow with Beth's envelope inside. The notebook says $9,000 went into "subject's envelope," then "Pouch: two bags. Seal noted: red." At the end Beth thinks: "The fare bag was always red. So that was the one the teller had written down: the boat's." I *think* this means the money came in the boat's bag, which Ned carried and locked up on night one, so it points at Ned. But in the same scene Beth reasons that only Tess, the teller and herself saw the slip. Is she accusing Tess or Ned? I had to flip back to chapter 4, and I'm still not sure.
- **"They missed the one they'd paid."** I couldn't parse it. Who is "they": the shooter, Portland, Q? Who is "the one they'd paid"? Her, because the money was planted in her name? The shooter missed her and hit Dane? Investigators who flagged her and missed the real payee? It's meant as the key line of the chapter, and it read to me as a riddle.
- **"Russ was the *Second Wind* and four hundred traps, and a badge."** A badge? Nothing before this told me Russ is any kind of officer. "Badges wrote things down" only works if I know that.
- **Why the church?** No reason is given for going there, or for waiting for dark.
- **"…church…" on an unfamiliar handheld.** Who is clearing buildings, and how did they get to the church? "Nobody knew to," and yet they came. Is that a clue, or just the hood doing what the plot needs?
- **"Ned came on deck at two."** Two in the morning or two in the afternoon? The flashlight says night. But she left the church in the early afternoon, soaked to the hips. If it's 2 a.m., she sat on wet riprap for twelve hours in a gale in soaked jeans. If it's 2 p.m., why the flashlight?
- **Haskell / Ned.** Dane calls him "Haskell" and Beth calls him "Ned." The surname appeared once, two chapters ago. Read aloud, I'd have missed that they're the same man.

## Where I stopped believing

- **A marshal is shot, and the response is a text.** A federal officer has been shot at a Coast Guard landing, and the next contact is Pike's "Status?" No surge, no boat, no helicopter. The lifeboat just leaves with the inspector. Nobody on a lit harbor road comes out after a gunshot at dawn. Dane turning down LifeFlight fits him. The system around him shrugging doesn't fit anything.
- **Taking the notebook to protect the fish house.** She's already said it's "in a book that went to Portland." So taking the notebook doesn't un-report anything. Carrying it across an island where a man is hunting her puts the very information she's protecting in her coat. I believe she'd leave him, because "Don't write me down" has been her line since chapter 3. I don't believe the stated reason.
- **Leaving a wounded man asleep behind an unbarred door,** the man who took a bullet turning her round. She can't bar it from outside, the page says so, and she goes anyway. It fits her pride, so I half accept it. But the chapter works hard (the open case, the hand an inch over his chest) to make me forgive her, and I could feel that work.
- **Hours wet in the cold** (see the timing question above). She's been soaked to the thigh or hip repeatedly for five chapters. At some point it stops being toughness and turns into a body that doesn't obey weather.

Every chapter so far also ends on a line built to snap shut ("The float lights were dark," "And I watch who watches me buy it"). The rhythm is now predictable.

  - "an inch": the hand an inch off his shirt, plus ch. 3's "an inch off her zipper," ch. 4's "an inch from her face" and "an inch from the edge"

  - "a second" as a standalone beat
  - Fragment runs: "Then nothing. The stove. The first snow..."

- **"She gave it to him in order with nothing in front of it."** That's a writer's sentence, not Beth's.
- **"the sound of the boat keeping herself"** is lovely, but by now every chapter has one line like it placed just before the turn.
- Dane's under-fire monologue is still his own voice, but it's now a set piece I can predict: danger, then Dane narrating calmly, then a joke cut off mid-line.

## Listener pass (text-to-speech / audio)

- **The notebook entries won't survive being read aloud.** "*D5 0150 report. No indication.*" and "*D6 am. Q: CU written rpt. Pouch: two bags. Seal noted: red.*" come out as noise. Beth's decoding after them is thin, so a listener loses the chapter's key evidence. "The letter B" is also unclear by ear.
- **Italics carry the meaning.** Without visual italics, "every place we've slept, and B's family property" and "$9,000 cash in subject's envelope" don't sound like quotes from a document. "Witness, on Pike's line. Subject, in his pencil" needs a narrator who knows to lean on it.
- **The pronoun runs in scene 1** ("He turned them. His right hand went flat... She had heard it first. His hand on her back hadn't changed.") blur by ear. I couldn't tell who heard what first.
- **"On nine"** (her radio channel) means nothing to a listener who's forgotten chapter 2.
- **The run-on wade sentence** ("She went over the bank and down it on her heels and the snow went out from under her...") is the best audio passage in the chapter. It carries the breathlessness.
- **"Fuck."** It's the first hard swear in a book that's been fairly clean in its language. It's earned and funny ("That one wasn't the arm"), but it pops loudly in audio.

## Prediction section

What I think happens, so the author can check whether the page is steering me where it means to:

1. **Ned is the leak, or part of it.** He had the timer key. He was the only one Beth told the tide. He carried both bags on night one and "locked up." The red seal points at his boat. And his chapter-6 confession ("I thought it was shorts") reads like the start of a bigger one. Confidence: high. Maybe too high. If Ned is innocent, the book has piled up a lot on him.
2. **Tess is laundering Arsenault's money** through the store ledger (hundreds, never rung up as cash). She's guilty of something, but not the attempt on Beth. She'll end up helping.
3. **Q (Homeland Security) is running Arsenault or the *Margaret Ann* as their own operation.** That's why "hands off the vessel," and why Beth was flagged as a "subject": they're watching to see if the witness is dirty. Dane has known since the first ramp scene ("I watch whatever leaves your hands" now reads as surveillance).
4. **Dane's "Walked the bar" was a lie.** Either the *Margaret Ann*'s skiff brought him back that night (it came round the point right before he knocked), or someone else did. The book will make me doubt him once more before clearing him.
5. **Next chapter:** Dane wakes to an open door and a missing notebook. Ned either hands Beth over or turns out to be stalling for her. A Saturday first-light run to the lifeboat, probably in the nor'wester. Beth pilots something herself and uses the hundred-ton license.
6. **Ending:** she testifies, Dane leaves the Service or is pushed out, buys a boat, and she says "Put my name in" for the *North Reach*'s wheel.

## What I want to know next

- Is Ned about to hurt her? She's on his boat, alone, at night, with the notebook open on the table.
- Did Dane really walk the bar? And how did he get back that night?
- Who put $9,000 in her envelope, and why? To frame her, to buy her silence, or to discredit her before the grand jury?
- What does Dane do when he wakes and the book is gone?
- What is Q actually protecting on the *Margaret Ann*?

These are real questions and they pull. The danger is that the strongest one (who framed her) depends on a seal-color deduction I'm not confident I followed.

## Best moment

> Her own kitchen floor, under the blanket, with her back to the chimney. The Ashby kitchen. This cot, with him asleep on it. A pencil line with a date over it, in a book that went to Portland.

Followed by: "He had written it down and carried it into her bed." The whole book has been building to "Don't write me down," and here it is in pencil. It's the one passage where I felt what Beth felt, with no explanation needed.
