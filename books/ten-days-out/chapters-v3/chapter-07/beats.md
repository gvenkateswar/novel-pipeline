### Scene 1
**Setting:** The ramp head at the town landing, Thursday (Day 7), before six, full dark going gray in the east. The ebb a third gone and still drawing. The harbor road lit behind them to the store. Below, the float black on the water. Off the landing the *Margaret Ann*'s engine has just turned over, a low diesel knock coming across the water. Sensory anchor: the exhaust lying flat on the harbor, the smell of it on the wet air, and herring bait from the shed at their backs.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane. (The man in the skiff is unseen until the shot and stays unnamed.)
**Surface action:**
1. Open on Beth's line, in motion, no recap. The road lights are on, so the set that runs them and the float is running (one clause: the same set feeds both). Somebody has been in the box. "Ned set that timer. Ned and the town ring had the only keys to the box." Then, before Dane can answer: "Turn round."
2. Dane turns them, as he promised in Ch 06 ("Anything wrong at the ramp head, we turn round"). He doesn't argue. Pay "I'll know before you do": she heard it first, an oar knocking once on aluminum somewhere under the float, a sound that shouldn't be there at this tide.
3. The shot comes as they turn, from the water off the float, about sixty yards out (don't state the figure; let the flash be "out past the float"). The road lights behind them make them a shape on the ramp head. Dane takes the round across the outside of his left upper arm. He shoves her round the corner of the bait shed with his right hand before he says anything about it.
4. Danger registers, opposite (THE CORE): Beth goes silent, nouns and imperatives, then nothing. Dane talks more, low, continuous, present tense, transport patter: where he is, where she is, what his arm is doing, a joke at his own expense he doesn't finish. ("Okay" may open his patter once, mid-line, never as the first word of a line; "Talk to me" is retired.)
5. They run back down the lane behind the bait shed and onto the shore under the bank. The ebb has drawn off more; the last ledge now comes over her boot tops (no "-deep" words). She puts her bare hand on his arm in the dark to find the wound (gloves off: the chapter's one use), feels the wet and the heat, and finds it's a furrow and the arm still works below the elbow.
6. Under the bank, out of sight of the landing, the light coming. Out past the ledges a boat's light: the Coast Guard lifeboat, coming in for the float. It slows. It turns.
7. Dane gets the Iridium out with his right hand and can't lift his left to steady it above the shoulder. He hands it to her. She holds it up to the sky for him, and when it lands she reads it herself: *Pike: Lifeboat turned back. Not again today. First light Saturday. LifeFlight for the arm?* (In words exactly so, one line. This is the chapter's one screen.)
8. Dane swears, once, flat, at "Saturday": two more nights of her on this island. It is his first swear in the book, and it's about her, not the arm.
9. No medevac, in one exchange: the ballfield's in the open with that deck watching it, and the front is coming in by mid-morning. Dane decides it and says so. He thumbs back one word with his right hand.
**Subtext:** She has just said Ned's name out loud as a fact, and her next sentence already wants to move the blame to the ring. He hears that she knew the hour was in danger before he did, and doesn't yet know why she knew. The swear is the first time he lets her see what the plan cost him.
**Tension source:** Situational (the shot, the run, the boat turning away). Interpersonal underneath: her "Ned set that timer" sits between them unexamined while they run.
**Ends on:** Consequence, seen. The lifeboat's light going away south past the ledges with the inspector aboard, while she still holds the phone up to the sky for a man who can't lift his arm. One long sentence, at least 15 words.
**Who knew (World rule 11):** Dane, yes. Ned, yes, from her tide question (Ch 06). Tess, yes, through the county call she kept to herself. **True channel:** Ned, at the propane cage on Day 6, with his own timer key. **The page shows one end of the channel:** the locked box at the ramp head and its two keys.
**Tide check:** Day 7 high about 3:25, low about 9:40. Before six, about a third ebbed; by quarter past six, more. The shore under the bank is passable with the last ledge over her boot tops.
**Texture (2):** the timer box and its two keys; the lifeboat's light turning (no hull type, no station).
**Word count target:** 620 (floor 580).

### Scene 2
**Setting:** The fish house, Thursday, about seven, gray light. Snow beginning on the cove, the first flakes of the second system. The oil-drum stove lit low. Sensory anchor: the cold coming off his wet jacket on the nail, and the iron smell of blood on her fingers at the stove's warmth.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Surface action:**
1. Open mid-task, with no account of the trip back. Dane on the bench with his left sleeve cut away by the shears from his own kit. Beth takes her father's red Pelican case down off the shelf and opens it on the page for the first time: his boat first-aid kit, old and complete. Butterfly strips. She closes the graze the way she would a deckhand's: pinch the edges, lay the strips across, one at a time, from the middle out. (Get it right: a graze furrow, not a through-wound; strips across, not along.)
2. Longing: her bare hands on the inside of his arm, his breath on her wrist when she leans across him, how warm he is under the cold skin. One or two lines. No callus, no scar.
3. **The laugh, with a cost.** Dane, at himself: "By the time I tell this in Lowell it's a much bigger hole." Beth: "Tess'll have it through your heart by noon." He laughs, or she nearly does, and then the name sits there. The ring hangs by Tess's safe. Neither of them goes on with the joke. ("Then nothing.")
4. Dane takes the opening. His anger register is very polite and slow, with "Ms. Calloway" back: "You asked Haskell about the float. Below decks, where I couldn't hear it. Tell me what you said, exactly, the way you'd tell a man who's going to write it down." He has worked it out from her line at the ramp head; he wasn't in the forepeak.
5. She tells him exactly, her three lines and Ned's two, in the same order ("Is the float workable on the ebb?" / "Which tide?" / "Tomorrow's first."). No softening and no reasons first. Then her case, short and certain: Ned set the timer to run till light. Somebody with the other key turned it. The other key hangs by a safe behind a curtain where anybody at that counter can reach it.
6. Dane doesn't argue the ring. He gets out the notebook and the pencil from its spine and writes, holding the book flat on his knee with his right hand, and she watches her own words go down in his hand (the paper-against-hands friction, on a nerve the reader has watched him press since Ch 3).
**Subtext:** He's been shot because of a question she promised not to ask, and his answer is to write her down and report it, which is the thing she can't forgive. She's defending Ned by building the case against Tess before anyone asks her to, and she can hear herself do it. Neither says "you lied to me," and both have.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Her broken promise against his notebook. The laugh is the last easy thing between them.
**Ends on:** Decision spoken aloud. Dane: "I'm going to write that down. Then I'm going to sleep for one hour, because I can't hold the gun above my shoulder, and you're going to put the bar across that door." She drops the oak bar into its irons. He lies down with the gun on the bench by his right hand. (End on her hands on the oak bar, not on a clipped narrator line.)
**Texture (2):** butterfly strips from the Pelican case; the shears through the sleeve.
**Word count target:** 520 (floor 490).

### Scene 3
**Setting:** The fish house, Thursday mid-morning. Dane asleep on the cot, on his right side, the gun under his right hand on the bench. Snow coming harder across the cove. She lifts a corner of the gray blanket off the small window for light, and the snow-light falls gray-white on the page. Sensory anchor: wet wool drying on the wire and the pencil smell of the notebook's pages.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane (asleep).
**Surface action:**
1. She takes the notebook out of the jacket on the nail. Her reason is concrete: she wants to see what he wrote of her an hour ago. Her answer about Ned is the first page she finds, in his hand (paraphrase it, don't quote). One page back, her own plan from Wednesday night, every ledge of it, in his hand (paraphrase, don't quote). The waterproof paper is unspoiled by the wet jacket; the pencil hasn't run.
2. She turns back further, to before she let him write anything. **The four lines, quoted exactly and only these (bible CASE FACTS; see Flag 1 on line 4):**
   - "every place we've slept, and B's family property"
   - "$9,000 cash in subject's envelope, over her slip, credited and flagged"
   - "D5 0150 report. No indication."
   - "D5 pm. Q: CU written rpt. Pouch: two bags. Seal noted: red."
3. Give each line a body reaction or a fact she places, never a paragraph of thought:
   - **Line 1:** the fish house is written down. "Every place we've slept" carries both meanings, and she reads both.
   - **Line 2:** this stops her. The first time she has heard of the money, and she hears it from a page. Her slip; the envelope with the flap tucked, never sealed; the people who see the slip (Tess, herself, the teller). She thinks of Tess's hands, the teller's, her own handing down the pouch at Pruett with him watching it. **She doesn't think of Ned carrying the bags.** Leave him out of her list without comment. That's her blind spot, and a careful reader will see the gap. Nine thousand dollars she didn't earn, credited to her: the one insult aimed straight at her (owes nobody, takes nothing).
   - **Line 3:** Day 5, ten to two, the top of the tide. He was on the ledge reporting while the skiff's light climbed her window, and the gravel bar was over a man's head. "Walked the bar" was this. The light at the Ashby end wasn't his. (This starts answering the reader's question; who held the shore light stays for Ch 08 s2.) "No indication" of what: of her.
   - **Line 4:** her eyes go over it and back to the money. **No interpretation of the seal in this scene.**
4. **The reversal:** Pike's screen from Saturday, one clause in memory ("Do not disclose to witness"). Saturday she read it as the things they never tell witnesses. It was this, and the witness was the subject. The log was about her. Show it in what her hands do (she shuts the book on her thumb; she opens it again), not in a sentence that explains it.
5. She looks from the page to the strips on his arm.
**Subtext:** Every tender thing since the generator shed reads two ways now: the money questions, the bed, the plan she gave him as a gift. She's grieving, and the grief comes out as a bitter, exact sentence because that's how Beth's anger works. Under it is the fact that she was shot at as a woman somebody paid, and the bullet found him.
**Tension source:** Internal. What the page says against what she chose on Monday night. Quiet and dark, in the quiet register: her interior longer than any dialogue, plain and physical, landing on short sentences.
**Ends on:** Reversal, spoken. She says it aloud to the sleeping man, quietly: "They missed the one they'd paid." He doesn't wake. (No narration after the line explains it.)
**Texture (1):** the notebook itself, its waterproof paper and pencil. The four lines are the one place in the book codes appear in a run.
**Word count target:** 760 (floor 720). This is the chapter's centre and may run to 800, taken from s6, not from s2.

### Scene 4 — The rupture
**Setting:** The fish house, the same late morning, a little before the low. The snow is thick enough now to close the cove, the far shore gone. The ledges under the bank are dry behind the ebb. Sensory anchor: the oak bar's weight across both her forearms, and the cold iron of the irons it sits in.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane (asleep).
**Surface action:**
1. She puts the notebook inside her coat, against her ribs. She clips her handheld inside the coat (on nine, as always; it matters in Ch 09).
2. In her head, one or two plain sentences: the fish house is on paper now, in a book that goes to Portland, and from there to anyone who reads Portland. She won't sleep in a written-down place. (Her wound, acted on. No theme sentence.)
3. She takes the red Pelican case off the shelf, opens it on the bench beside the gun where his right hand will find it, the strips on top. She leaves it open. She has never left it open. (Observable; don't explain the message.)
4. **The bar, weighed.** She lifts the oak bar out of its irons. It's heavy across both forearms. She stands holding it and knows (shown, not named) what it means to set it down: a door she can't bar from outside, a man asleep behind it who can't lift his left arm. She could say his name and he would wake. She doesn't say it.
5. **Longing:** she holds her bare hand over his chest, an inch off, and feels the warmth come up off him, and takes the hand back without touching him.
6. She sets the oak bar against the wall beside the door, opens the door on the snow, and goes.
**Subtext:** She's choosing the boat and the island over the man, which is what she has always done when paper comes for her. The condemnable part (the outline's "Beth, Ch 7, lesser") is the unbarred door: she leaves him less safe to punish the notebook, and she knows it.
**Tension source:** Internal and moral. The bar in her hands is the whole argument.
**Ends on:** **Short withheld line (the chapter's only one).** The oak bar standing against the wall beside the door, where it lived. It echoes Ch 06's first paragraph, once and on purpose. Nothing after it.
**Texture (1):** the oak bar and its irons.
**Word count target:** 400 (floor 380).

### Scene 5
**Setting:** The Union Church on the Harbor Road, between the store and the town lane, Thursday early afternoon. Unheated, snow-light in the clear windows, the bell rope knocking in the wind. Behind it the lean-to woodshed stacked with the minister's split oak. Below the church, the bank and the harbor shore, the flood making. Sensory anchor: split oak and wet bark, and her own breath showing inside a building.
**Characters present:** Beth, Russ (seen passing, not spoken to), the man with the dragging heel (unnamed on the page).
**Surface action:**
1. **Two lines of travel at most:** the shore under the bank at the late-morning low in the snow, dry ledges, nobody on them; up the bank and in at the church's side door. She is waiting for dark and the night low.
2. **Not Russ.** Through the window, Russ's truck goes by slow on the Harbor Road, wipers going. She could step out and stop him. She stands at the window with her hand on the sash and lets it pass: he's another badge, and badges write things down. Shown through the hand on the sash and the truck's taillights. No speech.
3. Early afternoon. Outside on the road side, a handheld squawks: a man's voice through wind and static, too broken to place, a word or two she can make out ("…church…"). Make plain on the page, in her ear, that it is nobody she knows by voice. **Never Ned's voice and never one she recognizes.** The Ch 09 reveal depends on her knowing Ned's voice in a syllable. They are working down the buildings near the landing after the miss. They didn't follow her; nobody knew she would come here.
4. On the church steps, the step and the drag. She knows the walk.
5. **She acts.** Out the back through the vestry door into the snow, ten yards of open ground to the woodshed, the notebook hard against her ribs. In her head, one clause: if he takes her, he takes every place Dane wrote down, the fish house and the man asleep in it with the door unbarred. Through the lean-to; behind her she hauls a stack of the minister's oak down across the opening. The noise goes off like a door slammed, and now he knows where she went. That's the cost. It buys her the seconds. Over the bank and down onto the shore.
6. **The "and…and" peak (the chapter's one):** her body ahead of her, a long sentence running on "and" from the bank to the water (the snow and the weed and the ledge gone under and the cold through her boots and up her legs and the notebook up out of the wet in her fist). The flood is making, and above half tide the ledges are awash. She goes in to her hips and along under the bank, where a man with that heel can't follow on the weed. Beth may swear once here, at the cold.
**Subtext:** She has just left the man who was protecting her, and within hours the island proves she can protect herself. It also proves that what she carries endangers him. She chose the notebook, and now she has to keep it.
**Tension source:** Situational (the search, the run). Under it, the choice she made in s4 coming due.
**Who knew:** Nobody. They are clearing buildings near the landing, directed by someone with a handheld. No channel needed, and none implied.
**Tide check:** Day 7 high about 3:50 p.m.; above half tide from about 12:45 p.m. the ledges are awash. This is a short distance inside the town shore, not the fish-house-to-town route, so World rule 13 holds.
**Ends on:** Cut mid-action. In the water to her hips under the bank, moving, the notebook held up out of it, the step-and-drag going along the top of the bank above her and stopping. Cut there. Don't resolve it in this scene; s6 opens with her safe, which answers it.
**Texture (2):** the bell rope knocking; the minister's split oak coming down.
**Word count target:** 450 (floor 420).

### Scene 6
**Setting:** The stone pier and the *North Reach*, Friday (Day 8), about two in the morning, then the crew mess. Snow over, wind rising ahead of the gale, the brow's wheels grinding on the granite at every surge. The crew mess: the heater, a bulkhead lamp, a mug ring on the table. Sensory anchor: her wet clothes stiffening at the heater, and the forepeak pump's steady run coming up through the steel.
**Characters present:** Beth, Ned.
**Surface action:**
1. Open at the pier, with no account of the hours between. She has come along the riprap below the road at the night low, out of the store's sight, and waited under the pier's face for two, because Ned comes on deck at two to check the lines and the pump. She knows his hours better than her own. (This is why two.)
2. Ned at the head of the brow with a flashlight held low. He answers with weather before anything else (his habit; no new tell), a line about the wind going round and harder by morning. He lets her aboard without a question and takes her into the crew mess. Big, stooped, the green jacket. He goes for coffee. He doesn't say her name. (The optional 2692 lying on the mess table in his hand is permitted as texture; if it's used, give it one clause and no comment.)
3. Alone. She takes the notebook out of her coat. The edges are wet, the pages sound. She finds the line about the seal (refer to it; **don't quote it again**: "Seal noted" is capped).
4. **The seal, set aside, in her head only (R3-1, CASE FACTS):** one or two sentences of interior. The fare bag is always red. So that's the one the teller wrote down. She turns the page back to the money. **Never spoken, and nobody is there to hear it.**
5. Ned in the doorway with two mugs.
**Subtext:** She has come to the one person she has never checked, carrying the proof that she checks everyone else. The reader may be at 45% on Ned. Let the irony sit with no narration nudging it: no lingering on his face, no pause loaded for the reader.
**Tension source:** Dramatic irony and fatigue. The steel around her feels safe, and the reader can't be sure it is.
**Ends on:** **Cut on an unfinished exchange (R4-7).** Ned in the doorway with the coffee: "Where's your marshal?" Cut before she answers. No narration after the line.
**Texture (1):** the forepeak pump running under the deck (no impeller).
**Word count target:** 350 (floor 330).
