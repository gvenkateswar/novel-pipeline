"Your name was given to the Marshals on Friday, Ms. Marr," Quist said. "It was given in writing, by the witness. You will be asked where you were on Friday night."

She was Quist, the Q on Pike's screen, tall and very straight, her face still gray from the crossing, one hand on a shut legal pad. Beth had stopped inside the door, where the floor gave over the soft sills. With the generator off, the store was quiet enough to hear the urn.

Tess stood behind the counter with her glasses up in her hair and her lip swollen, her eyes on Beth.

"Right here. Net control on sixty-eight. Half the island heard me."

Then, to Beth: "You put my name in."

"Yes," Beth said.

"I sold you condoms."

Beth's hand was in her coat pocket, on the box.

Quist didn't open the pad. "A box bearing your price sticker was taken from that shelf at noon on Monday, while you were on your rounds. How many impellers were missed?"

"One. Forty-one fifty, on Haskell's account. Figured he'd been up for his phone."

"The spare key to your truck was left on the back step the night the bridge came down, and again that noon. How many keys hang on that hook?"

"Two."

"The captain's key to the float-light box was handed over on Wednesday, and it was kept," Quist said. "The town ring was never needed."

Tess set the coffee pot down.

"The captain has also stated that the men were told how the Calloway kitchen door is opened."

"Lift it and lean on it," Beth said.

Ned had come in by that door every winter of her life without being shown.

Tess bent below the counter and came up with a book: cloth, gray, soft at the corners.

"Two years of the *Margaret Ann* after dark," Tess said. "Every gallon, cash, twice the pump. Lyle's talk in the margins. Mind my spelling."

Quist turned a page. Upside down, in Tess's square hand, Beth read a margin: *store like this lives on the boat*. Tuesday's.

"You had that Tuesday," Beth said.

"Tuesday he still had a boat."

Lower on the page Quist's finger stopped on another name at the same rate: *Dorothy M.* Beth knew her at once, the green bay boat with the squared-off wheelhouse, and the man at her wheel, who waved to the ferry every morning he was out.

"How many nights for the *Dorothy M.*, Ms. Marr?"

"Nine since Labor Day. Somebody else's hundreds."

"Goddamn," Quist said to the page. "How many nights in two years did your float fuel a boat carrying fentanyl?"

"Count the hundreds," Tess said.

"Haskell asked me something at supper last night," Tess said to Quist, with her eyes on Beth. "You'll see him before I do. Tell him yes."

She turned to the cigarette rack and started straightening it.

"Yes to what, Ms. Marr?" Quist said.
