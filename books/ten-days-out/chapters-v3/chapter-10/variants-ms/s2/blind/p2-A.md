The woman at the counter was Quist, the Q on Pike's screen, tall and very straight, black hair pulled back hard, her face still gray from the crossing. Her hand rested on a shut legal pad.

Beth stopped inside the door, where the floor gave over the soft sills. Out back the generator was off. Tess stood behind the counter, glasses up in her hair, lip swollen, the coffee pot in her hand.

"Ms. Marr's name was given to the Marshals on Friday, in writing, by the witness," Quist said. "In Reyes's book the store vehicle was entered without a name. Ms. Marr will be asked where she was on Friday night."

Tess answered Quist with her eyes on Beth. "Behind this counter. Ask my lip."

Then, to Beth: "You put my name in."

"Yes," Beth said.

"I sold you condoms."

In her coat pocket Beth's fingers found Tuesday's box, what was left of it. Tess had never said on sixty-eight who it was for.

"A box with Ms. Marr's price sticker was taken off that shelf at noon on Monday, while she was on her rounds," Quist said. "Was it missed?"

"One impeller, thirty-eight fifty, on Haskell's account. Figured he'd come up for his phone."

"The spare key to Ms. Marr's truck was left on the back step the night the bridge came down, and again that Monday noon. How many keys on that hook?"

"Two."

"The captain's key to the float-light box was handed over on Wednesday and was kept."

"The captain states that he told them how the Calloway kitchen door is opened."

"Lift it and lean on it," Beth said, before Tess could.

Tess bent below the counter and came up with a gray cloth book, soft at the corners.

"Two years of the *Margaret Ann*'s night fuel. Every gallon after dark, every night my float was lit late, paid in hundreds. What Lyle said at my counter's in the margins."

Quist turned a page. Upside down, in Tess's square hand, Beth read one margin: *store like this lives on the boat*. Tuesday's.

"You had that Tuesday," Beth said.

"Tuesday he still had a boat."

Quist turned two more pages and stopped under another name. *Dorothy M.* Night fuel, the same rate, in hundreds.

"How many nights did the *Dorothy M.* take fuel after dark?"

"Eleven since Labor Day. Somebody else's hundreds."

Beth knew the boat by sight, a green hull from the head of the bay, with no face to put at her wheel.

"Goddamn," Quist said to the page. "How many of those nights did your float fuel a boat carrying fentanyl, Ms. Marr?"

"Count the hundreds."

Tess kept her eyes on Beth. "Haskell asked me something at supper last night. You'll see him before I do. Tell him yes."

She turned to the cigarette rack and began straightening it, her back to the room.

"That will be put to the captain in your words," Quist said. "What did he ask you?"
