### Scene 1 — THE LANDING
**Setting:** The head of the town landing ramp, Saturday (Day 9), a little before eleven in the morning, close to the morning low, so the ramp pitches steep down to the float. Clear, flat calm, the northwest air gone light. Beth stands beside the locked box at the ramp head (the one that runs the float lights). The bait shed's roll-up door is shut. Down at the float the Coast Guard lifeboat and the Marine Patrol boat have been alongside since eight. Sensory anchor: herring off the bait shed in the cold, and the ramp's hinge clanging once as the float lifts on a wash.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Ned.
**Want:** Beth wants the boat (she can't say it yet) and wants to see Ned go without being the one who put him there. Dane wants to be relieved with his dignity and stay on her island. Ned wants to give her the boat before they take him.
**Surface action:**
1. **Answer the arm in the first 80 words, observed, no recap.** Dane beside her with his left arm strapped across his chest inside his buttoned coat, the empty sleeve pinned. At the open collar she can see the bruise going black up his neck from the shoulder. The kicks opened the furrow, and the fire company's EMT closed it again with fresh strips and a pressure dressing in the store at about two. Nothing broken: the EMT ran his hands down the bone twice. He can't close the left hand. Put it as what she sees and one clause of what the EMT did. No stitches (an EMT doesn't suture).
2. **Where the dragger is (seen, one sentence).** Out past the outer ledges the *Margaret Ann*'s red hull lies stopped with a Coast Guard boat alongside her. She slipped her mooring before light and got as far as the ledges. Two men sit in the stern of the Marine Patrol boat at the float with their hands behind them, one of them wrapped in Ned's blankets (Arsenault and the boy, never named here).
3. **"Something low alongside the port quarter," paid (one line).** Across the harbor at the stone pier, a Marine Patrol skiff is towing the *Margaret Ann*'s aluminum skiff off the *North Reach*'s port quarter ladder, where the boy left it. One clause. Don't call back to Ch 09's wording.
4. **Dane relieved (the cost, seen).** Two relief deputies from Portland come down the Harbor Road from the town office and stop at the ramp head. One says Pike wants his weapon. Dane takes the gun off his hip with his good hand and gives it across butt first. He's off the detail; he rides over tomorrow as a passenger. Give Dane one line to her after it, in his register, aimed at himself (being a passenger). No "which" tail. Beth's longing beat: her hand closes on his strapped arm through the coat, once, and lets go. He goes down the ramp to the float with the deputies.
5. **Ned.** Two Marine Patrol officers bring him down the Harbor Road from the town office, cuffed in front, the green jacket, the clean collar from supper still buttoned. His statement to Quist is done. They stop at the ramp head because the ramp is steep and they want a hand on each arm. Ned looks at the water first, then: weather, once, in his understatement. Then **"Take her across tomorrow."** The inspector has been aboard since eight and accepted the temporary repair forward; Ned knows, and doesn't explain.
6. **She asks, out loud, for the first time.** **"Put my name in."** (Tag it. Beth, conclusion first, no reasons.) Ned keeps his subject: put it in herself, tell the district he said so. Then one more short line of his that admits his word won't carry what it did. Wording is the writer's; keep it understatement, never self-pity, and no "Bethie."
7. **The keys.** Ned asks the officer for the wheelhouse key off his ring. The officer works it off and puts it in Beth's hand. On the ring as it goes back into the evidence bag she sees the store key, and the small brass one that fits the box at her elbow. One clause. No comment, no word "timer."
8. Ned goes down the steep ramp between the officers. At the float's edge Dane, one-armed, takes his elbow for the step down into the Marine Patrol boat, and Ned lets him. **Beth's one interior clause:** Ned can't swim a stroke, and nobody on that float knows it but her.
**Subtext:** The man who should have put her name in can't, and he gives her the boat anyway. Dane hands over the one thing the book has said is his and goes on taking care of the man who got him shot. She has the key in her fist and hasn't been told she can use it.
**Tension source:** Cost on the page from the first line: the arm, the gun, the cuffs. Interpersonal underneath: she asks Ned in front of the officers and Dane, the first thing she has asked anyone for in the book.
**Texture (2):** the box at the ramp head and the brass key on Ned's ring; the steep ramp at the bottom of the tide.
**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** Cut mid-motion. One long sentence: Ned's weight going from Dane's good arm down onto the Marine Patrol boat's gunwale, the boat dipping under him, and the sentence still moving when the scene stops. No line of dialogue after it.
**Word target:** 500   **Word floor:** 480

### Scene 2 — MARR'S AT NOON
**Setting:** Marr's General Store, Saturday noon. The grid is back; the generator out back is quiet for the first time in nine days, and the store is still kept at fifty-eight. The counter, the urn, the curtain to Tess's office pushed back, the hook on the office wall with the truck keys and Ned's charger under it. Sensory anchor: coffee and kerosene, and the floor's give where the old sills are soft.
**Characters present:** Beth, Tess, Quist.
**Want:** Tess wants Beth to hear every word that clears her, standing where she can see Beth's face, and she wants to be the one who hands over her own book. Quist wants the ledger. Beth wants to be forgiven and won't ask.
**Surface action:**
1. **Open in motion.** Quist is at the counter, tall and very straight, black hair pulled back hard, gray in the face, a clean white bait bucket by her boot that she hasn't put down since the float. A legal pad with Ned's statement on it. Tess behind the counter, lip swollen, glasses up in her hair. Beth just inside the door. **Beth's one clause:** the Q on Pike's screen was this woman with a bucket.
2. **The bucket (Book 2, seen as an object, small laugh).** Tess, before anything else: "Whitcomb's." (Attribute it to Tess before the line.) Quist: he talked to her about the tide the whole way up the ramp and never once looked at her. Then Quist's line about Russ, plainly: **"He stayed off that boat for a week because I asked him to. Nobody stays off the boat."**
3. **Quist's statement line (one sentence, read to Tess; the protected Ch 10 line).** Wording is the writer's. Content, in this order, as one long sentence in Quist's brisk voice: Mr. Haskell says he came up the back way at **noon on Monday**, while Ms. Marr was on her rounds, for an **impeller** off that shelf (the box with **her price sticker** on it); that he took **the spare key to her truck off her hook** and left it on her back step **both nights, after her lights were out**; that he gave them **his own key to the box that runs the float lights**; and that he told them how **Ms. Calloway's kitchen door goes**, lift it and lean on it. (The door line is "the Quist latch line." Don't use the word "latch" or "lift and shove.")
4. **Two beats inside the sentence, physical, no narration:** at "noon on Monday" Tess sets down whatever she's holding (she carried his chowder down to the boat that afternoon). At the kitchen door, **Beth's one interior clause:** Ned had known that door since her father hung it. That's the latch payoff: it was never Dane she'd shown.
5. **Quist's number question:** **"How many keys on that hook, Ms. Marr?"** Tess: **"Two."** *(Optional, cut first if long:)* Tess unhooks Ned's phone from its charger under the hook and lays it on the counter in front of Quist.
6. **The county call, aimed at Beth through Quist.** Tess, to Quist, with her eyes on Beth: the county called her Wednesday about the landing and she kept it to herself, the same as she kept Ashby off the net. (Pays "You told the county?" and "Keep Ashby off the net." Beth listed "sat on the county's call" against her in Ch 08.)
7. **Tess looks straight at Beth** (never "won't look at anyone"). **"You put my name in."** / **"Yes."** / **"I sold you condoms."** Tag Tess's first line; Beth's "Yes" carries her name in an action beat or a tag. No reaction narrated.
8. **What Tess took from the safe.** From under the counter she brings a ledger she took out of the safe last night, a cloth-bound book (not the green fuel log on the counter), and hands it to Quist herself. Every gallon the *Margaret Ann* took after dark for two years, each night the float was lit late, paid in hundreds. In the margins, things Lyle Arsenault said at her counter, copied in her ballpoint. Quist opens it, turns a page, and Beth sees one margin line in Tess's square hand: Tuesday's, *store like this lives on the boat*. **Beth's one clause:** that was what Tess wrote on the back of her hand after Beth went out with the box (pays Tess's Ch 06 hand note; "back of her hand" and "ballpoint" count against caps, use one of the two).
9. **Quist's one swear, about the ledger** (World rule 2), and **"fentanyl" once**, in one line of hers with a number in it or a question (default: she swears, then says how many nights the float fueled a boat carrying fentanyl, as a question to Tess she already knows the answer to). Short.
10. **Tess's answer to Ned.** To Quist, eyes on Beth the whole time, sliding the ledger the last of the way across: Haskell asked her something at supper last night; Quist will see him before she does; **"Tell him yes."**
**Subtext:** Tess is cleared out loud of everything Beth named her for, in front of Beth, and then she convicts herself of the night fuel by her own hand and forgives the man who opened her bag. The forgiveness goes to the wrong person, on purpose. "I sold you condoms" means *I knew your business and kept it*, and Beth gave her name to Portland anyway.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Two women who walked each other home from every dance, and a fed with a bucket between them.
**Texture (2):** the price sticker on the impeller box; the ledger's margin.
**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** Reversal. "Tell him yes," said to Quist with Tess's eyes on Beth. Her line is the last words of the scene. No narration after it.
**Word target:** 540   **Word floor:** 510

### Scene 3 — THE COTTAGE (intimate scene 3 and THE DECLARATION)
**Setting:** Beth's cottage, Saturday night (Day 9), the grid back on. Two relief deputies in a car in the front yard on the road side with the engine running for the heater. The stove lit in the kitchen, the house warm for the first time in nine days. Then upstairs, her room over the kitchen, the window on the harbor. The guest mooring is empty below: no deck lights anywhere on the harbor. Sensory anchor: woodsmoke and wet wool in a warm room, and the cold coming off the window glass.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane.
**Want:** Beth wants him, chosen with everything known, and wants to ask him to stay. Dane wants to tell her what he's decided, himself, about where he'll be.
**Approach (about 200 words):**
1. He comes up the iced back steps from the harbor side, out of the deputies' sightline, no gun on his hip, the arm strapped. She has the door open before he knocks. (No "latch.")
2. In the kitchen he sits in the chair with its back to the door and doesn't look at the door (pays "Then don't sit with your back to me"). She sees it. One clause.
3. **Beth asks (her desire register):** **"Will you stay past Monday? I'm asking. I don't, mostly."** Dane answers in his fuller register: whatever Portland decides about the job, he's coming back here when they're done with him, and he'd like to come back to her. A plain statement, his own call about a person, said to her face. Wording is the writer's; keep it free of contrast forms ("X decides…, I decide…" is out; see Flag 13). No "which" tail.
4. She takes the lamp upstairs, and leaves it on in the harbor window, the first light anyone has shown on this hill in nine days. The store looks up at the back of the house. One clause: Tess will have it written down by morning. She leaves it on.
**The scene (real time; intimate action ≥ 500 words; floor 850 for the whole scene):**
5. She undoes the strapping herself and lays his bad arm on the pillow and tells him to leave it there. Plot touch 1: the arm, the bruise she can see now under the lamp.
6. **Humor inside:** he tries her father's sweater one-handed and gets it half over her head and stuck at the darned elbows. She gives a flat verdict on his technique, aimed at the man. He laughs, and she kisses him through it.
7. **Consent in their voices:** one explicit yes, and one check (a hand that waits, a question). **Protection in one line:** the box from Marr's in her coat pocket since Tuesday.
8. **Beth's register:** she gives orders and asks, both ("Like that?"). Dane goes quiet: her name, a question, few words. She is above him because of the arm. Frank words, sparingly and exactly (bible vocabulary). Never more than two sentences in a row of choreography; then a thought, a sound, a line.
9. **The chapter's one and…and peak:** her interior runs on *and* and she notices she isn't steering: the lamp in the window and the harbor that can see it and the boat at the stone pier she'll take off at first light and his good hand on her hip and the cold off the glass on her back, and then none of that. Plot touch 2: the crossing in the morning. Keep her nouns (lamp, harbor, boat, glass, stove) so it reads as hers.
10. Climax on the page, then **within 150 words, the declaration.** No recap of the book. In words, in each voice:
    - Beth, conclusion first: **"I love you."**
    - Dane: **"Since when?"**
    - Beth: **"You jumped."** (The first thing she liked about him. She gives no other reason.)
    - Dane, last: **"I love you. I've been wrong about you every single time since the ramp. It's the best run I've had in four years. Don't fix it."** (Bible tender line, with "I love you" in front. The writer may run the last three sentences together with commas if it reads better aloud; keep "Don't fix it" as the final clause.)
**Subtext:** Every earlier night they were hidden, watched or lying. Tonight there's a lamp in the window and two deputies out front, and she has decided to be seen. He comes with no gun and no order, which is the one thing he couldn't do for four years.
**Tension source:** Internal, plus the house: the deputies' engine running out front, the hour she has to be up.
**Texture (1):** the lamp in the harbor window.
**Point of view:** Beth (never Dane's during sex).
**Ends on:** Dialogue. Dane's declaration is the last line. Nothing after it: no clock, no sleep.
**Word target:** 870   **Word floor:** 850 (bible heat floor for scene 3; intimate action itself ≥ 500)

### Scene 4 — THE WHEEL (the turn)
**Setting:** The *North Reach*, Sunday (Day 10). The wheelhouse at quarter to six, still dark, then first light; the boat starboard side to the stone pier, the brow and the temporary ramp the inspector passed for foot passengers and light freight. Fair, light northwest air. Then the crossing, and the Pruett slip. Sensory anchor: the diesel's thrum through her soles when it catches, and the coffee she has made Ned's way, too strong to drink till Pruett.
**Characters present:** Beth, Kenny, Dane.
**Want:** Beth wants to take the boat across as master, by her own hands, and bring her in clean. In the way: nobody has told her she can keep it.
**Surface action (pay it in action; never show the license):**
1. **The ordinary pleasure, changed.** Quarter to six, the heater blowing. She wipes Ned's smears off the wheelhouse windows with a dry dish towel until they're clear, the first time since she was twenty-two. One sentence, no comment. (Don't use the word "rag.")
2. **She checks with her hands.** Down in the forepeak with a flashlight, her hand flat on the inspector's temporary repair forward, cold and dry, the pump quiet. One clause. (Don't write "palm flat." Use "her hand on it," or similar.)
3. Kenny on the lines, back over on yesterday's boat, still gray from Thursday's flu (one clause names who he is). Master and one deck, which is what the certificate asks; say it as a fact of the two of them, not in procedure voice. Islanders coming aboard over the temporary ramp with bags and dogs. The store's front window lit up the road, nobody in it.
4. **Dane** on the car deck among the foot passengers, one-armed, the empty sleeve, looking up at the wheelhouse. She sees him through the clean glass. No dialogue between them. Don't make the scene about him.
5. **She takes her off.** Single up; Kenny takes in the last line; the light air off the land does her work and she lets it, the stern walking off the granite. **Interior passage (the chapter's second):** plain and physical, her hands on the wheel and the throttles, the boat answering. She has taken this boat off a slip in her head every morning for a year. No and-chain; sentences built on clauses, one long one at most.
6. The crossing in one or two sentences. Off Pruett the state's barge with a crane on it, headed out for the island slip. **"Ten days"** once, in Ned's voice in her memory: ten days, if the state got a barge out (4 of 4).
7. **The gust (pays the wheel plant from Ch 01).** Coming into the Pruett slip, the gust off the hill that sets her toward the north dolphin, the one she has taken in her head every morning for a year. She has the correction on before it lands, and there is nobody beside her to make it after her. One sentence for the gust, one for the correction.
8. The bow into the slip, the ramp down. At the top of the Pruett ramp, the two relief deputies and a gray car, waiting to take her to Portland.
**Subtext:** It's the first thing in the book she has done that nobody handed her, and the boat behaves. Ned's smears are gone and she doesn't mind and she does. Dane watching is the man who likes watching someone very good at a job he doesn't understand; it's for s6 to say so.
**Tension source:** Situational and personal: one boat, one gust, no one to catch her mistake, and the question nobody has answered.
**Texture (2):** the temporary repair forward; the gust off the hill and the north dolphin.
**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** Question. Kenny, at the ramp, as she comes down the outside ladder with her bag for Portland: **"Who's taking her home?"** Unanswered. This is the chapter's one other short withheld line; s6 answers it.
