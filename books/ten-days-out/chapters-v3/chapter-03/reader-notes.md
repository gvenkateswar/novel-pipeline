## Where I got bored

- **The tag argument in the store.** "Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's. I won't have my money in Ned's color, and I won't have you in a house I can't see." My attention went here. I could feel this was a clue (the yellow tag came up in Ch 1 too), but it plays as a bookkeeping dispute in the middle of what should be a fight about where Beth sleeps. I read it twice and still didn't care about the colors.
- **"I put you to bed the winter your mother got on the Bangor bus."** Backstory handed over in dialogue so I'll know it. It stopped the scene for me rather than pushing it.
- **The geography from "Beth and Dane went out the front, the bell behind them, round to the side wall."** Russ stays, Ned is suddenly there with an urn cup, Dane keeps a corner. I stopped picturing it and just waited for the key to change hands.
- Small one: "His sleeves were shoved up past the elbow, and his forearms were paler than his hands." The shaving scene had me, and this is the one sentence where it felt like the camera was still running after the shot was done.

## Where I got confused

- **Who is Russ?** In Ch 1 Russ was "the *Second Wind*", a boat name in a list. Now he comes out of Marr's back door, sits in the store office, and Dane hands Beth to him. Is he Tess's husband? Does he work there? I had no face for him and had to guess.
- **The store staging.** "Russ leaned in the office doorway in the generator's roar, where he could see the back window and the counter, and nothing said at the counter could reach him." I couldn't build the room. Where is the generator? Why can he see but not hear? It reads like it's setting something up, but I couldn't hold it.
- **What Beth saw in the storm log.** "Beth's eyes went down the night's column and stayed there." Then she covers it with her palm and lies to Dane ("Soup for the shut-ins"). I know she saw *something*, and I know she's protecting Tess. I don't know what it was. It might be Tess's 22:40 note about the light on her steps, but Tess had already told her that out loud, so why hide it? I'm unsure whether I'm supposed to know yet or whether I missed it.
- **The message on the phone.** "Pike: Q's request approved. Log daily. Do not disclose to witness." Two unexplained names in nine words. I guessed Pike was his boss. Then Dane says he "can't tell" her about the other one, and in the next breath tells her Q is "Homeland Security. Portland." So did he tell her or not? It felt like he gave away the very thing he'd just said he couldn't.
- **"Firm at thirty-one."** I didn't connect this to the Webbers Cove until the store clock line spelled it out. Once it was spelled out, fine.
- **The print at the foot of the steps.** Beth sees the dragged heel, so the man in the mudroom was the hood. Does Dane see it? Does she tell him? It's never said. He's in the doorway right behind her, and it doesn't come up again.
- **"North end. Not the house. That's what I'll tell them."** Then the last line has him telling Portland "where she was." Is he holding back from Portland on purpose because he suspects the leak runs through his own calls? Or is "north end" just near enough? I think the ambiguity is deliberate and I like the question, but on first read I wasn't sure whether he was protecting her or reporting her.
- **"The old hauler's ridge across his palms."** Dane has hauler's calluses? He's a marshal from away. If it's a plant, it's an interesting one. If it's a slip, it's a big one. I honestly can't tell which.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Dane leaves his phone face up on the table with the message open.** In Ch 1 he turned the screen away from her with his thumb, "as if he did it in his sleep." Last night his phone's glow may be how somebody found her. And this morning he sets it face up, unlocked, open on "Do not disclose to witness," and turns his back. That's the plot needing her to read it, not the man.
- **Dane hands Beth to Russ.** Hours ago he asked "Who knew you were here?" and opened this chapter with "it makes it a short list." Then he gives her to an islander I've barely met with "take her in and keep her where you can see her," and runs off. A man this careful wouldn't do that.
- **Getting the Ashby key from Ned.** Beth is hiding Ned from Dane as a possible leak ("Beth hadn't given him Ned in the dark"). Then she asks Ned for the key to tonight's hideout, with Dane standing at the corner. I get that she trusts Ned and Dane doesn't, but neither of them says a word about it, and that felt like the book looking away.
- **The consent exchange.** "Do you want—" / "Yes." / "Do you?" / "Yes." It's correct and it's tidy, and it sounded more like a checklist than two people out of breath. "Under. Not over." is the version of this that sounded like her.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Everybody talks in quips.** Tess: "My money I can count." Russ: "Didn't, did you." Dane: "I went in like a man looking for the restroom." Tess: "Restroom's for customers." Beth: "I'm not your money, Tess." Each line is fine. Stacked one after another they all have the same timing: a clipped setup and a dry turn. In the store scene I couldn't have told you who said what with the tags taken off.
- **Repeated physical tics across the three chapters:**
  - Dane's hand going to his hip: end of Ch 1, twice in Ch 2, again on the north road.
  - "Snow on his shoulders": twice in Ch 2, twice in this chapter.
  - "Nobody but me and God": this is the second time.
  - Watching his mouth: the stove, and then again on the porch ("She would sooner watch his mouth").
  - "He let her hear that" (Ch 2) and "He let that stand a moment" (Ch 3).

  By the third chapter I'm noticing them as a pattern instead of reading past them.
- **Stock kiss phrasing:** "He tasted of the store's terrible coffee." "The breath went out of him through his nose, long and slow." "Her mouth was asking for things before she'd thought them." "She hadn't decided to." This is the kind of thing in every romance. The rest of the scene (the shaking housing, the cold hands, the zipper sticking and her swearing at it) is specific, and these lines stand out against it.
- **Explaining after showing.** "The call to hold it needed open sky, and open sky meant going outside and leaving her at the counter beside Arsenault. Dane stayed where he was." The last sentence would have done the work alone, and I'd have felt it more if I'd worked out what he was giving up myself.
- **Neat scene-ending lines, every time.** "She saw him decide to sit in it." / "She didn't look up." / "Up the slope the yellow windows stood lit for nobody." / "...where she was." Ch 1 and Ch 2 ended the same way, on a quiet, loaded closing line. By now I can see the button coming.

## What I want to know next

Plenty, and that matters most:

- **Who is the leak: Tess, Ned, or Dane's own phone?** The book has now quietly built three suspects. Tess has the fold of hundreds, the log Beth hid, the 22:40 note, and Arsenault's smile. Ned has "Nobody but me and God," he knew she'd be home, and he now knows about Ashby. Dane called Portland right before the mudroom door went quiet, and he just called them again. That's a real question, and I want it answered.
- **What is Q, and what is Dane logging?** Is Beth bait? Is Homeland Security after the island rather than Pelletier?
- **Where is the *Margaret Ann* going with her lights off?** I assume it's coming round to the north point, which has "water on three sides."
- **Who is the hood?** He knows the fish houses "like he'd built them" and rows like someone born to it, so he's an islander. Whose son is he?
- **Does Dane let the Webbers Cove go?** He missed Saturday noon to stay beside her. I want to know if it cost him the boat.
- **The hauler's ridge on his palms.** What's Dane's history on the water?
- **Will she ever let him not write her down?** "Don't write me down" ties back to her father and the man in blue pen in Ch 1. It's the emotional hook, and it works.

## Best moment

> She put her back to the engine housing. It was warm already through her coat and getting warmer, and it shook against her with every stroke. She took his jacket in her fist and brought him down to her.

And the stop, which is what makes the kiss scene mean something:

> He leaned in, and his jacket swung against her hip, and something in the inside pocket pressed into her through everything, square and hard. *Log daily*, and the other thing, the one he couldn't tell her.

The phone from the morning comes back as a physical object pressed between them at the worst possible moment. Then "His hands came out from under the wool, and the cold went in after them." I felt the cold. The runner-up is the razor tapped twice on the basin ("I can quit doing that." / "Don't."), which does more for her father and for the two of them in two lines than any of the backstory does.

## Prediction section

What I expect, written before Ch 04 so it can be checked:

1. Someone comes to Ashby tonight or tomorrow night. It'll come by water, probably from the *Margaret Ann*, because the north point is surrounded by water and the dragger went dark just as they arrived.
2. Tess is mixed up in the money side: cash coming through the store, maybe the bags and tags as a way to move it. She isn't the one trying to hurt Beth, and she'll turn out to be protecting her in her own way. (The cot blankets put away under the counter read as hurt, not guilt.)
3. Ned is clean, and the book will make me doubt him for at least one more chapter before he proves it, likely on the water, since he "couldn't swim a stroke."
4. Q's request is to use Beth to draw out somebody on the island, and Dane disagrees with it. She'll find out what's in the log at the midpoint, and it will be a betrayal and also not as bad as she thinks.
5. The hood is a local young man tied to someone Beth knows, possibly a Marr.
6. Dane loses the Webbers Cove and ends up on a working boat on this island.
7. They sleep together within the next two chapters, and only after he's told her something true about the log.

## Listener pass (heard, not seen)

- **"Pike: Q's request approved."** A single letter as a name is lost by ear. Out loud, "Q" sounds like "queue" or "cue," and with no visual colon "Pike" sounds like a word, not a sender. A listener won't know a text message is being read.
- **"The hood."** Used as a name for the man ("The hood stood at the propane cage," "The hood turned"). On the page I get it. By ear, "the hood" could be his clothing, a car hood or a gangster, and the first time it's ambiguous.
- **Name pileup in S2.** Russ, Tess, Ned, Arsenault, the *Margaret Ann*, Ashby, "the Connecticut place." That's seven proper nouns in one scene, two of them new, with no visual cue that a word is a boat name. A listener would lose Russ completely.
- **Tags in the store fight.** The "Beth said" tags on short lines ("Can't. Not tonight, Tess," Beth said / "I'm not your money, Tess," Beth said) come out choppy and repetitive aloud, but without them the speakers would merge, because everyone has the same rhythm.
- **The shed paragraph that starts "Then they warmed, and moved."** It's one long run of "and... and... and." Aloud it works: it speeds up and runs out of breath the way the moment does. It's the best-sounding passage in the chapter.
- **The opening line.** "They sat the rest of the night out behind the chimney, and nobody came back." This is clear by ear and drops the listener right back in.
- **The ending.** "...while he told Portland, in words she couldn't hear, where she was." This lands aloud, but the emphasis is uncertain: a narrator has to choose between irony and tenderness, and the text doesn't say which.
