Winner: B

**Why:** I bought an open-door book for this couple and this danger, and B gives me the shed: his cold hands, him warming them under his arms like a man in the ferry line, the engine keeping time, him listening at the door for the dark boat with his mouth on her. So the phone pressing into her bare stomach afterward actually costs something. A gets me to the edge and slams on the brakes.

**Where A lost me:** "He leaned in, and his jacket swung against her hip, and something in the inside pocket pressed into her through everything, square and hard." (~60%). The kiss and the hands under the wool were working. Then the phone ends it before anything happens, and the fight that follows feels like the book stopping the scene on purpose. I'd keep reading, but I'd feel shortchanged, and in this sub-genre that's how I start skimming to find out whether the next one is going to stall too.

**Where B lost me:** It didn't. The porch coda at the end (~90%), the "North end" phone call, slows down, but by then I'd been paid and I wanted to see what the dark boat means. "He stepped back half a pace, opened his jacket and stuck both hands up under his arms" (~30%) looked for a second like another stall, then it turned out to be the best thing he does in the chapter.

**Confidence:** high

**Same-author check:** Yes. The opening, the closing fight and the porch are word for word the same in both; only the middle of the shed scene differs.
