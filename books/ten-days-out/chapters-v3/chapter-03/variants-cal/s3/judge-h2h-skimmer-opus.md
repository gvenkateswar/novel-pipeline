Winner: B

**Why:** I'm tired and I came for the couple and the danger, and B gives me both at once: the dark boat out the door, the gun he turns away from her, the engine she uses to set the beat. A puts its hands on her and then stops before anything happens.

**Where A lost me:** At about 55%, "She took her hands back, and he stopped at once. His hands came out from under the wool, and the cold went in after them." The book is open-door, it had me leaning in, and then the phone in his pocket closes the door after one kiss and a hand on her back. From there to the end it's the same argument and the same porch call as B, and my thumb sped up through "I'd rather be kissed by a man I can check" because nothing had been paid for yet. Next I'd hit the porch, "He had the phone to his ear and his face turned up" (~88%), and skim right through it.

**Where B lost me:** Barely. At about 90%, the porch, "He had the phone to his ear and his face turned up. Down the slope the generator hammered in its shed..." I've had the generator hammering for a dozen paragraphs and now it's a man alone on a phone, so I skimmed to the last line. The sex scene itself never made me skim. The hands-under-the-arms bit, "patient as a man in the ferry line," slowed me down in a good way.

**Confidence:** High. The opening (generator and pull cord) and the closing (argument and porch call) are word for word the same. The only difference is whether the middle delivers, and B's does.

**Same-author check:** Yes. The opening and closing are the same text, with the same rhythms and the same "the cold went in" beat.
