Winner: B

**Why:** B gets straight to the two of them, and it's funny and warm: the armpit-warming, "How long does that take?", "Don't you laugh". The danger is still in it, one look at the dark boat and the gun hip turned away, but it never takes the scene off them.

**Where A lost me:** At about 17%. "A skiff could be off her by now. With the set going they'd never hear an outboard on the point, or a boot on the drive. The north point knew this engine's note." The engine is running and he's grinning, I want the kiss, and instead I get a paragraph of who could hear what. After that it asks permission over and over: "Yes?", "Higher. Will you?", "Here?", "Beth?", "Can you feel that?" The spruce snow and "He had taken the watch off her without a word" are lovely, but I had to get through the planning to reach them.

**Where B lost me:** It didn't. The nearest thing was at about 67%, "steady as the injector," where for one line I was thinking about the machine and not her.

**Confidence:** medium. A's watch-keeping is the best single idea in either version, and a reader who wants more suspense might take it. B is the one I'd read without my thumb moving.

**Same-author check:** Yes. The opening walk and the notebook ending are identical, and so is the plain, cold-weather voice.
