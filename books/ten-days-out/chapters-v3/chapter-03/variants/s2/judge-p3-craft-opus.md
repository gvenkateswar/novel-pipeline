2. **Why:** In B's store scene I could tell who was standing where and Tess sounded like a person with her own grudge. 3. **Where A lost me:** "The bell. A tall man in a good jacket, clean-shaven. Arsenault. The *Margaret Ann*, lit all through the blackout." (~32%). This came right after "Inside, the generator quit, and the lights with it," and Tess somehow both going through the curtain and holding the log open on the counter. It reads like stage notes. I came back for the hooded man's breath behind the curtain, which is the scariest thing in either version. Then I lost it again at "She got Tess in, hanging the keys straight on the hook, her knuckles in the charging cords among the phones, Ned's in its rubber band." (~50%), a sentence carrying three bits of business and no feeling.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Going back in, Beth picked the keys for Tess's Ford off the charging shelf where the hook had thrown them and hung them up again, her knuckles in among the cords and phones, Ned's in its rubber band." (~48%). It's the same inventory beat, and my eye slid over it. It only cost me a line, though, because Tess comes in talking right after it.

5. **Confidence:** high. B's middle also has a real ending (Tess putting the cot blankets back under the counter, one and then the other). A's ends on a hand on a log.
