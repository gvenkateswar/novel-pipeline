2. **Why:** In B, Tess folds two cot blankets before Beth even walks in, then puts them back under the counter without looking up, and that made me care about the store scene. A's version is a chase followed by a woman reading her ledger at me.

3. **Where A lost me:** At about 49%, in Tess's speech: "Net had you aboard. Your back steps had you home. Twenty-two forty." She held up her left hand. The time was on it in ballpoint, and in the storm log open between them." Then straight into "Your pay rides to Pruett in my bag every other week. Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's." That's one dense block of times, logs and bag colors, and my thumb sped up there. The chase just before it was good, with the two of them running together and her shouting "Sea coming. Stand still." But I also lost track of who was where in the key-hook paragraph ("the hook by the back door hung crooked from Dane's shoulder"). When was he even back there?

4. **Where B lost me:** It nearly did at the start of the middle scene, about 27%: "the time in ballpoint across her knuckles under a column of other people's gallons," with Dane already out the door. That opens on a timestamp and inventory with the man not in the room, which is the kind of opening I usually quit on. It got me back within a page, first with Arsenault's counted money, then "I put you to bed the winter your mother got on the Bangor bus" / "Then you know I don't go where I'm put." The Ned key bit tacked on after the store (about 63%) felt like an errand.

5. **Confidence: medium.** A's chase keeps the couple together and moving, and a different reader could pick it for that. I'd still rather spend my evening with B's Tess than with A's ledger.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The first and last scenes are word for word the same, and the middle scenes share the same lines and the same cadence.
