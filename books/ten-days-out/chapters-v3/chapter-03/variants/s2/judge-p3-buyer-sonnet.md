Winner: B

1. Why: In B I always knew where I was and who Tess was to Beth ("I put you to bed the winter your mother got on the Bangor bus"), so the walk up to the shed mattered; A's store scene had more menace but I kept losing track of who was standing where.

2. Where A lost me: "Beth went round behind the counter. The storm log lay open there in Tess's hand. She picked up a carton of milk left by the till." (about 38%). Tess has just left through the curtain, so how is the log in her hand? The blackout, the hooded man at arm's length and the dropped milk then ran together, and I stopped picturing the room. Tess's speech after she falls ("Shaking, bright, furious... Net had you aboard...") also arrives like a recital.

3. Where B lost me: "Over Tess's shoulder, through the office curtain and the back window, a man in a hood stood by the propane cage at the back step, facing in." (about 45%). It's convenient, and the chase after it is the same sea-in-the-lane business as A, so I skimmed to Tess's bag speech. It cost me a few lines, not the chapter.

4. Confidence: medium. A's close-quarters hooded man is the more exciting beat, and some buyers would take it. The Tess/blankets thread and the clean line through B won it for me.

5. Same-author check: Yes. The opening and the generator shed are identical, and the voice is the same in both middles.
