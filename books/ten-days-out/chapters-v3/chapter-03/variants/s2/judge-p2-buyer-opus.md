1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B I always knew where I was standing. The chase happens, then everyone talks about it in the store, so I'm ready for the shed when it comes. In A I spent the middle of the chapter working out who had gone out which door, and I almost gave up on it.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 45%: "She got Tess in, hanging the keys straight on the hook, her knuckles in the charging cords among the phones, Ned's in its rubber band." I'd already wobbled at about 30%. "Tess was through the curtain, and the back door banged open" and then two lines later "The storm log lay open there in Tess's hand," so is Tess in the room or not? By the keys line I'd stopped following and was skimming for Dane. Before all that, the hooded man standing in the curtain an arm's length from her, breathing, is the scariest thing in either version, and Dane's "Stay right there, friend" got me. But I couldn't enjoy it because I was lost.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 48%: "Your pay rides to Pruett in my bag every other week. Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's." Bag colors and whose money goes where. My thumb sped up there, and it had already sped up once at the Ford keys "on their MARR'S tag" at about 38%. It came back when he walked in with snow on his shoulders and her hand went to his collar. The scene's ending, with the minute hand passing twelve and Arsenault's money on the counter, is quiet, but I followed it. Russ's "Is she, though? Doesn't look it" is the line I'd tell a friend about.

5. **Confidence: medium.** A has the better scare and the more protective Dane, and on a better night I might have forgiven the muddle for that. B is the one I could follow in bed on my phone.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The opening and closing scenes are the same word for word, and both middles use the same island voice and the same props.
