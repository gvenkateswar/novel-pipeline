Winner: B

1. Why: In B, Tess sounds like a person nobody else on the island could be ("My money I can count." / "I put you to bed the winter your mother got on the Bangor bus."), and the scene reads as one continuous piece, while A's middle goes to fragments and shuffled logistics that blur who is where.

2. Where A lost me: "Inside, the generator quit, and the lights with it." (~32%). From there the scene runs on staccato stage directions ("The bell. A tall man in a good jacket, clean-shaven. Arsenault."). The line "She found the Golds behind her. When she turned, his money was on the counter, counted, and she still didn't know the price" is a clue I couldn't parse. Tess's "Twenty-two forty" speech comes after the chase and lands as a recap. I had to rebuild the room in my head more than once.

3. Where B lost me: "Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's. I won't have my money in Ned's color" (~51%). The bag-tag business is a small ledger and I skimmed it, though the "I'm not your money" exchange after it recovers me. The ending beat, Tess putting the two cot blankets back one at a time without looking up, is the best image in either scene.

4. Confidence: medium. A has the more frightening beat, the hood standing an arm's length away with something in his pocket. B has the better people and the cleaner prose. The shared kitchen and shed scenes are identical, so the whole call rests on the middle.

5. Same-author check: Yes. Same close-third, same dry cadence and same plain concrete nouns, and the two middles share lines.
