1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** B's middle scene keeps the same steady rhythm as the shaving scene and the shed scene around it, and I always knew who was standing where. A's middle breaks into fragments and I kept going back to work out who was in the room and whose hand was holding the book.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Beth went round behind the counter. The storm log lay open there in Tess's hand." (~33%). One line earlier Tess went through the curtain and out the back door, so whose hand is that? After this the scene turns into telegraph: "The bell. A tall man in a good jacket, clean-shaven. Arsenault. The *Margaret Ann*, lit all through the blackout." Then "Nobody answered him. Tess wasn't there to." Then Beth, on the back step holding Tess up, somehow shouts "Sea coming. Stand still." to Dane down a lane. Then Tess's whole speech lands in one block ("Twenty-two forty." Shaking, bright, furious...), and "What am I looking at?" has no clear speaker. The hood in the curtain gap ("a long wet whisper of rubber on wood... breath, slow, through a nose, close") is the best passage in either middle scene. It's real menace and it's close up. But it sits inside staging I had to puzzle out, and I stopped feeling it.

4. **Where B lost me:** "In Tess's office the hook by the back door hung crooked from Dane's shoulder, and the two keys for Tess's Ford lay on the charging shelf on their MARR'S tag; she hung them back with her knuckles in the cords, among the island's phones, Ned's in its rubber band." (~40%). That's a list of objects in a sentence that wants to be a camera, and my eye slid over it. The Arsenault coda also arrives after the scene's heat is spent and ends on a ledger beat ("Arsenault's money lay counted on the counter where Tess hadn't touched it"). Quiet, but it holds. Otherwise B didn't lose me. The chase reads cleanly ("like a man going into his own kitchen"). Russ gets a voice nobody else has ("Is she, though? Doesn't look it."), and Tess's speech is broken up by business (the bag, the safe she kicks shut), so it sounds like a person talking.

5. **Confidence: medium.** A has the stronger single moment, the hooded man at arm's length breathing. A reader who wants the menace up close could pick A. On rhythm and on telling the voices apart, B wins.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The opening and closing scenes are word for word the same, and the middle scenes share whole lines ("Me and God. I know," the restroom joke, the yellow tag that "sang").
