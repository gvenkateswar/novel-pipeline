Winner: A

1. Why: A puts the hooded man in the room with her, an arm's length away, smelling of bait and diesel, while her hand lies next to Tess's open log. That gives the palm-on-the-book gesture a reason and makes the Tess scene feel like the cost of the scare. B stages the same beats as a tidy chase and a list of keys on a hook, so the threat stays at a distance.

3. Where A lost me: "Beth went round behind the counter. The storm log lay open there in Tess's hand. She picked up a carton of milk left by the till." (~38%). The geography and who is holding what blur, and the scene opens already in motion with "Dane had gone in ahead with Russ" and no ground under it. The clipped back-and-forth ("Nobody answered him. Tess wasn't there to.") is close to showing off. I forgave it because the hooded man in the curtain gap is the best paragraph in the middle.

4. Where B lost me: "In Tess's office the hook by the back door hung crooked from Dane's shoulder, and the two keys for Tess's Ford lay on the charging shelf on their MARR'S tag; she hung them back with her knuckles in the cords, among the island's phones, Ned's in its rubber band." (~40%). It's a ledger sentence, and the middle drifts into inventory after that. Russ's "Is she, though? Doesn't look it." and Dane's "I'd like to see which boat he rows home to" read like lines that exist to move plot. Slightly later (~47%) "At the till, Tess had seen both hands" is neat, but by then the Tess confrontation is a speech delivered to a woman who isn't in danger.

5. Confidence: medium. A is murkier on the page, B is cleaner, and a reader who wants clarity could flip it. A has the sharper moment and B has nothing that matches it.

6. Same-author check: Yes. The outer scenes are word-for-word identical and the middle shares the same voice, Tess's speech, and the same devices (the keys, the yellow tag, the milk).
