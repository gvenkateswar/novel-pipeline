Winner: B

Persona: Craft. Model: opus. Pair: p1 (A vs B, middle scene differs).

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** In B the store scene turns on Tess, and she gets lines nobody else in the book could say ("No. My money I can count."). She ends the scene by putting the cot blankets back under the counter without a word. A's store scene is the same furniture with the chase stuck on the front, and Tess delivers it all as one crowded speech.

3. **Where A lost me:** "In Tess's office the hook by the back door hung crooked from Dane's shoulder, and the two keys for Tess's Ford lay on the charging shelf on their MARR'S tag; she hung them back with her knuckles in the cords, among the island's phones, Ned's in its rubber band." (~41%). It's one sentence of key-and-shelf housekeeping right after the best running in the chapter, and I had to read it twice to see whose shoulder hit what. Then Tess's speech sank it: "Net had you aboard. Your back steps had you home. Twenty-two forty." (~49%). That's the time, the steps, the cot and the porch all in one breath. On top of that comes "Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's.", which reads like a ledger.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Your pay rides to Pruett in my bag every other week. Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's. I won't have my money in Ned's color, and I won't have you in a house I can't see." (~51%). It's the same ledger line, and my eyes slid over it here too. B wins back the next three lines, though: "I'm not your money, Tess." / "No. My money I can count." / "Then you know I don't go where I'm put." That's two women arguing. A gives Tess a monologue. B's chase also costs something. Beth is held back on the step and watches it from a distance, so she gets less of "her shore" than A gave her.

5. **Confidence: medium.** A's chase is better: Beth runs because "he didn't know where the stones quit". A's noon beat also ends well ("Arsenault's money lay counted on the counter where Tess hadn't touched it."). But B's scene has a shape and a last image, and A's middle is a chase followed by errands.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The opening and closing scenes are word for word the same, and both middles are built from the same lines.
