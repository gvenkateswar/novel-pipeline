1. Winner: B

2. Why: B gives Tess and Dane actual voices and makes the dark harbor a real scare, while A plays the same beats a little flatter and ends with the couple looking safe rather than watched.

3. Where A lost me: Dane's refusal of Russ, ~35%: "Thank you. I can't, because any boat on this island could be theirs, and I can't read this sea well enough to know a bad one when I'm standing on it..." Nobody talks like that. It is the author explaining the plot through his mouth. Close behind it, the road scene with the unlit skiff (~60%) is a surveillance beat I watched from a distance, and "Calloway, you look like a wet cat" lands as a stray line.

4. Where B lost me: ~70%, "He looked at her, a man who had talked all day to Russ and Tess and the snow, and now he didn't say a word." The narrator steps in to tell me how to feel about his silence, and Dane has barely spoken all day anyway. A small stumble, not a stop.

5. Confidence: medium. The chapters share about 70% of their text, so the call rests on the store scene and the shed.

6. Same-author check: Yes. The opening and most of the dialogue are identical, and the voice is the same.

Craft notes behind the pick:
- B's Tess gets a real line ("I walked you home from every dance on this island, and I'll walk you home from this one") and a refusal that costs something ("Then tomorrow."). A's Tess is mostly logistics.
- B gives Dane a joke ("like a man looking for the restroom") and a quip at Arsenault ("The gentleman's having the free coffee"). That makes him sound different from the locals. A's Dane sounds like everyone else.
- B's shed kiss has physical comedy (the stuck zipper, "she said a word to it") and a better moment (his thumbs waiting, her hand over his). A's kiss is good but tamer.
- B's "so the engine's noise stood between them" is the best staging beat in either version.
- A's ending is warmer: her arm against his while he tells Portland where she is. B's ending is colder and more atmospheric, with the sheet dropping from her opening hand. I prefer B's, though a buyer might prefer A's.
- A's calibration risk is small. Neither version reads like A1 (procedure and codes). A's Dane/Russ speech comes closest to a business exchange.
