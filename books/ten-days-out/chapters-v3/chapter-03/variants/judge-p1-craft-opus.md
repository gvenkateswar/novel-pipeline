# Judge: p1, Craft persona (opus)

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** These two are nearly the same chapter. A is the one where people get to be funny and a little clumsy. Dane goes into the fish houses "like a man looking for the restroom," Tess says she walked Beth home from every dance, and Beth swears at a stuck zipper in the middle of a kiss. B trims all of that and ends up a slightly cleaner, slightly colder chapter that explains its own last image to me.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Ashby," Beth said. The Connecticut people's summer place on the north point, shut up for winter, water on three sides and one road, and Ned started its generator every storm, so a generator running there would only look like Ned. (~60%). A whole real-estate listing gets crammed in after one word of dialogue, and I skimmed it. Shortly after, "the harbor was black where Beth had been" (~66%) made me stop and work out what it meant, and I still don't know.

4. **Where B lost me:** "One of me against a dragger crew on the water is a body count, and I don't get to pick whose." (~53%). That's a marshal showing off how competent he is, and it sounds like every other marshal. Then at the end: "North end, not the house: that was what he'd said he would say, and she had nothing to check it against but his mouth." (~98%). It's the author stepping in to explain an ending that had already landed.

5. **Confidence: low.** It's close to a coin flip. B's store scene is actually better: the hooded man standing just inside the door, "the puddle spreading under his boots," is creepier than A's version, where he's glimpsed through two windows. B also has the better small exchange in the shed, "That's the phone." / "Yes." A wins because its people sound more like themselves, not because its sentences are better.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Most of the sentences are word-for-word the same. These are two edits of one draft.
