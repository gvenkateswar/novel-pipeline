2. **Why:** In A the middle scene starts with the limping man and Dane running into the sea, and after that Dane is in the store with her, so when she puts her thumb on the cold skin of his neck in front of Tess, it lands. In B I spent the first half of the scene in the store with Tess while Dane stood outside "at the dock rail," and I got him back only through the window.

3. **Where A lost me:** around 38-42%, in Tess's two speeches back to back: "You went down my back slope after him on top of the tide," Tess said. ... "Your back steps had a light on last night, and the net had you aboard. Twenty-two forty." followed by another Tess paragraph. I couldn't tell who she was talking to, Dane or Beth, and then "Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's. I won't have my money in Ned's color" is bag-tag business I read twice and gave up on. The chase got me back past it, and so did the thumb on his neck.

4. **Where B lost me:** around 30-32%, the Tess block that starts "Net had you aboard. Your back steps had you home, and a light going on them. Twenty-two forty..." and runs right on into "Cot's made up. You'll sleep at mine." It's then followed by "Through the front window, Dane stood at the dock rail with his back to the harbor." It's one long speech from the aunt while my man is outside a window, and that's where my thumb moved. Then "The phone worked only outdoors, and outdoors meant leaving her with the dragger's skipper" (~37%) read like somebody explaining the rules to me. The chase in B comes too late, after I'd already drifted.

5. **Confidence: medium.** Both versions use the same lines from Tess and Ned, and A's opening is a bit disorienting ("The planks down from her back steps..." and whose steps, exactly). Still, A keeps the two of them in the room together and gives her a real touch, and B doesn't.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** The first and last scenes are word for word the same, and so are most of the middle scene's lines, just reshuffled.
