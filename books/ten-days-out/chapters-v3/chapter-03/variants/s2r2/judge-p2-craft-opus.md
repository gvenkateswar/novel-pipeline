Persona: Craft. Model: opus. Read: chapters/chapter-03/variants/s2r2/blind/p2-A.md and p2-B.md (only the middle scene differs).

1. **Winner: A.**

2. **Why:** In A's store scene, Tess and Dane get to talk without a "Beth said / Tess said" stamped on every line, and Dane gets one look ("his eyes on her hand") that keeps the couple charged. B tags so much that the room goes flat right when the scene should be quick.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Russ took the office doorway behind her, in the generator's roar, with the lane and the counter both in sight." (~48%). Here the scene stops to check sight lines, and the third "Through the window, Dane was back at the rail" had already made the cutaways to the window feel like a tic. Before that, Tess's first speech is one long block ("Net had you aboard... You'll sleep at mine.", ~30%) that I read as a speech rather than heard as a woman talking.

4. **Where B lost me:** "'Then you know I don't go where I'm put,' Beth said." (~48%). That's the third tagged line in a row in the Tess exchange ("'I'm not your money, Tess,' Beth said." comes just before it), and her best comeback lands like a deposition. Right before it: "Russ took the office doorway as Beth went back in, where he could see the lane and the till, inside the roar." (~45%), the same sight-line bookkeeping as A, only clunkier. B does have the better single moments: "Marshal can eat on the porch" is the one real laugh Tess gets in either version, and "his face turned up the slope to her" beats A's man hugging a post. But the line-by-line rhythm is worse.

5. **Confidence: low.** It's close to a coin flip. Both middle scenes are staging-heavy, and B's two good beats nearly carry it. I picked A on ear.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scene, same jokes, same sentences ("Restroom's for customers," "Me and God. I know"), and the same slip where Ned gets two lines in a row ("'Nobody but—' Ned said."). This is one writer doing two passes.
