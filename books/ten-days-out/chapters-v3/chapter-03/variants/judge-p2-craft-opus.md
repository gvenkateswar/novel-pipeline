# Judge: Craft persona, Opus. p2-A vs p2-B (Ch 03)

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** In B the people finally sound like different people. Dane has a dry mouth on him ("I went in like a man looking for the restroom"), Tess gets a line only Tess would say about walking Beth home from every dance, and the kiss has friction in it (the stuck zipper, the bitten lip). A is the same chapter with the voices sanded flat, though A has the better last page.

3. **Where A lost me:** ~30%, Dane turning down Russ: "Thank you. I can't, because any boat on this island could be theirs, and I can't read this sea well enough to know a bad one when I'm standing on it, and whoever takes her across leaves a wake the whole bay can follow." That's a man reading out a risk assessment, one clause after another. He talks like that again at ~58% ("Everybody on this island walks that shore like that except me."), earnest and with no edge. After that I was reading A to get to the shed. A did win me back at the end: Beth walks out the door and stands "until her arm was against his... while he told Portland where she was" is the best sentence in either version.

4. **Where B lost me:** ~58%, the safe-house paragraph: "The Connecticut people's summer place on the north point, shut up for winter, water on three sides and one road, and Ned started its generator every storm, so a generator running there would only look like Ned. Her father's fish house had the tide over its sill till Monday." That's a list of reasons dressed up as a sentence. A has the same block and it's worse there. B also strains at the close: "out of her opening hand" and "the yellow windows stood lit for nobody" are the writer reaching. Also, "the harbor was black where Beth had been" made me stop and work out what it meant.

5. **Confidence: medium.** About 80% of the two texts is word for word the same. B wins on dialogue and on the *Margaret Ann* going dark, which is a scarier image than A's skiff. A wins on the ending: B has her drop the sheet and back away, A has her step out beside him. If the ending counted for more, it would be close to a coin flip.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same draft, two passes over it, and most paragraphs are identical.
