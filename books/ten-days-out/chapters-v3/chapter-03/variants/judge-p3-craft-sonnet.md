# Judge: Craft persona (Sonnet), Ch 3 p3-A vs p3-B

1. Winner: B

2. Why: B lets the hooded man stand silent in the store door and lets the dragger's lights go out without explaining them, while A has Dane narrate what the skiff is doing and has characters make speeches where a gesture would do.

3. Where A lost me: "Thank you. I can't, because any boat on this island could be theirs, and I can't read this sea well enough to know a bad one when I'm standing on it, and whoever takes her across leaves a wake the whole bay can follow." (~25%). It is one breathless sentence, and Dane sounds like a briefing. The same problem shows up later: "It's looking at water, not at the road. Stand still and we're two fence posts." (~58%). The skiff scene is atmospheric until Dane explains it. Smaller: "Tess's voice went sharp as a gaff" reads like a dial turned up.

4. Where B lost me: "He looked at her, a man who had talked all day to Russ and Tess and the urn and the snow, and now he didn't say a word." (~63%). It is a bit of authorial wink-nudging after the grin, and the grin landed fine on its own. The chase also fizzles. "He rowed," Dane says, and the pursuit becomes a report. I didn't stop, but my attention dipped there.

5. Confidence: medium. A's ending is the better beat, with Beth leaving the sheet and standing against his arm while "he told Portland where she was." That is character and irony. B's sheet "out of her opening hand" is the better image but she is passive. I still take B for sentence control and restraint. The two chapters are about 85% identical, so this comes down to the store scene and the ending.

6. Same-author check: Yes. The razor scene, the phone scene, the shed kiss and the Tess ledger beat are nearly word for word the same, and the voice is identical.
