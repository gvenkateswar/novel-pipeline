# Judge: Skimmer (opus), p3-A vs p3-B

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** In B the man with the dragged heel is standing in the store dripping on the floor with the couple right there, and then the whole dragger goes dark at once, so I stopped skimming. In A he's a shape out a back window and a skiff that comes and goes, and I'd already started sliding past both.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Tess was already sealing the morning bag, snapping a yellow tag through the zipper, fast, talking to Beth, to the bag, to the empty store. 'Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's. I won't have my money in Ned's color.'" (~44%). A color code for deposit bags, right after a chase, is where my thumb sped up. It kept moving through the 22:40 log business, and I didn't really slow down until the road. The skiff ("It's looking at water, not at the road. Stand still and we're two fence posts") at ~65% felt like a marshal giving a briefing.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Behind her Tess was snapping a yellow tag through the zipper of the morning's deposit bag and talking the whole time. 'Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's.'" (~44%). It's the same bag ledger, and I skimmed it the same way, but B pulled me back faster. "The store had always looked up at the back of her house" is one line, where A spends a whole paragraph, and then the bell rings and Dane comes in.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The two chapters are almost the same text: the shave, the phone, and the shed are word for word. A's ending (her arm against his while he tells Portland where she is) is warmer, and a romance reader might pick it for that. But B has the better threat, the dragger going dark, and the better last image, the sheet dropping out of her hand.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Most of the sentences are identical; one is a light trim of the other.
