# Judge: Buyer persona, Opus, p1 (A vs B)

1. **Winner: B.**

2. **Why:** In B the man with the dragged heel walks into the store and stands by the door with his hood turned toward her, puddle spreading under his boots, while I'm right there in the room with her. In A he's a shape out a back window. And B's ending, where she has "nothing to check it against but his mouth," left me wanting the next chapter more.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 45%, the store chase: "Beth was at the office doorway a step behind him: the hook with the two keys for Tess's Ford on their MARR'S tag, the shelf of phones charging under it, the back door banging, and the step empty." That's a list of shelf contents in the middle of a chase, and I started skimming. Right after it comes "Tess was already snapping a yellow tag through the zipper of the morning's deposit bag... 'Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's.'" I didn't know why I was being told about bag colors, and I skimmed through to "Don't you look at my book like the bank does." I came back for the generator shed and stayed to the end.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 45%, the same spot but shorter: "Going past, she saw the hook with the two keys for Tess's Ford, and the island's phones charging on the shelf under it," then "Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's. I won't have my money in Ned's color." Same inventory, same bag tags, just less of it. I sped up and slowed down again at "Net had you aboard," which Tess says low, "which Tess never did." That caught me.

5. **Confidence: low.** These are 90% the same chapter. A's kiss runs a little hotter ("She caught his lower lip in her teeth," her hand over his on her ribs), and an open-door buyer would notice that. I'd still take B's room with the hood in it, plus "That's the phone." / "Yes." Those two lines say more than A's longer speech.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** The same sentences word for word in the razor scene and the shed. One is a light revision of the other.
