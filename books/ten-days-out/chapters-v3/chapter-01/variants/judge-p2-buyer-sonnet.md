# Judge: Buyer, Sonnet, p2-A vs p2-B

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** I wanted to be in the room with Beth and Reyes, and A gives me more of that. He says "I'll try to look at you more," then actually does it. There's the wrist, the mother-and-the-counter banter in her kitchen, and the shoulders hunched at the cold window. In B he mostly stares at the harbor and talks to the window about forecasts.

3. **Where A lost me:** the boat-slot paragraph, about 60%: "Russ was the *Second Wind* and four hundred traps. Ned was the *North Reach*. The summer man was a varnished picnic boat with her father's lines under the paint. She had a hull for everyone she knew..." It stops the scene to explain her head. I was still with her, but my thumb sped up. The first-page binder inventory ("Handle down. Chain hard. The D-ring packed with slush...") made me squint for a few lines too, but the lobster crate rescued it.

4. **Where B lost me:** the same boat-slot paragraph, about 62%: "Everybody she knew sat somewhere in a boat. A man with no boat was from away, and from away was weather..." It's longer than A's, and I'd already had the idea. The other slow spot is the two-deputy speech on the ramp, about 35%: "...where a second deputy meets us, who would be here already except he's behind a plow on Route 1 doing about nine miles an hour." It's a man doing logistics at a woman. The ending kitchen scene, "He told the window the forecast she'd heard before light...", is thin and has no spark.

5. **Confidence:** Medium. The two are about 85% the same text, and B has a few lines I liked more. The line "a decision somebody had made about herring in August" is one. The voice-mimicking bit is another, since it gives Beth a secret. But A has more heat between the leads and a better close.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The scenes, jokes, dialogue and ending are nearly identical, and only the line-level choices differ.
