# Judge: Craft persona (opus), p2-A vs p2-B

1. **Winner: B**

2. **Why:** B's people sound like themselves. The plumber "chained the way he billed," Ned's bucket of steam, Tess's "Don't ma'am me. I charge extra for it," and a marshal who argues with logistics ("behind a plow on Route 1 doing about nine miles an hour"). A has the prettier sentences, but it keeps stopping to tell me what Beth feels about him, and after a while I could hear the author tapping me on the shoulder.

3. **Where A lost me:** around 38%: "I watch everything that leaves your hands. It's a habit. I'll try to look at you more." He says the subtext out loud, and from there A keeps naming the longing: "She wanted him to say something else, something stupid about the boots, so she could go back to not liking him" (~48%), and then in the kitchen, "She packed slower than she needed to. She folded the sweater twice." (~93%). Each one is fine on its own. Stacked up, I started skimming the interior paragraphs to get back to the talk.

4. **Where B lost me:** around 93%: "He told the window the forecast she'd heard before light and the marine forecast she'd heard after it, and asked the window whether forecasts out here were considered advisory." The kitchen scene goes to reported speech and goes slack. There's also a small dip at ~30%, where the "Other people's voices were a thing she did, alone, at the sink" paragraph steps out of the scene to explain her. It didn't lose me, but I noticed the gear change.

5. **Confidence: medium.** A's subpoena beat ("Somebody had sat at a desk in a warm room and spelled her right") and its rescue paragraph beat B's. If the reader is the Buyer and wants the couple's charge spelled out, A could win.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same skeleton, many of the same lines word for word. Only the touch differs.
