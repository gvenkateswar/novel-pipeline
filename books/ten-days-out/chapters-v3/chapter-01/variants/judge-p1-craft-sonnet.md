# Judge: Craft, Sonnet. Ten Days Out Ch 01, A vs B

1. **Winner:** A

2. **Why:** A has a narrator who notices things on her own terms. Beth sizes a man by his boots and then by her father's waiting. The ending gets a physical, wounded read on the subpoena ("It didn't give under her thumb the way a binder gave"). B has the same skeleton, but it is flatter and more explained, and its best jokes are the ones A shares.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 88%, in the kitchen: "My mother would like this kitchen," he told the window. "She'd also have wiped the counter by now. I'm making no comment about the counter." This is banter for banter's sake, and the "Counter's clean." / "It is. I said nothing." exchange reads as a bit being performed. Smaller stumbles: "as quiet as a hearse" (~8%) is a stock simile. The second "Under the open sky his phone buzzed" (~84%) is the same gesture I'd already seen at the ramp. It never actually lost me. I would have kept reading.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 58%, in Reyes's reply: "No, because the two o'clock is printed on a sign at the top of this ramp, and on a website, and in the window of the coffee place, and a printed schedule is the easiest thing in the world to wait for. A lot easier than a car." It is an argument built as a line, and I had to reread it to follow it. Also the climax sentence at ~64%, the long "and... and... and..." one ("the wet stuff of his jacket stiffened in her fists... and it was too long, and she went on holding"). It is trying to be breathless and mostly sounds assembled. Tess's "Everything else is two dollars over, for you." / "Why two?" / "Because you asked." is cute but muddled.

5. **Confidence:** Medium. They are the same chapter in two drafts. A wins on accumulated small specifics, not on any large difference in structure.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Same scenes, same beats, same jokes ("You're welcome." / "I didn't say anything."). They read as two passes over one draft.

Reasons, in short, for the craft read:
- A's phone call is the better scene. "She had watched a man in a good coat write her father down once, in an afternoon, in blue pen" carries the backstory in an image. B's "the last man who wrote her down had taken her father's boat" states it.
- A's "Nobody had waited on her like that since her father, and her father had mostly been waiting for the same thing" is a sentence only this book could have.
- B's strongest original touches are the barometer-versus-app joke and "rough across the base of the fingers, which was no kind of hand for a man in those boots." A has nothing quite like the app joke, which is a real point for B. It does not outweigh A's rhythm and eye.
- A's Reyes is a person with a tell (his eyes follow the pouch, "I watch everything that leaves your hands... I'll try to look at you more"). B's Reyes mostly talks cleverly, and his and Beth's registers sit closer together.
- Neither reads like the A1 anchor. Both avoid codes, ledgers and a couple doing business.
