Judge: Skimmer, sonnet. Texts: p3-A, p3-B (only the final scene differs).

1. Winner: B

2. Why: B gets to the skiff faster, Beth hands Reyes the heel-drag herself so the two of them are working a problem together, and the chapter ends with his hand going to his hip, which made me want chapter 2 more than A's "already moving."

3. Where A lost me: "While Tess talked, Beth watched every man on the slip walk, the plumber, the co-op boys, two old men down for the news. Not one of them dragged a heel." (~88%). It's a roll call, and the next paragraph, "She'd given the trooper the box and the tape and the plate and left out the man, and saying so now meant saying it to a man who wrote things down," is her thinking again. My thumb sped up through the sidewalk and the prints. "Under the open sky his phone buzzed" and "Sitting down, he had no limp to show her" read oddly. The concealment is a good idea but it's explained rather than played, and "Come here. Slowly." makes the skiff reveal feel staged.

4. Where B lost me: "'I see him,' Reyes said, low and even. 'He's sitting. He's not doing anything, which is what I'd do. Step back. Little more. Good.'" (~93%). It's a man giving procedure, and the thumb slowed there. The "Which boat?" / "Any of them." exchange on the dock is also thin. Neither lost me hard.

5. Confidence: medium. The scenes are about 85% the same text. B is tighter and has the better last line. A has the more interesting character beat, Beth hiding the heel-drag from the marshal, but it's buried in narration.

6. Same-author check: Yes. The shared first 85% is identical, and both endings keep the dry, nautical, clipped voice.
