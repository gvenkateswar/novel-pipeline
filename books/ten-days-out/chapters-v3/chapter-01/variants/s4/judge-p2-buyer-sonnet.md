# Judge: p2 A vs B, Buyer persona (sonnet)

1. Winner: A

2. Why: A's last scene lets me watch Beth spot the hooded man in the skiff while the marshal says "Come here. Slowly." and then nearly nothing, so the dread builds in the room with the two of them. B has Dane explain the threat to me ("which is what I'd do if I wanted to know which house was yours"), so I'm told to be scared instead of getting there myself.

3. Where A lost me: "He took the window. She fed the stove, and the late morning went by below them." (~86%). It's a soft, skippable stretch right before the good bit. A smaller wobble earlier: "While Tess talked, Beth watched every man on the slip walk" (~80%), because Tess hardly talks. My thumb never sped up. The "Come here. Slowly." line pulled me straight back.

4. Where B lost me: "Up the road his phone buzzed. He read it and typed back with both thumbs as he walked, and she watched him put the limp down for somebody she would never see." (~84%). The marshal has no limp, so I read it twice and stopped trusting the sentence. Right before it, the "Okay. He's not on the dock. Somebody took him off again, or dropped him and went. There's nobody on the road behind us... Which boat?" "Any of them." exchange (~80%) is the cop-procedure patter the calibration file warns about. It's the couple doing business, not the couple being in a room. I also tripped on "Dane said", because Beth has only ever heard "Reyes" or "the marshal" and she hasn't earned his first name yet.

5. Confidence: medium. The two scenes are the same beats with the same ending. A is better on the line level (the barometer bit, "Come here. Slowly.", the arm across her that is "all of him that touched her"). B's one real advantage is that "Who are we looking for?" / "Nobody." is a nice little lie from Beth.

6. Same-author check: Yes. Same voice and the same prose habits (the sea and rope detail, the dry humor, the Reyes-talks-too-much running gag). Only the final scene differs.
