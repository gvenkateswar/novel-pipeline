# Judge: p2, Craft persona, Opus

Read: blind/p2-A.md, blind/p2-B.md, docs/calibration.md. Nothing else.

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** In A, Beth steps over the dragged-heel track without saying anything and keeps her secret right up until the man is sitting in the window looking at her house, so the confession is pulled out of her by fear. In B she gives it up on the dock, so when they reach the kitchen window he's looking at something I already know about.

3. **Where A lost me:** It mostly didn't. The one place my eye sped up was the clipped list at the skiff (~88%): "Hood up. Oars shipped. Not fueling. Not fishing. Facing the hill." Five fragments in a row are the closest this chapter comes to the A1 ledger voice. It recovers on the next line, "Sitting down, he had no limp to show her."

4. **Where B lost me:** "She stayed. He went on talking, low, and she answered none of it." (~96%). The confession has already happened on the dock, so the window beat is a repeat, and here the scene sums up their talk when it should show it. A smaller bump: the narration calls him "Reyes" for the whole chapter, then switches to "Dane" in "'I see him,' Dane said" with no reason I could feel. B has the better single lines ("Any of them." and "which is what I'd do if I wanted to know which house was yours"), and "Who are we looking for?" shows him reading her, which I liked. But they're good lines dropped into a scene that has already let its tension out.

5. **Confidence: medium.** B's dialogue between the two of them is sharper in places. A has the stronger scene shape, and in A her silence is a choice the reader watches her make.

6. **Same-author check: Yes.** Same sentences up to the final break, and both endings use the same rhythm, the same barometer bit and the same callback to "I'm told."
