Persona: Buyer. Judge: sonnet. Texts: p3-A vs p3-B (final scene only differs).

1. Winner: A

2. Why: In A, Beth hides what she knows (she steps over the heel-drag track and keeps walking), then gives it up only when the hooded man turns up in the skiff, so I'm in the room with a woman who doesn't trust the man yet has to. B has her hand it over on the dock, which is tidier and less interesting.

3. Where A lost me: it barely did. The one slow spot is "While Tess talked, Beth watched every man on the slip walk, the plumber, the co-op boys, two old men down for the news. Not one of them dragged a heel." (~80%). It's a roll call, and my thumb sped up a little. The next line, "She'd given the trooper the box and the tape and the plate and left out the man, and saying so now meant saying it to a man who wrote things down," pulled me back.

4. Where B lost me: "Okay. One man, down the dock and not back up, which means a boat. Which boat?" / "Any of them." (~82%). The marshal explains the inference to me and nothing is at stake yet. She volunteers the information too easily, so the scene reads like a couple doing business. "They watched a long while" is also thin. B's better touches are "his right hand gone to his hip" at the end and "like a man who'd pay the dollar to be wrong".

5. Confidence: medium. B's gun-to-hip ending is a slightly harder hook than A's "already moving", and that's why it isn't high. A's skiff sequence ("Come here. Slowly.", "Sitting down, he had no limp to show her.", "I'll talk, I'm told it's what I'm for") is what I'd keep reading for.

6. Same-author check: Yes. They share one voice and one chapter, and everything before the last "* * *" is identical.

Calibration note: neither ending reads like A1. Neither has codes or a procedure list. B's dock exchange leans toward procedure more than A's does.
