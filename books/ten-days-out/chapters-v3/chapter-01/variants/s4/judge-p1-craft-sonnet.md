# Judge report: p1, Craft persona (sonnet)

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** A's last scene keeps Beth's dread of paperwork alive and ends on a physical act, while B explains her motive and slips out of her head.

3. **Where A lost me:** Barely. The thumb slowed at Tess's "Calloway. Heard you had a man on the eight. Ferry's quicker than the internet." (~83%). It's a small-town-gossip line I've read before, and it's the flattest thing in the scene. A recovers right away with the heel prints and the line "she watched her own words go in, the hood and the heel, for somebody she would never see... in his spelling."

4. **Where B lost me:** "She had given the trooper the crate and the tape and the plate and left the man out, and saying so now meant saying it to a man who wrote things down." (~85%). It explains what the scene had already shown. It also sets up a lie ("Nobody.") and then drops it two lines later when she tells him about the hood and the heel anyway. Two smaller slips: "Dane said" in Beth's POV, when she has only ever thought of him as Reyes, and "she watched him put the limp down for somebody she would never see," which I had to reread and still couldn't parse.

**What decided it.** The two scenes have the same skeleton. A has the better sentences: "Sitting, he had no walk to show her" is B's best line, but A has the inbox-in-his-spelling beat and the "Step back. Little more. Good." handling, which is how a marshal sounds when he moves a civilian. A ends on "his right hand gone to his hip," a gun and a door, and that's what makes me turn the page. B stops on "toward the back" with no gun, so the cliffhanger is softer. B is tighter and its "Nobody" is a decent hook, but A has more texture and it pays off the opening's fear of being written down. Neither reads like A1 (procedure and clipped codes). Both have the couple in a room, with the barometer, the window and the radio.

5. **Confidence:** medium. The scenes are about 90% identical, and a reader who prefers B's leaner trim and the "Nobody" lie could flip it.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. Everything before the last break is identical and both endings keep the same sentence habits (the "she said nothing" refusals, the island-specific nouns, the dry understatement).
