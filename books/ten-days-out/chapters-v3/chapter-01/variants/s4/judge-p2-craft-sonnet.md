# Judge: Craft, Sonnet. p2 final scene, A vs B

1. Winner: A

2. Why: A lets Beth hide the man in the first half and then gives him up in four flat lines at the window, so the confession lands. B has her blurt it on the dock and then runs Reyes through a tactical briefing.

3. Where A lost me: "She'd given the trooper the box and the tape and the plate and left out the man, and saying so now meant saying it to a man who wrote things down." (~84%). It explains the motive we'd already seen in her stepping over the track. B has the same line. It's a small stall in A, and the only one.

4. Where B lost me: "Okay. He's not on the dock. Somebody took him off again, or dropped him and went. There's nobody on the road behind us, which I'd like to keep true. Which boat?" (~80%). That is the competent-marshal-narrating-procedure voice. The scene turns into logistics. Reyes also stops sounding like the man from the ramp. Two lines earlier, narration calls him "Dane" in Beth's POV ("'I see him,' Dane said"), which is a slip.

Craft notes behind the pick:
- A's "Come here," he said. "Slowly." is a better hinge than B's "He went to the window." The sentence rhythm tightens exactly where the skiff appears.
- "Sitting down, he had no limp to show her" is in both. In A it's set up by her silently stepping over the track, which gives it a reason to work.
- A's "I'll talk, I'm told it's what I'm for" echoes "I'm told it isn't my best one" from the ramp. Reyes sounds like one person all the way through. B drops that echo.
- B's "Who are we looking for?" / "Nobody." is good. B then spends it by having her explain everything two beats later.
- A's closing image is better: "The skiff lay tied to the float, empty, and the store dock was bare." Then he's already moving. B's ending is nearly the same but softer ("Nobody on the store dock").

5. Confidence: medium-high. B's "Nobody" beat is a real rival, but A's concealment-then-reveal structure carries it.

6. Same-author check: Yes. Everything above the last break is identical, and both endings keep the voice: dry, nautical and clipped.
