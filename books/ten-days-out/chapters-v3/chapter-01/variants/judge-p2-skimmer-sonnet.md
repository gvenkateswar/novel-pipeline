2. **Why:** A keeps the two of them in the same space talking and touching more, with more banter and more charge, and its chain-checking and call-in passages are short enough that my thumb never really sped up.

3. **Where A lost me:** "Russ was the *Second Wind* and four hundred traps. Ned was the *North Reach*. The summer man was a varnished picnic boat with her father's lines under the paint." (~65%). It reads like a ledger of who owns what boat. I slowed down for it, then the line "She'd put him at the rail of that boat in the slop" pulled me back. The opening binder run, "Handle down. Chain hard. The D-ring packed with slush" (~3%), is list-shaped too, but it is only a paragraph and ends on the quarter turn.

4. **Where B lost me:** "Okay. Here's how this goes. You've handed the gentleman his pouch... a second deputy meets us, who would be here already except he's behind a plow on Route 1 doing about nine miles an hour." (~45%). It is a long procedural speech with logistics in it, and my thumb sped up. The paragraph about Beth doing other people's voices at the sink (~38%) is a stall in the middle of the standoff. Its boat-slot paragraph is the same ledger as A's, only longer. The kitchen scene at the end is thin, with Reyes talking at the window about forecasts, and it has less spark than A's.

5. **Confidence:** medium. The texts are about 85% the same and the difference is mostly texture. I would bet a little on A.

6. **Same-author check:** Yes. The beats, the lines and the gags are shared, and the two read like two passes over one draft.
