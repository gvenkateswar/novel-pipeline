1. Winner: A

2. Why: A sounds like a particular woman on a particular boat ("a decision somebody had made about herring in August", the plumber who "chained the way he billed, generously and with gaps", Beth doing everyone's voice alone at the sink), and B is the same chapter with that voice mostly sanded off.

3. Where A lost me: "Russ was the *Second Wind* and four hundred traps and a radio voice like a man reading a will. Ned was the *North Reach*..." (~72%). The paragraph explains too much. It tells me she can't place him, and the scene had already shown that. Even so, it's full of specific detail and I kept reading. The Tess exchange ("Don't ma'am me. I charge extra for it.") is a notch too sitcom, but it didn't stop me.

4. Where B lost me: "He could check a binder with her hands, and a bow line, and the weather. She had no way to check paper." (~75%), and the paragraph before it, "He couldn't make her get in a car. This could, and he must have known it before he unfolded it." The theme gets spelled out, and the competence reads as displayed. Earlier, "like loaves in a pan" and "the way a man keeps his cards" are fine but ordinary. The deputy's speeches ("a printed schedule is the easiest thing in the world to wait for") sound like a writer making the tactical point for him.

5. Confidence: medium. The two are the same skeleton with nearly the same beats and dialogue. The difference is texture, and a reader who wants lean could prefer B. I'd pick A at roughly 70/30. The wrist-hold paragraph is better in A: the run-on, "the way you'd have a fender or a dog gone overboard", and "her knuckles had started to ache" all earn it. B's version has more bodily specifics (pulse against bone, rough hand) but ends on a tidy "and she went on holding." Neither reads like the clipped procedural anchor A1. B is closer to it in places (more clipped, "Engine. Sleet ticking...") but still not close.

6. Same-author check: Yes. Same scenes, same lines of dialogue, same images ("fire truck", "picnic boat", the barometer), and they differ mainly in density of voice.
