# Judge report: p2 A vs B (Craft, Sonnet)

1. Winner: B

2. Why: B sounds like a particular woman with a particular mouth ("chained the way he billed, generously and with gaps"; "a decision somebody had made about herring in August"; Beth doing other people's voices alone at the sink), and A, though it has a few lovely turns, mostly sounds like a competent version of the same scene.

3. Where A lost me: ~38%, "He took that in. She watched him take it in, and file it, and not like where it went." It narrates the beat the dialogue just did. The same softness shows up at ~45% in "Brown eyes, tired at the corners," a stock face. The handshake paragraph ("Somewhere in there it had turned into two people on a cold deck holding each other by the clothes, and she couldn't have said which of them was supposed to move first") explains the moment instead of letting it sit. It is not a quit point, only where my attention slackened.

4. Where B lost me: ~50%, Reyes's "where a second deputy meets us, who would be here already except he's behind a plow on Route 1 doing about nine miles an hour." His speech runs a clause too long, and the joke is trying. At ~65%, "the way a man does it when he's decided before his boots have" is a muddled simile just before the jump. The jump paragraph itself ("by the scruff, by whatever came to hand") is stronger than A's.

5. Confidence: medium. The two are the same chapter at about 85% overlap, and A's kitchen scene (the counter joke, the folded sweater, the mutual watching) and its "spelled her right" ending are better than B's equivalents. I still go with B for the sentence-level voice and for the voice-mimicry detail, which only B has and which makes Beth a person. B's "man from the bank with a good pen... she had carried his folder out to his truck for him because her father asked" also beats A's version of that memory.

6. Same-author check: Yes. They share the same skeleton, dialogue and images, and read as two edit passes on one draft.
