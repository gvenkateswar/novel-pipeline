## Flags (read before drafting)

None of these blocks drafting. Each one notes a place where two sources disagree, or where a reader warning changes how a beat is built.

1. **Domain corrections applied (bible, end section).**

   - The ferry is the small boat: the dealer's reefer plus no more than four light vehicles. Don't count them on the page.
   - The manning rule is the line Beth cites at Pruett: "Master and one deck. That's the certificate. Without me she doesn't leave the slip."

   - Nobody says "material witness."
   - The reefer tell is three cues: the display dark, the box not cold under her hand, and the memory of the unit droning every Thursday.
2. **Ned's look-back can't happen at "the top of the brow" on Day 0.** The bible puts plant 1 there, but on Day 0 the boat is in the slip with the transfer bridge working. The brow only exists from Day 1 night, at the stone pier. **Use the top of the outside ladder to the wheelhouse.** Ned turns there with the bags in his hand and lifts that hand.
3. **"1 a.m." on the page.** The bible's times table allows it "once, in a clause." World Rule 6 and the outline's pacing flag allow only 8:40, 7:40, 1:15 and 11:40 as clock times in the sample. **Write "past midnight."** That follows the stricter rule and the reader's log warning.
4. **The trooper's line.** The outline puts Beth's call in s1. The bible's walk-on note says "reported in Ch 1 s2." **Both happen:** Beth's own end of the call is on the page in s1 (she speaks, so World Rule 4 is kept), and Dane cites the call in s2 ("You were told not to work").
5. **"Her palm on the box" must not be written as "palm flat."** That phrase is used once in the book, in Ch 3. Avoid "hand flat" too.
6. **Beth's bible flirting line 2 ("Pouch is zipped, tagged and going to the bank") drops "tagged."** CASE FACTS: she hands down the pouch and never sees the tags. Use "Pouch is zipped and going to the bank."
7. **No tag colours at Pruett.** The only colour in the chapter is Tess's yellow tag on the store bag on Day 0 night (s1, one clause, under the floods). The outline's seal clue puts it in Ch 1. It isn't explained until Ch 3.
8. **The subpoena has no date on the page.** Monday, and "the last sitting," land in Ch 2 s1 from Pike. In s3 Beth reads her name and the court, and nothing else.
9. **Ch 2 dependency: the back door is untouched in Ch 1.** Ch 2 opens with Dane finding out the latch sticks. In s4 they go in and out by the front door, and nobody mentions the latch, the mudroom or the back steps.
10. **The sample reader's two warnings are built in.**
    - *Boat thread.* One mention per scene: s3 (the exchange) and s4 ("There goes my boat"), and none anywhere else. s3 carries no broker, no price, no deadline, no "thirty-one" and no clock time for the viewing. The viewing is "this afternoon," one clause, so the s4 ending can land.

11. **Scene sizes.** s3 and s4 are under the definition's 700-word default. The outline sets them (650 and 450), and its pacing flag says the hinge is protected by trimming s3. So s2 gets 1,050 words with a protective floor of 900, s3 drops to 550, and s4 rises to 500 because it has to carry the chapter promise. The total holds at 2,900.
12. **Second deputy.** Research note 19 has a second deputy meeting Dane at Pruett, and the storm came early. That's optional, one clause, in s2 only if it falls out of the argument ("My partner's behind a plow on 1"). If it costs a laugh line, cut it.

## Header

**Chapter total target:** 2,900 words (S1 800 · S2 1,050 · S3 550 · S4 500). The orchestrator's ±8% band is **2,668–3,132**.

**Register:** a tense cold open (s1), then comic friction (s2–s4).
- s1 is Beth alone with what she saw, in her fear register: short, nouns, listening.
- From the ramp on it's a fight played as comedy. The tension sits in the jokes, and nobody wins clean.
- Ch 02 is tense throughout, so Ch 01's middle and end must stay funny.
- The one temperature spike after s1 is the jump and the jacket. Slow that down, lengthen the sentences, and play it in real time.

**The turn: Scene 2.** She casts off with the marshal still on the ramp, and he jumps. It can't be undone. She has kept her boat and lost her exit, and he has kept his witness and lost his afternoon.

**The longing.**
- **Primary, s2.** She hauls him in by the jacket front and neither lets go at once. His hand closes over her bare wrist, and that's the one sensory detail. Her interior gets a sentence longer than the dialogue around it, and she notices how long she's been holding on.
- **Secondary, s3.** "I want a boat I can't be called off of." She places people by their boats (the habit, once this chapter), and she has no slot for a man with no boat. She finds she's been trying to make one.

**The laugh.**
- **Primary, s2.** Beth's first line to him is a gear verdict before she has looked at his face: "Stand on the yellow. There's grit in the paint. Those boots are for a different ocean." / "They're for a courtroom." Then she prices them, to the dollar, without looking down twice: a hundred and ninety, the place on Route 1 that also sells fudge. He has nothing to say to that.
- **Secondary, s3.** "To who?" / "To me." / "Less than you're paying."
- **Tertiary, s4.** Tess: "Heard you had a man on the eight. Ferry's quicker than the internet."

**Cost:** her exit (she's on her island with him for the day, and then the weather); his viewing.

**Chapter promise (outline):** Who was the hooded man, and who keeps texting the marshal? s4 sets up both. On the slip Beth watches the walk of every man there for the limp, and none of them has it. On the Harbor Road, under open sky, his phone buzzes; he reads it and turns the screen from her.

### Scene 1 — Night freight
**Setting:** The *North Reach*'s car deck at the Cutter Island slip, Thursday, 8:40 p.m., near the top of the tide. Sensory anchor: sleet coming flat across the deck, orange under the sodium floods.
**Characters:** Beth Calloway, Ned Haskell, Wade Pelletier (the driver; unnamed here, seen in his cab), the hooded man (Doucette; unnamed, seen).
**Want:** Beth wants the dealer's truck chained down and the boat away on time, short-handed and done right. In the way: Ned wants her gone, which he never has, and then the box under her hand is wrong. The reader learns her want when she walks to the first binder instead of answering him.
**Tension source:** situational, then danger. Two men have seen her see the bricks, and one of them is walking onto her island.
**Surface action:**
1. Open in motion: Beth going down the trucks with her gloves off, reading the binders by feel (the chapter's one gloves-off beat). A binder gives a quarter turn it shouldn't, and she takes it up before she has decided to (bible interior line 1 is the model).
2. The dealer's reefer drives on. Its unit's display is dark, and on a Thursday that unit has droned every week. Ned, at the wheelhouse rail, watching it come aboard: "I'll work it short. Go home." He has never sent her home. She walks to the next D-ring and says nothing.
3. Chaining the reefer, she puts her bare hand on the box. It isn't cold.
4. The hooded man is stacking the last crates through the side door. The boat lifts on a sea, a crate in the doorway tips, the band springs and the lid comes up: lobsters, and under the top layer, wrapped bricks. The driver in his cab and the hooded man in the door both watch her see them.
5. Beth presses the lid down and re-bands it: "Lid's sprung. You'll lose your count." She finishes the binder. On her way aft she checks the stern chain across the transom, knocks the ice off the ring buoy's bracket, and passes the quarter ladder. One sentence, all in motion.
6. She goes up the outside ladder to the wheelhouse, past the floods' breakers on the aft bulkhead. In her fear register: short, and listening to the boat. Before the ramp comes up, the hooded man limps off onto her island. She watches the limp go up the slip.
7. Crossing, compressed into one or two sentences. The driver's dome light stays on in the cab under the wheelhouse windows the whole way over. Ned asks about the lid. "Strapped it." She tells him nothing more, and the reason is the lit cab below them.
8. At Pruett the reefer drives off, and she photographs its plate. One beat.
9. Back at the island near the top of the tide. The fuel float is lit, at an hour it never is (no clock). Ned with both bags under his arm, Tess's yellow tag bright in the floods (one clause, unexplained): "Go home. I'll lock up." At the top of the outside ladder he turns and lifts the hand with the bags in it (Flag 2).
10. Her kitchen, dark. She sits a long while with the phone on its twelve-foot cord, because calling it in means being written down. One sentence of the wound, no backstory. Past midnight she dials. Her end of it: what she saw, the plate. The trooper's line: "You stay home tomorrow, Ms. Calloway. Don't go to work."

**Subtext:** Ned tried to keep her off a load he was afraid of, and it reads as an old man's care. She keeps the bricks from Ned for one crossing, and she will never keep anything else from him. Calling the police is the hardest thing in the scene for her. Seeing the bricks isn't.
**Texture budget (set piece; cap six):**
- (1) The binders and D-rings, by feel: her competence, and the hands the book runs on.
- (2) The reefer cues (display dark, box not cold): she knows this load's Thursdays.
- (3) The stern group (chain, buoy bracket, quarter ladder) in one moving sentence: Ch 9's deck.
- (4) The outside ladder and the breakers: Ch 9's deck.
- (5) The yellow tag, one clause: the seal clue.
- The sleet under the floods is the anchor and doesn't count. The plate photo is an action, not texture. No vehicle count. No reefer brand, unless it's the unit's name in Beth's head once.
**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** **Consequence.** She hangs up, winds her father's alarm clock and sets it for the first boat. (This is one of the chapter's two allowed prop or withheld-action endings.)
**Word target:** 800. Items 1–6 get about 550 in real time; items 7–10 get about 250 and are told by tide and light, never by the clock.

### Scene 2 — The Pruett ramp (THE HINGE)
**Setting:** The Pruett Harbor ramp, Friday, 7:40 a.m., gray and coming on to blow from the east. The sodium lights over the ramp are still on in the overcast. Sensory anchor: the bow line stiff with ice, which cracks off in her bare hands as she pays it out.
**Characters:** Beth, Dane Reyes, the courier (one gesture), Ned (in the wheelhouse; one horn blast, no lines).
**Want:**
- Beth wants to work her boat back to her island and decide the rest herself. In the way: a marshal on the ramp with the trooper's order and a car at the top of the ramp. The reader learns her want with "I'll work her in and the two o'clock back, and I'm yours on the ramp at quarter to three."
- Dane wants her in the car now, by the book. In the way: he can't make her go, and his phone keeps buzzing about the one thing he wants for himself. The reader learns his want with "Here's how this goes."
**Tension source:** interpersonal. Her rule against his order, and both of them are right about something.
**Surface action:**
1. **The meet. The spark comes before the names.** He comes down the ramp in new boots and skids on the iced plate. Without looking up from the cleat: "Stand on the yellow. There's grit in the paint. Those boots are for a different ocean." / "They're for a courtroom." She prices them to the dollar without looking down twice: a hundred and ninety, the place on Route 1 that sells fudge. He has no answer. (This is the primary laugh, and it's the boots' one occurrence this chapter.)
2. "Ms. Calloway. You were told not to work." (From the trooper; his politeness is the anger register.) Wade Pelletier is in custody "which makes you the case," said in one clause, by name.
3. She hands down the courier's pouch. He watches the pouch, not her, and she minds. "Pouch is zipped and going to the bank. You can stop watching it. I'm the one casting off." (Flag 6.)
4. Her case: "Kenny's puking and there's no relief on a storm Friday." Then the rule, flat: "Master and one deck. That's the certificate. Without me she doesn't leave the slip."
5. Dane: "Okay. Here's how this goes." Hand the man the pouch, walk up the ramp, get in the car. "You're not arresting me." / "I'm not arresting you." / "Then I'm working."
6. The deal: "I'll work her in and the two o'clock back, and I'm yours on the ramp at quarter to three." No.
7. His phone buzzes. He checks it and angles the screen from her. "Am I keeping you?" He puts it away, and gives her the worst true thing he has, in his sentence shape: "The last witness who knew her own town better than I did is buried in it."
8. **The turn.** Ned's one short blast from the wheelhouse. She lifts the eye off the bollard and casts off. The bow ramp starts up and the gap opens a stride. (Optional, one interior clause: the gust sets them toward the dolphin and she knows Ned's correction before he makes it.)
9. He jumps, badly, in those boots. She takes the front of his jacket in both fists and hauls him over the lip. **The longing, slowed:** he's down on one knee on her steel, her fists are in his jacket, and his hand has closed over her bare wrist. She has him, and she doesn't let go, and neither does he. Her interior here runs longer than the talk around it: the sea under the ramp, the cold of his hand, and that she is still holding on.
10. She lets go first. "You're welcome." / "I didn't say anything." / "I noticed."

**Subtext:** She would rather be carried off her island in handcuffs than walk off her boat with a job undone. He has just told a stranger the thing he never says, to win an argument, and it didn't work. The phone is the broker. Beth reads it as somebody who matters more than she does, and that stings in a way she won't look at.
**Texture budget:**
- (1) The manning rule, one line in her mouth: she's right on the law, and it's the reason he loses.
- (2) The pouch handed down: the latent clue.
- The horn blast and the ramp coming up are action. No other procedure, no tag colours, and no "program" line.
**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** **Dialogue.** "I noticed." It's Beth's line, and nothing comes after it: no reaction beat and no look.
**Word target:** 1,050. **Word floor:** 900 (protective; this is the sample's hinge). If the chapter runs long, trim s3, never this scene.

### Scene 3 — The crossing
**Setting:** The lee of the deckhouse on the car deck, on the run back to Cutter. The boat is working in the slop past the ledges. Sensory anchor: spray coming over the bulwark in sheets, and his wet knee, where he went down on her deck.
**Characters:** Beth, Dane.
**Want:**
- Beth wants him to be a problem she can coil and stow, like the bow line she's working. In the way: he keeps being specific.
- Dane wants one thing settled before the island: she goes on the two o'clock, and he has paper that says she has to. The reader learns his want when his hand goes inside his jacket.
**Tension source:** interpersonal and comic. He was on her boat by his own choice and on her terms, until the paper.
**Surface action:**
1. Open in motion. She's coiling the iced bow line and he's dripping beside her. "For the record, I jumped. I'd like that on the record, since you seem to be the one keeping it." (Dane flirting line 1; "for the record," 1 of 3.) She doesn't answer, and goes on coiling.
2. A lobster boat is hauling off the ledges in the slop, and he watches it with his whole face. **The one boat exchange:** "What's a Webbers Cove thirty-four with a rebuilt Cat worth?" / "To who?" / "To me." / "Less than you're paying." He's looking at her this afternoon, one clause. No price, no broker, no deadline, no time.
3. Beth, three lines at most: the bank took her father's boat, and a man from away made her a picnic boat. Then her verdict: "You want a working boat to sleep on." / "I want a boat I can't be called off of."
4. **Interior, longer than the dialogue (the secondary longing).** Russ is the *Second Wind*; Ned is the *North Reach*; the summer man is the picnic boat. A man with no boat is from away, and she has no slot for him. She catches herself fitting him to the lobster boat in the slop. This is the habit's one use this chapter.
5. Dane: the two o'clock, her deal. Beth: "Last boat today." He says he'll be very interested in it, then.
6. **The reversal.** From the inside of his jacket, dry, he takes a folded paper and hands it to her. The grand jury subpoena. He can't make her get in the car, and this is the thing that can. She reads the court and her name typed in full, *Elizabeth Ann Calloway*. She's been written down. The date isn't dwelt on (Flag 8).

**Subtext:** Both of them have just said what they want out loud (the boat; to be let alone on her boat), and neither will say what it cost. He lost the argument at Pruett and has won it with paper. Paper is the one thing she can't check with her hands, and he must have known it before he unfolded it.
**Texture budget:**
- (1) The Webbers Cove exchange: his want, in one exchange and no more.
- (2) The subpoena: the legal fact that compels her, and her wound.
- No broker, no "program" line, no notebook.
**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** **Reversal.** Her full name in type, in her wet bare hand. She folds it once and puts it in her bib pocket, where it will get wet, and that's the last beat.
**Word target:** 550.

### Scene 4 — The island
**Setting:**
- The Cutter Island slip as the eight lands, then the Harbor Road, then Beth's kitchen.
- The kitchen window looks down on the harbor; the barometer hangs by the kitchen door.
- From late morning to a quarter past one. The wind is making from the east.
- Sensory anchor: the barometer's needle, low and still going down under her tap.

**Characters:** Beth, Dane, Tess Marr. Ned on the radio only.
**Want:** Beth wants him to see what she sees, that the two o'clock won't run, and to admit it. In the way: he believes schedules, and he has a dollar on it. The reader learns her want at "It won't run."
**Tension source:**
- Comic: who's right about the weather.
- Under it, situational: two questions the reader carries out of the chapter (the limp, the phone).

**Surface action:**
1. Tess at the slip for the store's freight. She looks at the marshal's boots before his face. "Calloway. Heard you had a man on the eight. Ferry's quicker than the internet." (The tertiary laugh. She calls him "Marshal." Tess's ballpoint on her hand is optional; the default leaves it for Ch 3.)
2. While Tess talks, Beth watches every man on the slip walk. None of them limps. One sentence, no comment. (Chapter promise, part one.)
3. Going up the Harbor Road under open sky, his phone buzzes. He reads it and turns the screen from her. She doesn't ask. (Chapter promise, part two.) They go into the cottage by the **front door** (Flag 9).
4. In the kitchen she pins the subpoena under the barometer and taps the glass. The needle drops. "It won't run. Pack if you want. You'll be unpacking by two."
5. "Dollar says it runs." / "I don't bet on what I can see." (Bet 1 of 4. Dane is wrong, and he doesn't know yet that the score starts here.)
6. A short passage of waiting, one or two sentences in plain time. She packs anyway, to make the point, and he stands at the kitchen window looking down at the harbor. No recap and no clock.
7. A quarter past one: the handheld on the counter, which she has turned to one-six. Ned's formal radio voice, a different man from the one on the boat: "All stations, all stations, this is the ferry *North Reach*. The fourteen hundred departure is cancelled. Next departure to be announced. *North Reach* standing by one-six and one-three."
8. "There goes my boat." / "It was never your boat."

**Subtext:** She has won, and winning means he's on her island for the night. Neither says that part. He has lost the viewing to keep a witness he can't make go anywhere, and he says it as a joke, which tells her exactly what it costs. Her answer is true in two ways, and she means both.
**Texture budget:**
- (1) The glass: she reads weather better than any schedule, and it's the reason for "It won't run."
- (2) Ned's formal cancellation, set as its own short paragraph: the voice she'll know in one syllable in Ch 9.
- Nothing else. The phone buzz is action.

**Point of view:** Beth.
**Ends on:** **Consequence.** "It was never your boat." The cancellation has cost him the boat, and Beth's line is the last on the page. No reaction beat. This is the chapter's ending as the outline sets it.
**Word target:** 500.

### Scene 4 (revised) — The island
**Setting:** The Cutter Island slip as the eight lands, late morning; then the Harbor Road past the head of Marr's store dock; then Beth's kitchen, whose window looks down on the harbor, the store dock and the fuel float. Wind making from the east. Sensory anchor: the morning slush on the dock planks, where one heel has scored a line beside every left print.
**Characters:** Beth, Dane, Tess Marr, Ned (the radio only), and the hooded man (a figure in a skiff, far off, never confirmed).
**Want:**
- **Beth** wants to get through the day without saying out loud that she left the limping man out of last night's call, and she wants to find him herself, on her island. In the way: Dane reads where people look. The reader learns her want at the lie ("Nobody.").
- **Dane**, once he knows, wants her on the two o'clock and off this island. In the way: the glass. The reader learns his want at "Dollar says it runs," which is now a hope.

**Tension source:** situational danger, live on the page. Under it, interpersonal: her lie, and his thumbs writing her words down.
**Surface action (about 500 words in all):**
1. **Tess at the slip (about 60).** "Calloway. Heard you had a man on the eight. Ferry's quicker than the internet." One price line to Dane, in her voice (she announces in headlines and talks prices): coffee's free to islanders, two dollars to him. Cut "first man off that boat who did," because the reader heard it as Beth's voice. Tess wheels her freight off up the store dock.
2. **The scan and the lie (about 70).** Beth watches every man on the slip walk, and none of them drags a heel. Dane has seen where her eyes went. Low, once Tess is out of earshot: "Who are we looking for?" / "Nobody." Then at once a task (her lying tell: too short, then something to do): "Take that." She hands him the line bag. One interior sentence carries the reason the reader feels. She gave the trooper the crate and the tape and the plate and left the man out, and saying it now means saying that to a man who writes things down. Don't explain any further.
3. **The store dock (about 110).** The Harbor Road passes the head of Marr's dock. In the morning's slush one set of prints goes down the planks to the fuel float, with the heel line beside every left one, and they stop at the float's edge where a boat lay alongside. She stops, and the truth comes out in her fear register, nouns only: "Man off the reefer. Hood. Drags his left heel." / "He went back with the truck." / "Walked off before the ramp came up." Dane knows the trooper's report: "That wasn't in your call." / "No." He doesn't ask why. His register changes. He talks *more*, low, steady, present tense, and moves himself to her water side, between her and the harbor. "Which boat?" She looks out over the moorings, the lobster boats and the dragger in off the weather (no name, no hailing port). "Any of them."
4. **The phone (about 40).** Under open sky the phone buzzes. He reads it, and this time he doesn't put it away. He types, thumbs quick, and she watches him put the limp down for somebody she'll never see. (Chapter promise: who is on the other end.) They go in by the **front door**.
5. **The glass and the bet (about 80).** She pins the subpoena under the barometer and taps the glass, and the needle drops. "It won't run. Pack if you want. You'll be unpacking by two." / "Dollar says it runs." He says it like a man who'd pay the dollar to be wrong. / "I don't bet on what I can see." There's no packing scene, no window banter and no instrument joke.
6. **The window (about 60).** Dane at the kitchen window. Below, off the fuel float, a skiff lies to its painter with one man in it: hood up, oars shipped, not fueling and not fishing, sitting facing the hill. Sitting down, he shows her no limp. Dane, low and continuous: he sees him, he's not doing anything, and doing nothing is what Dane would do. He steps her back from the glass with one arm and doesn't touch her otherwise. Beth says nothing; fear makes her silent.
7. **The radio, a quarter past one (about 45).** The handheld on the counter, turned to sixteen. Ned's formal cancellation as its own paragraph, word for word as in the original S4 item 7. Nothing after it about Ned.
8. **The close (about 35).** Dane, to the window, low, not a joke: "There goes my boat." Nobody answers it. She looks past his shoulder. The skiff is tied to the float, empty, and there's nobody on the store dock. He is already going through the house toward the back. (This hands straight into Ch 2's opening on the back step. Don't name the door, the mudroom or the latch; Flag 9 stands.)
