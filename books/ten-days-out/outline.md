## What changed in the back half (short)

- **The shape.** From Ch 10, Beth and Dane stop hiding and go after the people hunting her. They go to the kid instead of waiting for him (Ch 10). They set a trap together and are in the room when it springs (Ch 11). They go into the kid's last hideout and take him (Ch 13). Nobody hides somewhere, gets found, gets shot at and runs. No two chapters in a row have the same shape.
- **Dane is good at his job on the page.** He wins the Ch 10 argument on its merits. He reads the blood trail. He gets to the kid when the kid goes for Beth. He takes the kid's rifle, phone and skiff. He works out that the texts clear his boss. He saves Ned's son with one phone call. He catches the kid coming out of Beth's window. He pulls Beth out of a parting rope's way. He holds off the boat at sea. From Ch 10 on, he protects her at least as often as she saves him.
- **No more fires and no more rescues from the water.** The church fire, Ned stepping off the float and the kid going over the ferry's rail are all cut.
- **Ned is exposed by a confrontation.** Beth sits under a blanket where a bleeding boy should be. Ned comes in to save that boy, and she faces him down (Ch 11, about 63%).
- **The mystery moves every chapter.** Each one adds a new fact or a new question (see each chapter's **New fact** line).
- **Act 3's danger changes shape.** The kid is caught in Ch 13. A boat with no lights, sent by whoever supplies the drugs, takes over the threat (planted Ch 13, Ch 14, Ch 15; acts in Ch 16). Beth beats it at the wheel by taking her father's way through the rocks.
- **Everything the author asked to keep is kept:** Ned the traitor, exposed around 60–65% with Beth and the reader learning it together; Tess and Russ cleared and secretly together; the boss cleared; heat #3 in Ch 10 and #4 at the declaration; the rupture over witness protection; the repair; Beth at the wheel in the climax; the grand jury on Monday the 23rd with her testimony on the page; the HEA at Thanksgiving; Dane's boat; the September photo; "could see his light from there," owned by Ned; the captain's job in June.

## The book in one line

A ferry deckhand sees fentanyl spill out of a lobster crate, and the U.S. Marshal who falls for her on the first night has ten days to keep her alive on a storm-locked island where someone she loves keeps telling the killer where she is, until she and the marshal turn around and go after them.

### 10 · The Blood Trail · Beth · 2,200 · Heat #3
**Shape:** The hunt. They go to him for the first time in the book.
**Purpose:** Offense begins. Dane wins an argument on its merits and is good at his job where she can see it. They take the kid's rifle, phone and boat. The texts clear Dane's boss and leave her three. The fear turns into the tenderest heat.
**Opens with:** "'Not Ned,' Beth Calloway said, and heard how it sounded out loud."

- **s1 (450), the split rock, Tuesday dusk, the second storm coming in black from the south.** **Event:** Dane decides they go after the kid tonight, says why, and she agrees. First, one exchange about Ned, to their faces, both partly right. Beth: he took money, and that doesn't make him the one who sold the hour; he stood in the sea for her. Dane: he knew the hour, and so did two other people she loves, and he doesn't know which, and neither does she. "Every night so far he's picked the place. Not tonight." His case, plain: the kid is hit and bleeding, the sea is still down for maybe an hour, and the snow will hold his trail until the storm covers it. Tonight is the one night the kid is weaker than they are. Her case: it's nearly dark, a storm is coming, and the man has a gun. He answers each point. He goes first, she stays behind him, and if the trail goes toward houses, they stop. She leads the way along the shore, because it's her shore. "Fine." It's her choice too.
- **s2 (750), the trail and the east cove, last light.** **Event:** Dane reads the trail, they catch the kid at his skiff, and when the kid goes for Beth, Dane gets there first. Dane reads it out loud as they go, in plain words: here the kid stopped and tied his arm with his hood string, here he fell, here he turned downhill and not toward the houses. Beth: then he's going to his boat, and there's one cove on that side where a skiff can sit (his reading, her island). At the cove the kid is dragging his skiff down the stones one-armed, to run for Pruett while the sea's still down. The long gun is slung on his back and his phone is lit in his hand.
