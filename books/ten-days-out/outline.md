## The book in one line

A ferry deckhand sees fentanyl spill out of a lobster crate, and the U.S. Marshal who falls for her on the first night has ten days to keep her alive on a storm-locked island where someone she loves keeps telling the killer where she is, until she and the marshal turn around and go after them.

The kid's sworn statement after Ch 16 is the second witness that is enough. That's why the arrest is Monday, at the door. *(For the domain checker.)*

### 11 · Somebody's Boy · Dane · 1,800
**Shape:** The trap and the face-down.
**Purpose:** They set the trap together, with his boss's go, and are in the room when it springs. Beth faces the traitor down.
**Opens with:** "The kid wrote the way kids wrote, all small letters and no names, and by half past two in the morning Dane Reyes could write it too."
**The room looks different [audit note]:** the stove has gone out and the house is black. The only light is the phone, kept face down.

- **s1 (450), half past two.** **Event:** they build the bait together, his boss says go, and he sends it. **[A13]** Dane rang the leak's number at a quarter past two, and it was off. It's only ever on to send or to wait for an answer, so the only way to get it switched on, in someone's pocket, in a room with them, is to give the leak something to answer. Dane has the kid's way of writing from the thread. Beth knows what brings an islander out: "Nobody on this island lets a boy bleed to death in the snow." Dane names the cost to her face: whoever comes will be one of hers, brought by their own kindness. She knows. They argue the room to their faces and settle it: she's at the dead stove under a blanket where a hurt man would lie, he's behind the door, and if it's the kid, she goes out the back when he says go. He names the other risk: it's the kid's cove. **[A12]** He texts his boss the plan and waits. The answer: "Go." The bait: *marshal shot me. bleeding bad. cant walk. east cove the house up the hill. back door open. come alone. bring something for blood.* Four minutes later: *coming.*
- **s2 (600), the wait in the black.** **Event:** a sound down at the cove freezes them, and then a lantern comes up the hill on a walk they both know. **[A6]** First the false alarm: a knock and a scrape on the stones below. Dane is at the window with his gun, and Beth is not breathing. It comes again, and again, in time with the sea: the holed skiff, lifting and dropping on the tide. They don't laugh. Then, in the dark, for the one time in the book, Beth asks for his talking: "Talk to me." He tells her about the boat he means to buy: the kind, the price, what he'd fix first. In his head, plainly, how much he loves her. Then the lantern on the hill below, slow, a big man favoring one hip. They both see the walk. Beth: "I know whose it is." She gets under the blanket.
- **s3 (750), the kitchen.** **Event:** Ned comes in to save the boy, finds Beth, and she faces him down. He comes in saying "Son? It's all right, son," with his lantern, the ferry's first-aid box and his good blanket. He kneels at the stove and lays the blanket over the shape. Beth sits up. "Hello, Ned." Dane shuts the door and calls the number from the kid's phone, and Ned's coat buzzes. "Take it out." ### What the rupture is now (B1)

**The act.** On Tuesday night, at the dentist's kitchen window, Dane texted his boss about the kid's phone with the screen turned so Beth could read it (Ch 10, line 2137). A few minutes later he sent a second text, and he didn't turn the screen for that one:

*After Monday ask the AUSA for the material-witness hold. She doesn't go back on that ferry.*

The boss agreed. On Monday at two, after Beth testifies, the prosecutor will ask the judge for a material-witness warrant. Beth would be held in Portland, under guard, until the spring trial, or until she signs for witness protection.

**Why he did it.** Since Ch 01 Dane's method has been to ask, and every time he has asked, Beth has spent her own life. She told her three about the north-side house, and it burned. On the tower (Ch 08, after Part A) he wanted to tell her people five o'clock and bring the boat at three. His boss overruled him for her test, and Beth wanted her people told the truth by her. A Marine Patrol officer was shot at three. Then, on Tuesday, a pistol came up at her on the cove rocks. That night he stopped trusting the asking and did the one thing his method forbids. He did it knowing every fact. It's the condemnable choice the genre map asks for, and it's his.

**How she learns it.** In Ch 12 the boss is on speaker in front of her. The boss assumes Beth knows. Beth asks "What hold?", and Dane tells her all of it at once, to her face. Nobody overhears anything, and nobody misunderstands anything.

**Why it holds (the one-talk sentence).** *If they sat down and told each other the whole truth, this would still be a problem, because the truth is that he has asked for her to be held and won't withdraw it. He believes the hold is the only thing between her and New Bedford, and she won't be with a man who has her locked in a room for her own good. No conversation can undo a request filed with the prosecutor. Only he can, and in Ch 12 he tells her to her face that he won't.* Both are partly right. He's right that she'll probably die on Cutter, and she owns the beach. She's right that the decision was hers and that he went round her.

**Why it doesn't end in Ch 13.** She asks him to cancel it twice: on the boat in Ch 12, and on her step in Ch 13 with Russ's blood on her hands. He doesn't. Then Cole's man walks up to the town office in daylight (Ch 13 s5), which makes Dane surer than ever.

**What changes his mind (Ch 14).** On Friday night she takes the ferry off the pier, which he would have left, and holds the jammed wheel while the skin comes off her palms. She's right both times, and he cut the rope when she said to. He watches her price her own risk on her own water, and price it correctly. At dawn she says "Cancel it," and he cancels it on speaker, in front of her. He keeps his view: "I still think you should go. It was never mine to say."

**Required plant outside the range (Ch 11, one line, no plot change).** Ch 11 is Dane's POV, and he texts his boss in it (line 2221). If the hold isn't in his head there, Ch 12 is a POV cheat. Add one line after "*Go. Your call, your badge.*" (line 2225), in Dane's head:
