# Revision brief — Ten Days Out, second draft

*October 27, 2026. Built from three external review panels (`reviews/external/synthesis.md`), the pipeline's own manuscript read, and the whole-book repetition audit (`reviews/repetition-audit.md`). The author approved four decisions: Tess acts under duress from Day 7; Beth suspects Tess early and resists it; heat stays at a steamy 3 and is marketed as that; a targeted pass at about 32,000 words.*

This brief is binding for every agent in the revision. Where it conflicts with `outline.md` or `story-bible.md`, this brief wins; both have been annotated to point here.

## A. Plot facts that change (every chapter must agree)

### A1. Tess, before and after Day 7

- **Day 0–1 (unchanged motive):** two winters of off-books diesel to the Margaret Ann and others, cash after six, to carry the $1,800/month tank loan. On Day 0 Arsenault tells her the deckhand who saw is Beth and that Tess goes down with them unless she helps.
- **The $9,000 (new timing, said on the page in Ch 10):** Pelletier's 4 a.m. call to Arsenault never came, so by half past four Arsenault knew the truck was stopped. At a quarter past six on Day 1 he came to the store's back door with an envelope of hundreds and told Tess to put it under Beth's slip in the morning bag: a paid witness is a witness nobody believes, and a witness nobody believes is safe. Tess told herself it was the thing that kept Beth alive. She did it.
- **Day 1–6:** she passes what she learns (the check-in, the Ashby move through Ned, the landing time through the EMD notification) telling herself the men mean to frighten Beth off testifying.
- **Day 7 (new):** after the shots at the landing she knows better. At noon on Day 7 she tells Arsenault, at her back door, that she is done. He tells her the North Reach burns at her slip with Ned aboard, and that she will be on the float to watch. From Day 7 evening Arsenault keeps a man in her back office (the second crewman, then Arsenault himself on Day 8) and she runs the radio and the net with someone listening.
- **Day 8, the channel 9 call (Ch 09):** Arsenault is in the back office when Beth calls. Tess's too-long pause before "Okay, Bethie." is a woman with a man beside her. "Water side" at 11 pm is her breaking, and it is why Arsenault has the store's radio in his hand when Russ takes him.
- **Ch 10:** Tess's "They said they'd only scare you" is replaced. She says the true thing: the first days she believed it; after the landing she didn't, and she tried to stop, and he named Ned. Beth asks her about the $9,000 directly. Tess answers in one line. Beth does not forgive her on the page and does not hate her on the page. The saltines stay.

### A2. Beth suspects Tess early and resists it

- **Ch 03 (store):** Beth sees the log line and Tess's phone buzzing with the booster, and the thought arrives whole: *Tess knew I stayed.* She refuses it, physically and on purpose (she looks at the bread rack; she thinks of something Tess did for her once), and the narration lets the reader see her refuse it. Cut "The booster. Of course." or any line that underlines the clue for the reader instead of for Beth.
- **Ch 05 (after the scene):** when she tells Dane about the Friday call, she says out loud, "Tess knew I was home," and in the next breath defends her ("Tess has known where I sleep since I was nine"). Dane hears both halves and says nothing about Tess yet. The paint-can lid still leaves Tess off; now the reader knows why.
- **Ch 06 (the EMD line):** Beth hears "local emergency management" and knows exactly who that is on Cutter. She does not dismiss it as a county office. She chooses not to say it, and the cost is visible: her hands, what she does next, that she cannot eat. One short paragraph. Delete the reasoning about Pruett and Portland not phoning Tess.
- **Ch 07 (the notebook):** the pull toward believing Dane is the leak is partly that it lets her not believe it of Tess. Make that legible in one or two of her short sentences ("If it was him, it wasn't her."). Her going to Ned is the mistake of a person running from a conclusion; it costs her (she is alone on the road with Doucette guided by radio). Keep her craft in the escape; the choice of destination is the emotional error.
- **Ch 08:** the recognition becomes acceptance, not discovery. Ned's "Nobody but Tess. Tess doesn't count." is the thing that makes refusing impossible. Keep the four physical items, trimmed, as what she finally lets herself put together.

### A3. The Ch 7–8 repair and the third scene

- **Ch 08 loses the crew-cabin sex scene.** In its place, a fuller repair (about 700–800 words), in this order:
  1. Dane answers her accusation directly before anything softens. He names what he did: asked about money with his hands on her, wrote it down after, and chose the order over telling her. He does not hide behind the order: "I could have told you on Saturday and taken the consequences. I didn't. That was mine." No speech; four or five lines in his longer register.
  2. Beth verifies. She asks for the notebook and reads the D4 entry herself: *secondary location, B's family property, not disclosed*. She asks why he didn't write it. His answer: it was the one place he decided Portland didn't need, and he reported that too this morning. She checks the claim against what she knows (nobody has come to the fish house). That is the observable basis.
  3. She sets the condition: nothing about her goes in a book or up a phone that she hasn't heard first. He agrees and it costs him something he says plainly (Pike will not like it; he will do it anyway).
  4. Ordinary intimacy, not sex: one exchange about something they want that is not the case. She tells him she has a master's license she has never used. He asks what she'd run. She says the North Reach, someday, if Ned would ever be old. He tells her one thing about Lowell that is funny. They are briefly two people with a future.
  5. Then the bait plan, as already revised (two exchanges; he yields with his condition).
- **Sex scene 3 moves to Ch 10**, Day 9 night (Saturday), after the climax and before the first boat: Beth's cottage, the kitchen she left on Day 1, the stove relit, the latch that sticks. 800–900 words, steamy heat 3, frank language, no euphemism, in Beth's POV. It must be the first time in the book with no fear in the room, and that has to be felt as strange and then as relief. It changes something: she says the condition back to him as a promise; he keeps his hands where she can see them until she takes them. No image or line from the Ch 05 or Ch 06 scenes, or from the cut Ch 08 scene.

### A4. Ch 09 logic holes

- **Ned's night:** he goes up the Harbor Road as the decoy and spends the night in Russ's kitchen with Russ's scanner, as Beth told Tess. When Russ calls on 16 at about midnight, Ned hears it, and at first light he walks to the store and is there when Russ brings Tess and Arsenault down. Two or three lines total, placed where the reader would ask.
- **The gangway:** before Russ leaves for the store, Beth and Dane haul the brow inboard and chain it, so the pier side is closed and the stern really is the only way aboard. One beat.
- **Beth's descent:** in the Ch 09 positions, the plan already says that if the floods go out she comes down the inside ladder to the car deck, because she can work it blind and Dane can't. Dane objected in Ch 08 and lost. One line in the positions scene.
- **Russ's arrest:** make it plausible in his account at the pier: the cellar bulkhead behind the store that only islanders know, the back-office door that opens inward, Arsenault with both hands on the radio and his back to the cellar stair. Keep it to Russ's few formal sentences.

### A5. Ch 10

- Tess's scene per A1. Beth asks about the $9,000.
- The indictment comes Tuesday morning, not Monday at 4:10. Dane reads it to her over the phone on the Tuesday freight. Keep it to two lines.
- Fix the blocking: Beth stays at the binders; Dane comes down to her, or she comes up once and stays.
- **Russ is divorced** and the page says so the first time Jolene is mentioned (Ch 06: "Jolene, my ex-wife. Arsenault's her cousin."). Ch 09's "Jolene's cousin's boat" stands.
- **Book 2 hook:** Quist recruits Russ, plainly. On the float she tells him she needs someone who knows every boat in the bay by its exhaust and that the ledger has three harbors in it and one account; she asks him to work it with her through the winter. He says yes in his way. Dane reads in their hands that Russ has already decided. The reader should finish knowing Book 2 is Russ and Quist and the ledger.
- Coda length stays about the same; the new Day 9 night scene is paid for by trimming the grand jury (keep "I chose it" and the $9,000 statement) and the Day 9 noon scene.

## B. Prose rules for the revision (whole book)

### B1. Two voices
- **Beth (narration and dialogue):** short, bodily, concrete; works from water, weather, steel, people's hands; counts only when the number matters; dry humor that comes out sideways.
- **Dane (dialogue and his POV passages):** longer sentences, complete and level; subordinate clauses; reads people and risk, never counts; judges in full sentences ("That's a man who looks the other way for money, which is different from a man who tries doors."); allowed one dry joke per scene; never repeats Beth's words back to her as a fragment. Search for every place he echoes her ("Knees. Ninety seconds." "Lane. Back door." "Arm down. Hand soft.") and rewrite his side in his own syntax.
- In Dane's POV (Ch 02 opening, Ch 04 lines, Ch 08 opening, Ch 09 section, Ch 10 coda): add one or two sentences of his own interior in each, in his register: what it costs him to stay professional, and how he sees her.

### B3. Scene endings
No more than two scenes per chapter end on a short withheld-action line ("She didn't step out." "His pen was still moving."). Others end mid-action, on dialogue, or on an image that opens rather than closes.

### B4. Heat
Steamy heat 3, marketed as such. Ch 05 and Ch 06 scenes stay as they are (they were judged earned). The new Ch 10 scene follows the HEAT RULES in the bible.

### B5. The brief's heroine rule is revised
Old: "never a stupid choice in danger." New: her choices follow from what she knows; her blind spots are emotionally credible; her mistakes have costs. Her only real mistake in the book is emotional (refusing Tess, running to Ned), and it costs her.

## C. Process

1. Prose-writer revises Ch 03, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10 for the A items, each saved as `chapters/chapter-XX/final-v2.md` (the v1 `final.md` stays untouched until the pass is checked).
2. Continuity-checker reads all ten chapters together (v2 where it exists, v1 otherwise) against this brief, and runs a causal audit: for each attack, who knew, how it traveled, why the protector could not close the channel, why safer options fail.
3. Prose-writer fixes the issues.
4. Voice-editor does the B pass on all ten chapters with the audit counts and caps, chapter by chapter, saving `final-v2.md`.
5. Re-run `tools/repetition_audit.py` on the v2 files to confirm the caps.
6. Promote v2 to `final.md`; reassemble `manuscript.md`.
7. Cold read of the whole manuscript, with nothing but the manuscript.
