## What April found (summary, not evidence)

- Open-door, spicy romance (heat 3–4 of 5) is where self-published romance sales concentrate; BookTok drives discovery and promotes spicy titles. Dark romance is the fastest-growing segment. Clean is a smaller, loyal market.
- Spicy mainstream in practice: three to six full scenes in a 70k novel (scale down for a novella), direct anatomical language, heavy internal POV, each scene 2–5 pages and each one shifts the relationship.
- The Amazon ADULT filter is triggered by packaging, rarely by interior content: explicit covers, trigger words in title or subtitle, the 18+ checkbox, or books that are mostly sex with thin plot. Keep covers, titles and blurbs PG-13.
- Series outsell standalones roughly 3 to 1; a 30-day visibility cliff rewards rapid release. Income tracks catalog depth.
- One pen name per heat level.
- April's recommended niches: South Asian historical romantasy, South Asian romantic suspense (only ~11% of Kindle thriller bestsellers are indie, a gap), South Asian dark contemporary. All three were argued as blue-ocean because self-published South Asian romance barely exists in English.
- Warnings that apply to this book more than to the clean test: KDP requires disclosure of AI-generated prose at upload; US copyright does not protect prose a model wrote; romance readers actively hunt for AI authors and keep boycott lists. April recommended the human write the prose and use AI for outlining, editing and marketing. Decision on disclosure and any human rewrite pass is still open.

## Constraints for every agent

- Open door, heat 3–4. Scenes serve the emotional question; nothing should lift out as standalone erotica.
- PG-13 packaging. This becomes a World rule in the bible.
- Series opener with a satisfying HEA or HFN and a clear hook for book 2.
- Novella pacing: get to the romance fast; readers of this length expect momentum.
