## What the critical reviews say (grouped, own words)

**Tied by Treachery (the closest short comp in the open category).**
- The main complaint, raised by four of the seven critical reviewers: the blurb promised more angst and a bigger betrayal between the leads than the book delivers. The hero's "betrayal" was mild and he was immediately decent, so the make-up came too fast. Readers of this shelf wanted to see him grovel.
- Two noted it is very short and reads as a "quick" entry in a connected series ("Betray Me Once" → "Betray Me Twice"); they liked that for pace, docked it for depth.
- One flagged that the blurb used different character names than the book, spoiling a twist.
- 5-star reviewers praised: fast, one-sitting read; emotional opening; "true love wins" ending; the cheating-husband setup landing hard.

**Promises He Broke.** One critical review: slow when the heroine's job is discussed, and she forgave the hero too quickly. Almost no written reviews at all despite ranking; this is a ratings-only readership.

**Curvy Teacher and the President.** No critical written reviews. 5-star readers of this and the similar Knox shorts praise heat, speed, and the possessive hero. No signal on plot complaints.

**40 and (No Longer) Fighting It.** Two 3-star: one is positive apart from rating; the other says the story and characters are good but proofreading is poor (grammar, misspellings, inconsistent timeline details). Over-40 readers here say they like seeing found-family and a hero who turns out to be more than expected.

**Silver Seconds.** Five critical reviews, one theme: the chemistry between the over-40 leads felt flat and "underwhelming" even though the hero was described as attractive; one reviewer said each chapter had "too much or too little" of something. Two are ARC reviewers who liked the inherited-business setup and the hero's hidden-agenda complication. 5-star reviewers rate the spice at about 3 of 5 and say "finally a 40+ romance".

**Until I Remember (the trad-scale comp).** Twelve critical reviews with three clear clusters:
- Pacing: strong start, then it drags; "same thing over and over"; too much talking and too little happening; could have been shorter (five reviewers).
- Language: heavy swearing distracted several readers; two said it made them stop (three reviewers).
- Plot logic: the fake-marriage reason (the grandmother) didn't hold; the couple fell in love before the reader knew enough of their story (two reviewers).
- 5-star readers praised the banter after the amnesia wake-up, the small-town cast, and the emotional payoff.

## What this means for the heat question

- The Second Chances / Later in Life short books are plot-first betrayal dramas. Reviewers there talk about angst, grovel, forgiveness speed and blurb honesty. Nobody complained about heat being too high or too low, and nobody praised the heat. Heat appears to be incidental at roughly 2–3 in that template.
- The explicitly "steamy over 40" books (Silver Seconds, 40 and No Longer Fighting It) exist with a few hundred ratings each, so an open-door midlife readership is there, and it rates spice around 3 and asks for chemistry and competent editing. It is smaller than the betrayal-template readership.
- The trad-scale comp was punished for length and repetition, which argues for the novella, and for swearing, which the bible should set a rule on.
