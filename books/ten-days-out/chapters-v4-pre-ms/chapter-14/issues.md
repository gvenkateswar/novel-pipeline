# Chapter 14 — Continuity Issues

*Checked: `chapters/chapter-14/draft.md` against `beats.md`, `story-bible.md`, `outline.md` (incl. Ch 12–14 carry-forwards and the Ch 13 fix-round line), `continuity-log.md`, `book-brief.md`, and `chapters/chapter-13/final.md` (post-fix). Outline and log win where the bible is stale.*

---

**Issue 1**
- Problem: The "why didn't they just fly out?" question is open. Ch 13 final has the Coast Guard say "We'll fly when the icing clears," and Dane texts his boss "Need a flight when the icing clears." The outline's Ch 13 fix-round line binds Ch 14–18: the icing does not clear before Monday, and the same weather that grounds the helicopter is why Beth must go by ferry. Ch 14 never says it. Beth's Scene 1 line ("the one night the sea lets anything out") answers boats only. The boss's call lists the Coast Guard (no boat) and Marine Patrol (no boat) but not air. A listener hears "Sunday night or not at all" and asks why Beth can't be flown to Portland, and why shot Russ is still lying on the couch. Dane also never mentions the text he sent the boss in Ch 13 or the flight he asked for.
- Location: "Sunday night or not at all." (also: "Russ lay gray on the couch, shot through the thigh in her own doorway.")
- Suggested fix: Add one clause to the boss's call or Dane's N1 line, in the bible's DC wording: nothing flies, because the icing and low cloud don't lift till Tuesday. That covers Beth and Russ, and the line can end "so it's the ferry Sunday night or not at all."

**Issue 2**
- Problem: The "Marshal" slip is contradicted by Ch 13 final. In Ch 13 (Thursday) Beth says "Marshal—" and stops on the word, then says "I want you, Marshal. I haven't stopped. I'm still not going." So she has called him Marshal twice since Wednesday, once as the explicit heat word. The beat sheet's premise ("the map line is the only thing she has said that wasn't business") is stale after the Ch 13 fix round. The "They both heard it" reaction, "You called me Marshal," and "You only say it one way" all read as if the word had been absent for a day and a half.
- Location: "They both heard it. She hadn't called him that since Wednesday."
- Suggested fix: Delete the "since Wednesday" claim and have Dane's reaction acknowledge Thursday ("You said it on the step, too."), so the exchange is about her saying it again in the dark, not about it returning.

**Issue 3**
- Problem: Beth's "Say it to me" has no antecedent. Dane says "Tell her yourself. She's right here," and the boss then speaks directly to Beth ("Do you understand what you're choosing?"). Beth answers "Say it to me," but the boss just did. The line is left over from the outline's version, where the boss was still talking about Beth to Dane ("Say it to me, not to him"). As written it reads as asking for a repeat, and a listener can't tell what she wants said.
- Location: "I know what I'm choosing. I'm the one standing here. Say it to me."
- Suggested fix: Reorder so Beth speaks first when Dane hands the phone over ("Say it to me."), then the boss's two sentences, then Beth's "I know what I'm choosing. I'm the one standing here." No new words needed.

**Issue 4**
- Problem: Order of events in Scene 1 undercuts the one-talk decision. In the town office, before Beth has said anything about moving the boat, Dane already tells Tess the plan. Then at the pier Beth announces she's taking it off and Dane argues that it's safer to stay. Dane has already committed to the plan he then argues against, so the decision beat is hollow.
- Location: "'We'll be off the pier, inside the harbor, till light,' Dane told her."
- Suggested fix: Change the line to something that doesn't pre-empt the decision ("We'll be on the ferry till light," to Tess), and let "off the pier, inside the harbor" first appear at the pier.

**Issue 5**
- Problem: Ambiguous pronoun. "Told her" can read as Beth, who is the POV character and standing there. The next line ("Tess said nothing") only partly repairs it for a listener.
- Location: "'We'll be off the pier, inside the harbor, till light,' Dane told her."
- Suggested fix: "Dane told Tess."

**Issue 6**
- Problem: The rocks distance contradicts itself. The ferry is about a hundred feet (bible, 104 ft). The rocks are "thirty feet off" with the boat still swinging toward them, and then the rocks pass "a boat's length off." Closest approach can't be larger than the distance when the boat was still closing, and a ferry's length is more than three times thirty feet. The beat sheet's own numbers line has the same conflict (30 feet shown; "a boat's length to spare").
- Location: "The rocks were thirty feet off." and "The rocks went by in the white light a boat's length off."
- Suggested fix: Keep "thirty feet" for the first sighting and change the pass to a distance smaller than that ("The rocks went by in the white light a few feet off the side").

**Issue 7**
- Problem: Ned's rope knife custody is a gap. In Ch 13 final, Dane puts Ned's knife in Beth's hand on Russ's step ("Knife," she said. Dane put Ned's rope knife in her hand.) and it is never returned on the page. In Ch 14 Dane cuts the rope with it, and nothing says it came back. Beat sheet flag 7 provided the one-clause fix for exactly this case.
- Location: "He cut it with Ned's knife, and the end whipped back across the pier, nowhere near him."
- Suggested fix: In Scene 1, when Beth puts the flashlight in his hand, add one clause: she gives him Ned's knife with it ("and the knife she'd used on Russ's trouser leg").

**Issue 8**
- Problem: "Stop" has no antecedent. Dane's last spoken line is the shirt joke, and Beth answers it. Then "'Stop,' she told him, and he stopped" tells the listener he was doing something that needed stopping, but he wasn't. Separately, "The cold's helping. It slows the bleeding." is unattributed between two Dane exchanges and a listener can attribute it to Dane.
- Location: "'Stop,' she told him, and he stopped." and "'The cold's helping. It slows the bleeding.'"
- Suggested fix: Give Dane one more short line about her hands before "Stop" (so there is something to stop), and add "Beth said" to the cold-helping line.

**Issue 9**
- Problem: The fifth-day paragraph gives the wrong place and leaves out what he was supposed to ask. Per the log, Ch 08's "Ask me in five days" was said at the keeper's-house generator, not "up on the lighthouse" (the tower came later). "Ask her again" borrows the "again" from Dane's Ch 01 line ("Ask me again in ten days"). Nothing on the Ch 14 page says what he was supposed to ask, so a listener who doesn't recall Ch 08 can't tell what "he didn't ask" refers to. The paraphrase also echoes the spent "ask me in five days" (cap 1, spent in Ch 08) and the beat sheet's instruction to avoid it.
- Location: "On Monday, up on the lighthouse, she had told him to ask her again in five days. This was the fifth day, and he didn't ask."
- Suggested fix: Set the place as the keeper's house on Monday and name the thing he was to ask in a clause (check what Ch 08's declaration set up and use that), with no "again."

**Issue 10**
- Problem: The same feeling is stated three ways in four sentences. She "had wanted him to," she "wanted him," and she "wanted to be asked." The middle one is a flat repeat.
- Location: "She noticed, too, that she had wanted him to. She wanted him, in his open coat with nothing under it, and she wanted to be asked."
- Suggested fix: Cut "She noticed, too, that she had wanted him to." and keep the sentence with the coat and "wanted to be asked."

**Issue 11**
- Problem: Flat "wanted him" recurs at the same weight four times in about 2,000 words, in Scenes 1, 2, 3 and 4. Outline pacing flag 8 lists flat "She wanted." as a tic to keep out, while the bible allows plain wanting with no cap. The conflict is resolved by form: the plain statement is allowed, but the same sentence shape four times is a tic.
- Location: "...and she wanted him." / "she wanted him so plainly that her hands slowed on the wrench." / "She wanted him. She was angry with him. Both were true." / "She wanted him, in his open coat with nothing under it..."
- Suggested fix: Keep the Scene 3 line (it carries the angry/wanting pair). Change Scenes 1 and 2 so the want is carried by a specific physical detail of the moment and not the bare "she wanted him" construction.

**Issue 12**
- Problem: "Bind / bound" is not glossed and is the key word of the chapter's danger. Beth says "It might bind when the sea's on it," and the jam is narrated as "had bound." A listener who has never been on a boat, or never handled a stuck mechanism, may not take "bind" as "stick." The mechanism for the spin-back is also compressed: why the sea pushing on the blade spins the wheel once the rod lets go is not said.
- Location: "It might bind when the sea's on it." / "The rod she'd straightened had bound, with the blade under the boat turned all the way to one side, so the boat kept turning." / "Turning all the way could bind the rod again"
- Suggested fix: Replace "bind/bound" with "stick/stuck" in all three places, and add a clause at the free-spin: once the rod let go, nothing held the blade, so the sea shoved it and the wheel spun with it.

**Issue 13**
- Problem: Rope setup is unclear for a listener. "Post" is never placed on the boat or on the pier. The reader can't tell whether Dane, on the car deck, is lifting loops off posts on the boat or off the pier, or which end whips where when he cuts. "It won't come off the post" and "the end whipped back across the pier" depend on the answer.
- Location: "Dane lifted each off its post and threw it clear."
- Suggested fix: Place the posts once ("each off its post on the deck").

**Issue 14**
- Problem: Beth explains to Dane what he watched her do for two days. Dane is her guard and was with her during the repair, so the vise and "no new one this side of Pruett" are known to him. It reads as exposition for the listener put in a character's mouth. Dane's own opener, "You've had the steering in pieces two days," confirms he knows.
- Location: "There's no new one this side of Pruett, so I straightened it in the co-op's vise."
- Suggested fix: Keep the wheel-gear-rod-rudder explanation (Dane asked "What does that do?") and carry the vise and the Pruett fact as a half-line of narration, or compress the dialogue to "It's as straight as the co-op's vise could get it."

**Issue 15**
- Problem: Tess's line repeats what Dane's action just showed. He breaks the shotgun open, looks, and closes it; Tess then says it's loaded and that she loaded it. The information is delivered twice, and the check loses its meaning as a character beat.
- Location: "He broke it open, looked, closed it and handed it back." / "'It's loaded, Marshal. I loaded it.' Tess didn't look at Beth."
- Suggested fix: Put Tess's line first, as she holds it out, so Dane's check is him not taking her word and the information is said once.

**Issue 16**
- Problem: Narration restates what the dialogue just did. Dane has just said "She doesn't go into the program unless she says so. Write that down," and the narration adds "he had just taken it off the table." The gloss of "the program" is needed for a listener; the second clause is not.
- Location: "The program was the new name in the new state, and he had just taken it off the table."
- Suggested fix: Cut ", and he had just taken it off the table." Better, move the gloss to the line before Dane speaks.

**Issue 17**
- Problem: The cut-off bet is explained by narration. "Because she couldn't see any of it" spells out the joke, while the full line ("I don't bet on what I can see") was last on the page in Ch 06, so a listener still won't catch the callback and gets only the flat explanation.
- Location: "She stopped, because she couldn't see any of it: the rod, the wind, the morning."
- Suggested fix: Replace the explanation with the three things as an image ("The rod was under her feet, the wind was out in the dark, and the morning was hours off.") so the callback is felt without being labeled.

**Issue 18**
- Problem: Information channel not set up. Beth knows Tess "hadn't said one word on the radio," but no radio is on in the captain's cabin on the page, and the bible says nothing is private on the island radio and that Tess uses it to talk boats in. Also, "the fire truck's radio" sits on the town office desk beside Tess's keys, which doesn't fit the bible (fire company and the constable carry handhelds; Tess's base set is at the store). The keys sentence also telegraphs the surprise that Scene 3 is built around.
- Location: "Her keys lay on the desk beside the fire truck's radio." and "She had put the light on the rocks and hadn't said one word on the radio."
- Suggested fix: Make it the island handheld radio, and in Scene 3 add one clause that the radio on the cabin shelf stayed silent. Fold the keys beat into something less pointed (a coat-pocket sound or her hand on them).

**Issue 19**
- Problem: Unobservable from the cabin. At first gray, from the captain's cabin and past the harbor mouth, Beth can't see whether the driver of a fire truck on the harbor road looks at a boat off the point.
- Location: "Tess never once looked at the boat."
- Suggested fix: "The light never once swung toward the boat" (observable, same meaning).

**Issue 20**
- Problem: Past-perfect backstory cluster at the top, against the beat sheet's "never a recap" and World rule 7. In the first four paragraphs after the opener, five back-references arrive in a row: the kid's line "had said … yesterday," Ned in the cuffs, "had dressed her knee on Tuesday," "had ended it on Wednesday," and Russ shot. Each is a say-it-twice item but together they form a catch-up block before the scene moves.
- Location: "'They've got a boat,' the kid had said on her kitchen floor yesterday, and not one word since." through "Russ lay gray on the couch, shot through the thigh in her own doorway."
- Suggested fix: Keep the kid's line and Russ; drop "the same careful hands that had dressed her knee on Tuesday" and let "She had ended it on Wednesday" stand alone.

**Issue 21**
- Problem: The same two quotes are restated in a past-perfect pair at the sighting, after the opening already restated one. The beats require the link, but it lands as a recap sentence at the top of the scene's emotional peak.
- Location: "'They've got a boat,' the kid had said on her kitchen floor. 'It comes in from offshore,' Ned had said on the east road. Here it was."
- Suggested fix: Cut "the kid had said on her kitchen floor" and "Ned had said on the east road," and keep the two quotes with "Here it was."

**Issue 22**
- Problem: Why Beth is still needed is not answered on the page. Ch 13 put the kid in custody and Ch 12 put Ned in custody, so a reader asks why Cole walks Monday if Beth isn't there. The beat sheet had the boss answer (a text from a phone in nobody's name plus Ned's word won't arrest the lawyer; the kid isn't talking). The draft cut that line, and the outline carry-forward calls Ch 15's version a "restate," which it can't be if Ch 14 never states it.
- Location: "If she isn't in the room on Monday, Cole goes free at two."
- Suggested fix: Add a clause to the boss's line (the kid isn't talking, and Ned's word alone isn't enough), or log that Ch 15 s2 owns the first statement of it.

**Issue 23**
- Problem: The narration explains the rope-safety clause that the action shows. Dane stands to the side and the end whips back "nowhere near him"; the "because a cut rope under that much pull comes back like a whip. Two summers on a lobster boat had taught him that." sentence says it twice.
- Location: "He stood to the side of the rope's line, because a cut rope under that much pull comes back like a whip. Two summers on a lobster boat had taught him that."
- Suggested fix: Cut "Two summers on a lobster boat had taught him that." The earlier line "I worked the back of my mother's cousin's lobster boat two summers" already plants it.

**Issue 24**
- Problem: "Exactly" is repeated in the same four-hundred-word stretch: "I'm good at exactly this" (Scene 1), "He held the light exactly there" (Scene 2), "He held the spoke exactly where hers had been" (Scene 3). Not on the motif list, but it is a chapter-level tic created by the draft, and the first is a binding line.
- Location: "He held the light exactly there, crouched behind her..." and "He held the spoke exactly where hers had been."
- Suggested fix: Change the Scene 2 and Scene 3 instances ("right where she'd pointed"; "where hers had been").
