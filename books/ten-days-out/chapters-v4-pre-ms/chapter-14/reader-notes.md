# Reader Notes: Chapter 14, "The Wheel"

**Draft read:** `chapters/chapter-14/final.md`, as of Nov 6, 2026. Context: chapters 01–13 `final.md`, read in order first. Nothing else opened.

**Pull score: 3 of 5.** I'd keep reading, but the reason is the deadline and the boat, not this chapter. The middle (the wheel sticking, Tess's light) is the best action since the beach. The opening and the closing phone call stall it.

---

## Where I got bored

- **The phone call with the boss.** Two blocks of logistics in a row: "The Coast Guard is out on the storm's distress calls, and it won't come for a boat that hasn't done anything... Nothing flies in this icing until Tuesday at the earliest..." then "Marine Patrol's boat on your stretch is the one with the hole in it, and the state won't send its next one into a shooting. The kid isn't talking, and a text from a phone in nobody's name and Ned's word won't arrest that lawyer." It's a briefing: every exit gets closed one at a time, out loud. I skimmed it. I already knew there was no way off. The book has told me that since Chapter 2.
- **The steering lesson.** "The wheel up top turns a gear. The gear pushes this rod. The rod turns the rudder, the big steel blade under the back that steers the boat." It's useful once, but it's written like a picture book for a child.
- **The opening.** "The kid wouldn't say what boat, and by Friday night Beth Calloway had stopped asking." Chapter 13 ended on "They've got a boat," and the first sentence here lets the air out. Two days go by offscreen and nothing happens in them. The threat I was braced for just waited.

## Where I got confused

- **"Hold it still, Marshal." / "Last time you said that, you had hold of my coat." / "It's what you are." / "You only say it one way."** I read this three times. Does "that" mean "Hold it still" or "Marshal"? I don't remember her saying "hold it still" on the tower. If it's "Marshal," I don't know what "You only say it one way" means. It's clearly meant to be a loaded little exchange and I got nothing from it.
- **The shirt bandage choreography.** He holds a spoke with one hand, stands on one sleeve, splits the shirt with his "free hand," then wraps her palm. How many hands does he have? I couldn't picture it.
- **"Dollar says it holds till light." / "I don't bet on—"** She breaks off, then a line of narration about the rod and the wind. Why does she stop? Did something happen? Nothing does. It reads like a dropped line.
- **Tess's coldness.** "Tess didn't look at Beth," again, same as Chapter 13. I'm not sure what Tess blames Beth for. Russ getting shot? The light on the rocks tells me she still cares, but I couldn't say why she's angry.
- **Who's talking on speaker.** The boss's long lines come with no tag at first. I worked it out, but only after a sentence.
- **The timeline.** I had to count back: the fifth day after Monday makes this Saturday morning. The narration says so ("This was the fifth day"), which helps, but only because it stops to explain.

## Where I stopped believing

- **Russ.** In Chapter 13 he's shot high in the thigh with a strap cinched above the wound. Now nothing flies "until Tuesday at the earliest." That's five days with a gunshot thigh on the town office couch. If the strap is a tourniquet, he loses the leg. If it isn't, he should be bleeding or feverish. The chapter gives him one line ("lay on the couch with his leg up") and moves on. Nobody seems worried about him, which doesn't fit Tess at all.
- **"I worked the back of my mother's cousin's lobster boat two summers. I'm good at exactly this."** In Chapter 2 his knots "weren't hers, and he didn't pretend otherwise." Now he's pretending. Maybe it's a joke, but it played as the book forgetting what it said.
- **The father-and-the-bank detail.** "Her father had stood at a wheel every working day until the bank took his boat, and she had carried the papers out to the bank man's truck." Fourteen chapters in her father's house, his oars, his shack, his pantry drawing, and this is the first I hear of it, right as she takes the wheel. It felt delivered for the moment.
- **The apology.** "I decided your life for you because I was scared, and I called it my job. I still think you should go. It was never mine to say." Too tidy. It's the speech a couples counselor would want him to give, ready-made on a freezing bridge after a sleepless night. Dane usually talks sideways and jokes. This is the first time he sounds written.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Glosses for words the book already defined.** "The float, the floating dock that used to sit between boat and stone." "The car deck, the open deck where the trucks parked." "Marine Patrol was the state police on the water." "The program was the new name in the new state." Every one of these was explained in an earlier chapter. Repeated here, they read like the book thinks I wasn't paying attention. The last two are standalone one-line paragraphs, which is worse.
- **The "she wanted him" tic.** "She thought about turning over in the dark and dragging him down by his coat." "She wanted him. She was angry with him. Both were true." The last three chapters each have a version of this beat (Ch 12's mouth-on-his-while-he's-talking, Ch 13's "I want you, Marshal"). "Both were true" spells out a feeling the scene already showed me.
- **The recap.** "On Monday, at the generator in the keeper's house, he had told her he loved her, and she had put her answer off five days. This was the fifth day, and he didn't ask." I remembered. Beth remembering it right then is fine, but it reads as a reminder to the reader.
- **The ending pattern.** Again the chapter ends on a single ominous image: "Past the white water, off the point, the one light hadn't moved." Chapter 13 ended on the boat line, Chapter 12 on the smoke, Chapter 9 on "Which one?" I can feel the chapter-end shape coming now.
- **Narration quoting dialogue back.** "'They've got a boat.' 'It comes in from offshore.' Here it was." This is the book underlining its own plant.

## What I want to know next

- Is the fishing boat Cole's lawyer's people, and will it come at the ferry on Sunday night?
- Will she run the father's line through the ledges, the one drawn on the pantry door? (I'm almost sure she will.)
- Does the bent rod stick again at the worst moment? (Almost sure it will.)
- What does Dane say "on the other side," and what does she answer to the five-day question?
- Does she get the ferry for good, now that Ned's gone? Does Tess forgive her?
- Smaller: is Russ going to be all right? The book seems to have stopped asking, and I hadn't.

These are real questions, and the Sunday-night deadline pulls. But most of them come with an answer I can already see.

## Prediction section

What I expect from the next chapter or two, with confidence:

1. Sunday night Beth takes the ferry out through her father's unmarked line, so the fishing boat watching the harbor mouth misses her or can't follow. **85%.**
2. The steering rod sticks again in the ledges and she has to fight the wheel with her wrapped hands. **80%.**
3. The fishing boat comes at them anyway, and Dane uses the speaker-phone Coast Guard ("they'll come from an hour off"), or Tess's radio and light save them again. **60%.**
4. She reaches Portland and testifies Monday morning. Cole is indicted. **90%.**
5. After that she answers "Ask me in five days" on the other side: yes. Dane transfers to Maine and buys the leaky wooden boat. **85%.**
6. She takes Ned's job as captain. Ned gets some grace note (his son safe, a letter). **70%.**

If all of these happen in this order, I won't be surprised by anything left in the book. The only thing that could surprise me is the lawyer showing up in person, or the kid turning.

## Listener pass (read as audio)

- The glosses ("the float, the floating dock...", "the car deck, the open deck...") are even worse aloud. They sound like a narrator pausing to explain to a child.
- "'They've got a boat.' 'It comes in from offshore.' Here it was." In audio these quotes sound like someone in the cabin is talking. A listener would be lost.
- The boss's untagged speakerphone lines: in audio I wouldn't know at first whether it's the boss, the Coast Guard or Dane.
- "I don't bet on—" followed by narration sounds like a glitch in the recording.
- The hold-it-still / Marshal exchange is short and clipped. Aloud it goes by before you can work out what it means.
- What works aloud: the wheel sequence. "The wheel went heavy." "The wheel stopped." "Come on. Come on, damn you." Short, physical, easy to follow by ear. The shirt banter ("That was my good shirt." / "You don't have a good shirt.") also lands.

## Best moment

> A white light came on along the shore.
>
> It swung across the snow and stopped on the rocks, and behind it was the shape of the island's red fire truck on the harbor road. The rocks were far closer than she had guessed, and the boat was still swinging toward them.
>
> ...
>
> Tess. She had put the light on the rocks, and the radio on the shelf stayed silent.

Tess won't look at her, won't talk to her, and sits in the fire truck all night lighting her way off the rocks. Then at dawn: "On the harbor road the fire truck's light went off, and the truck drove away up the hill." That's the one place in the chapter where the book trusts me to feel something without being told. The wheel spinning back and taking the skin off her palms, with the old rope scar opening, is a close second.
