# Judge: Craft, pair p3 (A vs B), Chapter 12

1. **Winner: A**

2. **Why:** In A the confession is still running when the shot comes off the wall, so the gunfire cuts into Ned's betrayal and he throws himself on her before he finishes explaining why. Later A lets me inside Dane when he overrides her ("for once the judge was sitting behind him at the stove... She'd be right about this too"), so the fight between them costs both of them something. B shoots first, so the confession plays as a cool-down Q&A on the walk afterward, and B keeps me out of Dane's head at the exact moment he goes over her.

3. **Where A lost me:** About 55%, "Dane Reyes gave his boss the arrest, the number and the son, with Beth three feet away." It's a summary line that opens a cold new section, and from there the boss call reads like the couple doing business by speakerphone. Earlier, around 12%, "You're in the way of me." also clunked. It's a line nobody says, and B's version is better.

4. **Where B lost me:** About 8%, "'Snow's set in for the day,' Ned said. Then he answered her." Answered what? Nobody has asked him anything. The gunfight has just ended, and the big betrayal now comes out as a string of "Why my Thursday?" / "Why was the truck clean?" questions while they climb, with the danger already gone. B also explains things A trusts me to see: "He had just done it again, in the ditch. She had shoved him off for it."

   To be fair to B, its dialogue is better line by line. "You're lying in my spot. I'd like it back," "somewhere with fewer windows" / "Then walk faster," "He didn't make a joke after it," and Tess "in a man's coat on the open step, where the whole road could see" all beat A's versions. If B's best lines were in A's structure, that chapter would win easily.

5. **Confidence: medium.** The lines are about 80% the same. What decides it is A's scene order and Dane's interior paragraph, and B's sharper lines nearly close the gap.

6. **Same-author check: yes.** They're the same sentences shuffled into a different order, plus or minus a few lines.
