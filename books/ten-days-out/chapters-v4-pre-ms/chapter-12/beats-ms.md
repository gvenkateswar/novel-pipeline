## Rules for the whole chapter (for the writer and the dialogue doctor)

- Dane never swears. No "Bethie", no "your marshal", no "Dollar says", no "I don't bet", no "I'm asking". Every boat is "it". No Russ cap gesture, no "a teenager's knots", no "low and steady", no "Fair".
- **Plain wanting stated once in the whole chapter,** in s3 (keep "Beth wanted to cross the room and put her mouth on his"). Everywhere else the wanting is shown as touch, distance or where her eyes go.
- No contrast constructions ("not X but Y", "X, not Y", "It wasn't X. It was Y.").
- No past-perfect catch-up block at the top of any scene. Each scene opens on something happening.
- **The logistics budget is one line per scene at most.** A buyer who is tired of logistics is reading. If a fact is needed for logic, show it as something seen (snow to the knee on an unplowed road) and don't explain it.
- **Each scene ends a different way:** sound, gesture, quiet action, interruption, image. No neat button line closes any scene.
- No family-member reminders inside a tender beat. No father, no Ned's son and no Alton in the hand-on-the-ladder moment or the line before it. In s5 the house is "her house". Ned stays silent in the tender beat and speaks only after she lets go.
- **Dane's anger:** polite and slow, with "Ms. Calloway" once (s5). **Beth's anger:** plainer and bossier, with his full name once (s5). **Ned:** weather first, then the answer, and when he hides something he gives too much detail. He reports other men's words ("He said"). **Russ:** answers his own question once. **Tess:** a headline and a surname.

### Scene 1: The east road

**Setting:** The east road, Wednesday first light. New snow nobody has plowed, knee-deep at the mouth of the dentist's lane and squeaking under three sets of boots. An old stone wall runs across the top of a white field on the uphill side. A shallow ditch half full of snow lies on the downhill side.
**Characters present:** Beth, Ned (wrists roped in front with Beth's knot; she holds the end), Dane (a step wide on the uphill side, with the kid's rifle).
**Surface action:**
1. **The walk.** Beth leads Ned on the rope. He answers her Ch 11 "Why?" weather first ("Snow's set in for the day"), then plainly. This is the reader's **first word** of the night runs, so it is a confession: "Two years ago Cole came down the pier on a flat calm night. He said, after your deckhand's gone home, you take it back across alone. So I did. Two years I ran his trucks over in the dark. He gave me a phone for it. Only number he ever called me on."
2. **Beth catches the beach lie** (N1). She stops on the rope. "On the beach you told me you'd never opened a truck." / "On the beach I lied to you." Ned can't stand behind his own words, so he gives too much detail: "Fuel runs, I told the co-op. Nobody asks the captain why he moves his own boat."
3. **The son, and the rope.** Ned: "A man rang me on that phone the morning after. He told me the street my boy sleeps on." The rope goes slack in her bare hand. She walks again, and now it jerks at every step his hip takes.
4. **"Why was the truck clean?"** Keep the old answer: he called Cole when they tied up at Pruett, while she was on the ramp photographing the plate. "I told him you'd call. I know you."
5. **"Why my Thursday?"** Ned: "The blow was coming. No night run till it passed, and Cole wouldn't wait on it. He said you never open a truck." She has eleven years of never opening a truck, and they were what got her chosen.
6. **The door.** Beth: "Did you know he'd send somebody to my door?" Ned: "Cole said nobody'd be touched. I didn't ask him twice." (Flag 1.) Beth stops walking.
7. **The silence and the shot.** Dane has already stopped, head up toward the wall. In a storm the island always makes some sound, and up by the wall it makes none (keep). One crack off the wall, and snow jumps off the road ahead of Ned. Dane has her by the coat and in the ditch before the sound ends. Ned comes in after, hard on the hip with his tied hands, and rolls until his back lies between her and the field. She shoves him off with both hands.
8. **Dane's one shot.** Dane, on one knee at the lip of the ditch, fires once at the wall. A shape breaks off the far end, bent double, and runs west along the stones toward the village, the bad foot plowing a line. Dane's finger comes off the trigger. The first houses are past the wall, and he doesn't shoot toward houses. He doesn't chase either: he has a witness and a tied man in a ditch.
9. **In the ditch** (the answers after the shot, as written).
   - "Why was my picture on that wall in September?" / "In September Cole asked me who'd have the boat after me. I told him you. I was proud of it."
   - Dane: "Where does Cole get it?" Ned answers Beth: "It comes in from offshore. A boat. I never saw it."
   - Then, unasked, the beach: "So I came to stand where he'd have to shoot me first." He has just done it again in the ditch, and she has just shoved him off for it.
   - *Optional, one line, only if the scene is under its floor:* "I stood in Ellie Dyer's woodshed till Russ's truck went by" (continuity Issue 6). Cut it first if the scene runs long.
10. **The rise.** They climb out. At the top of the rise Dane's phone finds signal. He rings Russ: put it on the island radio, every door locked, a boy with a pistol coming in from the east, and nobody to go out and help a hurt boy. He says nothing about her. His hand comes flat on her back once, between her shoulders, and goes.

**Subtext:** Beth isn't asking questions for the case. She is asking who she has been standing beside for eleven years, and every answer takes something back. Ned wants to be punished by her and nobody else, and that's why he only ever answers her. His back in the ditch is the only apology he has, and she won't take it.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal (each answer is worse than the last) turning situational (one shot on an open road). This doesn't repeat Ch 09: there's no tree line and no boat, only a wall, a field and a ditch.
**Ends on (sound):** Below them on the harbor road a door shuts. Then another. The island is locking itself in.
**Word count target:** 620. **Floor:** 560.
**Turn:** Ned ran the drugs himself, alone, on her boat, for two years, and her honesty is why the load rode her run.
**Longing:** Dane's hand flat on her back, once, after the ditch. Also: when the silence comes, she looks at Dane before she looks at the wall.
**Laugh:** After Ned rolls on her, give one dry line. Either Beth, "Get off me, Ned. I've been under that truck once this week." or Dane, flat: "Ned. He's aiming at her. You're in the way of me." The dialogue doctor picks one, and it must not be a quip about the beach.
**Texture budget (2):** Cole's night-run phone; "fuel runs" and the co-op.
**Plants:**
- "It comes in from offshore. A boat." (→ Ch 13 "They've got a boat"; Ch 14)
- The September photo answered (→ Ch 15 the kid's statement)
- Ned's son (→ s3)
- The kid going west toward the village (→ Ch 13, her house)
**Keep from the old text:**
- The silence by the wall
- The crack
- Ned's back in the ditch and "Get off me"
- Dane's finger off the trigger
- The answers in the ditch
- The hand on her back
**Cut:**
- Old lines 11 and 13 (the recap of the three texts, the first-aid box and the logistics block)
- Any explanation of why they walk. The knee-deep unplowed snow shows it (N13).
**Pull-gate risk:** this is a Q&A. Keep every answer to two sentences at most and keep the body moving: the rope, the slack, the stop, the shot. No question may be one the reader can already answer.

### Scene 2: The harbor road

**Setting:** The harbor road at full light. Every door shut but one: Tess's store, with the porch light still on from the night.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Ned, Russ, Tess.
**Surface action:**
1. Tess's door opens as they come along the road. Russ comes out in his shirt sleeves in the snow with his shotgun and his radio. Tess stands behind him in the doorway in his brown work jacket, the cuffs pulled down over her fingers. Everybody stops.
2. "Late," Beth says. / "Later than that," Russ says (no cap). / "Not one word, Calloway."
3. Russ to Dane, ears red (this pays the Ch 03 porch call): "You asked me who I told you were at my place. I told her. Friday night, from my porch, before the shooting. Saturday at the store, when her phone buzzed and she hung up on it, that was me telling her I'd found you alive. Tuesday I kept to the harbor road because her store's on it. Did I lie to you to keep her name out of it? I did."
4. Dane: "You've owned the one lie that put you on my list. Take him." Behind them Tess's door shuts.
5. At the town office step, Russ takes his cuffs off his belt. Beth holds out the end of the rope, and Russ puts his hand on it. She holds on a breath longer, then lets go of her end.
6. Dane puts Ned's knife from his coat pocket in her hand and closes her fingers on it with his own, a second longer than it takes. She cuts her own knot, and Russ's cuffs close where the rope was.
7. One line of order: Dane sends Russ, with the truck and the shotgun, to the foot of the Calloway lane. A hurt boy goes to ground he knows.

**Subtext:**
- Beth watches Tess wear a man's coat on the open road, where the whole island can see, and she can't look at it for long.
- Russ has lied to the law to protect a woman, and he isn't sorry.
- Cutting the knot is Beth handing Ned from herself to the law. She does it with his own knife.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Russ and Tess are caught out in front of the person they lied around. Tess isn't speaking to Beth, and Beth knows why.
**Ends on (gesture):** In the town office doorway Ned turns once and looks back at Beth (the bible's look-back, paid). His hands are cuffed, so he nods. Then he goes in. Cut there, with no line after it.
**Word count target:** 260. **Floor:** 230.
**Turn:** Russ and Tess are out in the open, Russ is cleared, and Ned passes from Beth's rope into the law's cuffs.
**Longing:** Dane closing her fingers on the knife. Watching Tess in Russ's jacket, Beth finds she's standing close enough to Dane that their sleeves touch, and she doesn't move.
**Laugh:** "Late." / "Later than that." / "Not one word, Calloway." Plus Russ's question and answer ("Did I lie…? I did.").
**Texture budget (1):** Russ's cuffs, and the knot cut with the rope knife.
**Plants:**
- Russ's cuffs on Ned (→ s5, the rail; Ch 14–15)
- Russ at the foot of her lane (→ s5's end; Ch 13, radioed in at dusk)
- Tess's silence toward Beth (→ Ch 13–17)
**Keep:** old lines 61–83, minus the cap and the north-side house clause. Fold the Russ order (old 85) into one line.
**Pull-gate risk:** low. It's short and funny and a secret comes out. Don't let it grow.

### Scene 3: The town office, the phone and the son

**Setting:** The town office front room in the late morning. A clerk's chair at the stove, the stove ticking. The window sill is the one place with signal. Through the open back-room door, Ned sits against the wall in Russ's cuffs, the rope burn red above them.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Ned.
**Surface action:**
1. "Sit." / "That's mine. I say that." / "I'm borrowing it, because I spent a long time on that knee last night, and you've stood on it all morning like it owes you money." / "Once." / "Once is how everything starts with you." (Keep word for word.)
2. Ned's cheap phone buzzes on the desk and rattles against the kid's phone. Dane turns the screen to Beth: *the kid isnt answering ned. your son is. go finish it.*
3. **Ned's lunge (the event).** Ned comes up off the back-room floor in his cuffs, faster than his hip should let him: "Let me answer him. Let me tell him it's done. He'll leave my boy be." Beth reaches the doorway first on the bad knee, puts both hands flat on his chest and walks him back down the wall. "Sit down, Ned." He sits. Dane is already dialing.
4. **The number match,** two sentences. Dane holds the kid's phone beside Ned's: it's the same number that called the kid four times, and it rang Ned once, the morning after. Ned: "That's him. Cole's lawyer."
5. **The address.** Dane at the window rings Portland police on his own phone: his name, his badge, a car to an address now. "Ned. The address." / "Ninety-six Grant Street. Second floor, the door round the back."
6. **The scene's one plain-wanting line:** Dane is making a desk in Portland do what he says, for the man who sold her, and Beth wants to cross the room and put her mouth on his while he's still talking. Keep the line. She doesn't cross.
7. **The wait.** Ned's thumbs work at the rope burn, one wrist then the other. He never looks at the phone, only at Beth. The stove ticks.
8. **The son comes at a cost.** Dane's phone rings, and he listens. "They found him. He'd used. He's breathing. They've got him in an ambulance." (Flag 4: no "eleven minutes" by default.)

**Subtext:** Beth puts her hands on Ned to stop him, and it's the first time she has touched him since the beach. Ned would trade her again for his boy, right now, in front of her, and she knows it. Dane is good at his job for the man who sold her, and that's the moment she wants him most.
**Tension source:** Situational (the lawyer can reach Portland in a morning) turning physical (Ned coming off the floor). Then the clock of the wait.
**Ends on (quiet action):** Ned bends over his cuffed hands until his forehead is on his knees, and stays there. Beth pulls the back-room door half shut and leaves her hand on it.
**Word count target:** 380. **Floor:** 340.
**Turn:** The people behind Cole can reach into a family in a morning. The son is alive, and he isn't fine.
**Longing:** the plain line (6). Also, when she comes back from the doorway, she passes close behind Dane's chair at the window and he moves his elbow in to let her by. Neither of them needed that.
**Laugh:** "Once is how everything starts with you."
**Texture budget (2):** the two phones side by side; the Portland police call.
**Plants:**
- The son was using (→ Ch 15/16 the clinic; Ch 17 the letter)
- The lawyer's reach ("in a morning", → s4)
- The lawyer's number (→ Ch 15 statement, Ch 16 arrest)
**Cut:** "They're telling him they can get to his son"; "He means me"; Ned's "I never would" speech; "You heard him".
**Pull-gate risk:** the number match is logistics. Keep it to two sentences and put the lunge before it, so the scene's first beat is a body.

### Scene 4: The hold

**Setting:** The same front room, noon. Dane's phone is propped on the window sill on speaker. The boss's voice comes out small and flat and fills a room built for one clerk. The back-room door is half shut.
**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, the boss (voice only), and Ned (heard through the door, not seen until the end).
**Surface action:**
1. Dane has given the boss the arrest, the number and the son, with Beth three feet away (the line's one use, Issue 10). The boss: "A lawyer who can find a man's son in a morning can find a woman on an island."
2. **The program, as one paragraph with no break inside it:** "After she testifies there's witness protection. A new name in a new state. She can't come back here, and she can't tell anyone from here where she is. Not her friends, not this island, and not you. Reyes, ask her."
3. **Beth answers for herself** (this retires "She'll go"): "She's here. She's been here the whole call, and she can hear you fine. No." The boss: "Then our part ends after Monday. Think about it, Ms. Calloway. Call me back, Reyes." The call ends.
4. **The act, on the page.** Dane takes the phone off the sill and stays at the window. His thumb moves on it, and Beth watches it. He sends. Then he turns the screen toward her, the opposite of his habit, and tells her before she can ask, all of it, plainly. In his words, one sentence: after she testifies Monday, the prosecutor asks the judge to keep her in Portland under guard until the trial, or until she signs for the program. "You said no to the one thing that keeps you breathing. This one doesn't need your yes. You'd have heard it from a judge on Monday. You hear it from me."
5. "Cancel it." / "No."
6. **The boss rings back on speaker:** "The prosecutor's agreed to ask. Two o'clock Monday." Beth, to the phone: "Can you stop it?" The boss: "It rests on my deputy's word that you'll be killed if you go home. Only his word takes it back. I don't overrule the man on the ground." (B2)
7. **Beth names her leverage and refuses it.** She says it to Dane, not to the phone: "I could tell her I won't go in that room Monday till it's gone." A beat. "I won't. I'm going in that room. You don't get to make me trade it."
8. **She moves.** She takes her coat off the hook and goes. Dane: "Where?" / "My boat's broken." He can't leave a prisoner and can't leave her. He hauls Ned up off the back-room floor by the arm and gets him into his coat one-handed, the rifle on his shoulder. "Walk where you like. I'm paid to." That line goes **mid-beat**, never as the scene's last line.

**Subtext:**
- Dane does the one thing his whole method forbids. He goes round her, knowing every fact, because he has watched her spend her own life three times in four days.
- He tells her at once because he will not lie to her (bible: "What he would never do", no. 2). The telling is also his way of making the act stick.
- Beth refuses the program and the hold to her own face, as herself, with the people who decide her life talking about her as "she" in front of her.
- Refusing her leverage is her values on the page. She won't let Cole's case become a bargaining chip in their fight.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal, and it can't be talked away (one-talk test). The request is made, he says why in full, and he won't withdraw it. Only his word undoes it.
**Ends on (interruption, mid-motion):** Beth is out the door with snow blowing in across the floor. Dane goes after her with Ned on his arm. The phone on the sill is still on speaker, and the boss says "Reyes?" to an empty room. Dane's thumb ends the call as he passes. No line from Beth.
**Word count target:** 340. **Floor:** 310.
**Turn:** The hold. It's irreversible inside this chapter, because he holds the only key.
**Longing:** when he tells her, he crosses the room to do it, close enough that she can smell stove smoke on his coat. She doesn't step back, and that's what makes her angriest.
**Laugh:** "She's here. She's been here the whole call, and she can hear you fine."
**Texture budget (1):** the material-witness hold in one plain sentence. No statute, no "warrant" lecture.
**Plants:**
- The hold (→ Ch 13 "Cancel it", refused; Ch 14 withdrawn on speaker; Ch 16 the door held open)
- "Only his word takes it back" (→ Ch 14 "Withdraw it… Write that down")
- "I'm going in that room" (→ Ch 16)
- Beth speaking to the boss herself (→ Ch 14 "Say it to me")

**Pull-gate risk: highest in the chapter.** This is a phone scene, and phone scenes have scored 2 in this book. Rules for it:
- The boss gets two short speeches only.
- The act is a physical thumb on a screen, watched.
- "Cancel it." / "No." comes within 60 words of the send.
- Beth leaves the room with a prisoner in tow before anyone can explain anything further.
- If it runs long, cut the boss's "Think about it" line first. Never cut the leverage beat.

### Scene 5: The car deck

**Setting:** The ferry's car deck at the stone pier, from early afternoon to dusk, Wednesday.
- Snow on the steel, and the lumber truck and the bait truck still chained where they stuck.
- The ferry knocks against the bare stone where the float used to be.
- The steering hatch is aft, open, with a ladder down into the cold.
- Beth works with the ferry's big wrench.
- From the deck you can see across the harbor to the east rise and her house.

**Characters present:** Beth, Dane, Ned (B10).
**Surface action:**
1. **Ned to the rail** (B10). Dane moves one of Russ's cuffs from Ned's wrist to the car-deck rail, where Dane can see him from the hatch. Ned, weather first: "Wind's going round west by dark."
2. **Down the hatch.** Beth throws the hatch open and goes down the ladder on the bad knee with the wrench. Dane: "I'll hold the light." / "Don't hold the light." He stands at the hatch with the rifle and his back to the trucks, watching the pier and the road.
3. **What's wrong**, one sentence and no more: the rod that runs from the wheel back to the rudder. "The first storm bent it and tore it off the rudder." The pin at the top is frozen. She hits it with the wrench, and the sound rings through the whole hull.
4. **His case,** said down into the hatch. He's polite and slow, and "Ms. Calloway" comes back once:
   - New Bedford: "Testifying ends nothing."
   - The pistol at her on the cove rocks.
   - This morning's wall.
   - Then, with Ned ten feet away at the rail: "The man who loved you most on this island sold you for his son, and the next one will sell you for less." Ned looks at the sea and says nothing.
5. **His beach line** (matches Part A's Ch 08): "I wanted to tell your people five. My boss wanted her test, and you wanted them told the truth by you, and a man got shot in the shoulder at three. Every time I've asked you, it was your life you spent." (Flag 2.)
6. **Her case.** She comes up the ladder to the waist and works the rod from there, plainer and bossier, with his full name once:
   - "That's Cole winning, Dane Reyes. Slower. If I leave my island because a boy shot at me, then shooting at me works, and every one of them learns it."
   - "You're right about New Bedford. And the wall, and Ned. And the beach. I wanted them told, and a man paid for it. It's still my life to spend."
7. **The rod comes free** with a crack under the wrench. She holds it up into the gray light, bent along its length. She'll take it to the co-op vise in the morning, said in one line. It's a fact, and she puts no hope in it.
8. **"I'd lose you to keep you alive."** He says it with no joke after it. Beth: "You will."
   - Dane: "I know. It's the one thing my job can still do for you, and I've done it."
   - She reaches up, because the bad knee won't take the last rung with the rod in her other hand. He gives her his hand, and she comes up out of the hatch on it.
   - At the top, still holding his hand: "Then they stop. And so do we." Then she lets go.
   - He takes it standing. "I'm still your guard until Monday." / "Then stand there."
9. **The ferry has to be good for something.** She sits on the hatch edge with the bent rod across her knees (the image for Ch 13 s5) and starts on the next bolt, and he stands there. That's the afternoon, in action: she works and he guards. There's no summary line of silence. That was the pull-curve stop, and it's gone.
10. **Dusk.** Ned, at the rail and facing the east rise, says it first, in weather: "Wind's backed west." Then: "Somebody's lit your stove." Up on the rise, over the roofs, smoke is rising from her chimney into the last of the light. At the foot of her lane Russ's truck sits where it has sat all day. Her house has stood empty and cold for five days (Issue 11).

**Subtext:**
- They're both right, and each one knows the other is right. That's why it ends them.
- He's fighting to keep her alive by taking her life. She's fighting to keep her life by risking it.
- She takes his hand to climb out while she ends them, because she still wants it. He gives it to her while she ends them, because he'd give it to her anyway.
- Ned hears himself named as the man who sold her, and he answers with the wind, because he has no right to anything else.

**Tension source:** Interpersonal, adult and face to face, with the fight carried by work: the frozen pin, the wrench ringing, the rod coming out, the climb. There's a third person present who is the fight's evidence. Then situational at the end: someone is in her house.
**Ends on (image, no button):** Dane's rifle comes off his shoulder. Beth is on her feet with the bent rod still in her hand, and the three of them look up at the smoke. Ned's cuff chain ticks against the rail in the wind. No line closes the chapter.
**Word count target:** 520. **Floor:** 480.
**Turn:** "And so do we." They're over. Then smoke from her chimney: the kid has gone to ground in her house.
**Longing:**
- The hand on the ladder, held a breath past the words.
- Earlier, from the bottom of the hatch, she looks up and finds he's been watching her hands on the wrench and not the pier, and he looks back at the pier too late.
- Keep both shown. No stated wanting in this scene; the chapter's one is in s3.

**Laugh:** "Don't hold the light." Then one exchange in Ned's register:
- Dane, at the rail, after the third wrench blow: "Is she always like this down a hatch?"
- Ned: "Snow's letting up." A beat. "Since she was nine."
- Put this **before** his case, so the scene starts warm.
- "Since she was nine" names her age at the car-deck knot. It isn't a family member, and it's outside the tender beat.

**Texture budget (2):** the rod and the rudder in one sentence; the frozen pin and the wrench. Nothing else mechanical. **No hatch lecture** (that was the cold reader's close fourth-most-boring passage in old Ch 14).
**Plants:**
- "Don't hold the light" (→ Ch 14 "Tonight you hold the light")
- The bent rod to the co-op vise (→ Ch 13 s5 across her knees; Ch 14 the jam)
