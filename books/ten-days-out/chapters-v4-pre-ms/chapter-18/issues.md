# Chapter 18 · Continuity Issues

Draft read: `chapters/chapter-18/draft.md`. Checked against the beat sheet, outline (including every carry-forward through Ch 18), continuity log through Ch 17, `chapters/chapter-17/final.md` as revised (Ned in the county jail with a lawyer, his son in a Portland clinic, last line "Will they let me stay on the island?" / "Ask them."), the book brief, and the full bible. The outline and log win over the bible where they differ. Word count was not checked (the orchestrator's step).

## Issues

**Issue 1 (timeline, blocking)**
- Problem: The draft never says when Beth and Dane got back to Cutter, and its Tuesday placements cannot work. Ch 17 puts Beth and Dane in a Portland hotel on Monday afternoon, with Russ flying out "in the morning" (Tuesday). Portland to Pruett is two hours by road, then an hour by ferry. The ferry was left at Pruett at four on Monday morning. The beat sheet makes Wednesday her first run as the board's captain. Yet the draft puts Dane on Cutter on Tuesday to plug and bolt her door. It has Tess "not saying one word to her" on Tuesday while Russ is flown off the island that morning, which puts Beth on the island at that time. It also says Russ flew out Tuesday and came home on her ferry Wednesday night. Nothing in the chapter says how or when they crossed.
- Location: "Tuesday," Dane said behind her. / "Tuesday. The day after Portland. Russ flown off the island that morning, and Tess not saying one word to her, and Tess had sat down that same day and written to the board." / "He slid the new bolt he'd put on Tuesday"
- Suggested fix: Add one past-tense clause (S1 or S3) saying how they got back (for example, on the first ferry Tuesday). Then make Tuesday survivable: Russ's flight must slip to the afternoon, or the Tuesday line must stop implying Beth watched him go. Check this against "first run Wednesday" in the beat sheet and the log.

**Issue 2 (fact against Ch 01)**
- Problem: Dane was called in the small hours of Friday Nov 13 and was due to see the boat on Saturday Nov 14. He did not "get the call" on the Saturday.
- Location: "It was the boat in Pruett he'd been driving up to see on the Saturday he got the call instead."
- Suggested fix: "…the boat in Pruett he'd come up to see the day after the call came." Also drop "driving up". In Ch 01 he is already in a motel ten minutes from the harbor.

**Issue 3 (fact and order of events)**
- Problem: Ned sold her the first night. He called Cole from the boat while she photographed the plate at Pruett (Ch 12). That was before he hauled her out from the ferry and pier on Friday morning (Ch 02). "Hauled her out… and sold her by the end of the week" reverses the order. It also understates the sale, which makes the verdict sentence factually wrong.
- Location: "Ned Haskell had hauled her out from between the ferry and the pier on the first morning and sold her by the end of the week, and a page in pencil didn't change what that added up to."
- Suggested fix: Say he sold her the night before he hauled her out. Or cut the sale clause and keep one plain verdict about the man.

**Issue 4 (bet logic, and narration explains the callback)**
- Problem: (a) Dane bets "we're still bailing at noon." Beth says "Done," which puts her on the side that they will not be. Seconds earlier she said "It'll take up in a week. Till then you bail." She bets against her own stated forecast. The beat sheet's subtext ("she knew she'd lose and took it because she wants it to float") appears nowhere on the page. A listener sees only a bet that contradicts her. (b) "She couldn't see into the planks. Nobody could." is narration supplying the reason for her first blind bet. The beat sheet said the narration never explains it and Dane's face carries it.
- Location: "She couldn't see into the planks. Nobody could." / "Done."
- Suggested fix: Cut the narration line. Let Beth's "Done." stand, with Dane's stare as the whole callback. Give her one half-line of dialogue that shows she wants the boat to float (for example, "It might surprise us"), so the bet reads as hope and not a contradiction.

**Issue 5 (contrast construction, zero tolerance)**
- Problem: "I'm not. I'm writing to the board." is the forbidden "I'm not doing X, I'm doing Y" shape. The beat sheet marked it as a laugh, but the author rule has no exceptions.
- Location: "You're not speaking to me." / "I'm not. I'm writing to the board."
- Suggested fix: Change it to a direct statement that keeps the joke, for example Tess: "I write letters. I don't talk." Or Beth: "You wrote to the board and you won't talk to me." / Tess: "Yes."

**Issue 6 (plain wanting cap)**
- Problem: The cap is plain wanting once, in S1 only. The draft has "She wanted him, in those boots" in S1. It also has four more "Beth wanted…" statements: S4 "Beth wanted to go in there and put her own hand over his", S5 "Beth wanted them gone. She wanted the lamp out…", and S5 "Beth wanted that hand off the bolt and on her". That makes five, and three of them (two of those plus S4) use the same "Beth wanted [him/them/a thing done]" construction. The S2 "could have stood there and watched him… all afternoon" is a sixth, softer one.
- Location: "Beth wanted to go in there and put her own hand over his." / "Beth wanted them gone. She wanted the lamp out and the dishes stacked and Dane to herself." / "Beth wanted that hand off the bolt and on her, and she crossed the room to him."
- Suggested fix: Keep S1's line. Carry the S4 and S5 wanting in action or sensory detail. For S4, she looks through the doorway at his hand on his ribs and her own hand tightens on the towel. For S5, she is already crossing the room, or she stops listening to Russ and watches Dane's hand on the bolt.

**Issue 7 (contradictory sentence, and catch-up)**
- Problem: "The deputies had kept it at Pruett, and it was still in Portland" gives two locations in one sentence. A listener cannot hold both. It is also a past-perfect explanation inserted straight after "So I watch the door." The following sentence "He'd watch the door anyway" restates Dane's line in narration. By this point "watch the door" has been said in Ch 17 ("Can I be the one who watches it anyway?"), in Dane's dialogue here, and in this narration.
- Location: "He had no gun. The deputies had kept it at Pruett, and it was still in Portland. He'd watch the door anyway."
- Suggested fix: "He had no gun. It was in an evidence locker in Portland." Cut "He'd watch the door anyway."

**Issue 8 (who knows what, and the planted phone as a hook a listener can follow)**
- Problem: Nothing in Ch 15–17 shows Russ or Tess were told about the phone Dane found behind the bait truck's wheel. In Ch 15 he found it on the car deck while Russ slept and Tess was on the radio. Dane's line names it as if both know. Russ then infers instantly: "Then somebody on this island did." The source of "the kid swore" is also missing: when and to whom. The kid was a prisoner in the town office from Thursday morning, so the question "did the kid plant it?" is one a listener has not been given a reason to ask. The "big boat" leg of the hook is carried only by an unnamed light and a look. That is by design, but nothing says the light is the boat that came for the ferry.
- Location: "There's one more thing," Dane said. "The kid swore he never put that phone behind the bait truck's wheel." / "Then somebody on this island did. Who? I don't know." He was quiet a moment. "I will."
- Suggested fix: Put one clause in Dane's line identifying the phone ("the phone I found on the car deck Sunday, the one that told that boat where we were"). Add a clause for how he heard (the prosecutor, or his boss, told him on Monday or Tuesday). That also links the light to the big boat for a listener without the narration naming it.

**Issue 9 (catch-up block at the top of S1; plausibility)**
- Problem: Right after the opener and two exchanges, four consecutive sentences give background in the past perfect: bought on Tuesday, the boat in Pruett, came across Wednesday, sat on the mooring. The beat sheet bars catch-up at the top of any scene. Also, a boat "leaking from every seam" that sat on its mooring from Wednesday to Thursday morning, un-bailed, would not have water only over her ankles.
- Location: "He had bought it by phone on Tuesday without seeing it. It was the boat in Pruett he'd been driving up to see… It had come across on her car deck Wednesday and sat on Russ's empty mooring, leaking from every seam."
- Suggested fix: Cut to the phone purchase and the Pruett boat. Move "came across Wednesday" into a spoken line, for example Beth: "You bought a boat you never saw. And I carried it over." Fix the overnight leak: Dane or Beth bailed it Wednesday night, or lower the leak.

**Issue 10 (re-gloss and catch-up inside S4)**

This explains the reveal the dialogue has just made. "Not saying one word" echoes S1 ("didn't say one word") and Ch 17 ("not saying one word to Beth"), so the phrase has become a tic.
- Location: "Tuesday. The day after Portland. Russ flown off the island that morning, and Tess not saying one word to her, and Tess had sat down that same day and written to the board."
- Suggested fix: Cut the paragraph. "You're not speaking to me." already carries it.

**Issue 11 (narration quoting earlier lines back; stated emotion)**
- Problem: "She owed nobody a dollar" appears in S1 and again in S4 ("Beth owed nobody a dollar. She would owe Tess this for the rest of her life"). The S1 instance tells the reader why she pays at once, which her action already shows. The S4 instance restates the line spoken just before it ("I can't pay that back." / "No. You can't.") and states the feeling. "As if she were pricing them" quotes Tess's Ch 05 line about Dane's hands, in narration.
- Location: "She owed nobody a dollar, and she wasn't going to start with him." / "Beth owed nobody a dollar. She would owe Tess this for the rest of her life." / "Tess looked at Dane's hands first, as if she were pricing them."
- Suggested fix: Cut the S1 sentence. Cut the S4 two-sentence paragraph, or replace it with one physical beat (Beth's hand tightening on the towel). Drop "as if she were pricing them", or show Tess's eyes going to his hands without the gloss.

**Issue 12 (explains what both characters already know; speechifying)**

It is the explanation of known events the pipeline flags. - Suggested fix: Cut to a headline: "I know you would. I'd have put me on it." Keep "You never once asked me to my face."

**Issue 13 (Ned's name-off-the-letter is stated three times; "nothing in front of it" is unclear)**

- Location: "It began with her name. There was nothing in front of it." / "Ned took his name off my letter to the board. I need somebody from here to speak for me in June. Will you?"
- Suggested fix: For S4, cut the restated fact: "I need somebody from here to speak for me in June. Will you?" For the letter: "It began with her name, only that." Or have her notice that he had always added something and didn't (without printing "Bethie").

**Issue 14 (listener cannot follow)**

**Issue 15 (motif cap: "ham", and Russ's ask-and-answer tic)**

- Location: "Is it the same ham? It isn't." / "Do we mind? We don't." / "Didn't they? They did."

## Does the last exchange land as the book's ending?

Yes, with one caution. "Your ten days are up, Marshal." / "I know." / "So. Are you any good at your job?" / "Ask me every night." pays Ch 01's "Ask me again in ten days" in dialogue only, as intended. It reverses the asking (he now wants to be asked), echoes Beth's Ch 15 condition ("You ask me. Every time"), and promises every night without promising safety. It ends on a line of dialogue, with no image after it, which the plan required. The caution is that it depends on the listener remembering a Ch 01 exchange seventeen chapters back, and nothing in Ch 18 re-cues it except "ten days" (Issue 20 on the count). The hook beat just before it is also not named as the big boat (Issue 8). The payoff of "Ask them." lands in S3, where Dane asks both of them directly and the answer is Russ's "We don't" plus Tess's price line (Issue 22).
