# Sample read: Chapter 1

**Draft read:** `books/ten-days-out/chapters/chapter-01/final.md` (v2 rebuild, Oct 29, 2026), Chapter One: "Night Freight", about 3,300 words. I read nothing else.

**Persona:** Kindle Unlimited reader, 46, reads on her phone, three or four books a month in open-door romantic suspense. She reads for the couple and the danger in equal parts. She has already given up on two slow-burn books this autumn whose heat was all looks and hints. She pays nothing per borrow, so dropping a book costs her nothing.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

Finished the sample. I started drifting in the kitchen scene at "He looked at it the way he'd looked at the pouch. 'That's a very old instrument. I have an app.'" (about 88%), and the radio call cancelling the ferry pulled me back for the last few lines.

**I'd keep reading, but:** the man with the dragging heel went onto her island in scene one, and the chapter then spends its last quarter on a barometer and a clean counter. She never tells the marshal about him. I noticed that, and it bugged me.

## 2. Buy: yes

I'd open chapter 2 because I want to be stuck on that island in a storm with these two while the limping man is somewhere up the road. That is the ending's promise, not anything the last scene actually did.

## 4. Do I care about these two people yet?

Yes, about her. She calmly closes the lid on the drugs and says "Lid's sprung", and I liked her for it. Him, not quite yet: right now he's mostly charm and wrong boots, and I don't know what he wants besides a boat.

## 5. Did anyone make me laugh?

Yes, a smile and close to a laugh:
- "You'd have gone in." / "Probably. Decently, though."
- "Then you'll be the first man off that boat who did."
- "Your captain's toasting me. I'm choosing to take that as support."

## 6. Did anyone make me want?

Yes, twice:
- "the cold of his hand, colder than hers, wrapped all the way round the bones of her wrist like he had measured it ... and she was still holding on." This was the best moment in the chapter for me.
- "putting him at the rail of that boat in the slop, his sleeves shoved up and a trap coming over the side, and getting his hands right on it, and she made the next coil too tight."

For an open-door book the chapter gave me charge and no heat. That's fine for chapter 1. If chapter 2 is more looking and coiling, I'm gone.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. About 10%: "She finished the binder and walked aft without hurrying ... Her hand went along the stern chain across the transom, and she kicked the ice off the ring buoy's bracket on the way by, and the quarter ladder's rungs went past her knee, black and wet." I skimmed this: it's a tour of the boat right when I wanted to know whether they were following her.
2. About 70%: "She knew where everybody went: Russ was the *Second Wind*, Ned was the *North Reach*, and a man with no boat was from away, with nowhere on the island to set him down." I read it twice and still wasn't sure what it was telling me, so I moved on.
3. About 85–90%: the barometer, the duffel, "My mother would like this kitchen ... I'm making no comment about the counter." It's cute, but a man who saw her face is loose on the island, and here we are packing a sweater.

## 8. What I think is going on

Drugs are coming through the island on the dealer's reefer, and somebody local is in on it. The fuel float at Marr's being lit at that hour isn't an accident, and I'd bet Ned knows more than he says, since he sent her home and never asked again. He may be protecting her, or he may be in on it. He also took the store's bag with Tess's tag on it, and I half suspect money moves in those bags. The limping man is an islander, or he's staying with one. Reyes's buzzing phone means someone is leaning on him: maybe a leak, maybe he's being pulled off the case, and the "last witness ... is buried in it" line means it's happened to him before. Her father lost his boat to the bank, and I suspect he was mixed up in something like this once. Prediction: the storm keeps them on the island, the limping man comes to her house, and they end up in bed somewhere around the middle.
