# Sample read: Ten Days Out, Chapter 1 (round 2)

**Draft read:** `books/ten-days-out/chapters/chapter-01/final.md`, the v2 rebuild of Oct 29, 2026, with the new final scene (Tess on the slip, the skiff, the radio call). Nothing else opened.

**Persona:** KU reader, 35 to 55, reads on her phone, three or four books a month. She reads for the couple and the danger in equal measure. She quits when the heat is only hints or when the setup runs long. She has already been bored by two books with this feel.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

Finished the sample.

Warning (an "I'd keep reading, but"): a lot of this is boat talk I don't speak. I skated over binders, D-rings, dolphins, the "master and one deck" line, and "Hundred and ninety. The place on Route One that sells fudge," which I had to read twice before I got that she was pricing his boots. On a phone at 11 pm that adds up. If chapter 2 starts with more deck chores before he and I find out who's at the back door, I'm gone.

## 2. Buy: yes

The chapter ends with a man who knows her name sitting outside her house, the last boat cancelled, and the marshal's hand going to his gun, so I'm turning the page to see who comes in.

## 3. Pull score per scene

| Scene | Score | What I wanted next |

| 1. Night freight (dark reefer, bricks under the lobsters, the call) | 4 | To find out why Ned sent her home, and whether the limping man knows where she lives. |
| 2. The ramp (Reyes, the boots, the jump, the wrist) | 4 | For him to stay aboard and for one of them to admit that grab lasted too long. |

Average 3.75. It is not a 5 anywhere. Nothing grabbed me so hard I'd skip sleep for it.

## 4. Do I care about these two people yet?

Yes. Beth hides the drugs with a straight face, then sits in the dark not wanting to say her own name to a stranger, and I'm on her side. He's charming more than he's real so far. I care about her; I'm interested in him.

## 5. Did anyone make me laugh?

Yes, a few times, all small:

- "Your captain's toasting me. I'm choosing to take that as support."
- "You'd have gone in." / "Probably. Decently, though."
- "Coffee's free to islanders. Two dollars to you."

## 6. Did anyone make me want?

Yes. "Neither of them let go... the cold of his hand, colder than hers, wrapped all the way round the bones of her wrist like he had measured it... and she was still holding on." Then "Her right wrist still knew where his fingers had gone round it. She kept that hand on the line." That landed. So did her picturing him on the lobster boat with his sleeves shoved up and "getting his hands right on it." For an open-door book it is still only a hand on a wrist, which is fine for chapter 1 and not fine for chapter 3.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. About 2%: "The co-op's bait truck smelled like a decision somebody had made about herring in August. The plumber had chained his own van, the way he billed, generously and with gaps..." The opening is a tour of the trucks before anything happens. I skimmed down to the dark reefer.
2. About 10%: "Her hand went along the stern chain across the transom, and she kicked the ice off the ring buoy's bracket on the way by, and the quarter ladder's rungs went past her knee, black and wet. Then the outside ladder... Her shoulder brushed the breaker box..." This should be the scary walk away from men who just saw her see the drugs, and it reads like a list of boat parts. I skimmed it.
3. About 62 to 68%: the Webbers Cove exchange ("What's a Webbers Cove thirty-four with a rebuilt Cat worth?") through "She knew where everybody went: Russ was the *Second Wind*, Ned was the *North Reach*..." I skimmed the boat names. It's backstory dressed up as small talk, right after the best moment in the chapter.

## 8. What I think is going on

Ned is in on it, or at least paid to look away. He sent her home for the first time in eleven years on the night the reefer ran dark, didn't ask about the crate, and walked off with the store's bag that has Tess's tag on it. I think the money moves in those bags, which puts Tess or the store in it too. The limping man stayed on the island and was picked up at Marr's fuel float, which was lit when it never is, so Marr's is involved. Wade Pelletier is only the driver. The man in the skiff is coming for Beth now that the last boat is cancelled. I'd bet that cancellation was Ned's call more than the weather's. Reyes's phone gets turned away from her twice, and his last witness died, so I think there's a leak on his end and the person he keeps texting is the problem. His wanting "a boat I can't be called off of" means he got pulled off a case before, probably the one where the witness died. Her father lost the boat to the bank, and "a man in a good coat" wrote him down. I suspect that ties back to the same people.
