## Rules for all five scenes

These apply on top of every beat below. A variant that breaks one has failed the beats, however well it reads.

1. **No contrast constructions in any form,** in narration or dialogue: "It wasn't X. It was Y."; "Not X, but Y."; "not because X but because Y"; "less X than Y"; and the positive-then-negated shape ("North end. Not the house."). State the thing directly. The kept lines below have been checked against this (see the table at the end).
2. **Banned-list phrases (brief §3, bible FORBIDDEN PHRASES):** "Okay." as a pivot; "for the record"; "I'd like it noted"; "with his whole face"; "Talk to me."; "under open sky"; "longer than X needed"; "the way he always did" and near-forms; "Lumpy"; "like a man looking at weather"; Dane's "I'm told"; "Neither of them let go/moved"; "'Him,' Beth said."; "the weight of"; plus the bible's full list ("a beat passed", "her mind raced", "something shifted", and the rest).
3. **Tics to keep out of these five scenes** (book-wide counts are already over cap): "said nothing" / "didn't say a word"; "thumb" (one per scene at most); "good hand" (none in T4 or T5); the brow's wheels grinding; "an inch"; the bad hip; the green jacket (by that name); the store's windows yellow; the cove slapping; "the length of"; "a stride"; "let" as a narrating verb ("she let him", "let it be hers"); "Nobody on this island…" as a maxim.
4. **Spent motifs. Do not use:** "Bethie" (6 of 6; the only one allowed in these scenes is the kept T1 line, which is already counted); "Steel doesn't burn" (3 of 3); "Seal noted" (2 of 2); "Walked the bar" (3 of 3); "Not again today" (2 of 2); "Dollar says" (only the existing T1 instance, if kept). "Ask me sober" is at 2 of 2, counting the one T3 keeps, so it appears exactly once in T3 and nowhere else in these scenes. "Me and God" has one use left, which T3 may spend. "Ms. Calloway" has one use left, which T5 may spend.
5. **Registers (brief §2.11). A tag-strip test must sort every line:**
   - **Beth:** clipped, orders and verdicts; drops subjects; no questions except in desire or tenderness.
   - **Dane:** fuller, dry, procedural, whole sentences; quieter when he wants something; his lies go short.
   - **Ned:** weather first; keeps his subjects; "They said" / "He said"; too much detail only when lying; never comments on people.
   - **Tess:** headlines and prices, bright, fast, interrupts; surnames for everyone; "Beth" only when it's serious.
   - **Quist:** full official sentences, passive voice, one swear.
   - **Pike:** conditions and orders, no small talk.
6. **For the ear.** Tag at least every third line in any quick-fire exchange. Name the place or the person wherever a listener could lose who is where. No notebook shorthand.
7. **No recap at the top. No sentence that states the theme** (paper, pencil, being written down). Feeling is never named; a hand does the next thing.
8. **No hide-still-while-searched move.** It is spent. Any threat in these scenes makes someone act or choose.
9. **No polished one-line button at the end of a scene.** Each scene's assigned ending type is below.

## T1 — Ch 06 s3: the rock call, the walk under the bank, the *North Reach* forepeak

**Setting:** Wednesday, Day 6. First sun flat from the east on the granite ledge above Calloway Cove. Then the shore under the bank on the morning ebb (the ledges dry from about a quarter to six until noon; low water about ten to nine), past the turn of the shore where the low ground opens on the inner harbor, along the riprap to the stone pier. Then the *North Reach*'s forepeak: water over the floor plates to the second rung, rust and cold paint. Sensory anchor: the cold of the forepeak water climbing through her boots at the second rung.

**People present:** Beth, Dane, Ned. Three named. The heel man is seen at a distance on the dragger's rail and is not present.

**What the scene must accomplish:**
1. Land the extraction plan (lifeboat, first light, the town float) in one exchange, with no logistics run.
2. Make the walk a scene with a threat in it and a choice under that threat.
3. Leave the reader holding **two live suspects** (brief §2.2 completion question 1). Tess gets the hour by the county. Ned gets it by the tide question.
4. Give the daylight trip to the most watched boat on the island a reason (audit Finding 7e).
5. Deliver Ned's shorts confession and the wrench intact.
6. Make the tide question Beth's condemnable choice, with the interior beat the bible requires: she promised, Ned is the boat, and she asks anyway.

### New beats
1. **The call on the ledge.** Dane takes the Iridium call with his back to the wind; the words don't reach her. Glass flashes on the *Margaret Ann*'s wheelhouse; "Down." He finishes it crouched. "Pike?" / "Pike. Weather."
2. **One exchange of logistics.** Dane gives it in no more than two sentences, going down off the rock: the lifeboat at first light to the town float, *because it's the only lee in this swell*; the marine inspector riding over on her for the ferry (Ch 07 s1 needs the reader to know he's aboard); Beth goes back with them; the landing cleared with the county.    - **The clue, read two ways:** Beth knows the county rings its emergency director, and that's Tess. One thought in her head. She doesn't say it to Dane. (Tess weight. Keep it plain; don't underline it.)
3. **Why they're going to the boat (Finding 7e).** One or two lines on the shore. Beth won't leave a boat she crews without telling her master she's going; it's the one thing she would never do (bible). Dane sets the condition: Ned hears that she's going, never when. She agrees aloud. This is the promise the tide question breaks, so it must be on the page in Beth's own word or assent.
4. **The threat on the walk.** Where the shore turns and the low ground opens on the inner harbor, they come into the open in full view of the dragger. The *Margaret Ann* has moved off her guest mooring to a new one, the town landing square in front of her bow. A man goes aft along her rail, left heel dragging, and stops facing the shore. Glass again, or his stillness.
   - **The choice:** Dane's instinct is plan first: turn back and wait for cover. Beth decides at the water's edge. Turning back leads the glass toward the cove and the fish house. Going to ground is the move a watcher looks for. So they walk on, upright, in the open, at an islander's pace, to the stone pier.
   - **The cost:** they are seen all the way. The man on the rail turns with them, and the page shows him watching them go aboard the *North Reach*. Dane's hand under her elbow over the weed (the existing beat may stay). One line of Dane's procedural patter is allowed; this is a walk, not a crisis.
   - "Moorings swing." / "That isn't swinging." fits here. The innocent reading the reader can hold: the float is the only lee, so any boat covering the extraction would lie there. The suspicious one: somebody told her.
   - **Not allowed:** lying flat while the glass sweeps, or the watcher losing interest and turning away. They must act.
5. **The plan beat.** "Tomorrow we go down to look…" / "I'll know before you do." / "You will." This can come on the riprap. Then the promise ("Nobody. Not Ned.") if it wasn't spent in beat 3.
6. **Aboard.** Dane goes up the outside ladder to the wheelhouse to watch the pier. That puts him out of earshot, so Ch 07's "below decks, where I couldn't hear it" holds. Beth hears the forepeak before she sees it: the pump has quit.
7. **The forepeak.** Water to the second rung. Ned is down in it to the shins with the chewed impeller. "New one's in the mess." He goes up for it. **At the hatch Ned stops and looks back down at her (plant 3 of 3).** Then he comes down with the new impeller in a box with **Marr's price sticker on the end**, stated as a plain fact in one clause, here and only here. **At least two beats must separate the sticker from the reefer lie:** seating the impeller, her hands on the cold steel, his boat talk.
8. **The lie and the confession.** "That unit was dark Thursday." Ned's over-detailed lie (bible sample, keep it or keep its shape: "He always runs his unit. Thermo King, he runs it continuous…"). Beth keeps her hands where they are. Then the confession paragraph, kept whole, then "I thought it was shorts, Bethie." and the wrench. Optional: one short thought in Beth's head after "shorts" that glosses it for the ear (lobsters under the gauge, a dealer's license gone). It must not touch the kept paragraph.
9. **The pump catches; the water starts down the rung.** This is the image Ch 06 s4 recalls. Then the **required interior beat** (bible Character rule 3), one or two sentences: she said "Nobody" on the shore not an hour back; asking Ned isn't telling anybody, because **Ned's the boat**. Those three words appear in her head here, and they come back out loud in Ch 08 (T2). Then she asks.
10. "Is the float workable on the ebb?" / "Which tide?" / "Tomorrow's first."
11. **Ned answers, and the scene ends on his answer.** Ned works the tide out in his own register: weather and water, the float's inshore end grounding, the swell in the lee. He gives her the float detail that s4's plan repeats ("the last ledge still deep in the dark"; she will tell Dane "I know the float"). He is mid-task when the scene cuts: wiping his hands down his jacket, reaching for the tide table on the bulkhead, the pump running. He says nothing about people.

**The threat or cost on the page:** the dragger watching them cross open ground, and the decision to be seen; the glass flash on the rock; the forepeak water cold to the bone of her fingers; and the moral cost, her broken promise.

**Target length:** 1,000 words. **Floor:** 925. **Ceiling:** 1,125. (Current scene is about 1,025.) The extra walk beat is paid for by the logistics cut, about 150 words, and the sun paragraph.

**Ending type:** a cut on someone else's line. Ned's practical answer, mid-task, with no turn and no verdict. **Not** "Tomorrow's first." as the last line, and not Beth's reaction.

**Subtext:** she is checking Ned the only way she lets herself, which is by not checking him. Dane is letting her make the call on the shore because he has learned to, and that is the trust she is about to spend.

**Tension source:** situational (the watcher), interpersonal (plan first against the edge of the water), internal (the promise).

### Hand-offs
