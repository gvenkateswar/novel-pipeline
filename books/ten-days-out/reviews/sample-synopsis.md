# Ten Days Out: what happens in the first three chapters

On a Thursday night in November, Beth Calloway is checking the chains on the trucks aboard the Cutter Island ferry, where she has worked as a deckhand for eleven years. She puts her bare hand on a lobster dealer's refrigerated truck and it isn't cold. A crate lid springs open during tie-down, and under the top layer of lobsters she sees sealed bricks of pills. The driver sees her see them. So does a hooded man loading crates, who walks off the boat onto her island. She presses the lid shut, re-straps it, and goes up to the wheelhouse as if nothing happened.

By morning the driver is under arrest. Beth is working the early boat anyway, because the other deckhand is sick and the island's last freight before a nor'easter is on board. At the mainland ramp, Deputy US Marshal Dane Reyes is waiting to drive her to a safe house. He is quiet, watchful, and wearing exactly the wrong boots for a ferry deck. She won't leave her captain short-handed, and it turns out he can't make her: protection is voluntary. So he rides back out to the island with her and watches her work. She tells him the two o'clock boat won't run. It doesn't.

He hands her a subpoena for a federal grand jury in Portland, ten days away. For the night he moves her aboard the ferry: steel, a wheelhouse door that locks, a radio, and Ned, the captain who has taught her everything since she was twenty-two. At nine the storm surge tears the landing bridge apart with them on board. It comes down on the ferry's bow, and Beth and Ned fight the lines in the dark while Dane hauls wherever she tells him to. The damaged ferry can't carry a passenger until the Coast Guard has inspected her, and the island is cut off. Everyone saw them go aboard, so Dane slips Beth back to her own cottage in the dark. On the way off the boat, Beth tells Ned where she'll be. Nobody else.

At 11:40 someone lifts the sticking latch on her back door. Dane goes to it, and whoever it was runs. "Who knew you were here?" he asks. "You," she says, and she remembers him out on the porch twenty minutes earlier, talking to someone on a satellite phone.

In the morning there is a boot print by the step with a dragging heel. Through the wall she hears Dane say, "No. She doesn't know." At the island store her oldest friend Tess, who runs the storm check-in, has written Beth down as home, with Ned's initials beside it. The skipper of a storm-bound dragger from away buys cigarettes and looks at Dane like a man he's heard about. Ned gives them the key to an empty summer house on the north point. That night, in its freezing generator shed, Dane holds the light while Beth gets the engine running. Then he kisses her, and she kisses him back, and then she stops, because she doesn't know who he calls at 11:20.

Three people could have told the men on that dragger where she slept: the captain who taught her the boat, the friend who has known her since she was nine, and the stranger with a gun who sleeps between her and the door.
