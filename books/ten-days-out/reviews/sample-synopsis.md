# Ten Days Out: what happens in the first three chapters

On a Thursday night in November, Beth Calloway, eleven years a deckhand on the Cutter Island ferry, puts her bare hand on a lobster dealer's refrigerated truck and finds it isn't cold. A crate lid springs open during tie-down, and under the lobsters are sealed bricks of pills. The driver sees her see them. So does a hooded man loading crates, who limps off the boat onto her island. She presses the lid shut ("Lid's sprung. You'll lose your count."), re-straps it, and walks to the wheelhouse as if nothing happened. At one in the morning she calls it in from her kitchen.

By breakfast the driver is in custody, and Beth is working the morning boat anyway: the other deckhand is sick and the island's last propane before a nor'easter is on her deck. On the mainland ramp a deputy US marshal named Dane Reyes is waiting to drive her to a safe house. He is quiet, he is sure of himself, and his boots are for a different ocean. She tells him so. They have it out on the ramp. He tells her the island doesn't need her more than the grand jury does; she tells him to go home to whoever does need him. Then he says the worst true thing he has: the last witness who knew her own town better than he did is buried in it. That stops her for one breath. Then she casts off the line anyway. The ramp starts to rise, and he has to choose. He jumps, badly. She hauls him aboard by the front of his jacket, and for a second neither of them lets go.

On the way across he keeps checking his watch. At 3:40 that afternoon his father drives his last bus route in Lowell after thirty years, and Dane promised to be the last passenger. "He'll tell the whole route anyway. He tells everybody everything." Beth predicts the two o'clock boat won't run. At 1:15 the captain cancels it on the radio. Dane takes off his watch and puts it in his pocket.

He moves her aboard the ferry for the night. At nine the storm surge tears the landing bridge loose. A parted cable whips across the deck, and Dane takes her down flat under the rail with her fist in his collar while the bridge falls on the bow. Afterward her captain, Ned, looks at the wreck and says, "Well. That's the bridge." The boat is holed, and the island is cut off for ten days. Everyone saw them go aboard, so Dane slips her home in the dark. On the way off the boat Beth tells Ned where she'll be. They sit in her dark kitchen with their backs to the chimney under one blanket, and he tells her about the bus he missed and the marriage he lost to his job.

At 11:40 someone lifts the sticking latch on her back door. Dane goes to it, and whoever it was runs. "Who knew you were here?" he asks. "You," she says, and remembers him out in the yard twenty minutes earlier, talking to someone on a satellite phone.

In the morning she hears him through the wall: "No. She doesn't know." She asks him straight out what she doesn't know. He says he can't tell her, and won't lie to her about whether there's something. At the store her oldest friend Tess, who runs the storm check-in, mentions that she saw Beth's flashlight on the back steps last night. Beth keeps that to herself. They move to an empty summer house on the north point. In its freezing generator shed Dane holds the light while she gets the engine going, and then they're kissing against the warm housing with his cold hands under her layers. She stops it, and it turns into the fight they didn't finish that morning. He won't say who he calls at night. She tells him she'd rather be kissed by a man she can check. He goes out on the porch and makes the call where she can watch him do it.

Three people could have told the men on the storm-bound dragger where she was sleeping: the friend who can see her kitchen from the store, the captain who taught her the boat, and the stranger with a gun who sleeps between her and the door.
