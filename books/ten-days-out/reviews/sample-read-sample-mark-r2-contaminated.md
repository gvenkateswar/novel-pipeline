# Sample read: sample mark, round 2 (Ch 01–03)

**Drafts read:** `chapters/chapter-01/final.md` (with the S4 tournament winner), `chapters/chapter-02/final.md` and `chapters/chapter-03/final.md` (after the Oct 29 tournament rewrites). Read in order, on a phone, as a buyer.

**Contamination note:** no persona was supplied, so I read `research/chosen-angle.md` and `book-brief.md` to build one. The brief's approvals section names the leak, so that is now in my head. Item 8 below is written from the page only, and I've marked where the page and the brief happen to agree.

**Persona:** Donna, 54, office manager at a dental practice outside Columbus. On Kindle Unlimited. Last month she read three protection-plot romantic suspense books (two military-hero, one small-town sheriff) and quit a fourth at 20%. She's tired of heroines who run into the dark woods alone, slow burns that never catch, and books that spend pages on the town before anything happens. The last book she paid for, rather than borrowed, was $4.99. She's reading this on her phone at lunch and has six other samples waiting.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

Finished the sample. I'd keep reading, but: I skimmed most of the store scene in Ch 3 (about 72–84%), starting at "Marr's was full, because in a storm everybody on Cutter came to the store." Too many men and boats with names and I couldn't keep them apart. What carried me through was the floor scene at the end of Ch 2.

## 2. Buy: yes

Yes, but just barely. The night on the kitchen floor and the generator shed are the two best scenes I've read in this kind of book in a month, and they made up for the boat-talk.

## 4. Do I care about these two people yet?

Yes. She looks after everybody and doesn't say so. He talks too much and then goes quiet exactly when it counts. By the shed I wanted them both to get what they wanted, and I was scared of what his phone was doing.

## 5. Did anyone make me laugh?

Yes. "Dane." She wrote that too. "Like the dog." / "Like the dog," Dane agreed. And Beth doing Tess's roll call in a whisper: "Calloway, aboard. Marshal, aboard." A small pause, exactly Tess's. "Allegedly."

## 6. Did anyone make me want?

Yes. "That lasted until he turned his jaw to the light and ran his thumb along it to check, and she wanted it to be her thumb." The shed landed too, his cold hands on her back and her swearing into his mouth, and so did her stopping it when the phone pressed into her.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. Around 22% (Ch 1, coiling scene): "What's a Webbers Cove thirty-four with a rebuilt Cat worth?" and the rest of that run through "She knew where everybody went: Russ was the *Second Wind*, Ned was the *North Reach*, and a man with no boat was from away..." I skimmed the boat talk and didn't know who Russ was.
2. Around 42% (Ch 2, pier before the cable): "Below her the ferry lay in the slip with her bow shoved up under the bridge, the car ramp on its two steel towers," then Tess's roll call and "Beth doubled the lines that held the bow to the pier at dusk, and again in full dark." I couldn't picture the bridge and the towers, so I skimmed until something screamed.
3. Around 80% (Ch 3, store): "Tess snapped a yellow tag through the zipper of the deposit bag... 'Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's. I won't have my money in Ned's color.'" Then "'Ashby,' Beth said. Her father's fish house had the cove over its sill till Monday. The Connecticut people's place on the north point..." I gave up on the store here and just looked for where they were going.

## 8. What I think is going on

Ned. Beth told only Ned "Home. Don't tell anybody," and the man was in her mudroom by twenty to twelve. Ned sent her home the night of the truck for the first time in eleven years. Ned is the only one who knows about Ashby. And "Nobody but me and God" twice reads like a flashing light. (The brief I read for the persona agrees, but I'd have landed on him from the page by the end of Ch 2.) Tess is my second suspect: the roll of hundreds, Arsenault's line about the store having "a hard winter", and the way she snapped about the bank. I think she's in debt to the Gloucester boat. I don't really suspect Dane. The book keeps showing me he's decent: he stands between her and the water, and he won't lie about there being a secret. So the "You." at the end of Ch 2 felt like a scare rather than a real possibility. My guess on the phone: Homeland Security is using Beth to watch the Gloucester boat or Tess, and Dane has to log her movements for them. "Firm at thirty-one" is just his boat price, and the book told me that straight away.
