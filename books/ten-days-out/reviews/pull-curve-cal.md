# Whole-book pull curve (calibration read): Ten Days Out

**Draft read:** `books/ten-days-out/manuscript.md`, 31,034 words, built Oct 30, 2026, commit bad62d3, Chapters One to Ten. I read nothing else except `research/chosen-angle.md`, and only to build the persona below, because no persona was passed to me.

**Persona.** Dana, 41, dental hygienist outside Columbus. She reads on the Kindle app on her phone at lunch and in bed. She has KU, but she pays $4.99 when a sample hooks her, and that is what she paid for the last book she finished, a bodyguard romance. Last month she read two protection-plot romantic suspense books and finished one. She is tired of mysteries where she knows the bad guy at 40% and the heroine doesn't work it out until 85%, and tired of scenes where two people sit and go over who knew what.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

> "Town ring hangs by her safe. Second key to the float box is on it." (Chapter Eight, the wheelhouse scene, about 78%)

I had known it was Ned for two chapters. Here were two people going over who holds which key for the fourth time, and the heroine was still saying "Ned's the boat." I put the phone down. I read Chapters Nine and Ten only because I was asked to, and I scored them below on that basis.

## 2. Buy: yes

Yes. Chapters One to Three, the bridge coming down and the mudroom door going quiet, would have sold me the book before the middle let me down.

**Buy book 2: no.** What's left open is the *Dorothy M.* and Russ joining a task force. I don't want a book about either one.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. **Reading the notebook**, Ch 7, about 65–66%: "*D6 am. Q: CU written rpt. Pouch: two bags. Seal noted: red.*" followed by a paragraph explaining it. I skimmed it. I was decoding shorthand on a phone.
2. **The wheelhouse key scene**, Ch 8, about 78–80%: "Town ring hangs by her safe. Second key to the float box is on it." / "That ring has hung there since before she bought the store..." Who has which key, again. This is where I stopped.
3. **Quist questions Tess**, Ch 10, about 90–92%: "Your spare truck key was left on the back step the night the bridge came down, and again Monday noon. How many keys on that hook, Ms. Marr?" The third time through the same tally, after the climax.

Close behind: the mess-table back-and-forth in Ch 8 (about 72%, "The generator shed... All those money questions"), and the Tess-truck "22:40" scene in Ch 5 (about 38%).

Also: the man in the hood with the dragging left heel shows up so often that by Chapter Seven, "a step, a drag, a step" made me roll my eyes rather than tense up.

## 8. What I think is going on (who I suspected, and when)

- **Dane** was set up as the suspect at the end of Ch 2 ("Who knew you were here?" "You."). I believed it for about a page. By the generator shed (Ch 3, about 25%) I'd dropped him. The book puts a romance hero in bed with the heroine, so he isn't the leak.
- **Tess** was the red herring from the roll of hundreds in her vest (Ch 2, about 12%). By her truck scene in Ch 5 I read her as a decoy, and the condom-counter ledger confirmed that she was keeping Arsenault's records, not working for him.
- **Arsenault** was obviously the money from his first appearance in the store (Ch 3, about 23%).
- **Ned** flickered first in Ch 1 (he takes both bags, the fare bag and the store's: "Go home. I'll lock up."), again in Ch 2 ("Nobody but me and God," and he knows she went home), and in Ch 3 (the Ashby key is his). **I suspected him seriously at the end of Ch 4 (about 35%)**, when he walks up to the shed with diesel minutes after the hood leaves. I was fairly sure in Ch 6 (about 57%, the impeller confession, "So I never said"). Then she asks him about the float and the float lights go dark. I was certain at the top of Ch 7 (about 60%): "Ned and the town ring had the only keys to the box." The confession in Ch 9 told me nothing new except the red seal from his drawer.

So the page gave away the leak by about 60%, and the book spent the next 20% on the heroine refusing to see it.
