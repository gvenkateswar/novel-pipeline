# Pull curve, Ch 01–05 (r2)

**Draft read:** Ch 04 and Ch 05 rebuilt v2, Oct 29 (`chapters/chapter-01/final.md` through `chapters/chapter-05/final.md`, read in order, nothing else opened).

**Persona:** KU reader, 35–55, reads on her phone, three or four books a month. Open-door romantic suspense, and she wants the couple and the danger in equal measure. She quits when the heat is all hints or the setup runs long. Two books like this have already bored her.

---

## WARNING (above the scores): "I'd keep reading, but…"

I finished Ch 05. I would read on, but I nearly closed the app in the middle of Ch 04. I put the phone down at **"Pull it."** (end of Ch 04 scene 2, about 58% through Ch 04, about 70% of what I was given). I only came back because the generator-shed kiss in Ch 03 still owed me something. Ch 04 is three scenes in a row where the bad guy stands far off and looks at things. By then he had already looked at something from a distance in Ch 01, twice in Ch 02 and once in Ch 03.

Finished all five chapters. Near-stop at "Pull it." (Ch 04, about 70% of the total). Why: the hooded man looked at another house from another hill, the two of them moved to another hiding place, and nothing had happened to anyone since Ch 02.

## 5. Did the first love scene deliver?

**Yes.** It landed at **"It's a very disappointing kit," he said.** The laugh made it theirs and not a template, and it told me why there's no sex proper without anyone lecturing me. It landed a second time at **"…then she lost the sum halfway, and let it go… and she noticed she wasn't steering any of it."** The one dip: the "callus across his palms" and "red tag on his wrist" call-backs feel placed, and I noticed them as placement. It is hands and mouths only. Some KU readers will feel short-changed after a chapter and a half of waiting. I didn't, because of the kit.

## 6. Which stretch has nobody under threat?

Ch 04 from **"The set had run dry at noon"** through the end of the chapter, except for the roughly one page with the man on the spine. That stretch holds the bank-box flashback, the fare-bag Q&A, Ned's fuel delivery with her license musing, and the whole bar rehearsal ("Monday, at dead low…" to "…you stay inside where I put you."). Nobody is in danger anywhere in it, and that is where I put the phone down.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. **Ch 04, about 45%:** "The *Ruthie C* was thirty-eight feet and named for her mother…" through "…as if she'd brought him a pie." I skimmed it. Ch 01 already told me the bank took the boat, and this is the long version while the engine sits there.
2. **Ch 04, about 50%:** "Envelopes from the store. Slip inside. My name on the front in Tess's hand. Flap tucked. Never sealed." / "Who sees the slip?" I didn't know why he was asking about bags, and I didn't care enough to work it out.
3. **Ch 04, about 85%:** "In to the knee. The cold took her calves and squeezed. In to the thigh…" It's a practice wade, and I knew we'd be doing it for real.

## 10. Suspects, from the page only

The question is who is telling the hood where she is.

- **Ned, 30%.** "Nobody but me and God" twice. He's the one she told "Home." He handed over the Ashby key and says he runs the generator out there every storm. He carried both money bags off the boat on night one, and the fare-bag questions point at the boat being "alone with it." The book keeps making me love him, which makes me nervous.
- **Tess, 25%.** The roll of hundreds. Her truck in the drive at two and again at the fire. She keeps a book on everyone and sat on Friday night. "Don't you look at my book like the bank does."
- **Portland / Q / Pike, through Dane's calls, 20%.** Every time Dane phones in, someone turns up. "Do not disclose to witness." I think Q is using her as bait or is looking at the ferry money, not at the drugs.
- **Russ, 12%.** He asks where she's sleeping, wants her out on his boat, and took the path behind Marr's when he could have gone home.
- **Arsenault, 8%.** He obviously runs the dragger, but as the leak he's too easy.
- **Dane himself, 5%.** I don't believe it. The book wants me to wonder.

What I think the secret is: the ferry's money bags, the store's yellow and the boat's red, are a cash route. That's what Homeland is really logging, and someone in Beth's own circle, probably Ned with Tess covering, is part of it. The leak and the money are the same person.
