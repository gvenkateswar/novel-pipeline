## Blocking

### Finding N1: they cross at night, the one time nobody will come
**Where:** Ch 14 s3, the boss: "after an officer was shot the state won't send the next nearest one out at night into what's left of this sea." Ch 15 s2: "They cast off at ten." Ch 16 s1: "out here in the dark, a lost ferry looks like the storm." The grand jury sits at ten Monday. The crossing is "three hours at best the short way."
**Question failed:** 4 (safer options) and Dane's competence.
**The problem:** The reader has just heard that the state's only reason for no escort is the dark. The book then says the dark is what lets the big boat attack a ferry unseen. A three-hour crossing that leaves at ten lands at one, nine hours before the grand jury. Leaving at five, in daylight, gets her there by eight, maybe with the escort the state refused only for the night. They are aboard at the pier from Saturday dawn, so the reader also asks why not Saturday or Sunday in daylight. Nothing in the outline closes either option. "The whole coast knows the window" implies a weather window, but the outline never says what the window is, and Dane picks the hour that suits the enemy.
**What the architect must answer:** why ten on Sunday night, and not daylight on Sunday or Monday.
**Suggested fix:** one line in Ch 14 s3, and Dane says it, not the boss. The harbor mouth is white water until Sunday evening, and the next front comes in before light Monday ("By five it's blowing forty again. Sunday night or not at all"). Then take "at night" out of the Marine Patrol line ("won't send the next nearest one into what's left of this sea, with another blow coming"). The escort stays closed, the night becomes forced, and the reader gets a clock.

## Non-blocking, ordered by what they would cost the reader

### N2: Finding 1's fix sits in the scene the outline says to compress
**Where:** Ch 10 s3 (200 words) has to hold the storm, the two-hour path, the kid's direction, Dane's line, the key, the kiss at the door, "Door first," the wedge, the rifle, seeing the blood, cleaning and closing the cut, and her watching. Pacing flag 1: "If it runs over, s3 and s5 compress."
**The problem:** 200 words can't carry all of that. The writer will cut the safety logic first, because it isn't dialogue, and Finding 1 reopens on the page.
**Fix:** raise s3 to 400. Take the words from s2 (700) and s5 (250). Change flag 1 so that s5 and s2 compress and s3 never does.

### N3: the leak's phone is off while the leak is waiting for an answer
**Where:** Ch 10 s5: at two, *where r u. answer me.* Ch 11 s1 [A13]: "Dane rang the leak's number at a quarter past two, and it was off. It's only ever on to send or to wait for an answer." Four minutes after the bait goes out at half past two: *coming.*
**The problem:** Fifteen minutes after "answer me," the leak is waiting for an answer, so by the book's own rule the phone should be on. A missed call from the kid's number, or from Dane's, would also tell the leak something has changed.
**Fix:** one clause. Ned turns it on every half hour to look and off again; Dane found that out by ringing at a quarter past. Or move Dane's call to before the two a.m. text. Say which phone he rang from, and why that doesn't warn Ned.

### N4: "the one door" against "back door open"
**Where:** Ch 10 s3, "He wedges the one door"; s4, his back to "the door." Ch 11 s1: the bait says *back door open*, and "she goes out the back when he says go." Ch 11 s3: "Dane shuts the door."
**The problem:** It's either one door or two. A listener who heard "the one door" will stumble on "back door open."
**Fix:** Ch 10 s3: "He wedges the front door and bars the back." In s4, the door she turns him to face is "the door to the room."

### N5: the road back, and Ned's walk
**Where:** Ch 10 s3: the shore path back is "two hours in the dark." The east road exists (the kid's camp, Ch 05; the walk in, Ch 12). Ned says *coming* at about 2:34 and is at the door before about four, at 64 on a bad hip. Ch 12 s1: Dane walks Beth and a cuffed Ned down the east road at first light and is shot at from the trees.
**The problem:** If the road is quicker, the reader asks why they didn't take it on Tuesday night. If it isn't, Ned can't arrive in time. And on Wednesday morning, Dane walks his witness through the woods the kid ran into, the night after he refused the shore path because the kid could shoot down onto it.
**Fix:** one line in Ch 10 s3. The road is an hour, but it runs through the spruce the kid went into, and the shore is the only other way. Ned comes by the road with a lantern, because he isn't afraid of the kid. In Ch 12 s1, Dane chooses the road in daylight, knowing the risk, because the shore path below the bank is worse in snow, with a cuffed man who can't climb. He walks first. That way the ambush is a risk he named, not a surprise.

### N6: an armed man heads for the village and nobody is told
**Where:** Ch 12 s1, "the kid breaks off inland toward the village." From then until dusk, Ch 12 has Tess's door, the son and the rupture. Ch 13: the kid is in Beth's house overnight, and the raid waits for first light.
**The problem:** Dane knows a hurt man with a pistol is going toward houses with people in them, and the outline gives no warning, no search and no watch. Overnight on Wednesday, nobody is said to be watching Beth's house, so the kid could just leave.
**Fix:** Ch 12 s2, one line: Tess puts it on the island radio, everyone stays indoors and the empty houses on the harbor road get locked. Ch 13 s1, one line: Dane and Russ took turns on the house through the night.

### N7: Beth waits on the kid's way out
**Where:** Ch 13 s1: Beth is "at her father's fish shack below." In Ch 01, the kitchen window is the family's way out, and it leads to the shack.
**The problem:** Dane is driving the kid out of the window that leads to his witness. A reader who remembers Ch 01 will mark it against him.
**Fix:** put her anywhere off that line (in the fire truck with Tess down the road), or give one line on why the shack is safe: there's stone between it and the window, and Dane stands in the only path.

### N8: who uncuffs Ned in Ch 16?
**Where:** Ch 16 s3: "Dane uncuffs one of Ned's hands to the wheel." Dane is "on the upper deck, a bullet torn along his ribs," and Beth goes up to him after.
**The problem:** Dane can't be in the wheelhouse freeing Ned's hand and on the upper deck hit at the same time.
**Fix:** Beth has his key from Ch 15 ("Take it. If I'm up top, you're the one who decides"). That also gives her the moral choice by her own hand.

### N9: the Coast Guard is "an hour off," and the chase runs longer than that
**Where:** Ch 14 s3: "if it comes at them… the Coast Guard will come from an hour off." Ch 16 s1: Beth calls them. They land at four. "The Coast Guard finds nothing by morning."
**The problem:** If the call goes out around eleven, a Coast Guard boat is due by midnight, and the reader waits for it through s2.
**Fix:** one line in s3. The Coast Guard boat finds them after the ledges, the big boat is gone, and it follows them in. Or Beth can't raise them until the ledges because the big boat is between her and their channel.

### N10: Ch 14 s3 is still a briefing
**Where:** Ch 14 s3 (550 words): the boat seen, four logistics lines from the boss, then the repair, the boss to Beth, the fifth day, and "What do you want, Dane?"
**The problem:** The Ch 08 cold reader called the boss "a briefing being read to me." Four bullet facts from a speaker, followed by the repair, is that same shape in the scene that carries the point of no return.
**Fix:** Beth says the Marine Patrol line herself ("The one on this stretch has a hole in it. I watched it go"). Dane says the lawyer line, and N1's line. The boss gets the Coast Guard and "Cole walks at two." Raise s3 to 650, taking the words from s1.

### N11: two outline phrases will turn into tics
- **"I'm asking":** Ch 15 s1 ("Can I kiss you? I'm asking."), Ch 15 s2 ("There may be a boat out there. I'm asking."), Ch 16 s1 ("Say go"), Ch 17 s2 ("Will you stay? I'm asking."), on top of Ch 09's "I'm asking for it." The pipeline repeats every good line until it's a tic. Keep the Ch 15 s1 kiss and Beth's in Ch 17. Make the other asks a look, or one word.
- **"In his head, plainly" / "In her head, plainly":** Ch 11 s2, Ch 13 s1, Ch 13 s3, Ch 14 s2, Ch 16 s2, Ch 16 s3. Pacing flag 8 bars the flat "She wanted." The outline's own shorthand will produce it. Mark these lines as intent for the writer, not as text.

### N12: Ch 12's ambush reruns Ch 09's
**Where:** Ch 12 s1: a shot from the trees, "Dane gets Beth and Ned down… and fires back, and the kid breaks off." Ch 09: shots from the trees, "Beth, get down!", Dane fires back, the kid breaks off. It's the same three people, too.
**The problem:** This is the beat the Ch 09 reader called "the chapters are starting to look the same," three chapters later.
**Fix:** change one move. Dane sees the kid first (blood on the snow on the road ahead, fresh) and takes them off the road before the shot. Or the kid's one-armed shot hits Ned's lantern, or the snow at Beth's feet, and Ned is the one who pulls her down, out of reflex, before Dane can.

### N13: one sentence keeps the first aid as the way into bed
**Where:** Ch 10 s3: "she watches him be good at it [A5a]. That's what she'd been waiting for."
**The problem:** That sentence makes the tending the trigger again, which is the Ch 04 / Ch 07 path Finding 5 closed.
**Fix:** cut it. She already started it at the door, so the first aid is an interruption she puts up with.
