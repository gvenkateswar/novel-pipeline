## 4. Do I care about these two people yet?

Yes. She won't be put anywhere and he keeps standing between her and the water. The bit where the phone in his pocket presses into her and kills the moment is the first time I worried about them as a couple and not only about who's after her.

## 6. Did anyone make me want?

Yes. The razor: "That lasted until he turned his jaw to the light and ran his thumb along it to check, and she wanted it to be her thumb." Then it pays off in the shed: "She pulled off a glove and put her bare hand along his jaw where she'd watched the razor go that morning." Also the ramp: "she leaned back against the whole weight of him ... and his hand closed over her wrist and held."

The heat isn't all hints, but so far it's one interrupted make-out. If Ch 4 or 5 only teases again, I'll start to feel cheated.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. **About 78% (Ch 3 S2):** "Going back in through the office, Beth picked the two keys for Tess's Ford on their MARR'S tag off the charging shelf and hung them back on the hook, her knuckles in the cords, Ned's phone in its rubber band among the others." Then straight into "Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's. I won't have my money in Ned's color..." I skimmed the whole clue pile. On a phone I'm not going to scroll back to Ch 1 to check whose bag had which tag.
2. **About 22% (Ch 1 S3):** "She knew where everybody went: Russ was the *Second Wind*, Ned was the *North Reach*, and a man with no boat was from away, with nowhere on the island to set him down." Together with the Webbers Cove price talk, this is where my eyes slid.
3. **About 34% (Ch 2 S1):** "He shut the door between them and tried it from his side: a clank, a rattle, the whole door bucking. Then he lifted and shoved, and it came, loud. The third time it opened without a sound." I didn't know why I was watching a man practise a door until it paid off at the end of the chapter. When I read it, it was just a door.

## 8. What I think is going on

The *Margaret Ann* (Arsenault) is the drug boat. The man with the dragged heel works for it, rows like a local and knows the fish houses, so he's from the island. Pelletier was just the driver. Somebody on the island told the heel man Beth was home and not on the boat. Dane is reporting on her daily to Homeland Security behind her back ("Do not disclose to witness"). I think they're letting her sit out here as bait to pull the network into the open, and that's what he can't tell her. The "thirty-one, Saturday noon" is the boat he wants to buy, and that was a red herring aimed at Arsenault at the store. Tess is in money trouble and in somebody's pocket: a roll of hundreds, Arsenault's cash she wouldn't take, "don't look at my book like the bank does," the time on her knuckles. The fuel float at Marr's was lit the night of the load, and that's Tess's dock.

**Who told the man in the mudroom where she was:**
- **Ned: 40%.** He sent her home the night of the load for the first time in eleven years, as if he knew what was on that truck. He asked about the crate. He's the only one she told "Home. Don't tell anybody." "Me and God" is said twice, which makes me trust it less.
- **Tess: 25%.** The cash, the knuckle times, she can see Beth's back steps from the store, and her fuel float was lit that night. But her hurt about "I didn't put you on the air" read as real, and the blankets going back under the counter made me sad for her, not suspicious.
- **Dane, or someone at his end in Portland: 20%.** The glow under his chin on the step right before the mudroom door went quiet, the secret log, and he tells Portland where she is in the last line. I don't think he's dirty, but his reports are leaking.
- **Russ: 10%.** "Asking because somebody will." He's always around and nobody watches him.
- **Someone I haven't met: 5%.**

**Behind the smuggling:** Arsenault and the *Margaret Ann* 60%, with Ned or Tess pulled in for money.
