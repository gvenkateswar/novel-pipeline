# Pull curve, Ch 01–05 (round 3 read)

**Draft read:** Ch 04 round 2, Ch 05 with round-2 edits, Oct 29. Files: `chapters/chapter-01/final.md` through `chapters/chapter-05/final.md`, read in order. No other file opened.

**Persona (given by orchestrator):** KU reader, 35–55, reads on her phone, three or four books a month. Reads open-door romantic suspense for the couple and the danger equally. Quits when the heat is all hints or the setup runs long. Has been bored by two books like this before.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

I didn't stop, but it was close. I read to the end of Ch 05. I nearly put it down in the second half of Ch 04, from "He asked the next thing the way he'd have asked for the wrench" (about 70% into Ch 04, roughly 62% of what I was given) through Ned and the funnel. A man had just had his hand a foot from her head, and then I got a fare-bag seal, who sees the deposit slip, a bank flashback I had to read twice, and Ned pouring diesel. I kept going because Ch 03 had already stopped the heat once and I wanted to know if this book would ever deliver. Without that, I'd have gone back to the other books on my phone.

**I'd keep reading, but** the only reason I'm still in it is the Ch 05 love scene. If Ch 06 opens with more shopping and who-knows-what talk, I'm out.

## 2. Would I keep reading past Ch 05?

**Yes.** Barely. "I walk into Marr's in daylight and watch who watches me buy it" is the first time she goes on the attack, and I want to see that. It's KU, so it costs me nothing to try one more chapter.

## 3. Pull score per scene, Ch 04 and Ch 05 (1–5)

**Ch 04**
- **Scene 1, Lookout Hill (3).** I wanted to know who the young pale face was. Russ going down "like a cow lying down" woke me up. The boat being sold landed for a second. After that I wanted nothing in particular. I already knew the dragger was bad.
- **Scene 2, behind the generator (3).** The hiding was the best ten minutes in the book so far, a 4, with the bare hand on the steel, the diesel dripping toward his boot and her fingers on Dane's mouth. Then it sagged to a 2 with the red tag, the envelope, the deposit slip, the *Ruthie C* paragraph and Ned's funnel. The handprint in the frost at the end didn't hit me, because I'd watched him put it there. What I wanted next: for them to get out of that shed.

**Ch 05**
- **Scene 1, smoke and Tess's truck (3).** Tess showing her hand with 22:40 on it was good, and so was "My money I can count." But the start confused me: why was she soaked on the shore at first light? What I wanted next: to know whether Tess was lying.
- **Scene 2, chowder, fire, the bar (4).** The fire, the truck shape in the drive, and the flashlight crossing the water while she stood in it waiting for the sea to draw back. I was holding my breath. I wanted to see whether they'd get across.
- **Scene 3, the fish house (4).** Finally. The kit joke made me laugh, and she was the one asking for things. I wanted it to keep going, and I also wanted him not to ask a case question in bed. He did, and that worked on me.
- **Scene 4, the suspect talk (3).** Her saying Ned's name out loud to a man with a notebook mattered. But "Cove." on its own line, "Three things you didn't count," and "Neither one had been in her *You*" all made me stop and reread. What I wanted next: for her to stop hiding. She does, in the last line.

## 8. What I think is going on (from the page only)

Somebody on the island is feeding the hooded man and the *Margaret Ann* where Beth sleeps, and the book wants me to suspect Dane's daily log to Portland. I think the log is a red herring and Dane is clean. My money is on Ned. He sent her home the night of the reefer for the first time in eleven years. He asked about "that crate of his" when she hadn't told him a crate opened. She told him "Home," and Ashby was his key. He arrived at the shed right after the hood left. His flashlight beam "crossed the shed once" on the way down. And the light on the far shore goes "Short. Then long," which reads like a signal. Tess is set up to look guilty (the hundreds in her vest, the truck, asking where Beth sleeps, her book), so I think she's the decoy who turns out to be protecting Beth. Russ asked "where you sleeping" himself and then pinned it on Tess, which I noticed. Prediction: the money is going through the store's deposit bag on the ferry, and Ned's red fare bag or Tess's yellow one is the pipe.
