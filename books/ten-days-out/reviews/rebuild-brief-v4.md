## Keep

- Beth Calloway and Dane Reyes as people: her competence and dry humor, his talking through a crisis. Their ages and wounds.
- The premise: Beth sees something she shouldn't, becomes a federal witness, and a U.S. Marshal has to keep her alive for ten days until she testifies. A storm cuts the island off.
- Length about 30,000 words, open door at heat 3–4, four heat scenes, the HEA, PG-13 packaging, series opener, the pen name.

## Change

1. **Romance first, suspense around it.** The book is a romance with danger in it. Every chapter moves Beth and Dane: flirting, arguing that tips into flirting, wanting, holding back for a reason the reader accepts. Their first scene together (Ch 01) has obvious spark a half-listening reader can't miss. Both say things to each other they wouldn't say to anyone else. The viewpoint character thinks plainly about wanting the other.
2. **Dual POV**, alternating Beth and Dane by chapter or scene, each POV named in its first line. The reader hears him want her too. (Calibration: dual POV +79; hero POV books 4.09 vs 3.63.)
3. **Chapter 1 is exciting.** She finds the drugs, named plainly ("fentanyl", "drugs"), and knows she's in danger, and reacts at that size. Someone comes after her before the chapter ends. Dane arrives by the end of Ch 01 at the latest, and they clash and spark at once.
4. **Danger acts.** Something bad actually happens at least every second chapter: an attack, a fire, someone hurt, a near-capture. Nobody watches from a distance for three chapters.
5. **Plain language.** Beth works on a ferry, but the story is told in ordinary words. At most two boat, legal or police terms in the first 1,000 words, and each one is explained plainly the first time. Plot-critical facts are said in plain words and restated in the next scene (`docs/calibration.md`, Oct 30).
6. **The mystery is about people.** The traitor is someone close to Beth. Beth and the reader suspect them at the same time; no 20% gap where the reader is ahead of her. No clues built on keys, bags, seals, tags, tides or logs. At most six named characters the reader must keep straight.
7. **Heat on the page early (lane B, front-loaded).** First steamy scene by about 20–25%, real tension and at least one almost-kiss before it. Each scene grows out of a fight, a fear or a choice, and changes the relationship.
8. **The rupture passes the one-talk test** (`genre-maps/romance.md`). The author, Oct 30: "I definitely hate the classic misunderstanding, I think it's stupid and immature… I always appreciate mature handling of conflict." So no misunderstanding, overheard half-sentence or withheld secret drives any conflict between the leads. When they disagree, they say so to each other's faces, in the scene, and they are both partly right. The fights are adult, and the arguing can be part of the flirting.
9. **Short chapters:** 16–20 chapters of 1,500–2,000 words, each ending on a question the next opens on.
