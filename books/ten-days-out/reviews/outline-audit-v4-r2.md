## Part 2: new blocking items

### B1: the lighthouse on Day 4 night
**Where:** Ch 08 s2 ("Beth picks the beach herself, the north beach below the lighthouse. Then she calls Tess… Then Beth calls Ned, and then Russ… Now the island knows they're alive."). Ch 10 s1 ("They didn't go back to the lighthouse, because Ned and all of them knew they'd waited below it."). The traitor's path table, row 07–08: "The lighthouse: The boss only."
**Questions failed:** 1 and 4.
**The problem:** The outline's own reason for leaving the lighthouse on Day 5 was just as true on Day 4 evening. That's when Beth told all three, from the lighthouse tower, the beach below it. So the table's "the boss only" is false from Ch 08 s2 onward. The reader hears Ch 10 s1's line and thinks: they knew last night, too, so why didn't he come for her in the tower? Dane, who has just argued against every call, also sleeps there without comment.
**What the architect must answer:** Why is the lighthouse safe on Day 4 night and unsafe on Day 5 night?
**Fix (one line, Ch 08 s2 or the top of Ch 09):** Dane, on the stair: "He won't come up a stone tower with one door and me at the top of it. He'll wait for us to stand on open sand." That's why the kid picks the beach. After the beach fails, open sand is gone, so the tower is next, and Ch 10 s1's line then reads as the next step and not a contradiction. Correct the table row too.

### B2: Ch 09 s1. Dane hears the confession and does nothing
**Where:** Ch 09 s1 ("for two years he took Cole's cash to run his trucks across at night… Dane watches all of it and says nothing yet"), then s2 at 3 p.m. ("Ned goes home along the shore").
**Questions failed:** 4 and 5, and adults talk (genre map).
**The problem:** At 2:30 a deputy marshal learns that one of the people who has the 3 o'clock hour has run Cole's trucks for two years. The pickup is in thirty minutes, on open sand under a tree line. The safer options are plain: move Beth off the beach into the rocks, keep Ned in sight, call the boat in early or off. The outline gives none of them and no reason against them. After the ambush Ned walks home alone. A reader will say "obviously" at the confession and then watch the professional stand still. That makes Dane look slow, which the brief forbids for Beth and the reader won't forgive in him either. It also turns the ambush into the reader's chapter and not the characters'.
**What the architect must answer:** What does Dane do in those thirty minutes, and why isn't it enough?
**Fix (two lines in s1, one in s2):** Dane says it to Beth's face: "If he sold the hour, they're in those trees now." He can't call the boat off, because there's no signal on the beach (it's only in the tower, Ch 08), and a boat turned away means six more days on the island. So he chooses to go anyway: he moves Beth to the waterline at the rocks' end and keeps Ned in front of him, where he can see his hands. That's a choice made knowing the facts, with its cost (the officer shot) on the page. In s2, after the ambush, Dane lets Ned go home only because he won't leave Beth to take him, and Russ, the only other armed man, is still a suspect. Say that in one line.
**Non-blocking line for Ch 12:** Ned has to say why he came to a beach where he knew a shooter was waiting, and why he didn't simply warn her. "If I warned you, they'd know it was me, and my boy was in their hands. So I came to stand where he'd have to shoot me first." That pays the wading-in and the confession together.

### B3: nobody asks Tess about the Dyer call
**Where:** Ch 04 end ("Somebody walked in and used her phone." *Who?*); Ch 05 s1 (in Tess's store, Tess hangs up a phone, Dane asks Russ who he told, and nobody asks Tess anything).
**Questions failed:** 3, and adults talk.
**The problem:** Ch 04 ends on "Who?" and Ch 05 opens in the store of the woman who took that call and radioed it. A marshal asks her what the caller sounded like. Also, Russ's line "Mrs. Dyer's been at her daughter's in Bangor since October" makes the reader ask why the woman who runs the island store, and knows everyone's business, believed a call from that house at all. Left unasked, it reads as the outline protecting its mystery.
**What the architect must answer:** What did Tess hear, and why did she believe it?
**Fix (one exchange in Ch 05 s1):** Dane asks Tess. Tess: caller ID said Dyer, a man's voice, the line all static, and he said he was the Dyers' nephew come out to check the house before the storm. "I sent Russ because the man said a tree was through the roof. What was I supposed to do, ask for ID?" Then she hangs up the phone when Beth walks in. Her answer is checkable and a little too quick, which makes her a better red herring. It also means the "man's voice" is on the record without being Ned's.

### B4: helicopters and the island's own boats
**Where:** Ch 08 s2 (the Day 5 rescue is a Marine Patrol boat); Ch 13 s2 ("So it's you or nobody"); Ch 14 s1 (Russ shot in the thigh, Saturday night); Ch 15 s2 ("Russ on a stretcher, with the ferry as his ambulance"); Cast (Russ "and a lobsterman"; "two lobstermen" in the store).
**Question failed:** 4.
**The problem:** "Why don't they send a helicopter?" is the first thing a reader asks in any story about a stranded island, and it gets louder when a man is lying on a cabin floor with a gunshot wound. The outline never mentions aircraft once. Next, the island is full of lobstermen. Russ has a boat, and the kid crossed the bay in a skiff on night 1. "Only the ferry can lie at the pier" doesn't answer why a lobster boat can't take her off from its mooring in the Sunday window, or in the Day 5 lull.
**What the architect must answer:** Why can't an aircraft or an island boat do what the ferry does?
**Fix (one line each, three places):**
- Ch 02 s2, as the storm comes in: the harbor's lobster boats drag their moorings and go up on the rocks, or have already been hauled out for the winter. Russ's boat is one of them, and Beth watches it go.
- Ch 08 s2, the boss: no helicopter will fly in this icing. The boat in the lull is the only thing that can come.
- Ch 13 s2, added to the boss's list: the Coast Guard helicopters are on the same distress calls, and the window is low sea, not low wind, so nothing flies.

### B5: the hand on the rail, then a pause to ask permission
**Where:** Ch 15 s2 end ("Dane leans out into the dark and sees… a hand on the rail below him"); Ch 16 opening line ("Beth Calloway said go when Dane asked, because he'd asked") and s1 ("The kid let his skiff go and came over the stern. The deck lights die… so Dane goes down").
**Questions failed:** 2 (how does he get from the rail to the hatch?) and 4 (a marshal who sees the hand acts on it).
**The problem:** These two lines are adjacent: the cliffhanger and its answer. As written, Dane is looking straight at the intruder's hand. The next thing the reader gets is Dane asking Beth, in the captain's cabin, whether he may go down. The obvious move is to go for the hand, then and there. The reader will feel the gap at the climax's hinge, and it makes the payoff of Beth's condition look staged.
**What the architect must answer:** What happens between the hand and the ask?
**Fix (one line at the top of Ch 16 s1):** Dane is on the upper deck behind the cabin, and the hand is on the car-deck rail a story below him. He shouts and fires, and the hand is gone. The deck lights die. Somewhere among the two stranded trucks a man is moving toward the engine-room hatch. Dane, at the cabin door: "I'm going down. Say go." The ask now costs seconds they don't have, which is the point of her condition. Optionally, in Ch 15, have the kid wait in his skiff at the harbor mouth, where she has to slow for the ledges, so a rowing man with a shot shoulder isn't chasing a moving ferry (NB5).

## Part 3: non-blocking (one line each)

- **NB1, the Dyer house as hideout (Ch 10 s1).** It's the one house they know the traitor walked into. Dane should say so, and Beth should answer: "He went in to use a phone, and nobody hides where they've already been." Then the choice reads as a risk taken, not an oversight.
- **NB2, the store (Ch 05 s1).** Decide the bed after the two lobstermen have gone, or out on the steps. Also, "Ned, at the counter with his coffee, hears all of it" is staging an attentive reader marks. Have Beth tell Ned directly, as she tells Tess and Russ, so he's one of three told and not the one who overheard.
- **NB3, Ned walked up the harbor road in cuffs (Ch 12 s3), and the bait (Ch 13).** The outline's own N2 logic is that the whole road sees everything. Say the road is empty at dawn in a full storm, with shutters closed, or walk Ned along the shore, so "nobody has told him Ned was arrested" holds.
- **NB4, the second phone, Day 5 night (Ch 10).** With a confessed Cole runner who knew the hour, a reader expects a search of Ned's house. One line: Dane won't leave Beth, and the only other armed man, Russ, is himself on the list. That's why they test instead.
- **NB5, the skiff and the moving ferry (Ch 15 s2).** See B5. He waits in the dark at the harbor mouth.
- **NB6, Ch 01's requested line.** `test-v4.md` already has it: "Her eyes went to his mouth and stayed there a second too long, and then came back up to his, and she didn't look sorry about it at all." Adding the outline's requested line would say the same beat twice in one exchange. Don't add it, or replace line 175 with it.
- **NB7, the kid's name.** Russ, the constable, knows the kid's trailer and that he sterned for Cole, and "nobody knows his real name." Give one reason (he was paid cash, the trailer is Cole's, he answered to "kid"). Otherwise a reader asks why Dane's office can't pull Cole's employee records.
- **NB8, the clearance rule.** The Marine Patrol landed Dane at night (Ch 01) and came back at first light (Ch 02) without anyone clearing it. One line in Ch 08: the clearance applies once the island declares itself closed, and Tess closed it on Day 1. Send it to the domain checker with the rest.
- **NB9, Ch 13 s1.** "Dane gives the boss the rest of Ned's confession in two sentences." Keep it to two. It's the first time the reader hears these facts, so it's new information, but it arrives as a report.
- **NB10, Ch 02 s1.** It's still the most crowded scene in the book: the refusal, two pieces of offstage news, Dane's conclusion, three reasons from Beth, two from Dane, the report to his boss, the flirting and the boat leaving. The architect flags it too. The beat sheet should give each piece of news one line and run all of it inside the argument against the running engine.
- **NB11, the lawyer's arrest (Ch 17 s1).** He's arrested within six hours on a wounded man's word, and he's waiting at a grand jury door. Romantic-suspense readers will allow it. The domain checker should say whether "taken for questioning" is the honest phrase.

## Part 6: when can a reader name the traitor, and when does Beth?

**The reader.**
- From Ch 01 to Ch 05, Ned's only marks are "Then you didn't see it. Not tonight" and his gray face. The text sets them as fear of the men twenty feet under them, and the Ch 02 pier save outweighs them.
- From Ch 06 (34%) a genre-savvy reader suspects all three, along with Beth ("three of them were hers"). Some will lean toward Ned on the rule that the suspect with no visible lie is the one. That's a lean, not a name, and Beth is on the same list in the same scene.
- The one staging tell is "Ned, at the counter with his coffee, hears all of it" (Ch 05, NB2). Fix it, and nothing singles him out before Ch 09.
- The reader names him at Ch 09 s1 (~47%), at the cash confession.

**Beth.** She suspects her three from Ch 06. She's thinking Ned "behind the last rock" (Ch 09 s2 end, ~51%) and names him out loud at Ch 10 s1 (~52%).

**The gap is about 4–5 points.** That passes brief item 6, **on one condition: B2.** If Dane hears the confession and stands still on open sand, the reader isn't just ahead of Beth for five points. The reader is ahead of the marshal while the shots come, and that is the kind of slowness item 6 forbids.

**After the name, the trap still turns.** The reader and Beth both expect Ned, and the church burns: the place pointing at Tess. That's a real reversal inside the gap, and Tess's confession in Ch 11 s3 settles it for both at the same line (~62%).

**Other withheld facts:** the kid's survival in Ch 14 is shown, not withheld. The Book 2 couple is hidden inside Russ's "nobody" and revealed in Ch 11. Neither gives the reader an early lead.
