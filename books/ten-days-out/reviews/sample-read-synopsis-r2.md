# Sample read: synopsis, round 2

**Draft read:** `books/ten-days-out/reviews/sample-synopsis.md` (the one-page synopsis of chapters 1–3, new draft, as it stood on 2026-10-28). Nothing else in the book folder was opened.

**Persona:** default buyer from `genre-maps/romance.md`, adjusted for open-door romantic suspense. She is 35–55 and reads mostly on KU, on her phone in bed, three or four books a month. She reads for the couple and the danger equally. She quits when the heat is all hints or when the setup runs long. She is tired of heroines with no job, instant love, generic small towns, and books that read like they were written to a list. She has already been bored by two books like this one.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

I finished the sample.

I'm reporting this one anyway, "I'd keep reading, but...": after the bridge falls, the danger stops moving for about a third of the page. Once he's telling her about his marriage under a blanket, I was reading on the strength of the first half.

## 2. Buy: yes

Yes. A woman who presses a sprung lid shut over a brick of pills and says "You'll lose your count" is someone I'll follow for ten days on a cut-off island. What sold me was her, not the mystery.

Average: 3.4. The closing paragraph ("Three people could have told...") isn't a scene. It reads like the back cover, and I didn't score it.

## 4. Do I care about these two people yet?

Yes for Beth, not quite for Dane. She has a job she's good at and a nerve I believe. He's mostly a good jump and a sad story, and I don't yet know what he wants other than to have made that bus.

## 5. Did anyone make me laugh?

Yes. Ned: "Well. That's the bridge." Also, smaller, "his boots are for a different ocean."

## 6. Did anyone make me want?

Yes. "She hauls him aboard by the front of his jacket, and for a second neither of them lets go." And the shed: "kissing against the warm housing with his cold hands under her layers." That was the first moment the heat felt like more than a hint. Then she stopped it, and I understood why, but I'm an open-door reader at the end of chapter 3 and I noticed.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. About 35%: "At 3:40 that afternoon his father drives his last bus route in Lowell after thirty years, and Dane promised to be the last passenger. 'He'll tell the whole route anyway. He tells everybody everything.'" I skimmed it. It's a setup for something, and I could feel it being set up.
2. About 55%: "They sit in her dark kitchen with their backs to the chimney under one blanket, and he tells her about the bus he missed and the marriage he lost to his job." That's the bus a second time, plus the job-ate-my-marriage cop. Nothing in danger, nothing between them changes.
3. About 25%: "Beth is working the morning boat anyway: the other deckhand is sick and the island's last propane before a nor'easter is on her deck." That's the book explaining why she's allowed to be there. I didn't need the excuse. I'd have believed she'd go anyway.

## 8. What I think is going on

Ned is the obvious leak, because she tells him where she'll be and the latch lifts that night. So I think Ned isn't it. Tess's flashlight line means someone with a light was on Beth's steps, and Tess either saw them or is covering for whoever it was. My bet is that the limping man in the hood is an islander Beth knows, probably someone Tess is close to. "She doesn't know" is about him, or it's that the marshals are using Beth as bait to draw the dragger crew in, and Dane hates it. I don't believe Dane is the leak; the book wants me to suspect him, and he makes the porch call where she can watch to clear himself. I expect the father's bus route to come back as a phone call or a recording near the end, when Dane chooses her over the job he lost his marriage to. The three-suspect list told me plainly that it's a whodunit with three doors. I'd rather have found that out myself.
