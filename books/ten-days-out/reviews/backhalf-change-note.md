# Ten Days Out: what changed in the back half, and why

*Story-architect, Nov 5, 2026. The author approved changing the shape of Ch 10–18 ("update the writing based on the above feedback, and finish the book"). The new outline is `outline.md`; the old one is saved as `outline-v4-pre-backhalf.md`.*

## The problem

The cold reader said the book was going in a loop. Four times so far, the same thing happened: Beth and Dane hide somewhere, the man finds them, there's shooting, they escape, and then they crouch behind something and argue over who could have told him. The old Ch 10–12 would have run it a fifth time, with a third fire (the church) and a second rescue from the water (Ned off the float). The reader also stopped believing Dane is good at his job: he had been rescued five times and gave in on every argument.

## What changed

1. **They go on the offense.** From Ch 10 they stop waiting to be found. They follow the kid's blood trail and catch him at his boat (Ch 10). They set a trap for the leak and are in the room when it springs (Ch 11). They go into the kid's last hiding place, Beth's own house, and take him (Ch 13).
2. **Dane is good at his job where the reader can see it.** He wins the argument to go after the kid. He reads the trail. He gets to the kid when the kid goes for Beth. He takes the kid's rifle, phone and boat. He works out from the texts that his boss is clean. He saves Ned's son with one phone call. He catches the kid coming out of Beth's window. He pulls Beth out of the way of a snapping rope. He holds off the boat at sea with the rifle. In the back half he protects her at least as often as she saves him.
3. **No more fires, no more rescues from the water.** The church fire, Ned stepping off the float, and the kid going over the ferry's rail are all cut.
4. **Ned is caught by a confrontation.** Dane and Beth have the kid's phone, so they text the leak as if the kid were bleeding to death. Ned comes up the hill in the storm with his good blanket to save the boy. Beth is sitting under the blanket where the boy should be, and she faces him down. The phone rings in his coat. He owns the Dyer call ("I could see his light from there"). This lands at about 63%, and Beth and the reader learn it at the same moment.
5. **Every chapter is a different kind of chapter:** a hunt, a trap, a walk in handcuffs and a fight, a raid, a storm, a supper and a departure, a chase at sea, a courthouse, a holiday table. Danger stays active, and each chapter adds a new fact or a new question.
6. **The last act has a new threat.** Once the kid is caught (Ch 13), he warns them: "They've got a boat." The people who bring the drugs in from offshore send a boat with no lights to stop the ferry at sea. Beth beats it at the wheel by taking her father's way through the rocks in the dark (his pencil map is on her pantry door in Ch 13). Dane holds it off from the upper deck and is grazed doing it. The boat gets away, and that's Book 2's case.

## What stayed the same

Ned is the traitor, and Tess and Russ are cleared and secretly together. The boss is cleared. Heat scene #3 is in Ch 10 and #4 comes after the declaration. The fight about witness protection still passes the one-talk test, and so does the repair. Beth is at the ferry's wheel in the climax. The grand jury on Monday the 23rd, with her testimony on the page. The Thanksgiving ending, Dane's leaky boat, the September photo ("the girl on the boat"), and the captain's job in June. The book still lands near 36,000 words, with nine back-half chapters of 1,700–2,200 words.

## Things the author should know

- The content note loses "a suicide attempt" and one of the "two fires."
- Beth's hands are rope-burned from Ch 14 on, not fire-burned. Russ is shot in Ch 13, not Ch 14.
- The bible has to change to match. The list is at the end of `outline.md`, and only the author (or the orchestrator under autopilot) can approve it.
- Risks I'm watching: the reader will probably be sure of Ned before Ch 11, so that chapter's pull has to come from how he's caught, not who. A faceless boat in the climax could feel like it came from nowhere, so it's planted three times first. And Ch 14 deliberately echoes Ch 02.
