# Pull curve, Ch 01–05: Ten Days Out

**Drafts read:** `chapters/chapter-01/final.md` through `chapters/chapter-05/final.md`, as they stood on 2026-10-29. I read nothing else.

**Persona:** KU reader, 35–55, reads on her phone, three or four books a month. Reads open-door romantic suspense as much for the couple as for the danger. Quits when the heat is all hints or the setup runs long. Two books like this have bored her before. She bought this one after the Ch 01–03 sample, and she told herself that if the next love scene also stopped because of the phone, she would quit.

**Warning, above the scores:** I'd keep reading, but Ch 04 nearly lost me. Between the man searching her house and the shed fire there are about 3,000 words where nothing threatens anybody. I skimmed most of that stretch.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

I read to the end of Ch 05. My closest call was in Ch 04, at about 73% of what I was given:

> "The *North Reach* would keep till the barge, and then till June, and then Ned would give up her wheel, and the district would hire a man from away."

By this point I'd had the pouch walk-through, Russ explaining the island, and Ned bringing diesel, all in a row, and now I was getting a job-application subplot. I thumbed ahead to see when something would happen. The bar crossing at the end of the chapter is what kept me in the book.

## 2. Would I keep reading past Ch 05?

**Yes.** They finally slept together and someone burned the shed down, so I'll give it a few more chapters. But "I go to the store and buy what I need" is a weak last line, and if Ch 06 opens with more counter talk I'm gone.

## 5. Did the first love scene deliver what the sample promised?

**Yes.** No phone, no interruption. It's on the page, it's explicit, and it's funny in the middle. It landed here:

> "It's a very disappointing kit," he said.
> She laughed out loud.

And it delivered here:

> "she came with her heel hard in his back and her hand over her own mouth, and then took the hand away, because there was no one in the world to hear her out here, and let the sound go."

Small gripe: the kit means it's hands and mouths only, so it reads like a workaround and not the real thing. The case barges back in with "Who had Ashby," but only once they're done, so I let it go.

## 8. The three most boring passages

1. **Ch 04, Russ scene, ~65%:** "Who's emergency management on this island? Tess is. Since town meeting. Slow putting her hand down. She had LifeFlight on the line yesterday..." A paragraph of island information, and I couldn't tell who was saying it.
2. **Ch 04, generator scene, ~70%:** the pouch walk-through, from "She gave it to him in order. Envelopes from the store, a slip inside..." through "Who sees the slip?" It's a detective quizzing a witness while I'm waiting for the kiss to come back. I skimmed it.
3. **Ch 05, Tess scene, ~84%:** "Through the gap Beth could see the booster's antenna on its shelf. It ran off the store generator: inside fifty feet a phone worked, and past the dock it was a brick." Wiring and logistics. Ned's license paragraph in Ch 04 ("Put my name in. Four words.") comes a close fourth.

## 9. What I think is going on (page only)

The drugs or the cash ride the ferry in the zipped pouch, with the store's yellow seal next to the boat's red one, and someone on Cutter is feeding the dragger crew where Beth is. Three people knew the house on Friday night and the Ashby place after: Ned, Tess, and Dane himself, through whatever he logs for Pike and Q. Here's how I'd bet:

- **Ned, 35%.** He sent her home for the first time in eleven years on the night of the reefer. He took both bags. He's the one alone with the pouch on the boat. He knew "Home" on Friday and gave her the Ashby key. He keeps the island's only working phone on Tess's shelf and "hasn't come off that boat." "Me and God" sounds like a man asking to be the twist.
- **Tess, 25%.** The roll of hundreds, asking twice where Beth was sleeping, keeping her out of the log, and her truck at the fire. But she's flagged so hard I think she's the decoy, and the truck keys on an open hook look like they're there to clear her later.
- **Dane's side (Pike/Q, a leak from the daily log), 25%.** "Keep witness in place" and "hands off the vessel" read like someone wants her left on the island as bait.
- **Russ, 10%.** He took the path that comes out behind Marr's and kept offering to run them across.
- **Dane himself, 5%.** I don't believe it, but the book keeps poking at it.

Prediction: her father's red Pelican case in the fish house matters. The red and yellow seals will show who opened the pouch. Ned is in it somehow, maybe forced into it by the boat job or the money, and Tess turns out to be protecting Beth.
