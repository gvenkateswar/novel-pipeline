# Sample read: synopsis, round 3

**Draft read:** `books/ten-days-out/reviews/sample-synopsis.md` (one-page synopsis of Ch 1–3, new draft, as on disk 2026-10-28). Nothing else in the book folder was opened.

**Persona:** default buyer from `genre-maps/romance.md`, adjusted per the orchestrator. KU reader, 35–55, reads on her phone in bed, three or four romantic suspense books a month. Reads for the couple and the danger equally. Quits when the heat is all hints or the setup runs long. Has already been bored by two books like this.

**Caveat on the format:** this is a page of summary, not prose. A summary can't be boring the way 9,000 words can. A pass here only means the events would hold me. It says nothing about whether the sentences will.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

Finished the sample.

I came close to drifting once, at "On the way across he asks her, grudgingly, what a used thirty-four-foot lobster boat is worth" (about 30%). That's the second straight paragraph of logistics after the ramp jump. The storm pulled me back.

## 2. Buy: yes

Pills under the lobsters, a man at the latch at 11:40, and a kiss with his hands under her layers by the end of chapter 3. That's the book I came for, as long as the prose moves as fast as this page does.

## 4. Do I care about these two people yet?

Yes. She keeps her nerve over a sprung crate and won't leave her boat for a marshal. He gives up the thing he wants (the boat, the 31) to stay in the room with the man buying cigarettes. Each of them pays for something on the page.

## 5. Did anyone make me laugh?

Yes. Ned: "Well. That's the bridge." / "Does it do that a lot?" / "Just the once."

Also "Am I keeping you?" and "It was never your boat."

## 6. Did anyone make me want?

Yes, twice. First: "she hauls him aboard by the front of his jacket. Neither of them lets go right away." Then the generator shed: "kissing against the warm housing with his cold hands under her layers." The blanket against the chimney nearly made three, but they spend it talking about his boat.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. "On the way across he asks her, grudgingly, what a used thirty-four-foot lobster boat is worth. 'To who?' 'To me.' 'Less than you're paying.' He has a viewing in a boatyard at 3:30 and a broker who wants thirty-one thousand by Saturday noon." (about 30%). A price, a time, a deadline, a broker. I skimmed it the way I skim a ledger.
2. "The boat is holed and won't carry anyone until the Coast Guard has seen her." (about 55%). Right after the bridge comes down, this is paperwork.
3. "they sit against the chimney under one blanket and whisper about everything wrong with his boat." (about 60%). One blanket, in the dark, and they talk about the boat again. That's the third time. In the actual chapter this could be lovely or it could be a page of hull talk. If it's hull talk, I'm gone.

Smaller one: "At five past eleven... At 11:20... At 11:40..." reads like a log. It works on the page here. Stretched over pages of prose, it'll feel like a timesheet.

## 8. What I think is going on

Someone close to Beth is feeding the dealers, and Ned is the obvious pick. She told him and only him where she'd be, and a pickup with no headlights came to the house within the hour. That's too obvious, so I suspect Tess. The "flashlight on the back steps" means someone was at Beth's house the night before, and Tess is either covering for that person or is that person. The limping hooded man lives on the island and will turn out to be someone Beth knows. The dragger skipper is the muscle.

"Log daily. Do not disclose to witness" means the marshals are using Beth as bait, or they already know there's a leak on the island and haven't told her. Dane will tell her by the midpoint and that will be the fight. The "31" is only the boat. I believe him on the money. Losing the boat is how he'll prove he's staying, and I expect him to end up with her father's old boat, or with a working boat on her island.
