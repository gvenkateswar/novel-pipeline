# Ten Days Out: what happens in Chapters 1–3

*Synopsis for the sample reader. Built from `outline-v4-draft.md`; Chapter 1 follows the written test chapter.*

**Chapter 1 (Beth, then Dane).** Beth Calloway, 33, has worked the deck of the little car ferry between Cutter Island, Maine, and the mainland for eleven years. On a sleeting November night the island's lobster truck comes aboard with its cooling unit silent, which makes no sense: dead lobsters are a lost load. Then a wave tips the boat and a crate splits open on the deck, and under the lobsters are bricks wrapped in gray tape, one torn and blowing white powder. Beth knows it's fentanyl. Two island kids have died of it, and one of them was a boy she taught to row. A scared young man in a hoodie looks up at her and says, "You didn't see this." When she tells the captain, Ned, her late father's best friend, he says quietly, "Then you didn't see it. Not tonight." She photographs the truck's plate anyway and calls the state police from her kitchen at midnight. The case goes federal, and a U.S. Marshal is on his way by police boat. At two in the morning someone breaks down her back door and says her name. She goes out the window in her socks and runs through the snow to her father's old fish shack, with a flashlight following her footprints.

Now we're with Dane Reyes, the marshal, who was told this was an easy job and bought new boots for it at a shop that also sells fudge. He chases the man off into a boat with no lights. When he opens the shack door, Beth swings an oar at his head. She looks at his badge, then his face, then his feet: "Those boots are for a different ocean." He knows right then he's in trouble, because he has never wanted to make a witness laugh. He catches her when her knees give out and wraps her in his coat. He wants her off the island until she testifies to a grand jury in ten days. She says no. And only the police and his own office knew she'd called.

**Chapter 2 (Beth).** At dawn they have it out in her kitchen. He thinks someone in his office leaked her call. She thinks the kid on the deck saw her, and nobody had to tell him anything. Each of them is partly right. He can't force her to go, and if his office did leak, a hotel they book is the worst place she could be. So he does something he hasn't done in four years: he changes the plan for a witness, and he tells his boss he's staying. The police boat leaves ahead of the biggest storm in years, and the island is cut off. Beth's people meet her at the dock: Ned, telling her to go; Tess, who runs the store, hugging her hard; and Russ, the island's one part-time police officer, asking where she'll sleep. "Home," she tells all of them. In the storm's first hour the ferry breaks loose. Beth nearly gets crushed between the boat and the pier, and Dane yanks her back and doesn't let go. They save the boat, but it won't run until it's fixed.

**Chapter 3 (Dane).** The power is out, and Dane is wearing her father's darned wool socks. In the dark kitchen Beth tells him she wants to kiss him and won't apologize for saying so. He wants it badly, and he tells her no and why: the last time he stopped watching the door, the woman he was guarding died. After midnight the man comes back. Dane fights him in the dark while Beth stands by with her father's shotgun. The man is thrown out into the snow and starts shooting at the house. Beth grabs Dane's hand. "I know a place."
