# Ten Days Out: what happens in Chapters 1–3

*Synopsis for the sample reader, revision 2. Built from `outline-v4-draft.md` (revision 2). Chapter 1 follows the written test chapter.*

**Chapter 1 (Beth, then Dane).** Beth Calloway, 33, has worked the deck of the little car ferry between Cutter Island, Maine, and the mainland for eleven years. On a sleeting November night the island's lobster truck comes aboard with its cooling unit silent, and Cole Varney, the island's lobster dealer, is at the wheel. A wave tips the boat, a crate splits open on the deck, and under the lobsters are bricks wrapped in gray tape, one torn and blowing white powder. Beth knows it's fentanyl. Two island kids have died of it, and one of them was a boy she taught to row. A scared young man in a hoodie looks up at her: "You didn't see this." The captain, Ned, her late father's best friend, tells her quietly, "Then you didn't see it. Not tonight." She photographs the truck's plate anyway and calls the state police from her kitchen at midnight. At two in the morning someone breaks down her back door and says her name. She goes out the window in her socks and runs through the snow to her father's old fish shack, with a flashlight following her footprints.

Then we're with Dane Reyes, the U.S. Marshal sent to keep her alive, who was told this was an easy job and bought new boots for it at a shop that also sells fudge. He chases the man off into a boat with no lights. When he opens the shack door, Beth swings an oar at his head. She looks at his badge, then his face, then his feet: "Those boots are for a different ocean." He knows right then he's in trouble. He catches her when her knees give out and holds her against him in the snow, and she looks at his mouth a second longer than she needs to, so he'll know she did. He wants her off the island until she testifies in ten days. She says no.

**Chapter 2 (Beth).** Beth wakes up wanting the marshal, which is the last thing she needs. At first light a police boat waits at the landing with its engine running and a storm coming in, and she won't get in it. The truck was empty when the police stopped it, so what she saw is the whole case against Cole, and she won't let his people learn that shooting at a witness works. Dane can't make her go, and he won't leave her alone on an island with an armed man, so the boat leaves without them both. In the storm's first hour the ferry tears loose from the pier, and old Ned drags Beth out from between the hull and the pilings. Dane jumps aboard to help, badly, and afterward he holds onto her a beat too long. "You can let go." "I know."

**Chapter 3 (Dane).** They spend the night at the constable's house, with Russ on the porch with a shotgun and the power out across the island. In the dark kitchen Beth kisses Dane, and he kisses her back, hard. He tries to tell her why it's a bad idea: she's the only witness, and Cole's lawyer will use it. She doesn't let him get far. They won't lie about it, she says, and a jury in Maine has heard of two grown adults in a snowstorm, and a man stops kissing a woman when she tells him to, and she didn't. He has no answer. "Tonight's a bad night." "Then tomorrow." "Probably sooner." At one in the morning a false emergency call pulls Russ off the porch, and twenty minutes later the kitchen window blows in under gunfire. Dane gets her down, talking low the whole time while she goes silent and listens, and they run out the back into the storm. Beth grabs his hand: "I know a place."

**Coming in Chapter 4:** the place is an empty loft over the fishermen's co-op, and they don't make it to morning without finishing what they started in the kitchen.
