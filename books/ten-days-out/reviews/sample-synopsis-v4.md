# Ten Days Out: what happens in Chapters 1–3

*Synopsis for the sample reader, revision 1. Built from `outline-v4-draft.md` (revision 1). Chapter 1 follows the written test chapter with three small edits.*

**Chapter 1 (Beth, then Dane).** Beth Calloway, 33, has worked the deck of the little car ferry between Cutter Island, Maine, and the mainland for eleven years. On a sleeting November night the island's lobster truck comes aboard with its cooling unit silent, and Cole Varney, the island's lobster dealer, is at the wheel. Then a wave tips the boat and a crate splits open on the deck, and under the lobsters are bricks wrapped in gray tape, one torn and blowing white powder. Beth knows it's fentanyl. Two island kids have died of it, and one of them was a boy she taught to row. A scared young man in a hoodie looks up at her and says, "You didn't see this." The captain, Ned, her late father's best friend, tells her quietly, "Then you didn't see it. Not tonight." She has never seen him afraid. She photographs the truck's plate anyway and calls the state police from her kitchen at midnight. A U.S. Marshal is on his way by police boat. At two in the morning someone breaks down her back door and says her name. She goes out the window in her socks and runs through the snow to her father's old fish shack, with a flashlight following her footprints.

Then we're with Dane Reyes, the marshal, who was told this was an easy job and bought new boots for it at a shop that also sells fudge. He chases the man off into a boat with no lights. When he opens the shack door, Beth swings an oar at his head. She looks at his badge, then his face, then his feet: "Those boots are for a different ocean." He knows right then he's in trouble. He catches her when her knees give out and wraps her in his coat. He wants her off the island until she testifies in ten days. She says no.

**Chapter 2 (Beth).** At first light the police boat waits at the landing with its engine running and a storm coming, and Beth won't get in. The truck was empty when the police stopped it, so what she saw is the whole case against Cole. She's the only one who can put a face to the man in the hoodie, and he lives on her island. Dane can't force her, so he stays. Then Beth asks Ned, in front of the marshal, why he told her she didn't see it, and Ned tells them both something he has never told anyone. The police boat leaves. In the storm's first hour the ferry tears loose from the pier, and Ned drags Beth out from between the boat and the pilings. Dane jumps aboard to help, badly. They save the boat, but it won't run until it's fixed. Nothing gets on or off the island now.

**Chapter 3 (Dane).** They spend the night at the constable's house, with Russ on the porch with a shotgun and the power out across the island. In the dark kitchen Beth kisses Dane, and he kisses her back, hard. Then he stops and tells her why: she's the only witness, and if Cole's lawyer can tell a jury the marshal was sleeping with her, they'll stop believing her. She says she'd tell the truth if anyone asked her. Neither of them wants to stop. At one in the morning a call comes over the island's emergency radio about a tree through an old woman's roof, and Russ goes. Twenty minutes later the kitchen window blows in under gunfire. Dane gets Beth down, talking low the whole time while she goes silent and listens, and they escape out the back into the storm. Beth grabs his hand. "I know a place."
