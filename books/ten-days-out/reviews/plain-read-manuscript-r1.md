# Plain read: Ten Days Out, whole manuscript

**Draft read:** `books/ten-days-out/manuscript.md`, Nov 6 revision r1, 17 chapters (Ch 1 "The Last Truck" through Ch 17 "Full Price"). I read nothing else in the book folder.

**Verdict: PASS.** Details are at the bottom.

---

## 1. Retelling (what I got, by ear)

Beth Calloway works the deck on a small island ferry in Maine. One November night the lobster truck comes aboard with its cooling unit switched off. A crate splits, and under the lobsters are bricks of fentanyl. The page tells me straight out that it's drugs ("There are drugs in the lobster truck"), and that two island kids have died of it. A scared young man in a hoodie with a dragging left foot sees her see it. She calls the state police. That night the same man kicks in her back door. She gets out the kitchen window in her socks, hides in her dad's fish shack and swings an oar at the man who comes to the door. That man turns out to be Dane Reyes, a federal marshal sent to protect her. She makes fun of his new boots and he falls for her on the spot.

The truck was clean by the time police stopped it, so Beth's word is the whole case. She has to tell a grand jury in Portland on Monday the 23rd or the dealer, Cole Varney, walks free. She won't leave the island. A storm wrecks the dock, breaks the ferry's steering and puts the island's boats on the rocks, so nobody can get off.

For the next ten days the limping kid keeps finding her wherever she sleeps:
- he shoots through the constable Russ's window after a fake emergency call pulls Russ off the porch;
- he hunts them along the shore to a net loft;
- he burns down the summer house where they're hiding;
- he waits on the rocks after a wave sweeps Dane into the sea and Beth hauls him out;
- he shoots at a police rescue boat on the north beach.

Each time, someone must have told him where she was. Only three islanders she loves knew each place: Tess at the store, Russ the constable, and Ned the old ferry captain who was her father's best friend.

Dane wounds the kid on the beach, and they take the kid's phone. Dane texts as the kid, saying he's bleeding and needs help. The person who walks out in the storm with a first-aid box and a blanket is Ned. He has been running Cole's drug trucks for two years. He sold Beth out the first night because Cole's people threatened his son, who is on drugs. Beth ties his wrists with the knot he taught her.

Dane and Beth fall out when Dane asks a judge to keep Beth in Portland under guard. She breaks it off. They catch the kid in her own kitchen, and Russ gets shot in the leg. A man called Mercer, who says he's a fisherman, turns up, and his big boat sits offshore. Dane withdraws the hold and tells his boss he's involved with Beth. Beth tells him she loves him.

On Sunday night she takes the repaired ferry across with Ned and the kid aboard, and Mercer's boat comes after them shooting. She leads it into her father's secret path through the rocks, and the big boat smashes on a rock. Dane is shot along the ribs. Ned steers so Beth can go to Dane. The kid confesses that Cole's lawyer paid him and that Mercer's boat brings the drugs in.

Beth testifies, and Cole's lawyer is arrested in the courthouse lobby. Dane signs himself out of hospital, waits on a bench and asks to stay with her. At Thanksgiving on the island, Russ and a still-angry Tess let him stay. One loose end is left: somebody on the island planted a tracking phone on the ferry.

## 2. What's at stake

Beth is the only witness. Cole Varney's people, a local kid with a gun and later Mercer's boat, will kill her before Monday. If she doesn't get to the grand jury room that morning, Cole goes free at two o'clock. Later the stake also turns personal: someone she loves is selling her out, and she could lose the island or Dane to stay alive.

## 3. The romance

Yes. It came through clearly and early, and I never had to work it out from a small gesture. The moments I felt:

- **Ch 1:** "He'd protected maybe forty witnesses, and he had never once wanted to make one of them laugh." And: "Her eyes went to his mouth and stayed there a second too long... 'You're warm, Marshal. I'll give you that.'"
- **Ch 2:** "She watched his hands. Then his forearms. She wanted them on her. She thought it in exactly those words."
- **Ch 3:** "I'm going to kiss you. If you've got a reason why not, you've got about three seconds." He stops because of what Cole's lawyer could say about it. That told me what the obstacle was, in plain words.
- **Ch 4, 7, 10, 16:** the love scenes. They're plain, and each one is tied to what just happened (cold, being hurt, the stove, daylight).
- **Ch 8:** "I'm in love with you." She feels it ("She loved him. She had known it since the rock") but says "Ask me in five days."
- **Ch 12:** the break. "Then they stop. And so do we." I felt that one.
- **Ch 14:** "I love you. I have since the rock, when you opened your hand."
- **Ch 16:** "Will you stay?" "...And I want you, every day you'll have me."
- **Ch 17:** "So. Are you any good at your job?" "Ask me every night." The callback to Ch 1 landed.

## 5. Where I lost the thread

- **Ch 2:** "Half past ten. You called at midnight. He was on his way before you ever picked up the phone, so nobody on my end had to tell him anything." I had to replay the times. I got it in the end: someone told Cole before she even called the police. This pays off when Ned confesses in Ch 12.
- **Ch 4 to 6, how far Ned can walk:** Beth reasons that the Dyer house is "an hour round by the road, with that foot," so "there's two of them." Ch 6 says Ned's "hip couldn't get him up the harbor road without a rest." Then in Ch 11 Ned confesses he made the Dyer phone call. I wondered how Ned got out to the point on that hip. Nobody explains it.
- **Ch 8:** "Then Tess has three o'clock, and by supper so does the store. Let her tell her people five, and bring the boat at three." I lost who "her people" were and what the "five" was. I think Dane wanted Beth to give a false hour and the boss said no, but that took a second listen. The boss's "And me" at the end of the chapter landed fine.
- **Ch 14, which day it is:** the chapter starts "By Friday night," goes to first light, then "by three," then "By eight it was full dark... Tess had watched the harbor from the store steps all Saturday." I lost which day the crossing was until Ch 15 said "At ten tomorrow morning she went in to the grand jury." Saturday goes by off the page, and by ear I couldn't follow it.
- **Ch 14, the planted phone:** "It shows the boat off the point where we are." I needed a beat to understand it was telling the bad boat where the ferry is. Then came the logic about the bag being dry under the spray, which I couldn't follow by ear. Ch 17 says it again more plainly ("Somebody on Cutter walked down and taped it there"), and that's where I actually got it.
- **Ch 16:** "He had said it first, into his boss's phone on Saturday, and this was the bill." I had to think back to Ch 14 ("I'm involved with her. Write that down too"). I got it.

## 6. Verdict: PASS

- **Main events:** I could retell every chapter's main event. The drugs in Ch 1 come through in plain words ("There are drugs in the lobster truck," "Fentanyl," plus the dead island kids). I didn't need to recognize gray-tape bricks from TV.
- **Stakes:** clear from Ch 2 and repeated often: testify Monday or Cole walks at two, and the kid is hunting her.
- **Plot-critical facts:** none reached me only through a word I didn't know. Who the traitor is, why he did it, the trick with the steering lie and the escape through the father's pencil line are all said in plain words.
- **Romance:** came through from the first scene and grew every few chapters, with spoken lines I could quote ("I'm in love with you," "Ask me in five days," "And so do we," "I love you. I have since the rock").

Worth fixing if there's another pass, none of them blocking:
1. Ch 14's day jumps.
2. How Ned reached the Dyer house on his bad hip.
3. Ch 8's "tell her people five" line.
4. Ch 2's "indict" and Ch 12's "rudder" and "sheared." "Rudder" could be explained where it first appears in Ch 12, not in Ch 14.
5. "Vise," which is never explained.
