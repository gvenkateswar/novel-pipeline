# Outline audit (logic gate): Ten Days Out, outline v4 draft

*Plot auditor, Oct 30, 2026. Read: `outline-v4-draft.md`, `reviews/rebuild-brief-v4.md`, `genre-maps/romance.md`, `chapters/chapter-01/test-v4.md`. Audited text: `outline-v4-draft.md` as of Oct 30. The question throughout: would a reader who has only the page accept this?*

Blocking rule applied: an unanswered question 2 (how did it travel), 3 (why couldn't the channel be closed) or 4 (why do the safer options fail) blocks. So does a failed one-talk test on any conflict between the leads, per the author's Oct 30 instruction. Finding 11 blocks under brief item 6, which is binding.

---

## Blocking findings (ordered by what they would cost the reader)

### Finding 1: the premise. If Cole was arrested with the drugs, Beth is not "the whole case"
**Where:** The clock; Ch 02 s1; Safer-options rows "testify later" and "by video".
**Question failed:** 4.
**The problem:** "Cole Varney was arrested on Route 1 at about one in the morning **with the truck**." In the test chapter the bricks went back into the crate and the truck drove off loaded. If the troopers stopped a truck full of fentanyl with Cole at the wheel, the arresting officers can carry the indictment, and Beth is a supporting witness, not "the whole case." A reader who knows any TV crime drama will ask why ten days of killing are aimed at a deckhand when the drugs were seized. The outline never answers this, and the ten-day clock and every attack depend on it.
**Linked:** In the test chapter, "The driver set his brake and stayed in his cab." Beth has chained that truck down every Thursday for eleven years. She would know Cole's face and would tell the trooper who was driving. The outline has to say whether she saw him.
**What the architect must answer:** What did the troopers find in the truck at 1 a.m.?
**Suggested fix:** The truck is clean when it's stopped. Cole offloaded in Pruett before Route 1, and all that's left is powder from the torn brick in the seams. That holds him for a few days but convicts nobody. Beth saw the bricks and saw Cole in the cab. Her eyes, her photo and the residue are the case, and without her testimony he walks. Say it in one plain line in Ch 02 and restate it in Ch 03. The domain checker must also confirm "in person or he walks Monday" before the bible. If it fails, the architect needs a fallback clock now, not after drafting.
**Blocks:** Yes.

### Finding 2: Ch 01. How did the man get from Pruett back to Cutter by 2 a.m., and who sent him?
**Where:** Ch 01 s1–s3; traitor's path row 01; Safer-options last row.
**Question failed:** 2.
**The problem:** The man in the hoodie was in the back of the truck on the ferry to Pruett. The ferry was the last boat of the night, and Beth was home by midnight. At 2 a.m. he is in her kitchen on the island, and he leaves by "a small boat, no lights." The outline never says how he got back across an hour of open bay in sleet, whose boat it was, or when he got his orders. The causal chain the outline relies on ("He came that night because Ned told Cole she'd call") needs times: Ned calls Cole at Pruett, Cole tells the man, the man takes a skiff back. None of that is stated. A reader will notice that the man left the island on the ferry and was back on it three hours later.
**What the architect must answer:** The man's route and timing, and who told him what, from the Pruett landing to 2 a.m.
**Suggested fix:** One line in Ch 12's confession (or Ch 02, as Russ's news) gives the chain. The ferry docks about 10:30. Ned phones Cole in the Pruett lot. Cole sends the man back in his own skiff, which was tied at Pruett. Cole drives south and is stopped at 1. The man was sent before Beth ever called. That makes Dane's "within two hours of her call" inference wrong for a reason the reader can check later. Russ's line in Ch 02 that "the kid's skiff was gone from the Pruett float" plants it.
**Blocks:** Yes.

### Finding 3: Ch 03. They spend the night in the house the man broke into the night before
**Where:** Ch 02 s2 ("Home," she says, to everyone); Ch 03 s1–s2.
**Questions failed:** 4 and 5.
**The problem:** The man broke into Beth's house at 2 a.m. on Day 0, and he knows it blind. On Day 1, Dane (a marshal who "has protected maybe forty witnesses") lets her sleep there again, then checks the windows twice. The outline's say-it-twice line ("he came because she said 'home' at the landing") is beside the point, because the man needed nobody to tell him. Safer options a reader will name at once: the co-op loft that saves them in Ch 04, any other house on the island, or Russ's house with the constable in it. As written, this is the stupid choice the brief forbids. It isn't a costly mistake, because neither lead argues it.
**What the architect must answer:** Why that house, that night, with Dane's agreement?
**Suggested fix:** Make it an argument the two of them have to each other's faces, with a reason that holds: the power is out across the island, her woodstove is the only heat for a mile, and her father's shotgun is in the closet. Beth's own point: he's already been here, so the place he'll expect her is anywhere else. Dane loses the argument and says so. When the man comes anyway, Dane owns the call (Ch 04 s1 fight). A second option: they start the night somewhere else, and the storm or the power cut forces them back.
**Blocks:** Yes.

### Finding 4: the man's hideout is reported to Dane in Ch 05 and nobody checks it
**Where:** Ch 05 s1 (Russ: "a summer house on the east road was broken into overnight (plant: where the man is hiding)"); paid Ch 12; Ch 02 s2 ("nobody knows his name past 'the new sternman'").
**Question failed:** 4.
**The problem:** On Day 2 the constable tells a deputy marshal where the hunted man is probably sleeping. Over the next five days, through a fire, a near-drowning and an ambush, nobody goes to look. A competent protector would check it on Day 2 with Russ. The same goes for the man's name. He crewed on somebody's boat for two summers on an island where Tess says "the island already knows everything." His captain paid him and knows his name.
**What the architect must answer:** Why doesn't Dane, or Russ, search the east-road house? Who hired the sternman?
**Suggested fix:** Have Dane and Russ search it in Ch 05 or early Ch 06 and find it freshly abandoned: a warm stove, food, a phone charger. That is an event, and it costs them an hour away from Beth. For the name, the sternman worked Cole's own boat for cash, under a first name only. Cole is in jail and isn't talking.
**Blocks:** Yes.

### Finding 5: the leak channel could be found in a day once they suspect the three
**Where:** Traitor's path, "Means" ("Ned's cell phone… texting a number the lawyer gave him"); Ch 06 to Ch 11.
**Question failed:** 3.
**The problem:** From Ch 06 ("four people knew the house") Dane has a list of four. His boss can ask the carriers for the three islanders' phone records as an emergency request, because a federal witness is being hunted. Ned's own cell would show texts to one unknown number just before every attack. The mystery would end in Ch 07, by phone. The outline says neither why Dane doesn't ask nor why it wouldn't work.
**What the architect must answer:** Why can't this channel be traced?
**Suggested fix:** Ned texts from a prepaid phone the lawyer's people left in the ferry's wheelhouse, never his own. In Ch 07 or 08 Dane does pull the three islanders' records, with Beth arguing against it to his face, and they come back clean. The suspicion holds, the reader sees the competent move made, and Beth gets a real cost: she fought to protect people from a check they passed. Ch 12's "means" line changes to the burner, and Ch 13's bait uses the burner.
**Blocks:** Yes.

### Finding 6: Ch 08–09. After the summer house burns, they tell the same three people the time and place of the rescue
**Where:** Ch 08 s2; Ch 09 s1; Safer-options row "tell nobody."
**Questions failed:** 4 and 5.
**The problem:** Both leads know that one of four people leaked the summer house, and the lighthouse, which only the boss knew, is still standing. Then they hand the same three islanders the rescue. The three reasons are each easy to close:
- **"A beach only an islander knows (Ned)."** Beth was born on Cutter and has worked its water for eleven years. If the north beach is "the only one a boat can land on in this sea," she knows it.
- **"The island's emergency radio to clear it (Tess)."** The Marine Patrol has its own radios, and Dane has a phone on the tower.
- **"The one truck that can get there (Russ)."** The lighthouse is on the north end, a mile from the north-side house. They can walk.

Then in Ch 09 s1 Beth walks into town in daylight to the ferry and the store, the two most watched places on the island, with the man loose. "They agree on three hours' notice" also contradicts a morning visit for a 3 p.m. boat. As written, two competent adults make the choice the reader can see is wrong, and the outline's only defense is that it's "her flaw". A flaw needs a reason the reader accepts at the moment it happens.
**What the architect must answer:** Name the one thing only these three can supply, and say why Beth can't supply it herself.
**Suggested fix (pick one):** (a) The Marine Patrol won't come for a witness without a cleared landing from the island's emergency director: Tess, by law. That forces one person, not three. Beth then chooses to tell Ned and Russ too, out of love, while Dane argues. That is her condemnable choice made knowing the facts. (b) They tell nobody, Beth picks the beach, and the man is there anyway because the lull and the only landing are obvious to any islander (see Finding 7). Then the ambush proves nothing, and the suspicion has to come from somewhere else. Option (a) keeps the outline's spine.
**Blocks:** Yes.

### Finding 7: Ch 09. The ambush doesn't prove a leak, and nobody says who knows the lighthouse afterward
**Where:** Ch 09 s1 ("the north beach, the only one a boat can land on in this sea"); Ch 09 s2 (Dane's reasoning); Ch 10–11 (back at the lighthouse).
**Questions failed:** 1 and 2.
**The problem:** First, if the north beach is the only landing and the lull is visible in the sky, any islander, including the man, could guess where and when a boat would come. Dane's "every time her people knew, the man came" is weakest at exactly the point where the outline needs it strongest. Beth would say so. Second, Russ "will drive." If he collects them at the lighthouse, Russ knows the lighthouse. They return there in Ch 10 and nobody comes, which clears Russ before the trap, and an attentive reader will work that out. The man also just lost them on the north end, a mile from an empty winter lighthouse he must know. Why doesn't he search it?
**What the architect must answer:** What only the leak could tell the man (the hour? the time the officers would wade in?). Where Russ picks them up. Why the lighthouse stays safe after Ch 09.
**Suggested fix:** The leaked fact is the hour. The lull is uncertain, the boat could come at noon or at four, and only the three knew three o'clock. Give Beth the counterargument in Ch 09 ("anybody could guess the beach") and let Dane answer with the hour. Russ picks them up at the bottom of the lighthouse road, not the door. The man doesn't search the lighthouse in Ch 07–08 because he saw Dane go into the sea and believes they drowned. Say it once, then pay it off: the island learns they're alive at the Ch 09 store visit.
**Blocks:** Yes.

### Finding 8: Ch 11. Dane watches the trap for four hours and doesn't try to catch the man
**Where:** Ch 10 s3; Ch 11 s1–s2.
**Question failed:** 4.
**The problem:** An armed marshal has set a trap, knows the target building and has hours to get ready. The man walks up, sets the church on fire and gets away unseen. The safer option, Dane at the church with Russ (if Russ is cleared by the school staying dark) or alone, ends the threat that night. Also unsaid: whether Beth told Tess to keep the church to herself. If she did, Tess telling Ned is a breach the reader understands, because it's Ned. If she didn't, Tess did nothing wrong and the "test" proves nothing.
**What the architect must answer:** Why Dane stays on the hill, and what Beth said to Tess.
**Suggested fix:** One line each. Dane won't leave Beth alone on the hill, and won't bring her within range of a man who shoots, so the trap is built to learn the leak, not to catch him, and Dane says so in Ch 10 s3 while they argue. Beth tells Tess "Don't tell anyone, not even Ned," and Tess, a near-sister, decides Ned doesn't count. That makes Tess's Ch 11 line hurt twice.
**Blocks:** Yes.

### Finding 9: Ch 13–15. The ferry runs on Day 8, and they don't cross until Day 10 at 9 p.m.
**Where:** Ch 13 s2 (the test run, "She'll take it across herself"); Ch 14 (the Day 9 bait); Ch 15 s2 (cast off at 9 p.m., Day 10); Ch 16 s3 ("Pruett at dawn"); test chapter ("the whole hour across").
**Question failed:** 4.
**The problem:** Since Ch 01 Dane has wanted her off the island. On Day 8 the only boat that can cross works. The safer option is to cross on Day 8 or Day 9, check into a Portland hotel days before Monday, and leave the man on the island. Instead they spend Day 9 using the witness as bait in a gunfight, then leave at 9 p.m. for a crossing the test chapter says takes an hour, and land "at dawn." If the storm is crossable on Day 10 night, the outline must say why it wasn't on Day 8. Catching the man isn't Dane's job; the witness is.
**What the architect must answer:** What stops the crossing on Days 8 and 9, and why 9 p.m.?
**Suggested fix:** The Day 8 test proves the steering, but the storm peaks on Days 8–9, and the boat can't clear the harbor mouth in that sea. Beth says so plainly. The forecast gives a window from Sunday night. They leave when it opens, and the crossing takes nine hours because she has to run slow and wide of the ledges in a following sea. Say it once in Ch 13 s2 and once in Ch 15 s2. The fish-shack bait then has its reason: the man will try for the ferry before the window, so they draw him away from it first. Dane says this to her face in Ch 13 s3.
**Blocks:** Yes.

### Finding 10: Ch 15. Nobody searches the ferry before casting off
**Where:** Ch 15 s2; Ch 16 s1 ("he hid in a stranded truck").
**Question failed:** 4.
**The problem:** The man is wounded, loose, and needs off the island. The ferry is the only boat that can cross, and it carries two closed trucks. A marshal who has been hit at every hiding place would search the car deck before the witness leaves the pier. The outline doesn't say he does.
**What the architect must answer:** Did Dane search the boat, and if so, how did the man get past him?
**Suggested fix:** Dane and Beth search it at 8 p.m., including both trucks, and find nothing. The man comes aboard over the stern from his skiff in the dark as they clear the harbor, and he scuttles the skiff. That's why the light comes on after they've cast off. Or have the man hide in the one place Dane can't open (the propane truck's locked tank compartment), and have Dane note it out loud as locked.
**Blocks:** Yes.

### Finding 11: reader knowledge. An attentive reader names Ned by Ch 02, nine chapters before Tess does
**Where:** Ch 01 s1 (Ned: "Then you didn't see it. Not tonight." / "He looked away first."); Ch 02 s2 (Ned tells her to go; his son "doing better"; "One line of spark between Russ and Tess"); Ch 09 s1 (Ned tells her to go a third time); Ch 10 title "Not Ned"; Ch 10 s3 (the one person she won't test).
**Question failed:** 9 (and brief item 6: "no 20% gap where the reader is ahead of her").
**The problem:** Four things point at Ned by 12%. (1) Ned's Ch 01 lines are the only early behavior by any suspect that's suspicious on the page. (2) His son is planted in Ch 02 with a shifty "doing better." (3) Romance readers know the Russ–Tess spark line marks the next book's couple, and a series-opener reader doesn't expect the Book 2 heroine or hero to be the traitor. That leaves one suspect from Ch 02. (4) Ch 10 is titled "Not Ned," which on audio is read aloud, and Ch 10 s3 then excludes from the test the one person a mystery reader always suspects. Tess's and Russ's seeds ("you put my store under," "fell asleep in his truck") come in Ch 05 and are milder than Ned's. Beth setting his line down in Ch 02 "with a reason the reader accepts" doesn't fix this; it makes Beth look slower than the reader, which is the gap the brief bans. The reader is about 50% of the book ahead.
**What the architect must answer:** What makes Ned look clear to a reader between Ch 02 and Ch 09?
**Suggested fix (needs at least two):**
- (a) Move the Russ–Tess spark line to after the Ch 11 reveal (Ch 14 s1, where the outline already has one).
- (b) Give Ned an act in Act 1 that clears him on the page. In Ch 02 s3 he saves Beth from the crush between the boat and the pier, not Dane. The reader will think a traitor wouldn't do that, and Ned's confession can explain it ("I never wanted you hurt").
- (c) Move the son plant out of Ch 02, to Tess as gossip in Ch 05 ("Ned's boy was in rehab again").
- (d) Give Tess or Russ one hard, visible piece of evidence: Russ's truck seen on the east road the night of the fire, or Tess on the phone at the store and hanging up when Beth walks in.
- (e) Retitle Ch 10 and Ch 11 (see Finding 18).
- (f) The architect's own flag 1: soften "He looked away first."
**Blocks:** Yes (brief item 6 is binding).

### Finding 12: the office-clearance logic. Who knew the loft, and how did the lighthouse report get out?
**Where:** Ch 05 s2 ("He reports their location to his boss in front of her, **as he does every night**"); Ch 04 ("She tells nobody"); traitor's path row 04 ("Nobody"); Ch 08 s1 (tells his boss from the keeper's house) against Ch 08 s2 (he climbs the tower "for a phone signal"); Ch 04 end (Russ finds them at the co-op wharf at dawn).
**Questions failed:** 1 and 2.
**The problem:** The midpoint inference ("the places only Dane's boss knew didn't get hit, so it isn't his office") depends on an exact list of who knew each place. The outline contradicts itself three times. (1) If Dane reports "every night," the boss knew the loft, and nothing happened there, so the boss is cleared by Day 2, not by Ch 09. (2) Ch 08 s1 has him calling the boss from the keeper's house, but s2 says the only signal is on the tower. (3) Russ finds them at the loft at dawn, so the loft isn't a "nobody knew" place by morning, and Ch 05 never says that's why they leave it. Also unsaid: why a man who suspects his office (Ch 02) reports at all. "He trusts his boss and nobody else" first appears in Ch 05. It has to be said in Ch 02, when the suspicion starts.
**What the architect must answer:** For each place, who knew it, through which channel, and when.
**Suggested fix:** On the loft night there's no signal at the co-op (or the storm is down), so Dane doesn't report, and he says so in Ch 04 s1. They leave the loft because Russ found them. In Ch 02 s1 Dane says, in front of Beth, that he'll report to his boss alone, by phone, because she's the one person in the office he'd trust with his life. Ch 08's report moves into s2, from the tower stair.
**Blocks:** Yes.

## Non-blocking findings

### Finding 13: the rupture's danger goes away offstage, in a prosecutor's line
**Where:** Ch 12 s3 to Ch 14 s3; Ch 17 s1 ("the man in the hoodie is talking and Ned will testify, so Cole's people are coming apart").
**Question:** 10 (the one-talk test) and the ending.
**Judgment of the one-talk sentence:** "…because Cole's people may want her dead after she testifies, and the only protection Dane's job can give her is a new name… which is the one life Beth won't live." **This passes.** It's an outside force plus a value clash that both leads can state plainly and still be stuck on. Dane's "She'll go" is a choice the reader will condemn, made knowing the facts, which the map asks for. The repair (Ch 14 s3 accountability and verification, Ch 15 s1 condition and the ordinary scene) is a conversation between equals followed by an action, with both present. It passes.
**The problem:** The repair settles who decides. It doesn't touch the danger. That is dissolved in Ch 17 by a prosecutor reporting offstage events, which is exactly the "report news that happened offstage" the brief bans. The testimony itself, the book's central act, happens behind a door the POV character can't go through. The reader is told the outside force has gone away rather than watching anyone face it.
**Suggested fix:** Keep the risk alive into the HEA as a priced cost. "Let him be the one who watches the door" already carries it, so cut "coming apart" and leave Cole's people a real threat Dane has chosen to live beside. Or put the dismantling on the page: the man in the hoodie, hauled back aboard in Ch 16, starts naming names to Dane on the wheelhouse floor. The testimony can stay offstage if Ch 17 s1 gives Beth's first line coming out of the room and one thing it cost her.

### Finding 14: Ch 02. Beth's refusal to leave needs a reason as concrete as the danger
**Where:** Ch 01 s3, Ch 02 s1.
**Question:** 5.
**The problem:** "I'm not running… and letting them think it worked" is pride against a man who just chased her through the snow. A reader will accept it once. Dane's flip ("a hotel his office books is the worst place") is a reason for him to choose the place himself, not to stay. Neither scene closes these options: he drives her somewhere off the island that only he knows, or the Marine Patrol boat brings a second deputy or leaves an officer.
**Suggested fix:** Give her one concrete reason the reader can weigh. The man lives on the island, and she's the only person who can pick him out by his face. If she leaves, he's gone into the summer people. Or: she won't leave Ned to run the ferry alone, which is ironic later. Add one line from the boss: nobody else can get out before the storm.

### Finding 15: the coaching risk is named and never charged
**Where:** Ch 05 s2 ("if Cole's lawyer learns he's sleeping with his witness, they'll say he coached her"); Ch 05 s1 (the whole island learns from the condoms); Ch 15 s1 (a public kiss); Ch 17.
**Question:** 6.
**The problem:** It's the honest reason for holding back, and the outline drops it. The island knows by Day 2, and nothing ever comes of it.
**Suggested fix:** Charge it once. In Ch 12 s3 the boss cites it ("Cole's lawyer already has it") as a second reason for the program, or the prosecutor asks Beth about it in Ch 17 s1 and she answers it straight.

### Finding 16: Ch 06. The protector is asleep when the house burns
**Where:** Ch 06 s1 ("She wakes Dane").
**Question:** 5.
**The problem:** After two attacks there's no watch. As written it's a lapse nobody pays for.
**Suggested fix:** Make it the cost of his own Ch 03 rule. He fell asleep on watch beside her, and he says so in Ch 07 or 08. Or Beth took the watch and he relieved her and slept through. Either way it costs on the page.

### Finding 17: the same attack, seven times, and three fires
**Where:** The man appears in Ch 01, 03, 04, 06, 09, 11 and 14 and gets away each time (the outline's flag 3 counts four). Fires in Ch 06, 11 and 14.
**Question:** the "story has to MOVE" rule ("every chapter changes the situation so the next chapter can't be the same kind").
**The problem:** The pattern repeats: a place is shared, he comes at night, they run. One man gets past an armed marshal, a constable and a shotgun seven times. Three night fires will read as one idea used three times.
**Suggested fix:** Change the mode of the Ch 14 attack (no fire; he comes by water under the shack and is wounded in the water). Have Dane wound him in Ch 09, so the dragging heel becomes blood in the snow by Ch 11. Make the church fire the only fire after Ch 06.

### Finding 18: the chapter titles give away the answers
**Where:** Ch 10 "Not Ned"; Ch 11 "The Church Burns."
**Question:** 9.
**The problem:** Ch 10 ends on "Church or school?" and Ch 11's title answers it before the first line. On audio, titles are read aloud.
**Suggested fix:** Retitle with objects that don't answer anything ("The Mug," "Four Hours on the Hill").

### Finding 19: Ch 10 s1. Beth keeps her suspicion of Ned from Dane for one chapter
**Where:** Ch 10 s1 ("for one second she thinks it… She shoves it down"); Ch 10 s3 (she refuses to test Ned); Ch 11 s1 (she tells him).
**Question:** 10.
**Judgment:** The one-talk sentence for the fight over Ned ("…neither of them knows which… testing Ned costs her something she can't get back") **passes**: if she'd said "I thought it too," she would still refuse the test. The outline's audit line "Nothing between them is… kept from the other" is not true as written, though. Her silence has a reason the reader accepts (she can't stand the thought), and she pays for it in Ch 11 by confessing it. That's allowed. Correct the claim, or have her say it out loud in Ch 10 s3 during the argument and still refuse. That is stronger, and it makes her condemnable choice fully informed.

### Finding 20: smaller safer options, one line each
- **Ch 15, Ned as crew.** Why the handcuffed traitor and not Dane on the lines? It needs one line: in that sea someone who knows the boat has to hold the wheel while she handles the lines at Pruett, and nobody is left on the island to guard Ned.
- **Ch 15, the Coast Guard.** A constable with a gunshot wound and a woman with burned hands are medical evacuations, not "a witness." It needs one line on why even a medevac can't come (icing, and no helicopter can fly).
- **Ch 16 s1.** Why does Dane go down to the car deck instead of holding the wheelhouse stair? It needs one line: the light is by the engine-room hatch, and if the man reaches the engines they all drown.
- **Ch 13 s3, the bait.** Ned's arrest happened on the float in daylight, and the island "already knows everything." Why would the man trust a text from Ned's phone? It needs one line: Russ put Ned in the town-office back room without telling anyone, and the man texted that morning before the news could spread. Or he's desperate enough to come anyway.
- **Ch 12, Ned's son.** Cole's people have just threatened a man in Portland. A competent marshal calls it in. Give Dane one line to the boss.
- **Ch 04 end.** "He was supposed to be watching her road." Nobody asked Russ to on the page. Plant it in Ch 02 s2.
- **Ch 02 s3.** Why are two trucks aboard a ferry that has cancelled service? It needs one line: they were left from the afternoon run when the storm warning came in.

### Finding 21: dates, distances and the dead witnesses
- **Lowell.** The clock says Cole's people killed "a man in Lowell three years ago." Marisol died "four years ago." Is Marisol the Lowell witness or a second dead witness? A listener will merge them. Say which.
- **Distances.** Ch 15: "the grand jury sits at ten tomorrow, two hours from Pruett." Ch 16 end: "Four hours to Portland." Pick one.
- **Suspicion opens in Ch 06, not Ch 09.** Ch 06 s2 has Beth thinking "three of them were hers" and Dane saying "Somebody told him." The brief table's claim that both suspect "from the same moment (Ch 09)" is wrong. That's fine for the story, but the reader-knowledge math in Finding 11 runs from Ch 02, not Ch 09.
- **Ch 17 s1, the courthouse.** A detained defendant is not normally walked past a grand jury witness in the hallway. Send it to the domain checker. If it fails, use Cole's lawyer instead, who is already the man who threatened Ned.

### Finding 22: Beth is never physically hurt
**Question:** 6.
Dane is cut, nearly drowns, falls and is shot. Russ is shot, Tess is burned, an officer is shot. Beth comes through ten days without a mark. The stakes curve still climbs (house, trust, Ned, the shack, the relationship), so this is optional. One injury she has to work through (Ch 14 burns, or the Ch 11 smoke) would make the climax cost her body too.

**6. Stakes curve.** Act 1 costs her home as a refuge and her sense that the island is safe. Act 2 costs the summer house, Dane nearly drowned, an officer shot, her trust in her three people, and Tess burned by Beth's own test.
