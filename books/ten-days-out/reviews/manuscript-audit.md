# Manuscript audit (plot-auditor, manuscript mode): Ten Days Out

**Draft read:** `books/ten-days-out/manuscript.md`, the third draft (the output of the `revision-brief-v3.md` pass), 34,683 words by `wc -w`. This is the file sent to beta readers. Read in full, Ch 01–10.
**Also read:** `outline.md`, `story-bible.md` (consulted for intent only), `revision-brief-v3.md`, `docs/ai-fiction-tells.md` Part 7.
**Date:** 2026-10-27.
**Method:** Everything below is judged from the page. Planning files are used only to name where the page departs from what was intended. The outside panels' arguments about the Ch 07 decision and the trap were not consulted.

Findings are listed first, ordered by how much each would cost a reader. The event ledgers come next (who knew, the channel, why it wasn't closed, the safer options), then the nine questions, then the Part 7 checklist.

## Findings

### Finding 1. The ferry sails on Sunday without a repaired bridge, so the ten-day clock and "no other way off" fall apart
**Where:** Ch 02, Ch 06, Ch 09 close, Ch 10 opening.
**Question failed:** 4 (safer options), and 3 for the trap.
**The page:**
- Ch 02: "Ten days, if the state gets a barge out."
- Ch 06: "Three days and a storm until the ferry. No other way off."
- Ch 09, Day 9 morning, Ned on 16: "First departure Sunday at first light. Passengers and light freight."
- Ch 10, Day 10: "the bow walked off the stone, slow, the twisted bridge sliding across the forward glass."
- Ch 10, Day 9 noon: "Two engines came across a harbor so flat it held the sky, and quit at the pier."

The *North Reach* leaves Cutter from the stone pier with the transfer bridge still twisted. Its captain, a licensed deckhand and a working boat were all at that pier from Day 1. Day 8 evening is "clear and bitter. The harbor lay flat," and on Day 9 the sea is glass by noon. A reader will ask why Ned couldn't run passengers off the stone pier on Day 9, or on Day 8 evening when the *Margaret Ann* herself is expected to sail. The book never names what the barge is needed for. Once the boat leaves without it, every "until the ferry" deadline in Ch 06–09 reads as arbitrary.
**What the writer must answer:** Give one concrete reason the boat can't carry passengers before Sunday (for example a hull or steering inspection after the surge, a Coast Guard hold on the certificate, or swell at the stone pier that only drops on Saturday), and plant it in Ch 03 or Ch 04.

### Finding 2. The trap: the safer options that remain open on Day 8
**Where:** Ch 02 (LifeFlight), Ch 08 three o'clock and half past three, Ch 09.
**Question failed:** 4. This is completion question 3 of `revision-brief-v3.md`, and the page only partly answers it.
**The page:**
- Ch 02: "'LifeFlight lands on the ballfield,' she said." Dane answers about the chimney and the door. Nobody ever answers about the helicopter.
- Ch 08, 3 p.m.: "The glass had come up four since noon... By dark the northwest would have the sea laid down outside, and she'd go."
- Pike, as Dane reports her: "neither will run the ledge field across the bay in the dark with the swell that's left, so first light is the earliest anyone from the mainland sets foot on Cutter. The state police won't cross until the boats do."

Pike's answer closes boats. It doesn't close:
- **(a) An aircraft.** It is daylight between 3:00 and dark, the weather is clearing, and the ballfield was named on page in Ch 02. A state police or Coast Guard helicopter could put officers at the store before the dragger sails.
- **(b) Intercepting the *Margaret Ann* at sea.** Pike's "Arsenault is off the float with Tess aboard" is said only about blue lights at the store.
- **(c) The asymmetry.** A Gloucester dragger crewed by men from away will run the same ledge field in the same dark that the Coast Guard refuses. Ch 06's line about the lifeboat's rules ("no landing under fire, no second try") half-covers this, but nobody restates it on Day 8.
- **(d) Beth's own body on the deck.** Her voice on channel 9 is needed. Her presence aboard is not. The page's only answer is "In the dark you can't find that deck. I can." That reason exists only because of the decision in Finding 8.

**What the writer must answer:** Add one line in Pike's half-past-three answer that grounds aircraft (a ceiling or icing, or the only helicopter tasked elsewhere), and one clause saying why intercepting at sea with Tess aboard is worse than the boat.

### Finding 3. The Day 8 ferry location was visible and was told to three channels before Beth's broadcast, and the bait lie contradicts what watchers could have seen
**Where:** Ch 08 (twenty to seven, seven o'clock, the brow), Ch 09 (half past five).
**Questions failed:** 2 and 3.
**The page:**
- Ch 08, Dane to Pike at 11:20 p.m., on the same sat link Beth had suspected: "I think she's gone to the ferry."
- Ch 08: "Through the glass a figure came down the pier road, one arm held against his ribs... Ned stepped out onto the aluminum brow and stood in his way." Dane takes a call "there on the open stone... Then he turned his head and looked across the harbor at the store, and held on it." Beth "watched from the top of the wheelhouse ladder."
- Earlier: "Across the harbor the store was a bar of yellow." The store and the pier are in each other's sightline.
- Ch 09: "Anyone on the hill with binoculars would see the captain walk off his own boat with his bag." The book itself says the ferry is watched.
- Ch 09, the bait: "'And your marshal?' ... 'Down with the arm. Running a fever. I left him where he's warm.'"
- Ch 08: Ned, who "tells [Tess] everything at supper," and who told her the Ashby place, walks ashore at about 7:30 a.m. knowing exactly where Beth is: "Somebody's got to get Russ... I can walk the Harbor Road and nobody looks twice." Dane's objection is only for Ned's safety. Nobody tells Ned not to go near the store.

**Who knew Beth was on the ferry before 5:30 p.m.:**
- Ned, from 2 a.m.
- Pike and Quist, from 11:20 p.m.
- Dane, by guess and then by sight.
- Russ, by about 8 a.m. through Ned.
- Anyone watching the pier at 7 a.m. in daylight: a wounded marshal facing down the captain on the brow, with the witness visible at the ladder head.

The text never closes Ned's habit, and it never shows that the 7 a.m. scene went unseen. Tess "fishing" on the 3 p.m. roll ("She'd say it only for somebody beside her who wanted to hear who answered") implies the network doesn't know. But the page has given them every chance to know, and it never says why they didn't. If they did know, the lie about the fevered marshal elsewhere should have failed.
**What the writer must answer:**
- Put Dane's arrival out of sight. He could come up the riprap and the seaward ladder in the half-dark before the light, the way Russ later does, with Beth inside the glass. Alternatively, have Beth's lie account for him having been seen ("he came for his bag and went back").
- Add one line in which Dane or Beth tells Ned to keep away from the store, and why.

### Finding 4. Dane has held the bank-bag clue since Saturday and never acts on it
**Where:** Ch 02 (Dane's POV at the slip), Ch 03 (store), Ch 06 (landing call), Ch 07 (notebook), Ch 08 (Dane's night).
**Questions failed:** 3 and 5. Read as a professional's competence.
**The page:**
- Ch 02, Dane's POV: "A short woman in a red vest came down the ramp... She handed a canvas zip bag with a seal tag up over the rail, and the captain put it inside his jacket."
- Ch 07, his own log: "*D2 Sat 1000 — Quist. $9,000 cash deposited subject's acct, D1 a.m., w/ paycheck, via store bank bag. Hundreds. Teller flagged.*"
- Ch 03: he stands at the stove and watches Tess finish Ned's barge news before Ned has said it on the radio ("'—out of Rockland Wednesday, if it lays down.' Tess rang up the water jugs").
- Ch 03, the same day: "I'd rather be in the one house only Ned knows I asked about."
- Ch 07: "'No. Nobody.' ... 'Only us.'"
- Ch 08, Dane's night: "On the morning of Day 1 he had watched the store's bank bag, with the nine thousand in it, go from Tess Marr's hands inside that same captain's jacket."

From Saturday, Dane's log says the planted cash entered through a bag that only two people handle: the woman who packs it and the captain who carries it. He has seen both, and he has watched information flow from one to the other. On Saturday he takes the captain's key. On Wednesday he accepts a "local emergency management" notification without asking who that is. He suspects only the witness ("*D3 — financial questions. No indication.*"). Ch 08 presents him connecting the bag to Tess only after Pike names her. A reader with the page in hand gets there in Ch 03. The same applies to Quist, the case agent, who has the bag from Saturday and the boat from Sunday.
**What the writer must answer:** Give Dane (or Quist) a reason the bag doesn't point at the store. For example, the credit union can't say whether the cash went in at the store or at the teller window, or Dane asks Beth who packs it and she defends Tess. Otherwise let Dane raise it and be shut down on the page.

### Finding 5. Pike's quoted authorization uses a plan that doesn't exist yet, and it hands the decisive condition to the witness
**Where:** Ch 08, half past three.
**Questions failed:** 2 and 5.
**The page:** Pike's words, as Dane quotes them: "'Calloway stays behind the locked wheelhouse door unless the floods go dark. You hold the deck. Whitcomb goes for Marr the moment anyone starts across.'" Only after this does Dane propose the floods on breakers that "nobody but you can reach," and only then does Beth add "And when we know it's the water, they go dark... If the floods go dark, I come down."

**The order is wrong.** Pike sets a condition about the floods going dark before anyone has proposed controlling the floods.

**The condition belongs to Beth.** Beth holds the breakers, so Beth decides when she is allowed out from behind the door. In Ch 09 she throws them herself.

**It departs from the brief.** `revision-brief-v3.md` says Pike "does not authorize bait." On the page Pike first refuses ("she has not authorized bait") and then, after one pause, signs off on the grand-jury witness standing as a lit decoy four days before she testifies. That is the least credible procedural moment in the book, and Pike's reasoning for the reversal is "She was quiet long enough that I thought I'd lost the satellite."
**What the writer must answer:** Move the floods condition into the second call ("Pike hears the rest of it from me before anything happens"), and give Pike one line of reason for the reversal: what she weighs and what makes her accept it.

### Finding 6. Russ is cleared by a fact the book never established, and it contradicts Ch 06
**Where:** Ch 08, the crew-mess repair.
**Questions failed:** 1 and 2.
**The page:**
- Ch 08: "'Russ didn't hear about the landing until the fire company was called out for the standby,' Dane said, 'and by then it had already gone to whoever was waiting for it.'"
- Ch 06, after the shot: "'Any station, any station, this is Cutter Fire, Chief Grindle, at the firehouse. Was that gunfire at the town landing? Does anyone require the EMT? Over.'"

No standby call-out appears anywhere before the landing. Grindle's question reads as a chief who was not told. Dane has had no source for this since 7 a.m. (Pike gave him one name). The beat also defeats the pact made four lines earlier ("I want every name that comes into your head"). The first name Beth offers is dismissed with an unsupported assertion.
**What the writer must answer:** Plant the standby in Ch 06 (Dane hears it on the call, or Grindle mentions it), or let Russ's clearing come from somewhere the page shows.

### Finding 7. The trap is set up before the evidence that makes it necessary
**Where:** Ch 08, seven o'clock (about 7:30 a.m.) against three o'clock.
**Questions failed:** 3 and 5. Completion question 3.
**The page, at about 7:30 a.m.:**
- Dane to Ned: "And tell Russ to come along the top of the riprap under the pier at dusk, where the store can't see him."
- Beth: "If it comes to the store, he goes in the way the kids do. Bulkhead behind the propane cage. Not the front."

The evidence the brief requires to make waiting fatal (the fuel hose, the crew stowing for sea, Tess's changed voice on the roll) arrives at 3 p.m. So the dusk rendezvous and the store entry are planned seven hours before anyone knows the boat is sailing or that someone is sitting with Tess. The brief's chain was evidence, then the conclusion that waiting kills her, then the plan. The page runs it as plan, then evidence, then confirmation.
**What the writer must answer:** Either give the morning summons of Russ a reason that isn't the trap (to hold the pier, or to watch the store), and cut "If it comes to the store…" until after 3 p.m., or move Ned's errand to after the roll.

### Finding 8. Going dark gives up the armed defender's only advantage, and the page doesn't weigh it
**Where:** Ch 08, half past three; Ch 09, eleven o'clock.
**Question failed:** 5.
**The page:**
- Dane: "A man coming over the stern under those lights is a man I can see."
- Beth: "And when we know it's the water, they go dark. Then he comes over the stern blind."
- Ch 09, Dane: "Whatever came, he would not fire into a dark she was somewhere in."

A man coming out of a black harbor onto a deck lit by floods is the one who is lit. Dane is in shadow in the alley. Killing the floods makes the attacker no blinder, takes away the only gun, and turns the fight into Beth's hand-to-hand. It works because the author lets it work. The page offers only "In the dark you can't find that deck. I can." It never says what the dark costs the attacker that the light didn't.
**What the writer must answer:** Give Beth one concrete reason the floods help the man coming from the water. For example, the floods throw the stern gate into shadow below the bulwark, or they light Dane's alley mouth from the quarter. Or have Dane name the cost and accept it.

### Finding 9. The float lights at the landing were dark, the page says Ned controls them, and nobody ever accounts for it
**Where:** Ch 06 (Day 7 dawn).
**Questions failed:** 1 and 2, and an open plant.
**The page:** "The float lights were dark. Not their hour. In November the timer held them till seven. Ned set that timer. Ned never let those lights go dark."

Ned's confession in Ch 08, Tess's statement in Ch 10 and Quist's account at Pruett in Ch 10 all leave it out. The clue points the reader at Ned on the morning of the shooting and is never paid.
**What the writer must answer:** One clause in Quist's Ch 10 account or Tess's statement about who killed the float lights and how they got to the timer.

### Finding 10. Homeland Security warned Russ about the *Margaret Ann* on Friday, two days before the case connected the boat
**Where:** Ch 06 (Russ on the float) against Ch 04 (Lookout Hill).
**Questions failed:** 1 and 2 (timeline).
**The page:**
- Ch 06, Russ: "Woman called me Friday. Homeland Security, Portland, before the lines went. Said stay clear of that boat."
- Ch 04, Sunday, Dane: "Quist ran the boat... She has a Gloucester registry, Arsenault as owner and two crew listed."

Either HSI flagged the dragger on Day 1, before Beth identified the sternman, or Russ is lying. If HSI flagged it, Quist knew from Friday that a suspect boat sat on the mooring under the witness's window and never told her own detail. If Russ is lying, that is a plant nobody pays.
**What the writer must answer:** Change Russ's day to Sunday or later, or have Dane react to "Friday" on the page.

### Finding 11. Reader knowledge: Tess is clear by Ch 02–03, Dane's innocence from Ch 02, and the official reveal comes in Ch 08
**Where:** the whole book.
**Question failed:** 9.
**Clues that give it away:**
- Ch 01: the float lit after six, the tank loan.
- Ch 02: "Tess didn't. Something small hit the counter at her end and rolled, a coin, and she said 'Hell's bells' under her breath and laughed. The laugh came late." In the same call she says she is emergency management director.
- Ch 03: "*Tess knew I stayed.*" Beth's own thought, set in italics as a realization, in the third chapter.
- Ch 05: Beth names her outright ("Tess knew I was home").
- Ch 06: "Local emergency management on Cutter wore a red vest."
- Ch 08: Pike's "Teresa Marr."

An attentive reader has Tess by the end of Ch 03, five chapters before the confirmation. Dane's Ch 02 POV ("his work was to get her there with nothing on the way that a lawyer could use") settles that he isn't the leak. Ned's complicity is visible in Ch 01 ("He reached up with one finger and turned off his hearing aid"; "He let her skip it now"). The giveaway clue is Ch 03's "*Tess knew I stayed*," which tells the reader in plain words.

The consequence for the reader: Ch 07's crisis (Beth reading the notebook as proof against Dane) and Ch 08's "Teresa Marr" rest on suspicions the reader dropped chapters ago. The suspense in Ch 07–08 is dramatic irony, the reader waiting for Beth to catch up, not a question. If that is the intent, the page should lean on it. If it isn't, the Ch 03 italic line is the one to soften.

### Finding 12. Ch 07: Beth clears the fish house, leaves it anyway, and Ch 08 then repeats the deduction as if it were new
**Where:** Ch 07, eleven; Ch 08, mess.
**Question failed:** 5. Partly credible, with the cost shown on the page.
**The page:**
- Ch 07: "Nobody but the two of them knew this door, and nobody had come to it. So he hadn't sold this place. That cleared one line."
- Ch 08: "If it had been him, the fish house would have burned first." This is staged as a realization.

Her stated reason for not waking him is "If he was the leak, telling him she knew was the worst thing she could do". It sits beside her own conclusion that the one place he kept from Portland is the one place nobody attacked. The safer option she names to herself is to stay behind the bar in the only location that has not leaked and face him at daylight. She rejects it on feeling ("she couldn't stay in a room with the man who wrote it"). That is allowed: the choice is emotional and it has a cost on the page (the barnacles, the church woodpile, "nobody on the island who knew where she was"). But the Ch 08 beat no longer lands, because the reader watched her make the same deduction twenty pages earlier. She also leaves a wounded, sleeping man with the bar off the door. That is never weighed.
**What the writer must answer:** Keep the deduction in one chapter only. If it stays in Ch 07, Ch 08 needs a different proof. If it moves to Ch 08, Ch 07 must leave the fish house unexamined.

### Finding 13. The Day 1 back door needed no leak, and the page doesn't say so
**Where:** Ch 01 close, Ch 02, Ch 05 list.
**Questions failed:** 2 and 3.
**The page:**
- Ch 01: "The *Margaret Ann* rode the guest mooring... A figure stood on her afterdeck... It was turned toward shore. The guest mooring lay right below this window."
- Ch 02: Dane "did not flatter himself that it was the pallet" when half the island watched him walk to her door. The 9:15 boat left without them, and nothing sailed after it.
- Dane: "nobody knows you stayed, and nobody will."
- Ch 05, Beth's list: "Friday it's three... You. Ned... Tess."

The network had eyes on the house from the mooring and the whole island saw the marshal go up the hill. With no departure after 9:15, anyone could infer she was home. The "Tess knew I stayed" beat, and Beth's three-name list, treat the check-in log as the necessary channel when the page has already shown a direct one.
**What the writer must answer:** Have Dane or Beth name the watched house in the Ch 05 list. That keeps Tess as a suspect without the logic claiming more than it can.

### Finding 14. The Ashby timing: the network had the house from Saturday supper but searched the empty Cape on Sunday and struck on Monday
**Where:** Ch 03 to Ch 05.
**Question failed:** 2.
**The page:**
- Ch 08, Ned: "I told nobody where you were. Nobody but Tess."
- Ch 08, Dane: "Whoever had all three had them at one table." That is Ned's supper at the store.
- Ch 04, Sunday 6:50 a.m.: the sternman walks round the back of Beth's empty Cape for ninety-one seconds.
- Ch 05: the fire comes Monday at ten.

Why search the old house after the new one has gone to Tess, and why wait two nights? The page doesn't say: weather, an order, or Ned not getting to supper until Sunday.
**What the writer must answer:** One clause from Ned about when he told her (for example "Sunday supper"), or one from Quist in Ch 10.

### Finding 15. The Tess-to-Arsenault channel is never named; Ch 10 supplies it after the fact
**Where:** Ch 03, Ch 10.
**Question failed:** 2. Low cost.
**The page:**
- Ch 03: "Tess's phone lay face-down by the register. It buzzed against the wood."
- Ch 10, Tess: "Thursday noon I told him at my back door I was done." Also: "Quarter past six Friday morning he was at my back door with an envelope."
- Ch 10, Quist: "sat at the end of the drive in the store's pickup, keys off the hook in Ms. Marr's office... Arsenault talked him down your shore path on a handheld from the boat."

The reader can infer the cell phone or the back door, but no line ever says how Day 1 evening, Saturday's Ashby news or Wednesday's landing time reached the dragger. Quist's Pruett speech delivers new facts (the store pickup, the radio guidance on Day 1) as a block of explanation after the action.

### Finding 16. The three o'clock roll is invented the moment the plot needs it
**Where:** Ch 08, three o'clock.
**Question failed:** 4. Low cost.
**The page:** "Since the second system came in yesterday, Tess had added a roll at three, for the back-road people who went to bed at dark."

The net has been fixed at 0800 and 1800 since Ch 02. The roll that carries the decisive evidence is announced on the page where it is used.
**What the writer must answer:** Plant the three o'clock roll in Ch 07 (the Icom on scan picks it up Thursday afternoon), or move the evidence to the regular net.

## Event ledgers

### Day 1, 11:40 p.m.: the back door
- **Who knew she stayed home:**
  - Dane.
  - Tess, from the 5:40 p.m. landline call ("I'm home. Don't send anybody. Keep it off the air.").
  - Anyone who read the open spiral log.
  - Pike, from Dane's 11:20 call (the red herring).
  - Any watcher. The *Margaret Ann* was on the guest mooring below the window (Finding 13).
- **Channel:** Not stated. Tess's booster cell (implied, Ch 03) or eyes from the mooring. Ch 10: Arsenault guided Doucette "on a handheld from the boat."
- **Why it wasn't closed:** Beth trusted Tess, and the page makes that credible ("Tess has known where I sleep since I was nine"). She also kept the call from Dane ("I've taken care of the check-in"). That is a concealment, and Ch 05 corrects it.
- **Safer options:**
  - Let Dane deal with the check-in, or call Grindle instead of net control. Not closed, but the island's rule makes calling Tess the ordinary choice. Credible.
  - Move on Day 1. Closed: "I don't have another house I've walked."

### Day 4, 10 p.m.: the Ashby fire
- **Who knew:**
  - Ned (the key).
  - Tess, through Ned at supper (Ch 08).
  - Russ, who heard the generator Saturday night.
  - "Four houses with ears."
  - Dane.
  - Pike and Quist through the log ("*D2 — moved: Ashby place, north pt. Key via Haskell*"). Ch 05 says Pike knew only that they had moved, but the D2 log line was in Quist's book.
- **Channel:** Ned to Tess at supper, then Tess to Arsenault (unstated).
- **Why it wasn't closed:** In Ch 03 Beth knows "he told Tess everything. The whole island knew that." She has just thought "Tess knew I stayed," and she takes Ned's key the same hour. That is credible as denial ("She held on to the clock until the other thing let go"). Dane watched Tess finish Ned's sentence and still says "only Ned knows" (Finding 4).
- **Safer options:** Hallorans' or Lathams', or a house whose key doesn't go through Ned. Partly closed by sightlines ("Nobody at Ashby's. Water on three sides"). Asking Ned not to tell anyone is not closed; "Nobody but me and God" stands in for it.
- **Timing:** Finding 14.

### Day 7, 6:00 a.m.: the landing ambush
- **Who knew:**
  - Dane.
  - Pike and the Portland office.
  - Quist ("conf. Quist").
  - The Coast Guard station.
  - County emergency management, then the island coordinator, Tess (Ch 08: "Portland told the county, because the procedure says you tell them before you use a town landing").
  - Beth, who overheard the time and place on Wednesday at the kindling block.
  - Russ is excluded by an unplanted standby (Finding 6).
- **Channel:** Procedure to Tess, then Tess to Arsenault (the second step is never shown; Finding 15). Tess's Ch 10 statement ("After the landing I didn't [believe him]") confirms she passed it.
- **Why it wasn't closed:** Dane didn't know who the coordinator was, and he owns that on the page in Ch 08 ("I should have asked who else those words covered, and I didn't"). Beth knew and kept silent. She owns it ("That's mine. Yours is still yours."), and the cost is shown ("If she had said a name on Wednesday, it would be a clean arm"). This is the book's clearest condemnable choice, and it works.
- **Safer options:**
  - Ask Portland to withhold the time from the county. Closed by procedure.
  - Use a different landing. Not discussed.
  - LifeFlight. Raised in Ch 02 and never answered (Finding 2).
  - Dane's own bank-bag knowledge should have led him to ask who the coordinator was (Finding 4).

### Day 7, 10:58 p.m.: Beth leaves Dane for the ferry
- **What she knows:**
  - The notebook: every location, the landing, "*ASSESS... Do not disclose to subject*," the $9,000, and the struck D6 line.
  - The overheard "She doesn't know. Daily. Understood."
  - Tess's log line, the "local emergency management" phrase, Ned's reefer lie, and Ned's suppers at Tess's.
- **Her reasoning on the page:**
  - The 17 minutes until the alarm: "On the cove road he'd have her before the bend... The shore he couldn't follow."
  - The objective is the fixed VHF and the locking door, not Ned.
  - No transmitting in the dark: "Sixteen was open to every set in the harbor, and the *Margaret Ann* kept hers on."
  - Ned is a risk she accepts: "Ned could have the boat. He couldn't have the wheelhouse unless she opened it."
  - She rules out the store: "Too many people in and out, and a phone in the back office."
  - The open book is her message: "The open page would tell him she'd gone on her own, and why."
- **Brief C1 compliance:**
  - She does not switch off the alarm: yes.
  - VHF and door as the objective: yes.
  - Dawn call to Pike by name: yes.
  - Ned as an accepted risk, with the reason visible: yes.
  - Icom on scan, no transmitting: yes.
  - The kept lines ("If it was him, it wasn't her," the church, the heel): yes.
  - Notebook retelling shortened: largely. The notebook is quoted entry by entry in Ch 07 and walked again in Ch 08 ("She went past her kitchen door and the Ashby key and the landing, back to Monday"). That is acceptable.
- **Open problems:** The fish-house deduction undercuts both her reason and Ch 08's beat (Finding 12). She leaves the bar off a wounded sleeping man's door, which is never weighed.
- **Safer options:**
  - Stay barred in the fish house and confront him at daylight. Named and rejected on feeling. Credible, with a cost.
  - Wake him and ask. Rejected: "she couldn't check an explanation." Credible.
  - Take the Iridium and call Pike herself. Closed by "None of it was hers," and because Portland might be the leak. Credible.

### Day 8: the Ch 09 trap
- **Who knew, by design:**
  - Tess, on channel 9.
  - The man beside her (Arsenault).
  - Half the island's scanners ("Only Tess would act on it").
  - Pike, by the call before the plan and the call after it.

The one person left who could put Arsenault in that store was the woman reading the roll." The evidence-to-conclusion chain is on the page. It arrives after the plan's pieces were set (Finding 7).
- **Safer options:** Boats are closed by Pike. Aircraft, interception at sea and Beth's own presence aboard are not (Finding 2).
  - Russ going to the front door now: closed ("If Russ walks up to that front door alone, he's the thing that gets her killed").
  - Blue lights: closed.
  - Waiting for first light: closed by the sailing.
- **Credibility risks:** Pike's sign-off (Finding 5) and the floods (Finding 8).
- **Brief C2 compliance:**
  - Beth's admission (in order, in register): yes.
  - The emergency-management gap: yes.
  - The binoculars at three: yes.
  - The roll: yes, though the roll is newly invented (Finding 16).
  - Pike's call on the page before the plan: yes.
  - Pike's answer closing the safer options: partly (Finding 2).
  - Pike "does not authorize bait": departed from (Finding 5).
