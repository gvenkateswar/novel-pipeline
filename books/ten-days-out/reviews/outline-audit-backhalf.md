# Outline audit: back half (Ch 10–18)

*Plot auditor, Nov 5, 2026. Read: `outline.md` (all of it, including "Audit answers (back half)" and "For the author's next bible review"), `reviews/backhalf-change-note.md`, `reviews/rebuild-brief-v4.md`, `continuity-log.md` (Ch 01–09), `genre-maps/romance.md`, `chapters/chapter-08/final.md`, `chapters/chapter-09/final.md`, and the cold-reader notes for Ch 06–09. ## Blocking

### Finding 1: the night in the house above the kid's cove
**Where:** Ch 10 s3–s4. "a shut house up the hill above the cove, night… They light the stove." Heat #3 follows. The kid "gets into the spruce" with a pistol, minutes earlier and a few hundred yards away.
**Question failed:** 4 (safer options) and 5 (does the choice follow from what he knows). It also breaks the competence ask.
**The problem:** An armed man who just pistol-whipped Dane is loose in the trees above his own cove. Dane takes Beth into a house over that same cove, lights a stove, and they make love. Nothing in the outline says anyone keeps watch, why they don't walk back, or why the kid won't come back for his rifle and phone. In Ch 04 Dane "sits up facing the door, gun in reach." Here the outline gives him no such beat. A reader who was just told "Dane is good at his job" will stop believing it at this exact point. Three safer options are left open: (a) walk back to the village or the town office, (b) go back to the lighthouse, which only the now-cleared boss knows, (c) hole up but stand watch, with no sex.
**What the architect must answer:** why this house tonight, and what Dane does about the kid.
**Suggested fix:** in s3, one line each. The storm hits as they climb, and the shore path back is two hours in the dark, along rocks the kid can shoot down onto. The kid went east and inland, bleeding, away from the cove ("he won't come back to a boat with a hole in it, toward a man who's got his rifle"). Dane wedges the one door and puts the rifle by the bed, and the bait in Ch 11 will need them right here. Put the watch in s4 as part of the heat: he won't lie with his back to the door, she turns him so he can see it, and that becomes something that belongs to the two of them. Keep it out of the lines that lead into the scene.

### Finding 2: why not take the kid's skiff off the island
**Where:** Ch 10 s2. "the kid is dragging his skiff down the stones one-armed, to run for Pruett while the sea's still down… puts a rock through the bottom of the skiff." The answer exists only in the audit table: "Nobody can run a skiff to Pruett in the second storm."
**Question failed:** 4.
**The problem:** On the page, the kid is about to do exactly what the audit says can't be done. Beth is the best boat-handler on the island, and the sea is "still down for maybe an hour." The reader's question is immediate: take the skiff, get her across tonight, and the book is over. The scene as outlined never asks it, and the audit answer contradicts the kid's own action.
**What the architect must answer:** on the page, why Beth won't take it when the kid would.
**Suggested fix:** one exchange in s2, in Beth's expertise, before the rock goes through the hull. She looks at the sky to the south: "He'd have been swamped by the ledges. An hour across is the ferry. In that, it's three, and the storm's here in one." / "He was going to try." / "He's got nothing to lose." That also makes the kid more desperate, which helps Finding 6.

### Finding 3: Russ knocks on an armed man's door
**Where:** Ch 13 s1–s2. "Russ is at the front with his shotgun, to make the noise… Russ knocks, the kid fires through the front door and hits him in the thigh."
**Question failed:** 4 and 5. It also breaks the competence ask.
**The problem:** Dane plans the raid. Standing a man square in front of a closed door with an armed man behind it is the textbook mistake, and anyone who watches police television knows it. As outlined, Russ's wound is the plan's fault, so it is Dane's fault. This chapter is meant to be Dane winning on skill.
**What the architect must answer:** whether Russ is shot because Dane planned it badly, or despite a good plan.
**Suggested fix:** Dane tells Russ to bang on the door from the side, behind the stone of the chimney or the woodpile. Pick one of two versions and put it on the page. (a) The kid fires through the wall beside the door, where nobody could have known the boards were thin. (b) Russ steps square into the doorway to shout the kid's name, because he has known the kid two summers and wants to talk him out. That is Russ's own choice, emotionally credible, and it costs him. (b) is better, because it gives Russ a want in a book that has used him as plumbing.

### Finding 4: no escort at sea, when they know a boat is waiting
**Where:** Ch 15 s2. "Marine Patrol's boats can't lie against a pier with no float in what's left of this sea." The Coast Guard "won't come for a boat that hasn't done anything."
**Question failed:** 4.
**The problem:** By Ch 15 the reader has been told twice that a boat is waiting (Ch 13 "They've got a boat"; Ch 14 s3, seen at dawn). The threat is at sea, but the Marine Patrol line only closes the pier. Nothing closes the obvious safer option: meet the ferry outside the harbor mouth and escort it across. A federal witness transport with a known hostile vessel lying off is precisely the case where a marshal's boss asks for an escort. A reader at 84% asks this before the climax, and if it isn't closed the climax reads as arranged.
**What the architect must answer:** why nobody escorts the ferry.
**Suggested fix:** use a fact already on the page. The Marine Patrol boat on this stretch is the one the kid holed on Tuesday (Ch 09: "He's put one through the side! We're taking water!"), and the state won't send the next nearest one out at night into what's left of the storm after an officer was shot. Add the Coast Guard line. Say it in Ch 14 s3, where the reader first asks (see Finding 16), and restate it in one line in Ch 15.

### Finding 5: Ch 10 runs the Ch 04 / Ch 07 template a third time
**Where:** Ch 10 s3–s4. "Beth knows whose house it is and which pane to break. They light the stove… She cleans his scalp cut first, bare-handed, gloves off." Then heat #3.
**Question failed:** shape (the author's "the book was looping" ask; brief item 10, "every chapter changes the situation so the next chapter can't be the same kind of chapter").
**The problem:** The cold reader on Ch 07 named this sequence word for word: "Break into a shut building. She makes it warm. He's hurt and she tends him. They have sex… The danger is starting to read as the thing that gets them into bed." Ch 04 is the loft, the heater, his arm taped, heat #1. Ch 07 is the keeper's house through the window that doesn't lock, warming him, heat #2. Ch 10 is a shut house through a broken pane, the stove, his cut cleaned, heat #3. The chapter's first half is new (the hunt). Its second half is the old loop, and its gloves-off detail is a tic the Ch 06 reader already listed. The rebuild exists to stop exactly this.
**What the architect must answer:** what makes heat #3 a different kind of scene from #1 and #2, outside its tenderness.
**Suggested fix:** pick at least two of these. (a) Reverse the care: she is the one hurt (the kid's pistol came up two steps from her, and she went down on the stones; her knee or her hand is cut), and Dane tends her with the competence the book has been withholding. (b) No breaking in: the house is one she has a reason to own (she has its key from closing it up for the summer people), so the entry is not a repeat. (c) She starts it at the door, out of the fear, before the stove is lit, so warmth and first aid aren't the path into bed. Drop "gloves off" here, and keep the bare-hands plant for Ch 13 (Russ's leg), where it pays.

## Non-blocking, ordered by what they would cost the reader

### Finding 6: two chapters with no physical danger, at 59–70%
**Where:** Ch 11 ("the wait in the kid's own cove") and Ch 12 ("the lawyer's threat to Ned's son, beaten on the page"). Brief item 4: "Something bad actually happens at least every second chapter: an attack, a fire, someone hurt, a near-capture."
**The problem:** After the cove fight (Ch 10 s2, ~55%), the next physical event is the shot through Beth's door (Ch 13 s2, ~72%). In between come the texts, heat, writing the bait, the wait, the confrontation, a walk with three answers, a phone call and a fight. That is about 6,000 words of people talking in rooms. Ch 12's danger is a text message about a man in Portland, solved by phone in eleven minutes: that is offstage news, which the "it has to move" rule bars as a danger beat. Ch 11 s1 (writing the bait) and s2 (the wait, his boat) are two talk scenes in a row. The kid, "hurt and phoneless," is loose with a pistol through all of it and does nothing on the page.
**Suggested fix:** put the kid on the page in Ch 12 s1. On the east road at first light, walking a cuffed Ned, the kid fires from the trees: one shot, wild, one-armed. Dane gets Beth and Ned down and returns fire, and the kid breaks off inland toward the village. That gives Ch 12 a physical beat, shows why the kid ends up at Beth's house (it's the way he ran), keeps "the kid is weaker than us" true, and lets Ned's three answers come out of a ditch, under fear, instead of on a walk. In Ch 11 s2, give the wait one false alarm before the lantern: a sound down at the wrecked skiff, the risk Dane named in s1, and they hold still. The architect's own pacing flag 2 asks for "more dread in s2."

### Finding 7: Ch 14's parting rope is the third pull-her-clear
**Where:** Ch 14 s2. "A rope parts under the strain… Dane gets her down flat a breath before the loose end whips across where she stood."
**The problem:** Ch 02 has a ferry rope parting in the storm at this pier, and Ned hauls Beth clear. Ch 08 has Dane catching Beth by the coat on the walkway. Ch 14 has a ferry rope parting in the storm at this pier, and Dane pulls Beth clear. The architect calls it an echo on purpose, but the beat is the same move on the same boat. A reader whose main complaint was the loop will hear it as a loop. The competence beat it is meant to carry (Dane is "good at exactly this") gets spent on a reflex grab, not on the skill he claims.
**Suggested fix:** let Dane's two summers on the lobster boat show as skill, not rescue. He sees the rope going before she does and calls it, or he takes the last line off under load his way, with a knife at the right moment, and she watches him do it right. If the rope burns have to stay (they carry to Ch 17), she earns them by holding the last line one second too long because she won't lose the boat. That's her choice and her cost, with nobody pulling her anywhere.

### Finding 8: the boss on speaker is becoming the new loop
**Where:** Ch 10 s3 ("Dane calls his boss from the house's one bar, in front of Beth"), Ch 12 s2 ("Dane gets his boss on speaker, in front of all of them"), Ch 12 s3 ("The boss, on speaker"), Ch 14 s3 ("with his boss on speaker"), Ch 15 s2 ("The boss, in one line each"). The front half has it in Ch 02, 03, 05 and 08.
**The problem:** That's five speaker calls in six chapters. The outline uses them to prove "no misunderstandings," but the cold reader on Ch 08 called the boss's briefing "a briefing being read to me." The device now carries logistics in Ch 15 s2 and does the same job in four other scenes.
**Suggested fix:** keep two on the page: Ch 12 s3 (the rupture) and Ch 14 s3 (the reversal, on the record). Reduce Ch 10 s3 to one reported line ("She said keep it on"). In Ch 12 s2, Dane calls Portland police directly, and the eleven minutes play on Ned's face, not on a speaker. Cut Ch 15 s2's call and fold its facts into Ch 14 s3 (Finding 16).

### Finding 9: Ch 14's reversal can read as Dane giving in again
**Where:** Ch 14 s3. "he decided her life for her because he was scared, and he called it his job… 'She doesn't go into the program unless she says so.'"
**The problem:** The Ch 08 cold reader: "His pattern is to object, say 'Fair,' and give in." In Ch 14 the danger has just grown (the boat is lying off), and Dane drops his position. If he also drops his belief, the reader sees the pattern again and the rupture looks like it was never adult. The genre map asks for the repair to be "a conversation between equals followed by an action."
**Suggested fix:** in his words, keep his view and give up the decision: "I still think you should go. It was never mine to say." His action is the Maine office request, already in the outline. Let her answer with the risk priced in, so neither one capitulates.

### Finding 10: the lawyer's text implies a channel the outline denies
**Where:** Ch 12 s2, the lawyer's text: *the kid isnt answering ned.* Audit answers: "The kid has no other phone." Ch 10 s3: "four texts from one number."
**Question failed:** 2.
**The problem:** If the lawyer has been trying the kid, he has been trying the phone in Dane's pocket, which "Keep it on" leaves switched on. Dane would have seen those calls. Either the kid's phone shows a second number, or the lawyer reaches the kid some other way, and the outline says neither. A related gap: in s2 the kid's phone is "lit in his hand" at the skiff. Who was he calling?
**Suggested fix:** the kid's phone has a second thread. A second number has called it four times since the beach and never texted. That's who the kid was trying at the skiff, and nobody answered. On Wednesday that same number texts Ned's phone, and Dane matches the two numbers on the town-office floor. That's another competence beat, and it's evidence against the lawyer (see Finding 14).

### Finding 11: where did Ned get a hidden phone on a cut-off island?
**Where:** Ch 12 s1. "Cole's lawyer called me the morning after, on that phone." / "I called Cole from the boat before you'd got your phone out."
**Question failed:** 2.
**The problem:** Ned uses the hidden phone on the boat on Thursday night, before the lawyer's first call, and the island is cut off from Friday morning. The outline never says where the phone came from.
**Suggested fix:** one clause in Ned's first answer: it's the phone Cole gave him two years ago for the night runs, the only number Cole ever called him on.

### Finding 12: Dane answers the phone against a direct order
**Where:** Ch 10 s3, the boss: "Keep it on. Don't answer it yet." Ch 11 s1: they write the bait and send it at half past two.
**The problem:** It's on the page that the boss said don't answer. Half an hour later Dane runs a lure operation with his witness as the bait, and the outline doesn't say whether he told anyone. A reader taking stock of Dane's competence will mark this as him going rogue, or as the book forgetting.
**Suggested fix:** change the boss's line to "Keep it on. If it talks to you, your call." Or have Dane text her one line before he sends the bait ("Answering it. Will call at six."). Either one keeps him a professional.

### Finding 13: why not just ring the number at each door?
**Where:** Audit answers: "Calling it only helps if you're standing next to whoever has it. That's the trap."
**Question failed:** 4 (a minor case).
**The problem:** The bait isn't the only way to stand next to the suspects. Dane could walk to Tess's, Russ's and Ned's doors in the morning and dial. As outlined, that's safer for Beth and quicker.
**Suggested fix:** one line in Ch 11 s1. Dane rang the number at two-fifteen, and it was off. The leak's phone is only ever on to send or to wait for an answer (that's the plant "off between texts"). The only way to get it switched on, in a pocket, in a room with them, is to give the leak something to answer. The bait does that.

### Finding 14: the lawyer walks free from Wednesday to Monday
**Where:** Ch 12 s2 (the threatening text, the son saved); Ch 17 s1 ("The deputies arrest him on the kid's sworn word").
**The problem:** By Wednesday the boss knows that Cole's lawyer threatened a cooperating witness's son. A reader will ask why he's free to stand at the courthouse door on Monday.
**Suggested fix:** one line from the boss in Ch 14 s3: a text from a phone in nobody's name, plus the word of a man in cuffs who ran drugs for two years, isn't enough to arrest a lawyer. The kid's sworn statement in Ch 16 is the second witness that is, and that's why the arrest is Monday.

### Finding 15: Dane's talking-through-it tic is spent before Ch 16 needs it
**Where:** Ch 10 s2 ("Dane reads it out loud as they go"; "Dane comes down the rocks talking"); Ch 13 s2 ("He talks to the kid the whole time, specific and calm"); Ch 16 s1 (the radio talk Beth steers by; "You stopped talking").
**The problem:** The Ch 06, 08 and 09 cold reads all flagged the trait as "a template" and "a macro." Ch 16's payoff depends on the reader still hearing his talk as his, and on its stopping being a shock. Two more full uses before Ch 16 wear it down.
**Suggested fix:** in Ch 10 s2, Dane reads the trail in silence and shows her with his hand, and she says the words. That's a new shape for them, and it plants silence as the thing he can do when it matters. In Ch 13 s2, he says two lines to the kid, not a running commentary. Keep "Talk to me" in Ch 11 as the one time she asks for it.

### Finding 16: the Ch 15 logistics are answered a day late
**Where:** Ch 15 s2: the boss's three lines (Coast Guard, Marine Patrol, Cole walks), then the search and the loading list.
**The problem:** The reader asks "who deals with that boat?" at dawn on Saturday (Ch 14 s3), when they first see it. Answering it on Sunday night makes Ch 15 s2 open on exactly the logistics the "it has to move" rule bars. It also leaves the threat sitting idle and unexamined for about 36 hours of story time.
**Suggested fix:** Dane calls it in from the car deck the minute they see the boat (Ch 14 s3), and the Coast Guard and Marine Patrol answers land there, in front of her, at the moment of most fear. Ch 15 s2 then opens on the search, already under way. Give Ch 15 s1 one look out the town-office window: the light still out there, under the ham comedy, so the ordinary scene has a floor of dread.

### Finding 17: a one-hour crossing takes six hours
**Where:** Calendar: cast off at ten, land at Pruett "about four a.m." "Pruett, an hour across on a normal night." Ch 16 runs "from about eleven."
**The problem:** On a normal night they'd have landed before Ch 16 opens. A reader who has the hour in their head will notice.
**Suggested fix:** one line in Ch 15 s2 or Ch 16 s1. In what's left of the sea she can make a crawl, it's three hours at best, and the chase drives her the long way, inside the ledges and round the point.

### Finding 18: how does a grand juror know about the relationship?
**Where:** Ch 17 s1. "A juror asks whether she's involved with the deputy who protected her."
**Question failed:** 2.
**The problem:** Defense counsel isn't in a grand jury room, and the lawyer's taunt at the door wasn't heard by the jurors. A stranger asking this out of nowhere makes a reader ask how they knew.
**Suggested fix:** the prosecutor asks it, because the defense will raise it at trial and she wants it on the record first. Beth answers it anyway, at the same cost. Route the fact through the domain-checker.

### Finding 19: the suspect list gets argued twice in Ch 10, on an answer the reader already has
**Where:** Ch 10 s1 ("one exchange about Ned, to their faces") and Ch 10 s3 (the texts; "It's one of mine").
**Question failed:** 9.
**The problem:** The cold reader had Ned from Ch 07 ("My guess, from the page, is Ned"). By Ch 09's confession, every reader does. That puts the reader at least two chapters, and for the cold reader four, ahead of Beth before the Ch 11 reveal. The architect accepts this ("the question at the door is how, not who"), and the accepted risk is fine. The cost is that Ch 10 gives two passages to the who-knew conversation, which the Ch 09 reader called the loop: "the same conversation as chapter 6's… with one more name added."
**Suggested fix:** cap s1's Ned exchange at four lines and keep the hunt argument whole. In s3, give the texts to the reader on the page and let Dane's deduction be two sentences. Everything after "It isn't my side" belongs to Beth's face, not to logic.

### Finding 20: "a boat with no lights" now names two boats
**Where:** Ch 01 (the kid "leaves by a boat with no lights," his skiff); Ch 13–16 ("the boat with no lights," the big boat).
**The problem:** A listener will merge them. It's either a false clue (that the Ch 01 boat was the big one) or plain confusion about which boat is which. The brief is written for a half-listening reader.
**Suggested fix:** the big one is "the big boat," or "the boat showing one light," every time. Keep "no lights" for the kid's skiff only.

### Finding 21: smoke at dusk from a careful man
**Where:** Ch 12 end ("smoke is rising from her own chimney" at dusk). Ch 10 sets the rule: "smoke can't be seen in a storm at night."
**The problem:** The kid washes his dishes and has picked unwatched houses for a week. Lighting a stove before full dark breaks the rule the book just taught.
**Suggested fix:** one line in Ch 13 s1, in Dane's head. The kid lit it a half hour early, because he's been bleeding for a day and a night and the cold is winning. That's how Dane knows he's weaker.

### Finding 22: the testimony retells Ch 01
**Where:** Ch 17 s1. "In the room, her first words to strangers, plain: the silent truck, the bricks, Cole in the cab."
**The problem:** This is the one scene in the book that is, by design, a character going over what the reader already knows. The rule bars that.
**Suggested fix:** in one sentence, the first words are said and the narration moves on. The scene's event is the juror question (or the prosecutor's, Finding 18) and what saying yes costs her.

### Finding 23: small errors a careful reader will catch
- Ch 10 s3: "four texts from one number," with three listed. If the fourth is the two a.m. *where r u*, it arrives after the scene. Fix the count or the list. The numbers ledger says "four on the page."
- Ch 17 s2: "That pays 'Ask me in five days,' seven days late." It's seven days after (Mon 16 to Mon 23), so two days late.
- Ch 16 s3: "everyone he has left is aboard." Ned's son is alive in Portland (Ch 12 s2). Change it to "everyone he has wronged is aboard," or cut it.
- Ch 14 s3: the boat is seen "off the back of the island" from the car deck in the middle of the harbor. Put it off the point, visible past the harbor mouth.
- Ch 16 s2: "Ned, cuffed at her shoulder." The outline never says who put the prisoner in the wheelhouse with the witness. One line: Beth asked for him at the ledges, and Dane agreed to her face, which is also a one-talk beat.
- Ch 15 s2: Dane puts a life jacket on Ned. Give the cuffed kid one too, or a reader marks it against Dane.
- Ch 17–18: one line that a trial is still ahead (spring) and she'll testify again. Otherwise Beth's Ch 09 "After Monday nobody needs me dead" is left standing, and the rupture's own argument says it's wrong.

## The author's specific questions

**Does the story move, scene by scene?** Every scene has an Event line, and most of them are real.

The weak scenes:
- **Ch 11 s1–s2:** two talk scenes in a row (Finding 6).
- **Ch 12 s1:** answers given on a walk. It's new information, not recap, but it's talk (Finding 6's fix gives it fire).
- **Ch 15 s2:** opens on logistics (Finding 16).
- **Ch 17 s1:** the testimony as retelling (Finding 22).
- **Ch 10 s3:** deduction on an answer the reader already has (Finding 19).

**Do any two chapters in a row share a shape?** No. Hunt, trap, reckoning, raid, storm, supper and departure, chase, courthouse, table: each is a different kind of chapter.

The repeats are not between neighbours:
- Ch 10's second half repeats Ch 04 and Ch 07 (Finding 5, blocking).
- Ch 14's rope repeats Ch 02 and Ch 08 (Finding 7).
- The speaker call recurs throughout (Finding 8).
- Ch 10 and Ch 11 share one room for about 3,000 words. Their shapes differ, but the room needs a different look in Ch 11. Light: the stove is out, and it's black in there.
- The architect's own flag about Ch 10 and Ch 13 (both hand to hand with the kid) holds. Differ the moves.

**Is Dane visibly competent?**
- **On the page:** he wins the Ch 10 argument, takes the rifle, phone and skiff, clears his office from the texts, builds the bait, disarms Ned at the door, matches numbers (if Finding 10 is taken), saves the son, takes the kid alive, crews the ropes, and holds the big boat off.
- **Holes:** the night above the cove with no watch (Finding 1), Russ at the door (Finding 3), answering against orders (Finding 12), the reversal reading as giving in (Finding 9), and the rope rescue standing in for skill (Finding 7).
