# Sample read: Ten Days Out, synopsis v4 + Chapter 1 test v4 (round 2)

**Drafts read:** `reviews/sample-synopsis-v4.md` (synopsis revision 2, Ch 1–3) and `chapters/chapter-01/test-v4.md` (Chapter One: The Last Truck). Nothing else opened.

**Persona (from orchestrator):** buys steamy romantic suspense, reads on her phone, mostly Kindle Unlimited, tired of books sold as steamy that turn out to be slow burns.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

I finished the sample.

**Warning, ranked above the score: I'd keep reading, but.** The synopsis says Chapter 2 is her refusing the boat, a storm, a rescue, and a hug that goes "You can let go." "I know." That's the slow-burn stall I'm tired of. If Chapter 2 reads the way the synopsis does, that's where I quit. Chapter 1 earned me opening Chapter 2. It didn't earn me finishing it.

## 2. Buy: yes

Yes, as a KU borrow. The truck going up the ramp silent and the flashlight following her footprints got me in, and the synopsis promises a kiss in Ch 3 and sex in Ch 4. I would not pay $4.99 on this sample.

## 4. Is the romance obvious by the end of Ch 1? Do I care about these two?

**Obvious: yes.** "Her eyes went to his mouth and stayed there a second too long, and then came back up to his, and she didn't look sorry about it at all." Also his: "he wanted, very badly and very unprofessionally, to find out what her mouth would do if she ever actually smiled."

**Care: yes, a little more about her than him.** She goes out the window in her socks and then mocks his boots, and I like her. He's mostly a nice guy with a good joke. I don't know what he wants besides doing his job and getting her to smile.

## 5. Did anyone make me laugh?

Yes. "You're an ice cube with opinions." And "Who told you that?" / "The government."

## 6. Did anyone make me want?

A bit. "Her face was six inches from his. He could feel her breathing against his chest, fast and shallow." It's warm, not hot. For a book sold as steamy, Chapter 1 is a look at a mouth and a borrowed coat.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. **~5%:** "Beth Calloway had chained down every vehicle on the deck since she was twenty-two. Eleven years of Thursdays. Cars, a plumber's van, the propane truck, and last, always last..." I skimmed the list. "Knew its sound better than she knew her own voice" is the kind of line I've read a hundred times.
2. **~30%:** "She almost didn't. When the bank took her father's boat, a man in a good suit had written down everything about him in blue pen. She'd hated being written down ever since." A man just watched her see the drugs. I don't need the bank right now.
3. **~90%:** "Dane didn't answer. He was thinking the same thing, and worse. Only the state police and his own office in Portland had known she'd called." Plus the closing "somebody in an office he trusted had given that man her name." It spells out the mystery for me instead of letting me feel it.

Also: reading the synopsis first and then Chapter 1 meant I read every good beat twice. It took the edge off the oar scene.

## 8. Who leaked her name, and how sure

**Prime suspect: the state trooper who took her call.** He went from bored to wide awake, put her on hold for nine minutes, and "when he came back his voice had changed." That's the only phone in the chapter, and the book lingers on it. I'm about **50%** sure.

Second: someone in Dane's Portland office, because he says so at the end. About 25%. Third: Russ the constable, in Chapter 3. A fake emergency call pulls him off the porch and twenty minutes later the window gets shot in. That's too convenient. About 15%, at least for Chapter 3. Ned is scared, not crooked, but he knows more than he's saying about Cole.

A reader nitpick that made me doubt the whole leak: the hoodie guy watched her see the bricks and watched her walk up to the captain. Cole's people didn't need anyone to leak her name to know she was a problem. Unless the point is that they knew she *called*, Dane's "somebody gave that man her name" didn't feel earned. "Nobody wants to hurt you" doesn't tell me either way.

## What I want to know next

- Who on the phone line or in the office sold her out, and whether Dane works it out before the next attempt.
- Whether Ned is in on it or just afraid of Cole.
- Mostly: when do they actually touch with intent. The synopsis says Chapter 3 and Chapter 4. I'm holding the book to that. If Chapter 2 is the storm and a long hug, I'm gone before the kitchen.
