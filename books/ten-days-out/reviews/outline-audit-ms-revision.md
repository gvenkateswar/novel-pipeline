## Blocking items

### B1. Under the override, Dane still keeps the hold from her for a night, and Ch 11 becomes a POV cheat
**Where:** override; calendar row "Tue Nov 17, about midnight … Dane texts the hold request"; "What the rupture is now: The act" ("at the dentist's kitchen window"); Ch 11 (Dane's POV); Ch 12 s4.
**Question failed:** 10, on the central rupture. The genre map rules out "a lead who 'decides not to tell' to protect the other." Question 5 also fails, for Dane.
**The problem.**
- The override drops the Ch 11 plant. It says Dane tells Beth "last night he asked his boss," so the request is still sent Tuesday at midnight. He tells her Wednesday. That leaves about twelve hours in which he knows and she doesn't. Those hours cover all of Ch 11, which is told from inside his head.
- In those hours he builds the bait with her and lets her lie on the daybed while "The blade came out over Beth." He tells her about his boat in the dark, and "under every word was how much he loved her." He never once thinks of the text he sent two hours earlier. A reader who learns in Ch 12 that it was already sent will feel the Ch 11 narration lied to them.
- The motive breaks in the same hours. Dane asked for the hold at midnight because he could no longer stand her spending her own life. At half past two he put her on the daybed as bait, and the Ch 11 page says "You're right, which I'm going to hold against you."
- A one-night silence is still a decision not to tell. It doesn't drive the fight, but it puts a secret back into the rupture, and the override exists to keep secrets out.

**Fix (recommended).** Move the act to Wednesday morning, in the town office, after the son is reached. The boss's own line, "A lawyer who can find a man's son in a morning can find a woman on an island," is the trigger. Dane texts the request from the window while Beth is at the back-room door with Ned. Before the boss rings back, he tells her: what he asked for, and why ("You'd have heard it from a judge on Monday. You hear it from me."). Then strike every Tuesday trace:
- the calendar row;
- "at the dentist's window, after the cove";
- "I didn't turn the screen round";
- Beth's "you'd already sent it";
- Open question 1;
- the Plants row "The hold | Ch 11 s1 (the unturned screen)".

Ch 11 is then clean as written. Ch 15's "He had never once lied to her" (3251) also holds.

**Alternative, not recommended.** Keep Tuesday. Restore one open line in Ch 11 in Dane's head: what he sent, and that he'll tell her at first light. Beth then throws the bait at him in Ch 12 ("You put me on that daybed two hours after you sent it"). This keeps a one-night secret, so it needs the orchestrator's explicit sign-off against rebuild-brief item 8.

### B2. Beth has three safer ways to undo the hold, and the page closes none of them
**Where:** Ch 12 s4. The one-talk sentence says: "No conversation can undo a request filed with the prosecutor. Only he can."
**Question failed:** 4 (why doesn't the safer option work), for Beth. Question 10 fails too: one call might dissolve it.
**The problem.** The rupture holds only if the hold can't be undone without Dane. As planned, Beth has three ways round him, and an adult would try at least one on the page:
1. **Speak to the boss directly.** The boss is on speaker in the room. In Ch 14 Beth does exactly this ("Say it to me"). In Ch 12 she only says "Cancel it" to Dane.
2. **Tell the prosecutor herself.** She'll be alone with the prosecutor on Monday morning, before the motion is made at two. A prosecutor who "wants you in the room" won't want to jail her own star witness against her will in front of a defense lawyer.
3. **Use her leverage.** She is the whole case. "Withdraw it or I don't get on the ferry" ends the hold in one sentence.

The wording doesn't help. The amendment says the prosecutor "will ask" on Monday at two. The override says the boss "confirms it's filed." If it's only a request held by the boss, then Dane's "No conversation can undo" is false on the page.

**Fix (three lines, Ch 12 s4).**
- The boss, when Beth speaks to the phone: "It rests on my deputy's word that you'll be killed. Only his word takes it back. I don't overrule the man on the ground." That makes "Only he can" true, and it is what makes Ch 14's "Withdraw it" / "Written" work.
- Beth names her leverage and refuses it: "I could say I won't go in Monday till it's gone. I won't. I'd go in if you had me in chains." This puts her values on the page and closes option 3.
- Make the wording consistent: "the prosecutor's agreed to ask," not "filed."

### B3. Mercer's channel for "you've got a man shot" doesn't exist on the page at early afternoon on Thursday

- Ch 14 2777: "Thursday night Tess had swapped the strap for a tight bandage, as the Coast Guard told her on the radio."

When Mercer speaks his line, nothing has gone out on the island radio. The same plant is supposed to carry Ch 15 ("Every boat hears the island radio").
**Fix.** In Ch 13 s3, on the step, put Tess on the fire truck's radio to the Coast Guard in one line, in the open: "Cutter fire, we've got a man shot in the thigh, strap's on." Keep one clause in Ch 14 saying the radio told her how to swap the strap.

### B4. The "Captain"/"June" deduction is contradicted by the dialogue the plan writes for it
**Where:** Ch 13 s5.
**Questions failed:** 1 and 2.
**The page (as planned):**
- "Mercer, to Beth: 'You'll be the captain.' / 'Not till June.' / 'June. I'll call you Captain anyway.'"
- Dane's thought: "Mercer has been listening to Tess's radio. He knew about June, which only her people knew."

**The problems.**

**Fix.** Mercer says it unprompted: "Captain. Come June, anyway." Dane's thought becomes: only Ned and Cole had June in September, so this man is Cole's. Keep the radio thought for the shot man only (B3).

### B5. The Thursday "lull between storms" reopens why nobody flies out
**Where:** Ch 13 s5 set-up ("Thursday early afternoon, the lull between storms"); Mercer "Bare-headed in the cold," landing a skiff and walking in; Ch 13 2699 ("We'll fly when the icing clears"); Ch 14 s3 ("Nothing flies before Tuesday").
**Question failed:** 4 (why didn't they fly out or call for help).
**The problem.** A lull in daylight, calm enough for a skiff to put a man ashore, is the window a reader expects the helicopter to use. It could take Russ, who is shot in the thigh on a strap, and Beth with him. The plan promises the helicopter "when the icing clears" and then shows weather that looks clear.
**Fix.** No lull. Mercer lands in the lee of the point in snow that hasn't stopped. One clause in Dane's head: the icing is in the cloud, not on the water, and a skiff in the lee isn't a helicopter in that ceiling. Or have the Coast Guard say so when Dane rings at noon.

### B6. With a named skipper off the point since Thursday, the escort question is open again, and "broke nothing" fails once his name is checked
**Where:** Ch 14 s3, the boss's replacement line; the plot-logic row "Why nobody boards the supply boat (F7)"; Ch 17 item 5 ("no Hal Mercer on any license out of Gloucester").
**Question failed:** 4 (why not call for help; why not the Coast Guard).
**The problems.**
- **The escort.** The new boss line drops the old clauses "The Coast Guard's out on the storm's distress calls" and "If it comes at you, call them, and they'll come from an hour off." On Sunday night a Coast Guard boat does reach them in about an hour (Ch 15, 3211). So a boat can work in that sea. With a photographed man and his boat lying off the island for three days, a reader asks why nobody stations one at the harbor mouth for a ten o'clock crossing.
- **The name.** Dane sends the name and photo on Thursday. The page doesn't reveal that the name is false until the following Thursday (Ch 17). A one-name license check takes minutes. Once the boss knows the name is false, a skipper who "broke nothing" has lied to a federal officer, and "laying in out of the weather" stops being a reason not to board.

**Fix.**
- Restore the clause, inside the boss's one plain line: "The Coast Guard's two boats on this stretch are on the storm's distress calls. If that boat comes at you, that's a distress, and they'll come, from an hour off. Not before."
- Move the license fact to Saturday: "The name comes back to nobody. In this sea that still doesn't put a team aboard." The sea is then the only closure, and it's one the reader can see.
- In Ch 17 keep only the Book 2 echo, the gold tooth.

### B7. The planted phone's job is contradicted by the climax, and Dane's "nothing put off from the boat" has no source
**Where:** Ch 14 s5 ("From the point they can see the pier all day. In the dark they can't see it leave."; "nothing put off from the big boat"); Ch 15 3153; Ch 15 item 4 ("came in blind on her lights"); Ch 17 3893.
**Question failed:** 2 (how did the information travel), and the series-hook rule (a hook the reader can repeat).
**The page:**

- Ch 15: the big boat follows "on her lights."
- 3095: Dane switches the phone off about two hours before they cast off.

**The problems.**

**Fix (pick one, each one or two lines).**
1. Give the phone a job the eye can't do. For example, it tells them whether the ferry goes tonight at all, so the big boat can lie in the lee instead of sitting on station all night in a gale. Then make Dane switching it off the tell: "When it went dead, they knew." That costs him, and it explains how the boat is there at half past ten.
2. Or tie it to Mercer's walk (see N2). He carries it ashore on Thursday and hands it to someone on the point road. Someone on Cutter switches it on Saturday. That answers why he came ashore and sharpens the Book 2 "who".

In every case:
- Change "call log … first switched on" to "its first incoming call was Saturday, twenty to one" (or the carrier's activation text).
- Give Dane one line where Tess tells him what she watched through the binoculars.

## Non-blocking findings (by reader cost)

### N1. Ned's confession repeats his Ch 09 line word for word, and that line is now a lie Beth doesn't catch
- Ch 09 (kept): "Six years Cole's truck has come across on my deck, and I never once opened it. I'd guessed. I let myself not know."
- Ch 12 s1 (planned): "Two years I ran his trucks across in the dark. He gave me a phone for it." / "You knew." / "I guessed, and I let myself not know."

A man who ran the trucks alone at night for two years on a dedicated phone didn't guess. On the beach he told her he had only looked away. An adult in Beth's place says so. **Fix:** Beth: "On the beach you said you'd guessed." Ned: "On the beach I lied to you." Cut the repeated line. Add one clause on why a ferry crossing at night went unnoticed for two years on an island of 240 (for example, "Fuel runs, I told the co-op. Nobody asks the captain why he moves his own boat.").

### N2. Mercer's reason to come ashore is weaker than what it costs him
He gives a federal marshal his face, his voice, a name and a photo. In return he learns things the plan says he can get other ways:
- "the state of the ferry": Ch 14 s5 says "From the point they can see the pier all day";
- "who guards the town office": the radio and the lit window;
- whether the kid is alive.

A smuggler who stays anonymous offshore is safer. The threat "I'd wait for Tuesday" only makes the marshal surer. Three logistics also need a line each:
- **Distance.** The point is "an hour round by the road" (Ch 04), and Tess's truck takes half an hour out the point road (3281). "Dane follows to the top with the rifle" leaves Beth on the step for a long time. Either Dane watches from the step with Tess's binoculars, or someone holds the step.
- **The decoy risk.** A marshal who walks away from his witness behind a stranger is the classic decoy. Name it, and say why it's safe or who covers.
- **Adults ask.** Dane sees the kid turn his face to the wall and doesn't ask "Is that him?" Ask it, and let the kid say nothing.

**Fix:** give the walk a job only boots on the ground can do. Option 2 under B7 does it: he brings the phone ashore for the person on Cutter.

### N3. Dane lets Mercer see the prisoner
"Through the open door, the kid lies tied on the back-room floor." A marshal with a stranger at the step shuts that door. Either he does, and Mercer sees the kid only because the kid is moved past the window, or he doesn't and pays for it: Ch 15's "you and the boy" becomes his mistake, and he owns it in a line.

### N4. The repair is one-sided after the merge

Mercer has moved closer since. Let "It was never mine to say" be the reason and her seamanship the thing that made him see it, in that order.
- **Beth's accountability is cut.** The merge cuts old Ch 15's "Wednesday I said, 'And so do we.' For both of us. Same thing I hated you for." That is the only line in which Beth owns her half. Without it the repair is a man reversing himself while a woman sets terms. That is close to the grovel the genre map rules out. Restore it in Ch 14 s3, after her condition.

### N5. The Coast Guard loses a crawling, holed boat it has in sight

The replacement for 3361, "Its one light went out, and the snow came down between them," doesn't beat radar. **Fix:** the Coast Guard boat comes alongside the ferry first, because a man is shot aboard and that's the distress. By the time it turns, the dragger is gone into the snow. Then "looked all night and found nothing" (3547) follows.
