**Persona:** KU reader, 35 to 55, reads on her phone, three or four books a month. She reads open-door romantic suspense for the couple and the danger equally. She quits when the heat is all hints or the setup runs long, and two books like this have already bored her.

**Warning, above the scores:** I'd keep reading, but the Ch 03 store scene lost me. One scene scores 2. Under the pull gate that counts as a fail no matter what the average is.

## 6. Did anyone make me want?

Yes. At the razor: "That lasted until he turned his jaw to the light and ran his thumb along it to check, and she wanted it to be her thumb." Then the generator, where his cold hands go on her back, she swears into his mouth and he laughs against her teeth. That's where the heat finally landed. For an open-door reader it's still hands under a sweater at about 30% of the book, and it gets stopped. I'll accept one stop for a good reason. Two and I'm gone.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. **About 1%, Ch 01 opening:** "The co-op's bait truck smelled like a decision somebody had made about herring in August. The plumber had chained his own van, the way he billed, generously and with gaps, and its aft binder gave a quarter turn..." The herring line is good, but I didn't know what a binder was and skimmed to the dark reefer.
2. **About 25%, Ch 01 deck scene:** "She knew where everybody went: Russ was the *Second Wind*, Ned was the *North Reach*, and a man with no boat was from away, with nowhere on the island to set him down." Boat names for people I hadn't met. I skimmed it.
3. **About 80%, Ch 03 store:** "Tess yanked the yellow tag through the zipper of the morning's store bag so hard the bag jumped. 'Yellow's mine. Red's the boat's...'" I skimmed the ledger. I could tell it mattered and couldn't tell why. Then "Beth hadn't given him Ned in the dark, and she wasn't giving him Tess now" made me feel I'd missed something.

## 8. What I think is going on

Somebody on the island is running the money and somebody is leaking where Beth sleeps. The two may not be the same person. Ned sent her home the night of the reefer for the first time in eleven years, didn't ask about the crate, took both money bags, and knew she'd gone home the night the mudroom door went quiet. Now he's handed her the Ashby key, so he knows where she is again. Tess has hundreds in her vest, gets touchy when anyone reads her book, and saw the light on Beth's steps. I think Tess is laundering or covering for somebody, maybe Ned, and is more scared for Beth than guilty of hurting her. The other leak is Dane's phone. Every time he calls Portland, somebody turns up. I think his "Q" request is Homeland watching someone close to Beth, possibly Beth herself or her father's old business with the bank, and the log is about her. The hooded man is an islander who rows like he was born doing it. Arsenault's dragger going dark is a signal to somebody.

**Suspects, from the page only:**
- Ned: 35%. He knew she was home, he sent her home that first night, he took the bags, and he has the key.
- The leak is through Dane's Portland calls (Pike or someone above him): 25%.
- Tess: 20%. Money, the ledger, she saw the steps. More likely an accomplice than the one sending the hood.
- Arsenault, the *Margaret Ann*: 12%. He's too obvious, but the boat lights are cues.
- Dane himself: 5%.
- Russ: 3%.
