# Sample read: Ten Days Out, synopsis v4 + Chapter 1 test v4 (r1)

**Drafts read:** `reviews/sample-synopsis-v4.md` (synopsis of Ch 1–3, "revision 1") and `chapters/chapter-01/test-v4.md` (Chapter One: The Last Truck). Nothing else opened.

**Persona (given by orchestrator):** buyer of steamy romantic suspense, reads on her phone, mostly Kindle Unlimited, tired of books sold as steamy that turn out to be slow burns.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

Finished Chapter 1. It's short and the ferry opening grabbed me. But I stopped in the synopsis, at "Then he stops and tells her why: she's the only witness, and if Cole's lawyer can tell a jury the marshal was sleeping with her, they'll stop believing her." (Ch 3 paragraph, about 80% of the synopsis.) That's the line where I know what this book is: one kiss, then a speech about ethics, then ten days of "we can't." I've read that book four times this year.

## 2. Buy: no

I'd finish Chapter 1, but nothing here promises heat. The first kiss is in Chapter 3 and the hero stops it on principle, which is exactly the slow-burn bait-and-switch I'm tired of. And the sentences read like a clean adventure book, not a steamy one.

## 3. Pull score per Chapter 1 scene

- **Ferry / the truck (Beth): 4.** I wanted to know what the hoodie guy would do and why Ned was scared. The silent cooling unit is a good hook. The paragraph explaining the truck's sound I skimmed.
- **Kitchen call and the break-in (Beth): 3.** I wanted her out the window, and then I wanted to know who had the flashlight. The blue-pen-banker paragraph stopped me cold right when I wanted the scare. The break-in is tense, but "Beth didn't think" and "She ran" are things I've read a hundred times.
- **Shack (Dane): 3.** I wanted the two of them to touch for a reason other than hypothermia. The boot joke and "The government" are fun. Then it turns into feet-and-feelings first aid and a "you're leaving" / "no" standoff I could have written myself. The last line about the leak in his own office is the only thing that made me want Chapter 2, and it's a suspense hook, not a romance one.

Average 3.3.

## 4. Do I care about these two people yet?

Yes, a little. Beth calling it in anyway because of the boy she taught to row, then mocking his boots while she shakes, is a person. Dane is mostly a nice guy with a joke, and I don't know what he wants besides her.

## 5. Did anyone make me laugh?

Yes. "You're an ice cube with opinions." And "Who told you that?" / "The government."

## 6. Did anyone make me want?

One line: "he wanted, very badly and very unprofessionally, to find out what her mouth would do if she ever actually smiled." It's the only heat in the chapter, it's in his head, and it's about a smile. For a book sold as steamy, that's thin. The "neither of them wants to stop" in the Ch 3 synopsis is a promise, and the same sentence breaks it.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. "She almost didn't. When the bank took her father's boat, a man in a good suit had written down everything about him in blue pen. She'd hated being written down ever since." (about 30%) I skimmed it. It's backstory dropped into the middle of the scare.
2. "She knew its sound better than she knew her own voice. The cooling unit on its nose roared the whole hour across, so loud that Ned turned up the radio in the captain's cabin to talk over it." (about 3%) Truck explainer. I got it from "Tonight it came up the ramp silent."
3. "'Your feet. Can you feel them?' / 'Yes. They hurt.' / 'Good. That's good. Hurting means they're still yours.'" (about 80%) He's holding her and they talk about her toes. I wanted the hold to mean something.

## 8. What I think is going on

**Is the romance obvious by the end of Ch 1? Yes.** "He was in trouble. He knew it right then, standing in the snow holding an oar. He'd protected maybe forty witnesses, and he had never once wanted to make one of them laugh."

**Who leaked her name:** my first guess is the state trooper, or someone above him. He put her on hold for nine minutes "and when he came back his voice had changed," and the book points straight at state police plus the Portland office. About 40% sure. Ned is my close second, about 35%. He knew she'd seen it, he was scared of "those men," and the synopsis says he tells them something he's never told anyone, which reads like confessing he's been paying them off or covering for them. One snag: the hoodie guy saw her on the deck and it's a tiny island, so he didn't need a leak to come to her door. When Dane says someone in his office "had given that man her name," I didn't fully believe it, because I could think of an easier answer myself.

**What I want to know next:** who in the "office he trusted" it is, what Ned has never told anyone, and who the hoodie guy is on the island (whose son, whose nephew). On the romance side I want to know whether they sleep together before the ten days are up. The synopsis tells me the book will make me wait, and that's why I'd go back to my library.
