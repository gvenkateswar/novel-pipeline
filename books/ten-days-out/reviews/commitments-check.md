## Part A. Open items, ranked by how much a reader would trip

**Issue 1**
- Problem: Ch 10 s4 ends with Beth handing the wheelhouse key to Kenny as her answer to "Who's taking her home?". That reads as "you are". The next scene says nobody aboard can take the boat off the slip, and Kenny can't be master under the certificate ("Master and one deck"). Two adjacent scenes give opposite answers.
- Location: "Who's taking her home?" Kenny said. / Beth put the wheelhouse key in his glove." against Ch 10 s5: "The *North Reach* lay at the Pruett slip with nobody aboard who could take her off it, and the island had no boat until she got there."
- Suggested fix: Have her keep the key: "Beth put the wheelhouse key in her own coat and zipped the pocket."

**Issue 2**
- Problem: "Then Monday" still has no reason (fix-pass 04–06 confirms it is not done). Dane has just said "We move tonight" because the man is checking houses, and the reply puts the move off a day with no cause given. A listener hears a non sequitur.
- Location: "He's checking houses," Dane said. "We move tonight." … "Then Monday."
- Suggested fix: Give Beth a half line before Dane's reply: "Cove's over the sill till Monday's tides."

**Issue 3**
- Problem: §2.2 asked for the Ch 01 send-home to read as kindness. The writer's added line, flagged by the writer as off-brief, makes the opposite point: this night is the anomaly. It points the reader at Ned on page one, which is the book's number-one diagnosis.
- Location: "In eleven years he had sent her home twice, and she'd had a fever both times."
- Suggested fix: Cut the sentence. "I'll work it short" and "Glass is going down, and you've got the first boat" already give the reason.

## Part C. Completion questions (answered from the page only)

1. **At the end of Ch 06, two possible leaks?** Yes, a fresh reader can answer this.
   - Tess: "The county would ring the island. The island had one person to ring." Also *Ashby* written on her hand, and the hundreds in her vest.
   - Ned: "Home" in Ch 02, the Ashby key, and the tide question.
   - The courier is a third hand on the bags.
   - Ned is still the heavier suspect (Issues 3 and 4).
2. **Who resealed the bag, and when does the reader learn it?** Ned, in Ch 09 s6: "I sealed the bag again with a red one out of my drawer." / "Mine are yellow, Haskell." Yes. At the end of Ch 08 the reader cannot say for sure. Dane's "The money rode over in the boat's bag" points at the boat, and Beth's private misreading stands against it.
3. **Why no landing Thursday, and why no boarding?** "It would not put in to a float that had just been shot over, with her standing in the open." / "Homeland Security wants her to run to her buyer, and a boarding today keeps the buyer home." Yes.
4. **Why did Ned give them the boat Friday, and what did he expect?** "Land side watched, so they'd come by water, where you'd see them first. You wanted a trap. I gave them one." He didn't expect fire: "I never thought of gas." Yes.
5. **Why did Tess sit on the gray book until Saturday?** "Tuesday he still had a boat." This rests on Arsenault's Tuesday threat, "A store like this lives on the boat. I'd hate to see it have a hard—". Yes, by one inference.
6. **How did Tess learn Beth wrote her name?** Quist: "Your name was given to the Marshals on Friday, in writing, by the witness". Yes.
7. **What does Beth risk or do in Portland?** She names her captain under oath ("The captain," she said. "Edward Haskell."), then leaves at once to take the boat home ("Tell him she sails from Pruett at three."). Yes. The risk is the name. Nothing else is at stake in the room.
8. **Who is at the wheel when the ramp rises?** Beth: "in the wheelhouse window, Beth had the wheel". Yes.
9. **What question goes into book 2?** "Who was at the wheel of the *Dorothy M.* those eleven nights?" Yes, though it is only a name until Issue 6's clause lands. Dane's referral in Arlington is the second open question.
10. **What did walking out cost, and what does the hand on the strips answer?** He tracked her one-armed: "the sleeve was black from the shoulder to the cuff", and "her palm came away brown". The hand answers Pike's account of what his self-report costs him ("it follows him into every courtroom he stands up in. He asked that it be logged as his conduct alone."). Yes.
