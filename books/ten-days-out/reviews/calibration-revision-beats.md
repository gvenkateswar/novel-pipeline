## T6 — Ch 03 s3, the Ashby generator shed (about 25–28% of the book)

**Why.** Lane B readers expect the heat either front-loaded (4–6 scenes) or held for a late slow burn. Ours lands at 42%, 58% and 95%, and the sample reader called it "all hints until about 43%," her quit trigger. This scene is where the heat arrives. Today it stops at hands under the wool and a yes, and it reads as a hint. After the rewrite it is the book's first full open-door scene. It is on the page, sensory and close, and Beth gets something real from it. They still don't sleep together: Ch 05 stays their first time, and the Ch 04 "not yet" stays true.

**Keep, word for word:**
- the walk up the north road and the *Margaret Ann*'s deck lights going out;
- the shed, the primer, "Long and even, and don't yank it.", "Third one," / "Is that a rule?" / "It is for this one.", and the engine catching;
- "Is that what you look like when something works?";
- the stop, cued by the notebook in his inside pocket ("*Log daily*, and the other thing, the one he couldn't tell her");
- "I'd rather be kissed by a man I can check…";
- "Don't write me down.";
- "Ms. Calloway." … "No. And I'm not going to keep talking till it sounds like yes.";
- "That boat went dark," he said. "I have to call it in.";
- the porch call, "North end. That's what I'll tell them, and nothing closer.", and the last line ("…and she lost the rest").

**Change:**
1. **The middle runs longer and further, roughly 350–450 more words of heat.** After the yes, his hands go where she tells him. She is the one who asks: her desire register is questions and orders. He is quiet and says her name. Her coat is open and the thermal is pushed up. His mouth is on her and his hand is in her jeans, and she comes against the warm, shaking engine housing with the cold at the door. The scene changes the relationship: she has let him see her come undone.
2. **Consent is plain on the page.** Each step is asked and answered, in their voices.
3. **The stop comes after, and it costs more.** She is still shaking when his jacket swings and the notebook presses into her. She stops it with "I'd rather be kissed by a man I can check," which now lands harder.
4. **No phone interrupts the heat.** The phone (*Log daily*) is the reason for the stop. The call to Portland still comes after, on the porch.
5. **Don't build it like Ch 05.** Ch 05 has the dissolving and-chain; don't use it here. Use short, physical sentences timed to the engine's beat. The sensory anchors are the engine's beat in the housing at her back, his cold hands and then warm ones, the hauler's ridge across his palms, and the yellow windows up the slope. Don't use "she noticed", "asking him for something", or a stove ticking.

**Length:** 1,450–1,600 words (currently 1,134). The book has room: it is at 30,435 and its ceiling is about 31,000 after this round.

**Variant leads:**
- **V1:** "Lead with Beth's want. She asks for each thing; the scene is her taking what she wants and then choosing to stop."
- **V2:** "Lead with Dane going quiet. The man who talks through every crisis says almost nothing; the heat is in what he does and how carefully."
- **V3:** "Lead with place and danger. The dark dragger below the hill, the engine, the cold: the heat happens inside a threat they both know about."

**Heat rules:** open door, 3–4 on the 5-point scale, from the brief. Explicit, sensory, close POV, not clinical. No crude vocabulary: keep to the level of Ch 05.

## Pass V — villains and the love-scene builds

**Rule 7, one-note villains.** In the calibration set, villains score −55 to −57.
- **Arsenault** gets one want of his own, in a line or a beat in each of Ch 06 s2 and Ch 09 s6 or Ch 10 s2. He owes the Portland buyers. The dragger isn't his; he runs her for them. And he is scared of what happens to him if the load is lost. Let one compliment-threat slip into fear. In Ch 06 the threat to Tess is made while he is not in front of the federal witness by choice: he thinks Beth is a nobody deckhand.
- **The boy from the water, Doucette,** gets a face and a want: young, from an island across the bay, paid to do one job. In Ch 04 s1, one line on what Beth sees in him. In Ch 09 s5, he can't swim and grabs the buoy because he was raised on an island. In Ch 10 s1, in the Marine Patrol boat, he is a kid in a borrowed life jacket who looks at the island once.
- Pair: each villain beat shows a person, not an explanation.

**Rule 2, sameness in the love scenes.** Ch 03 (now the hands scene), Ch 05, Ch 06 and Ch 10 must each have their own build. Ch 05 keeps its build: her orders, the and-chain, the stove, "After."
- **Ch 06:** he leads, slow and deliberate. No cutaway to the drum or stove. It ends on something said, not on a quiet "After." It keeps the existing kept lines ("Hawser. Twenty-three.", the cot's legs walking, her name as the question).
- **Ch 10:** in her own bed, lamp on, eyes open. Make it the only one where she doesn't give orders and they laugh in the middle (the sweater). It ends on his staying line and "Since when?" / "You jumped."
- Pair: each scene changes something between them, stated in one action.
