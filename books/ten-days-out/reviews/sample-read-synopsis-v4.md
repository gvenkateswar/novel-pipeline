**Persona (built by the reader, none was supplied):** 38, reads on her phone at night and on the train, two or three books a week, mostly KU. Last month: two bodyguard/protector romantic suspense books and a small-town Alaska one, all open-door. She's tired of books sold as steamy that turn out to be sweet slow burns with one kiss at 60%, and of the hero who refuses her "because of the last woman he couldn't save." The last book she paid for and finished was $4.99. She has eleven unread samples on her phone.

## 1. Where I stopped

Finished Chapter 1. It's short and it moves; I never got close to putting it down mid-chapter. I came closest at Dane's opening run, "He fell twice in the new boots. The second time he lost his flashlight in the snow and left it" (about 60%), because I'd just watched the footprints and the flashlight from Beth's side and now I was getting the same thing again from his.

Then I read the synopsis for Chapters 2 and 3 and stopped there. Chapter 2 is a kitchen argument, then a scene of neighbors at the dock, and Chapter 3 is her asking for a kiss and him saying no because a woman he was guarding died. I've read that refusal in the last three books I bought.

## 2. Buy: no

The hook is good and the boots joke is cute, but the writing reads like a clean small-town romance with a drug plot added, and the synopsis tells me there's still no kiss by the end of Chapter 3. I'd go to the next sample on my phone.

## 4. Is the romance obvious by the end of Ch 1?

**Yes.** "He was in trouble. He knew it right then, standing in the snow holding an oar. He'd protected maybe forty witnesses, and he had never once wanted to make one of them laugh." It's sealed by "he wanted, very badly and very unprofessionally, to find out what her mouth would do if she ever actually smiled."

**Do I care about these two people yet?** Yes about Beth, barely about Dane. She makes fun of his boots while she's frozen and shaking, and that's a person. He's a nice guy in the wrong boots, and so far that's all I know about him.

## 5. Did anyone make me laugh?

Yes, once properly. "You're an ice cube with opinions." "Who told you that?" / "The government." got a smile.

## 6. Did anyone make me want?

A little: "find out what her mouth would do if she ever actually smiled." It's the only moment in the chapter with any heat. She's leaning on him with her face six inches from his, and the scene keeps it polite. For a book I'd be buying as steamy, that's thin.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. "She almost didn't. Her father had lost his boat to the bank, on paper, to a man in a good suit who wrote down everything about him in blue pen, and she had hated the idea of being written down by anybody ever since." (about 35%) The backstory stops the scene right when I wanted the phone call. I skimmed it.
2. "He drew his gun. He went through the house fast, every room, and she wasn't in any of them. Then he saw the window over the sink, wide open, and the marks in the snow under it. Small feet. No shoes. And a second set of tracks..." through "He fell twice in the new boots." (about 58–62%) I'd already seen all of this from Beth's side.
3. "And unless Dane was wrong, somebody in an office he trusted had given that man her name." (the last line) It repeats "Only the state police and his own office in Portland had known she'd called... Somebody had told him" from twenty lines earlier. I'd already got it.

Honorable mention: "Ned Haskell was at the wheel, with his white beard and his reading glasses pushed up on his head." That's a stock sea-captain description.

## 8. What I think is going on

The man who broke in is the hoodie kid from the deck. The book tells me so: his left heel drags on the deck, and Dane finds tracks with "one heel dragging." He's local ("nobody from away could find this cove in the dark"), probably the brother or son of someone Beth knows. My guess is that the leak isn't in Dane's office at all but on the island. Russ, the part-time cop, asking where she'll sleep is the line I'd flag; he's the one who'd hear about a state police call. Ned isn't the villain. He's scared because he knows who's behind the truck, maybe someone he owes, maybe tied to how her father lost his boat. The trooper's nine-minute hold is the other way the call could have gotten out. Dane will stay on the island, the storm will cut them off, and the "Each of them is partly right" in the synopsis tells me the leak will turn out to be both: someone on the island and someone in an office.

## What I want to know next

Who sent the kid to her door within two hours, and what Ned owes the people running the truck. On the romance side, nothing I don't already know. I can see the slow burn and the "I can't, the last witness died" coming, and the synopsis confirms both.
