# Sample read: synopsis

**Draft read:** `books/ten-days-out/reviews/sample-synopsis.md` (one-page synopsis of Ch 1–3, new draft), as it stood on 2026-10-28. No other file in the book folder was opened.

**Persona:** the default buyer from `genre-maps/romance.md`, adjusted for open-door romantic suspense. She is 35–55 and reads on her phone in bed, mostly through KU, three or four books a month. She reads for the couple and the danger in equal parts. She abandons a book when the heat is all hints or the setup runs long. She is tired of heroines with no job, instant love, generic small towns, and books that feel written to a list.

---

## 1. Where I stopped

Finished the sample. It is one page. The paragraph where I started skimming was the ferry-to-Coast-Guard paragraph (about 45–60%), where the storm turned into rules about who can board what.

## 2. Buy: yes

On KU, yes. Pills under the lobsters and the closing three-suspect line get me to borrow it. But I'm borrowing it for the danger. Nothing on this page makes me want him for her yet, and in an open-door book that's half of what I came for.

## 4. Do I care about these two people yet?

No. I care about Beth: eleven years on the deck, her bare hand on a truck that should be cold, staying on for Ned. Dane is a pair of boots, a sat phone and a gun. I have no idea what he wants for himself, so I don't care about them as a couple.

## 5. Did anyone make me laugh?

No. The closest was a small smile at "She tells him the two o'clock boat won't run. It doesn't." and at "wearing exactly the wrong boots for a ferry deck." Neither is a line anyone says out loud to anyone else.

## 6. Did anyone make me want?

One moment, and it landed mostly because of what came after it: "Then he kisses her, and she kisses him back, and then she stops, because she doesn't know who he calls at 11:20." There is no look, touch or thought about him before it, so the kiss comes out of nowhere. Nobody wanted anybody until the page told me they kissed.

## 7. The three most boring passages

1. "The damaged ferry can't carry a passenger until the Coast Guard has inspected her, and the island is cut off." (about 55%) I skimmed it. It's a rule, not a scene.
2. "For the night he moves her aboard the ferry: steel, a wheelhouse door that locks, a radio, and Ned..." (about 45%) An inventory. I already knew it was a boat.
3. "At the island store her oldest friend Tess, who runs the storm check-in, has written Beth down as home, with Ned's initials beside it." (about 75%) A clue with a label on it. I could feel the book handing me a suspect.

Runner-up: "She won't leave her captain short-handed, and it turns out he can't make her: protection is voluntary." (about 25%) The argument I wanted got settled by a regulation.

## 8. What I think is going on

Ned. She told him and nobody else. His initials are on Tess's list, which means he passed it on. He's the one who keeps choosing where she sleeps, first the ferry and then the summer house on the north point. And the trucks have been riding his boat for eleven years. Either Ned is in on the pills, or he talked and Tess's list did the rest, which makes Tess the innocent leak. Dane's porch call and "No. She doesn't know." feel too obvious to be the real betrayal. I think he's reporting to his own people, and the thing she doesn't know is that Ned is already under investigation. I expect the dragger men to come to the north-point house next, and Ned gave them the key. The hooded man who walked onto the island is someone she knows.
