# Ten Days Out — Cold Reader Notes (v3, full manuscript)

Read start to finish in one pass, as a romantic suspense fan who picked it up on Kindle Unlimited. Only `manuscript.md` was read.

---

## 1. The three questions

### a. Chapter 7: why does Beth leave, and can I defend it? — PARTLY

**What works.** Her reason is on the page, and it's a strong one. She finds *CALLOWAY, E.* and the $9,000 line, then the back-pages log: *ASSESS — per Pike (Quist req.). Daily. Do not disclose to subject.* and *D3 — financial questions. No indication.* She realizes the generator-shed conversation "had been an interview." Coming right after the cot, that hurts, and I felt it with her. The best piece is that the book tells me she is choosing which story to believe:

> "A man from away she could walk out on. Tess she would have to walk past every morning of her life. / If it was him, it wasn't her. / Her father had believed the bank."

That is motivated reasoning, the book knows it, and it's the most human thing she does all book. I can defend her not waking him ("she couldn't check an explanation. She could check a binder by its handle") and leaving the book open so he won't come after her with a gun.

**What doesn't.** The tactics fall apart if I think about them for ten seconds, and the text half-admits it:
- Her own argument clears him. She leaves him asleep because "Nobody but the two of them knew this door, and nobody had come to it." That is the proof he isn't the leak. She only puts it together in chapter 8 ("If it had been him, the fish house would have burned first"). A woman who reads binders and footprints this well should get there in chapter 7. Leaving him safe *because* the hideout never leaked, while running from him *as* the leak, is a contradiction I noticed as I read.
- She runs to Ned. That's the man she caught lying about the reefer on Saturday, who "ate supper at Tess's table every night of the week." She says "She held both of those and didn't step round them," but in practice she does step round them.
- She walks the shore for two hours in the dark in a storm, unarmed, knowing someone is hunting her on the island. When she nearly runs into the heel at the churchyard, the scene is tense, but it also shows how reckless the plan was.

Verdict: I believe she'd do it emotionally. I don't believe this careful a woman would do it this way. I read the chapter as hurt plus denial, not as a sound choice.

### b. Have both lovers named what they hid, and do they act differently after? — YES (with reservations)

**Named:**
- Dane, chapter 8, crew mess: "I could have told you on Saturday and taken the consequences. I didn't. That was mine. The order didn't choose it for me." He also explains the struck-through D6 line ("*Subject and I.*") and why the fish house never went into the log.
- Beth, same scene: "Wednesday. On the ledge. You said local emergency management, and I knew it was Tess before you'd finished. I split kindling and said nothing. That's mine. Yours is still yours."
- "The parking lot's yours. You carry her. Not me." is the best line in the reconciliation. She won't let Boston excuse him.

**Behavior after:** clearly changed, and you can see it.
- Dane calls Pike "from the wing, with the door open, and you'll hear my side of it" (ch 8). On the wing he "bent his head to hers so she could hear the line" (end of ch 8). At 3 a.m. he does it again: "his head bent to hers so she had both ends of the line" (ch 9). In ch 10: "Pike hears about tonight from me... and you're hearing that first."
- Beth gives names as soon as they come: "Tess. Ned. And Russ." She reports the radio roll to him at once. On Tess's warning she acts in the open with Russ and Dane, with no private plan.

**Reservations:**
- Both confessions use the same formula ("That was mine" / "That's mine"). It sounds like two people who went to the same therapist, not a deckhand and a marshal.
- Beth never actually says *I thought you were selling me, and I left you asleep and walked out*. Hers is the smaller confession. Dane pays (suspension, file letter, transfer) and she pays almost nothing, so the balance tips toward him carrying the fault.
- The "you hear it first" beat comes back four or five times. By the third, it read like the book reminding me of the lesson rather than the lesson simply having been learned.

### c. Why must the chapter 9 confrontation happen, given the safer options in chapter 8? — PARTLY

**What the book gives me.** The safe option is to lock the wheelhouse and wait for first light. Beth's case against it is Tess. After hearing the roll call in a voice that isn't Tess's, she reasons: "Tonight the boat sailed. The one person left who could put Arsenault in that store was the woman reading the roll. He would not leave her on the island behind him." Dane puts it plainly: "If I put you behind that door with me and we wait for daylight, Tess has until dark." Pike rules out blue lights at the store: Arsenault would be "off the float with Tess aboard." I understood the stakes.

**Why I didn't fully buy it:**
- The obvious safer move is Russ at the store with Tess. Russ offers to arrest her, and Dane refuses because "Arsenault walks down to his mooring and sails." That's a reason to *catch Arsenault*, not to *save Tess*, and catching Arsenault isn't Beth's job or Dane's. The justification switches from protecting Tess to making the bust partway through the scene.
- The plan only saves Tess by luck. Making Beth the target draws Doucette to the boat, but nothing in it protects Tess. She survives because she gets "Water side—" out over the radio and Russ happens to know a cellar bulkhead "nobody from away knows." Russ ends up doing exactly the safer option, and that option is what works.
- Pike says "she has not authorized bait," and Dane answers "it's as near her line as I'm willing to go." That is bait. A marshal who just lost his detail for crossing a line, with one working arm, signs off on his witness going down to a dark car deck in her socks? I believed Beth would propose it. I didn't fully believe Dane would agree.
- The cellar bulkhead that wins the fight first appears in the last ten pages, after the fight.

Verdict: the climax is well staged and I was tense through it, but it's there because the book needs a climax, not because the characters were cornered into it.

## 2. Where I got bored, skimmed, or would have put it down

- **Chapter 2, Dane's arrival.** He reads the crowd at the slip, then the road ("from the head of the slip you had the first two hundred yards; from the store dock..."), then the captain. That's a lot of surveillance narration before anything happens. I skimmed the middle.
- **Chapter 3, the three-houses Q&A** ("How many ways into each of them?" / "And who can see the door from outside?"). It reads like a briefing.
- **The nightly 11:20 sat-phone call.** Porch, antenna up, facing the road, notebook. By chapter 5 I skipped every paragraph that started with the time.
- **Chapter 5, the tide explanation on the bar.** It pays off in the fire crossing, but the steps (forty-one, sixty-three, ninety) plus "Tell it to me like I've never seen water" stalled me.
- **Chapter 6, Day 6, pacing the bait shed** (fourteen steps, corrugated wall). Necessary setup, but I could feel it being set up.
- **Dane's procedural speeches in chapter 8.** The "I reported that you read the log... internal affairs... suspension without pay... transfer to the District of Maine..." paragraph, and Pike's patrol-boat-and-Coast-Guard paragraph. Chapter 10 then repeats the costs ("The costs had landed about where he'd told her they would").
- **Chapter 10, the handover at the brow.** Relief deputy reads a sheet, Dane gives a formal handover. Accurate, but it's paperwork.

**Where I'd most likely have quit:** between the end of chapter 4 and the bar walk in chapter 5. By then I was sure Tess was the leak (see section 4), the hunt was all hiding and logistics, and Beth's suspicion of Dane had no tension for me. The fire crossing in chapter 5 pulled me back in hard.

## 3. Where I got confused, or the logic didn't add up

- **The shore path.** Chapter 3, Beth: "He ran for the shore path... He knows where it goes." Chapter 4, Russ: "He knew the way." Chapter 9, Dane: Doucette "had been talked along the Harbor Road by radio because he didn't know the island." So who tried the latch? If it was Doucette, how did he know the shore path the first night? If it wasn't, who was it? Never resolved.
- **Who set the Ashby fire, and whose windshield was at the end of the drive?** Arsenault's crew came by boat. Whose car was on the north road? It's never explained.
- **Dane is slow about the bank bag.** His notebook says *via store bank bag* on Saturday. In chapter 2 he watched Tess hand that bag up. A marshal who reads everyone's hands never looks at the woman who packed the bag until chapter 8? He says he didn't know Tess was the emergency coordinator, fine, but the money pointed at the store from Day 2.
- **The two trucks on the car deck.** In chapter 2 the deck holds "one pickup and a pallet." From chapter 7 on there are "two chained trucks," later named as the fire company's propane truck and the store's box truck. When did they come aboard, with the bridge wrecked? In chapter 10 Beth unchains "propane first" at Pruett, so they crossed too.
- **Boat counts.** Nine lobster boats in the inner harbor (ch 1 and 3), then "Twenty-two boats where there should be twenty-four" (ch 4). Probably inner harbor versus whole harbor, but it made me stop and count.
- **Chapter 8, Pike call.** The speakers blur ("Coast Guard station. And county emergency management... I'll have the name for you at seven." Who said that?). Then "He said he understood, and she was gone." For a second I read *she* as Beth, not Pike hanging up.
- **Chapter 1, "Not in front of—".** Not in front of whom? Ned? It's never picked up.
- **Chapter 9 timing.** In Beth's section she kills the floods right after the brow is chained. In Dane's section the outboard approaches, cuts, and the paddle comes in *before* "The floods went out." It roughly fits, but I had to rewind.
- **Russ's ring finger "short a tip."** Dane notes it as if it's a clue, and it never comes back.
- **"Count me for six, Tess."** It's 5:30 and the net is at six, I think. It took me a beat.
- **"The cottage on the Harbor Road"** in the chapter 10 handover. Beth's house was a Cape everywhere else.

## 4. When I suspected the leak, and how that felt

- **Suspected:** chapter 2. "Tess didn't. Something small hit the counter... and laughed. The laugh came late."
- **Sure:** chapter 3. The tick in Saturday's 0800 column "for a call she had never made," then Tess takes a call and goes into the back office. Beth thinks it outright: "*Tess knew I stayed.*" The chapter 6 lines "Local emergency management on Cutter wore a red vest" and Tess smoothing the green ledger, "and then again," nailed it shut.

**How it felt:** like dramatic irony, not a whodunit. That's fine as far as it goes. Watching Beth refuse to see it ("She held on to the clock until the other thing let go") is a real character arc, and the payoff ("*Tess doesn't count.* ... In his mouth it sounded like what it was.") lands hard. The cost is that the *Dane might be the leak* thread, which chapters 3–7 lean on, never had a chance with me. **Chapter 2 is in Dane's head.** I'd been inside him, and he's clean. Every scene of Beth listening to his calls and watching his notebook pocket was suspense for her but not for me. The notebook reveal in chapter 7 hurt as a betrayal of intimacy, but it didn't make me doubt him for a second.

Ned as a red herring (the reefer lie) worked better. I wondered about him until chapter 8.

## 5. Do the two leads sound like different people?

**Yes, clearly, in both dialogue and narration.**

- **Beth** talks in fragments, logbook style: "Wind's backed northeast. The glass is down four since breakfast." Her narration counts things (rungs, steps, primer strokes, cartons) and runs on boat sense. Her sentences are short and declarative.
- **Dane** talks in long, balanced sentences joined with *and* and *because*, full of "I'd rather": "I'll plan on the two o'clock until the captain cancels it, because that's his call to make, and I'd rather unpack a bag than need one I didn't pack." His narration does the same, with dry asides like "which was worth knowing about the only captain on the island."
- Tess, Ned and Russ each have their own voice. Ned's terse "He'll live" and Russ's "I'm not a complicated man" are both good. Quist is thin.

**Problems:**
- Dane's formality never breaks. He's grazed by a bullet and says "Fuck" once, then goes straight back to complete compound sentences. Under fire or in bed he talks like a written report: "I'd like to stand in my mother's kitchen on Christmas morning with you in it, and not have to describe you to anybody." It's a lovely line, but nobody's mouth makes it after sex. By chapter 8 his speeches were mannered enough that I skimmed them.
- **Beth's narration has a tic:** setting two facts side by side and not resolving them. "She didn't try to make them agree" (ch 3), "She set the two side by side and left them there" (ch 4), "She set it down beside the other thing" (ch 5), "She held both of those and didn't step round them" (ch 7), "She quit holding the pieces apart" (ch 8). Once it's a character trait. Five times it's the author's habit, and it started to read machine-made.
- **Neat scene endings:** "She didn't step out." / "Beth reached over and switched off the kitchen light." / "His pen was still moving." / "She stopped, because that was everybody." / "Okay, Bethie." Most of them are good, but almost every section closes on one, and I started bracing for them.
- **Repeated "Okay," she said** as Beth's way of accepting something (ch 2, 3, 4).

## 6. Heat: how steamy, and does it match the front note?

The front note promises "steamy, open-door... on-page sex between consenting adults." It delivers **open door, about 3 out of 5**. The anatomy is explicit and the scenes are on the page, but they're short and broken up with suspense thoughts. I wouldn't call it scorching.

- **Ch 4, generator shed:** a heated kiss that stops at "That isn't a no. It's a not yet." Good slow-burn tease.
- **Ch 5, fish house:** hands only, explicit, with the disappointing-trauma-kit joke ("It's a very disappointing kit"). Funny, warm, and the consent is clear. It ends with "They knew the Ashby place" in pillow talk, which shows how the suspense keeps intruding.
- **Ch 6, cot:** the first full sex. This is the best of the four, because of the door beat (see Best moment). The scene changes her.
- **Ch 10, her own bed:** "what she heard all through it was her own voice, out loud, in her own house." It's a good closing beat for the sex scenes. Each one changes the relationship. None of them would work lifted out on its own, and that's a good thing.
- **The condom-buying scene with Tess** (ch 6) is the best heat-adjacent scene in the book. It's funny ("I'd have asked for a receipt"), and on a reread it becomes awful. "He took one from Tess's box. Neither of them said her name" is a killer line.

**Mismatch:** the first sex comes about halfway through, and the scenes run maybe 400–600 words each. A KU reader who sees "steamy" on the front may feel a little short-changed. The heat suits *romantic suspense with spice*, not *spicy romance with suspense*. The front note's content warnings were all accurate.

## 7. Does "I love you" feel earned? — PARTLY

The feeling is earned: the bar crossing, the bleed screw, "The parking lot's yours," the Christmas-in-Lowell talk, "Kenny owes me two." By chapter 10's bed scene I believed these two.

The scene itself is rushed. It's skipped ahead three weeks, said in a truck lane in two lines ("Hand me—" She stopped... "I love you."), then a brief kiss, and the page turns to Quist and Russ. The book's actual last image is Russ taking the envelope. The "Hand me—" interruption is cute and true to her, but the declaration is given less room than Dane's chapter 8 report to Pike. I wanted one more beat of them alone.

## 8. Would I read the next book, and who is it about?

**Probably yes.** It's about **Russ Whitcomb and Agent Quist** working Tess's ledger ("three harbors, one account"). The setup is obvious and works: she can't stand up on a float, he leans "as if standing were optional," and the "No. I'll have my traps out by Tuesday" line closes with his hand already on the envelope. Russ is the character I liked most outside the leads. Quist is a sketch so far (seasick, "cleanest fucking ledger"), so she'll need a lot more on the page before I'd root for her. I'd also want Tess in book 2 as more than evidence. She's the most interesting person in this book.

---

## 9. Rating and the one change

**Rating: 4 / 5.** (3.5 for the plot logic, 4.5 for the setting and texture.)

The book's real strengths are chapter 1 on the freight deck, the bar crossing, the condom scene, Tess's radio roll ("This voice set each name down by itself and waited before the next"), and Ned turning off his hearing aid "to news." The world is so specific I could smell the herring. The problems are a leak I solved in chapter 3, a romance suspicion I never believed because I'd been inside Dane's head, and a climax whose necessity I had to take on trust.

**The one change I'd most want:** make me doubt Dane for real, or else stop asking me to. As it is, the Tess clues land by chapter 3 and chapter 2 puts me in Dane's POV, so the middle third's "who's the leak / can I trust him" suspense is dead for me even while it's alive for Beth. That's where I nearly stopped reading.

## Best moment

Chapter 6, the cot:

> "She turned her head on the blanket to find the door. / She didn't know where it was. Behind her head, or past the stove, or off her feet somewhere. Since the night on the freight deck she had known the door in every room she stood in, and which hand for the latch. She reached for it now and didn't have it, and she let that be true, and turned her face back to his."

It ties the sex to the suspense, shows how much she trusts him at that moment, and gets paid off by contrast at the end of chapter 6: "She stood on the stove side of the room with the door in front of her, and she knew exactly where it was." Runner-up: "Okay, Bethie." at the end of the chapter 9 radio scene.
