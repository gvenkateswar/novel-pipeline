## Flags (read before drafting)

1. **Style Samples 1 and 2 are Chapter 01 scenes.** Sample 2 is the 3:40 labeling opening and Sample 1 is the knock. The bible says to match their rhythm without reusing their lines or details, so the prose-writer must not reuse: "forty-one things," LANES 1–5 / LANES 6–10 / FOYER / SIGN, the DEAD K strip, the burning-smell strip, the clock that runs four minutes fast, "I open at five-fifteen," "I have paperwork," "I have a key." / "So, no.," "Is that an estimate or a threat?," or "It's a Tuesday." The beats below use different specifics.
2. **Sample 1 has Ray walk past Ellen into the alley.** That conflicts with the post-review rule that he never enters uninvited, and the outline wins. In Scene 2 he stays outside the door until Ellen tells him to come in, and then he goes no farther than the vestibule mat.
3. **Lane 7 diagnosis.** Sample 1, Sample 3, and the bible's Ray sample line ("Your table on seven's been slipping since Thanksgiving") all blame lane 7's table. In the outline's Ch 02 set piece Ray says, "That's not the table. That's the clutch letting go a half-second late." So **in Ch 01 Ray names no part and gives no diagnosis**, and Ellen's narration describes only what she can see and hear. Don't use the "slipping since Thanksgiving" line in this chapter.
4. **Where the pool sheet is.** The outline says Denise shows "the pool sheet from the hardware store" at close. The sheet hangs behind Ray's register, and the Ch 02 pacing note has him seeing the empty square there every morning. **Fix used here:** Denise shows Ellen a photo of the sheet on her phone, so the paper sheet stays on Ray's wall for Ch 02.
5. **Verna in Ch 01.** The cast lists in the outline and the bible give Verna Ch 07 and Ch 09 only, but the Ch 01 set piece names her for the ruling. She is kept to the ruling and one line in Scene 4. Hold back her full introduction (gavel, Robert's Rules, the minutes from 1987) for Ch 07. The lavender parka can appear here as a single visual tag.
6. **Walk-ons.** The beat-sheet format asks for named characters only, but the outline says walk-ons stay unnamed. The opposing captain on lanes 5–6, the opposing leadoff bowler, and the PA hecklers are unnamed league bowlers and are listed as such.
7. **Not relevant to Ch 01, noted for later.** The bible's Denise entry puts her earflap hat at "Ch 07's boiler-outage league night." The outline has cut the parka league night and keeps the hat only as one object on a hook in Ch 07. The orchestrator may want the author to fix the bible line.
8. **Clean-rules hazard.** The rekey involves cylinder "pins," and the alley is full of pins and balls. No double meanings in the locksmith call or the ceremonial-ball scene. Leave the word "pins" out of the locksmith exchange entirely.

### Scene 1
**Setting:** Kettle Lanes, the counter and the office. 3:40 p.m. Low December sun through the glass-block window band lays pale squares across lanes 1 and 2. Sensory anchor: tearing masking tape, the squeak of a Sharpie, lane dressing and cold fryer oil, the baseboard heat ticking as it warms.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Denise
**Surface action:** Ellen peels Arlene's old tape (green ink, plain numbers) off the ten lane-light toggles and relabels each one in flight-strip format: lane, time, league or open, count, remarks, in block capitals. She labels the separate row of foul-light switches in a different format so nobody can mix up the two rows. She starts a pegboard strip board on the wall beside the counter with one card for tonight: TUES LEAGUE, the start time, the bowler count. In the office she takes the 2019 fuel-oil calendar off the nail and puts it **in the desk drawer, not the trash** (it goes back up in Ch 02). At 4:30 Denise comes in through the vestibule, stamps salt off her boots, and stops at the counter to read the new labels with her cat-eye readers. Ellen counts the room under her breath ("Two") as Denise enters. Denise sets her crossword and pen in their spot under the counter, as she has for twenty-two years, and asks a hard question as a joke: who is working the fryer while Ellen labels things? Ellen answers literally, with a time and a name (herself, 6:15).
**Subtext:** Ellen is putting her handwriting over Arlene's on every surface, because a room she has labeled is a room she controls. Denise is measuring whether this owner is another one who won't ask her anything. Ellen hasn't asked her anything yet.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal and quiet. Denise has worked this counter longer than Ellen has owned anything here, and the new labels are on her switches.
**Ends on:** Denise reaches past Ellen and flips the lane 4 toggle by feel, without looking at the label. The lane lights come on. "Twenty-two years," Denise says, "they've had numbers on them." Use this line or one like it.
**Word count target:** 550
**Notes:** Open at 3:40 with "six hours" of ownership (outline). No backstory beyond the strip format itself, and no weather opening. Ellen's interior runs on order and counts, not jargon. Zero ATC phrases. Do not reuse Sample 2's details (see Flag 1).

### Scene 2 — **MEET-HOSTILE, part 1: the knock and the locksmith call**
**Setting:** The front door and vestibule. 5:15 p.m., already dark. Through the door glass, the half-plowed lot and a pickup with its engine running. Sensory anchor: cold air coming in low across the linoleum when the door opens, the rubber mat, the boot tray, the 1974 cigarette-burn scar.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Ray, Danny (by phone), Denise (at the counter, crossword, not looking up)
**Surface action:** Three knocks on the glass. Ellen opens the door. Ray stands outside with snow on the shoulders of his GAGNON & SON jacket, holding a single key with an orange paper tag in his closed fist. He doesn't come in. He says what he's there for in as few words as possible: he opens on league nights. Ellen holds out her hand for the key. He doesn't give it to her: Arlene gave it to him, and if Arlene wants it back she can ask. Ellen's line about the note lands here (see ledger). Ray's answer is short. Ellen tells him he can wait inside and that he can't open anything. He steps onto the vestibule mat, wipes his boots, and stops there. He goes no farther. Ellen goes to the counter, takes the thin Ambrose directory from under it, and runs her finger down to LOCKSMITH. There is one listing. She looks at the stitching on his jacket, then dials the rotary phone. Danny answers at Gagnon & Son. Ellen asks for a rekey of all exterior doors. Danny, flat: the locksmith is his father, and it's Tuesday, so he's probably standing in her alley. Ellen: **"Say again."** (ATC 1.) Danny repeats it and books "the locksmith" for 8 a.m. tomorrow, reading it back to her the way he'd enter it on his tablet. Ellen writes a strip (LOCKSMITH, 0800, GAGNON, EXT DOORS) and pins it to the board in Ray's line of sight.
**Subtext:** Ray has opened this door every league night since 1996, and today he knocked, which is the most he can give up without giving up the key. Ellen wants him to break a rule so she can be right about him, and he won't. The key isn't hers to take and the door isn't his to open, and they both know both halves of that.
**Tension source:** Interpersonal. Two people who are each technically right, standing four feet apart on a rubber mat. Then the situational comedy: the only person who can lock him out is him.
**Ends on:** Ray reads the strip from the mat. "He's punctual," he says. Use this line or one like it.
**Word count target:** 700 (dialogue at least half)
**Notes:** Ray's key is held still in his fist, with no jingle (the jingle is saved for Scene 4). Don't reuse Sample 1's lines or have him walk past her (Flags 1 and 2). No "pins" in the locksmith exchange (Flag 8). Denise stays a silent presence. One pen stroke on her crossword after Danny's line would do.

### Scene 3 — **COMIC SET PIECE (Ellen ATC set piece 1 of 3) + MEET-HOSTILE, part 2: the ruined frame**
**Setting:** The counter's gooseneck PA, then the approach on lanes 5–6. 6:30 p.m., league start. Forty bowlers in the building, coats on the orange settees. Sensory anchor: the PA's hum before she speaks, the clatter of candlepins like a drawer of wooden spoons, the ball return rumbling under the floor.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Ray, Hollis, Verna, Denise. Unnamed league bowlers: the hecklers, the opposing captain on 5–6, and the opposing leadoff bowler.
**Surface action:**
- **PA set piece.** Ellen counts the room (an exact number, said or thought) and gives lane assignments over the PA in approach-control order, pair by pair: **"Lane four, cleared to bowl."** (ATC 2.) The league answers in fake pilot-speak ("Roger, tower." "Kettle Tower, requesting a hot dog."). Ellen answers the hot-dog request literally and procedurally, without an ATC phrase. Denise grades the heckle without looking up.
- **The FAA line.** Hollis comes to the counter, takes his blaze-orange cap off to think, and asks whether she flies. Ellen's one-line answer is the FAA retirement at 56 (see ledger). Hollis puts his cap back on and asks his real question: Arlene threw the first ball on opening night every year since 1971, so is the new owner going to throw one?
- **Lane logistics** (give the prose-writer just enough to make it plausible). Ray and Hollis are teammates on the lanes 5–6 pair. Ray is his team's leadoff and is on **lane 6** in the first string, two balls thrown, standing at the foul line with a leave and his deadwood set up exactly where he wants it for his third ball. Next door, on **lane 5**, the opposing leadoff has just finished a frame and the table has respotted a full rack. Hollis waves that bowler off and hands Ellen a house ball.
- **The throw.** Ellen has never bowled candlepin. She lofts it. The ball lands past the lob line, the foul light comes on (write-around: "the foul light came on"; don't state the penalty rule), the ball hops the gutter cap into lane 6, and it clears Ray's deadwood and his standing wood off the deck. A full second of silence follows, then the forty-bowler noise.
- Ellen walks back to the PA: **"Lane six, stand by."** (ATC 3.)
- **The ruling.** Hollis (Ray's captain), the opposing captain, and Verna in her lavender parka confer at the scorer's table. Verna rules: re-rack, re-bowl the frame. **Nobody cites a rule or a bylaw.** It is their call, made on the spot.
- Ray re-bowls from a full rack. He gets a worse result than the leave he lost. He writes the lower number on the paper score sheet himself, in square capitals, and doesn't complain.
- **(Reserve.)** If the prose-writer uses the sixth ATC phrase, it goes here and only here.
**Subtext:** Ellen's competence is on display at the microphone, and a minute later her lack of it is on display at the foul line, in front of the same forty people. Ray loses a frame he built by hand to the woman who just booked him to lock himself out. He takes the worse score without arguing, which is the first thing he does that she can't file under "hostile."
**Tension source:** Situational comedy turning interpersonal. Public humiliation, a fair ruling that still costs Ray something, and Ellen can't apologize over a PA.
**Ends on:** Ray, walking back from the foul line past her, says, "Lob line's ten feet out, Mrs. Daly." "Ms.," Ellen says. He is already sitting down. End on her "Ms." or on his sitting.
**Word count target:** 800 (the set piece warrants it; don't go past 850)
**Notes:** Check every sentence for accidental double meanings (balls, pins, lofting). Name no leaves with pin numbers. A leave name in Ray's mouth without numbers is fine, or name none. Hollis asks questions and speaks with no change in tone. Verna gets the ruling and nothing more here.

### Scene 4 — Lane 7 dies
**Setting:** Lane 7, the steel pit door by lane 10, and the counter. About 8:10 p.m., second string. Sensory anchor: a pinsetter cycle that sounds wrong, a hot-belt smell drifting up the approach, the bowlers on 7 turning around in their seats to look at the counter.
**Characters present:** Ellen, Ray, Verna, Denise. Unnamed bowlers from the lanes 7–8 teams.
**Surface action:**
- Ellen is walking a fresh coffee filter to the snack bar when lane 7's machine cycles slow, then slower. She stops, takes out her phone, and **records the next cycle** (plant for Ch 02). On that cycle the table stops partway down and stays there, with pins scattered on the deck and the sweep down. The motor hums, then quits.
- Ellen goes to the PA and uses **the voice** (the one use this chapter): **"Lane seven is unable."** (ATC 4.) Lane 7's team doubles up on lane 8. The bowlers comply. She does not hurry.
- She goes through the steel door by lane 10 with a flashlight, walks the catwalk, and looks at the stopped machine. She **touches nothing** (she does no repair she isn't qualified for). She comes back out and shuts the door.
- In Arlene's index-card file on the counter, the card marked MECHANIC has one entry: R.G. and a phone number. She brings it to Ray on the 5–6 settee and briefs him rather than asking (her flaw): what stopped, where, and when. Ray: Arlene's mechanic was him. The **key-ring jingle** (once this chapter) as he decides; then he puts the ring in his pocket. "Not my alley." He names no part (Flag 3).
- Walking back to the settee, Ray passes the trophy case and touches the glass with one knuckle without looking at it. Ellen sees it, and sees where: the center shelf with the LINDA GAGNON MEMORIAL trophy and the framed 187 card. No explanation, in narration or in dialogue.
- Ellen stays standing through all of it (she stands to take bad news). She sits on the counter stool only once she has decided: nine lanes, and a refund for lane 7. She counts the lineage for the lane 7 bowlers out of the register and hands it to each of them personally. No dollar figure on the page.
- **The March 1 line** lands here, in her interior at the register (see ledger).
**Subtext:** The only man who can fix her machines is sitting thirty feet from one, and his refusal is honest. He's hurt, he's a man who would rather be hurt than be used, and after Scene 2 it isn't his building. Ellen didn't ask him. She briefed him. Neither of them notices that difference yet. The reader does.
**Tension source:** Situational (a dead machine on night one, money going out of the register) and interpersonal (the refusal). Plus the trophy-case touch, which Ellen can't read.
**Ends on:** Verna, receiving her refund at the scorer's table, writes in her binder. "Arlene never refunded anyone, Mrs. Daly. I'll note it in the minutes." Use this line or one like it.
**Word count target:** 700
**Notes:** Use only safe generic machine terms (table, sweep, belt, motor) from Ellen's point of view. She describes, she doesn't diagnose. "Nobody's in the air" (from the bible's sample line) is optional and does not count as an ATC phrase.

### Scene 5 — Close: the pool
**Setting:** The counter, then the front door and the lot. 10:55 p.m., lanes dark, only the counter lights on. Sensory anchor: the register drawer rolling shut, the neon sign buzzing through the front windows, and outside, the sign reading ETTLE LANES in red against the snowbank (the one dead-K use this chapter).
**Characters present:** Ellen, Denise, Ray (in his truck in the lot, seen, not heard)
**Surface action:**
- Denise sprays the last rental shoes with her two-count and grades them out loud. Ellen squares the paper score sheets and banks the night: she runs the adding machine and tears off the tape. Night one is below zero.
- Denise, with her coat already on, takes out her phone and shows Ellen a photo (Flag 4): a hand-ruled sheet tacked behind a hardware-store register. Squares of dates from December into late February, initials and names in different pens, a header asking when the new owner folds. The **pool** lands here. Denise may use her bible sample line: "this town's had a pool on you since the closing. I'm just the one holding the sheet."
- Denise asks if Ellen wants to know who has Christmas Eve. Ellen: **"Negative."** (ATC 5.)
- **The right of first refusal** lands in Denise's one line: "If you miss, you have to sell, and Ray gets first crack." Ellen: "I'm aware. I signed it."
- Ellen enlarges the photo with two fingers. The last square, MARCH 1, is empty. Denise: "Ray won't buy one." Ellen asks why. Denise: "Ask him. It's his wall." Denise pockets the phone. Ellen does not try to buy a square (that joke belongs to the Ch 10 coda).
- Denise leaves. Ellen walks the building in order (ten lane lights, the foul-light row, the fryer, the coffee urn), locks the front door with the lock his key still opens until 8 a.m., and tries the handle twice. In the lot, Ray's truck is still there with its engine running. It doesn't move while she clears her windshield. It pulls out onto Kettle Road only after her own engine turns over and her headlights come on.
**Subtext:** Ellen now knows that the whole town knows her terms and has priced her failure. The one man who gains the most from it won't put a dollar on it, and he has just waited in the cold to be sure her car starts. She can't make the two halves of him add up, and that is the chapter promise.
**Tension source:** Internal: humiliation in pen, on a wall she has never seen. Situational: the night's loss on the adding-machine tape. And the open question of Ray's behavior in the lot.
**Ends on:** Ray's taillights turning onto Kettle Road, after her headlights come on. End on that action, not on a reflection about what it means.
**Word count target:** 650
**Notes:** This scene sets up the chapter promise: *Can she run this place without the one man who knows how, and what kind of man wants her building but won't bet against her?* Don't state it. The reader puts together the dead machine, "not my alley," the empty March 1 square, and the waiting truck. If the chapter is running long, the outline's fallback is to move the pool reveal to Ray's register in Ch 02. Don't take it unless the draft is more than 300 words over, because this scene carries the promise.
