# Reader Notes — Chapter 2: The Weather Hold

(Read: chapter-01/final.md, chapter-02/final.md, in order. Nothing else.)

**Verdict:** I'm keeping going. The suspense works, but the romance barely exists yet. For a book I bought as open-door, that's the big gap.

---

## Where I was gripped

- **The latch at 11:40.** Chapter 1 set up the sticky latch ("a sound she knew in her sleep and would never make by accident"), and here it pays off as the scariest thing in two chapters. I read the run from "It stuck. It dropped back a hair. It lifted again." to the running feet twice.
- **The cable parting and the harbor going dark.** "A sound she had never heard and knew anyway" works. So does the transfer bridge hanging half in the water under the *North Reach*'s floods. The island is cut off and I felt it.
- **The float coat.** She has one arm in the coat before she's decided anything, then stops, takes it off and hangs it up. That beat shows me who she is better than anything else in the chapter. Dane watches and says nothing, which is right.
- **The last page.** "Who knew you were here?" / "You." Then the reader gets the 11:20 porch call, the antenna, "something small and flat in his other hand," and she stays behind the brick. Good chill. I want to turn the page.
- **Tess's late laugh.** "The laugh came late." Four words, and they made me suspect someone I liked in Chapter 1.

## Where I got bored or skimmed

- **The count at the slip.** "Seven. Two men in orange bib pants…" and then a paragraph decoding it ("Pockets meant cold. Cups meant they'd come down to see."). It's a nice idea, but the cataloguing slowed the arrival. I skimmed.
- **The Harbor Road paragraph** ("From the head of the slip, the first two hundred yards. From the store dock…"). I didn't follow the sightline logic and didn't care to. Skimmed.
- **The check-in net exposition at 5:30.** Channel 68, Walt Grindle's fire crew, net control, emergency management director since town meeting, forty dollars of gas a day, channel 9. That's a lot of island procedure in one go. The *Ruthie C* memory was the only part that landed.
- **The barometer.** 1000, 996, 994, 992, 990, 986. In Chapter 1 the tapping was character. By the fifth tap in Chapter 2 it was a metronome. I started waiting for it at the end of each scene and skipping the number.

## Where I got confused

- **POV.** The chapter opens firmly in Dane's head: wrong boots, counting the slip, reading the captain. Then partway through the kitchen scene I'm in Beth's ("She didn't know what the hand meant. She put it where she put things."), and I stay there for the rest of the chapter. I had to stop and work out whose head I was in. Ch 1 was all Beth, so I'd assumed one POV. Either the switch is sudden or there's no break where I'd expect one.
- **"He didn't say she was right."** This sits right after Dane says "Two o'clock's dead," which is pretty much saying she's right. Then later he says "You were right about the boat. I'm going to stop arguing with you about water." He never argued. Both lines felt slightly off.
- **The options checklist.** "He went through it out loud, the way she'd run a checklist, and closed each one." Then one quoted line about private boats, and Beth's LifeFlight line. It reads as though a chunk is missing. I couldn't tell which options he was closing, or whether LifeFlight was her offering one or ruling it out.
- **Where the guest mooring lines up.** Ch 1: the mooring lies "square below this window," the kitchen window. Ch 2: Dane stands longest where the mooring "lined up with her bedroom window. She'd lined that one up herself at dawn." At dawn she was in the kitchen. Is the bedroom on the same side? I lost the house for a second.
- **Kenny.** In Ch 1 Ned says "I'll get Kenny out of bed." In Ch 2 there's "No deckhand," and Ned works the lines alone. Is that a clue, or did Kenny just stay sick? I noticed it and couldn't tell if I was meant to.
- **The legal deadline.** "Monday's the last day the grand jury sits before his hearing deadline." Whose? I first read "his" as Pike's. Then: "a hearing by Friday, and the case starts coming apart." I took it on faith. It's a ticking clock I believe in but don't understand.
- **The day count.** It works if Friday is the 13th (ten days counting today runs to Sunday the 22nd, grand jury Monday the 23rd). But the book never says today's date, so I had to do the arithmetic backwards from the subpoena. "One to spare" took a second read. The state's ten-day barge estimate landing exactly one day before the grand jury felt arranged.
- **Ferry times** were fine. The 8:00 out of Pruett arrives, she backs out at 9:15, and the 2:00 is cancelled at 1:15. Easy to follow. The morning bag handed to the captain matches Ch 1 nicely.

## Where I stopped believing

- **"Nobody outside my office knows where. Nobody will."** Seven people watched him come off the boat in a soft-shell jacket, and the man with folded arms tracked him to the top of the ramp. The old woman looked at his boots. A face in her kitchen window watched him walk up the hill. Tess has already "heard you had company off the morning boat." The whole island knows where he is. Maybe that's the point, but he's presented as sharp, and the line made him sound naive. Either he's lying to calm her or he's dumber than he looks. I couldn't tell which, and I don't think I was supposed to wonder yet.
- **Beth calling Tess and saying "I'm home."** It's believable emotionally. Tess is family to her. But in Ch 1 Beth clocked the lit float, the dragger and the loan, and she's careful about everything else. She tells Dane "Leave it to me," then waits until he's out of the house to make the call. Pretty sly for someone this careful. It works because she clearly half-knows it's a mistake (her eyes on the wall phone at the end). I bought it, barely.
- **Dane not chasing.** That one I believed. One deputy, protect the witness. Good.

## Where it sounded like AI

- **Everyone counts.** Beth counts rungs, cartons, boats. Dane counts the slip. "She'd counted him the way he had counted the slip." Dane also "said it the way she'd log it" and "went through it out loud, the way she'd run a checklist." The book keeps telling me they think alike. Their voices are nearly identical: clipped, procedural, fragment-heavy. Take the names off the Dane section and the Beth section and I couldn't tell you who's narrating.
- **Repeated tics.** "She put it where she put things" (Ch 2) echoes "Beth put it where she put the things she'd look at later" (Ch 1). The hand-flat-on-a-surface image appears in Ch 1 (palm on the box, palm on the wall) and again here (hands flat on the counter, palm flat on the glass). It's starting to feel like a template.
- **Neat scene-closers.** Nearly every scene ends on a small clipped button: "I heard." / "Okay," she said. + barometer / "She turned the lamp down a notch." / "the date showing." / "She didn't step out." One or two would be style. Every scene doing it makes the rhythm predictable.
- **"I'm the waiting room."** A writerly line. A tired deputy might say something like it once in his career, but it sounded composed.
- **"He'd write it down. He'd remember it either way."** Tough-guy shorthand I've read a hundred times.

## The romance: is it registering?

Barely. Two chapters in, here's everything I've got:
- the dish towel she holds out "before she'd decided to"
- his hand stopping on the word *lot*
- the blanket laid on the couch arm "without holding it out"
- the first time he says "Beth"

These are quiet and nicely done, but there's no physical noticing at all. I don't know what Dane looks like beyond "less gray at the temples." I don't know Beth's age. Neither of them registers the other's body, voice, hands or smell. Nobody's pulse does anything. In a book sold as open-door, by the end of Chapter 2 I'd expect at least one moment where she's annoyed at herself for looking. There's none.

Do I want them together yet? No. I respect them both, but I don't feel a pull between them. The ending, where she suspects him, is good for suspense, and it would hit harder if there were some attraction for the suspicion to cut against.

## What I suspect about the door

- **Not Tess herself, and probably not Ned.** The "never been in this house" logic rules out anyone who knows the latch. Tess must have been in that kitchen a thousand times, and probably Ned too.
- **Tess is the leak, though.** The late laugh, the lit float after closing, the eighteen-hundred-a-month tank loan, the *Margaret Ann* fuelling at her float at night, and "I'm home" said straight to her. She's my top suspect for who told.
- **The man who tried the door** is someone from the *Margaret Ann* (on the mooring below the house, deck lights on, a figure facing shore) or the hooded man with the bad left leg.
- **Ned.** His hearing aid going off, never looking at the float, letting her skip the deck round, the phone call before dawn, and he "favored a hip." The hooded man favored his left leg. Was that deliberate? I'm watching Ned.
- **Dane.** The door is tried twenty minutes after his 11:20 porch call, and he's holding "something small and flat" in his other hand. The book clearly wants me to wonder. I half-suspect it's a red herring, but it worked on me.

## What I want to know next

- Who was on the latch, and do they come back with the storm still blowing?
- Did Tess give her up? Does Beth confront her?
- What was the small flat thing in Dane's hand? Who is Pike, really?
- Is Ned involved? Why no Kenny?
- What's the *Margaret Ann* doing, and who's the figure on her afterdeck?
- How do they survive ten days cut off with someone already at the door on night one?

These are strong questions. The suspense engine is running.

## Would I keep reading?

Yes, on the strength of the latch scene and the ending. But I'm reading it as a thriller right now. If Chapter 3 doesn't give me some charge between them, I'll start to feel I bought the wrong book.

## Best moment

> Lift and shove, one motion. A hand that knew the latch never waited between. This hand lifted and waited, lifted and waited, the way you'd try a door in a house you had never been inside.

A detail set up as everyday texture in Chapter 1 becomes a threat. Best paragraph in the book so far.
