## Market position
- **Genre:** open-door romantic suspense, protection plot.
- **Categories:** Romance › Romantic Suspense (main), Romance › Contemporary, possibly Romance › Small Town.
- **One-line pitch:** A ferry deckhand who saw too much and the US Marshal sent to keep her alive are trapped together on a storm-cut island for ten days, and the leak may be sleeping on her couch.
- **Length:** ~30,000 words, 8–10 chapters.
- **Heat:** open door, 3–4 on the 5-point scale. Sex is on the page, in close POV, sensory rather than clinical, frank language (no euphemisms like "manhood"; comps were punished for it). Every scene changes the relationship or the suspense question. Nothing that works as standalone erotica. Three scenes is the ceiling for this length.
- **Packaging:** PG-13. No explicit cover, no filter-trigger words in title, subtitle or keywords, never the 18+ box on KDP. Blurb must describe the book honestly: romance-first, with the happy ending promised outright. Comps (Pen Pal, Lights Out) were punished for blurbs that oversold darkness or hid the ending.
- **Series plan:** book 1 of the Cutter Island series. A complete love story with a closed main plot and a real HEA/HFN. The book-2 hook is a thread left open in the wider case (the network behind the smuggling, a secondary character's story), never the couple.
- **Pen name:** to be set in `story-bible.md`. Must differ from any clean book's pen name.

## Working premise: *Ten Days Out*
Beth Calloway has worked the Cutter Island ferry for eleven years and never looked at what the night crews unload. The one time she does, two men see her. Deputy US Marshal Cal Reyes is sent to keep her alive until she testifies; the plan is one week at a mainland safe house. A November storm shuts the ferry down, the island is cut off for ten days, and the safe house becomes her own cottage with a stranger on the couch. Someone keeps finding her. Cal is one of a very small number of people who could be telling them where she is.

## Setting
- A fictional ferry-served island off the New England coast (Maine or Massachusetts), November. Year-round population in the low hundreds; summer people gone. One ferry, one general store, a volunteer fire company, a part-time constable, no bridge.
- Ferry texture: deck work, tie-downs, freight manifests, the night freight run, radio procedure, weather holds. Get the operational details right; the heroine's competence is the differentiator.
- Storm texture: the mainland link down, generators, VHF radio as the only reliable line, the island's own rules for who checks on whom.
- **Avoid:** bakeries, bookshops, inns, a cute-town ensemble. Keep the island working-class and specific.

## Must-haves (from the comp read)
1. **A competent heroine.** The single most repeated complaint on this shelf is a heroine who makes senseless choices in danger. Beth knows the island, the water, the weather and the people better than anyone sent to protect her. Her decisions must be the ones a smart person would make with the information she has.
2. **Suspense that gets stronger in the second half.** Comps collapsed or turned absurd after the midpoint. The ten-day deadline is stated early and counted down. The leak question is answered in the last quarter, and the answer must be fair: planted, not sprung.
3. **Heat tied to the suspense question.** Each sex scene raises or lowers Beth's trust in Cal at a moment when trusting him is dangerous. No scene that could be cut without changing what she knows or risks.
4. **Small cast with real weight.** Beth, Cal, and at most three islanders who matter (the ferry captain, the constable or store owner, one more). The leak must be a named person the reader has met. Red herrings are fine; cheating is not.
5. **A hero who is good at his job.** Cal is careful, quiet, and a reader of people. His restraint is professional, not cold. Whatever he hides must be something a decent man would hide.
6. **Closed main plot.** The immediate threat is ended and the leak is identified by the end of this book. The wider case can continue.
7. **Prose:** controlled and specific, incident over banter. One comp drew "missing depth at short length" complaints; this book earns its length with things happening, not with the leads wanting each other for pages.
8. **Language:** swearing is allowed and should sound like working people, not like a tic. The bible sets a rule on frequency.
