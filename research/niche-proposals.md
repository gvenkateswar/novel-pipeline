# Niche proposals, Book 2 (open-door romance novella, series opener)

*October 27, 2026. Constraints from `research/book-brief.md`: about 30,000 words (8-10 chapters), heat 3-4 of 5 with the scenes on the page, PG-13 packaging, series opener with a book-2 hook and a real HEA/HFN, new pen name.*

*Evidence used: `amazon-scan-book2.md` (Oct 27, 12 categories, 600 books), `neutral/amazon-scan.md` (Oct 26, 55 categories, 1,864 books), `neutral/raw/short-indie-charting.md` (75 short indie books), both market-analysis files, `april-vs-october.md`. I could not reach Amazon with my own tools (the orchestrator reads it through Chrome), so I did not re-verify rows. Every number below is copied from those files. Where a number was not collected I say so.*

## How to read the numbers

Columns in the scans: indie of 50 / recent indie (released in the last six months) / indie under 300 ratings (lesser-known authors reaching the list) / short indie (200 pages or fewer; a 30k novella is about 110-130 pages) / sales depth at #25. Sales depth is a Kindle rank or a per-day estimate from the Kindlepreneur calculator. The per-day figures are rough.

Series share ("in a series" / "book 1s") was collected only for the 12 categories in the Oct 27 scan. For the other categories I say "not recorded".

## The pattern that governs everything

1. **The categories with the biggest sales are the least open to this book.**
   - Mafia: 31 indie, 28 recent, 3 under 300 ratings, 1 short, median 387 pp, #25 at Kindle rank 295.
   - Billionaires: 35 indie, 23 recent, 4 under 300, 0 short, median 390 pp, #25 at rank 318.
   - Dark Romance: 40 indie, 37 recent, 11 under 300, 2 short, #25 about 158/day.
   - These are 300-600 page series by established names. A heat-3-4, 30k, first-book novella from an unknown pen name is the wrong product for them. The Oct 27 scan says the same: "The brief's 30,000-word, first-book constraint is what doesn't fit here."
2. **Short spicy indie books that chart are a narrow set.**
   - Heat-5 kink and omegaverse shorts (Imani Jay, "Knotted by", Siena Stone, Audra Roslin).
   - Monster, alien and orc shorts. These are mostly heat 4-5 and sit inside long series.
   - Rapid-release "neglected wife" and betrayal serials, 62-189 pp, under many pen names.
   - A heat-3-4 plot-forward romance is not in any of these groups. The charting list has almost nothing that looks like it.
3. **Where a book like this could escape, and what the scans support.**
   - (a) List in a category where short books already chart and the heat norm is tolerant: Later in Life, Second Chances, SF/alien romance.
   - (b) Plan books 1-3 before launch. Nearly every short book that charts is one entry in a series released fast (Wolf Creek Mates 1-2, Betray Me Twice, Ice Planet Barbarians). The neglected-wife serials show what the volume looks like: weekly releases of 62-189 pages. Three 30k books is about 90k words, which is one full-length-equivalent of writing for a three-title catalogue on launch. That is the one lever the evidence supports. Nothing in either scan shows a single 30k heat-3-4 book from a new name charting by itself.
   - (c) Escape via the "Short Reads" categories is closed. Short Reads/Romance (65-100 pp) has 37 of 37 indie short and #25 about 69/day, but a 30k novella is longer than the 100-page limit.

# SET A: Neutral angles (market evidence only)

Ranked best to worst for this brief.

### Angle A1: Second-chance, midlife, open-door (no betrayal)

**The gap it fills:** Readers who want a grown-up second-chance romance with heat on the page but without the "neglected wife / cheating husband" template that has taken over both Second Chances and Later in Life. The Oct 27 scan names the flood: "My Cowboy Husband Neglected Me...", "My Doctor Husband Neglected Me...", from three or four pen names, several 900-1,500 pages, published weekly. A competent non-betrayal book can stand out on blurb alone. Also supported by the web pass: one r/RomanceBooks thread complains that characters over 50 are written as elderly and "cutesy" rather than as sexual adults.

**Premise in one sentence:** Dana Whitaker, 47, a newly divorced restaurant owner, has sixty days to reopen the kitchen or lose her lease, and the only contractor who can do it is the man she walked away from at 24, now a widower with a teenage daughter and a grudge. If she loses, she loses the business and her last excuse to leave town again.

**Amazon categories and numbers:**

| Category | Indie of 50 | Recent | Under 300 | Short | Depth at #25 | Series / Book 1s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Second Chances (Oct 27) | 34 | 34 | 18 | 5 | Kindle rank 674 (between the calculator's 500 = ~200/day and 1,000 = ~100/day anchors; my interpolation) | 22 of 50 / 8 |
| Later in Life (Oct 26) | 41 | 38 | 20 | 7 | ~108/day (rank 1,141) | not recorded |

Second Chances median is 296 pp, the shortest median of the 12 categories in the Oct 27 scan, and it has the lowest share of series books (22 of 50). Later in Life median is 316 pp.

**Named short indie books proving the length (all Oct 26 charting list):**
- *Tied by Treachery* (Betray Me Twice 1), Wren Shaw, 155 pp, rank 322, Later in Life #6.
- *Promises He Broke*, M.L. Hall, 159 pp, rank 863, Later in Life #20.
- *The Wife He Lost at the Masquerade*, M.L. Hall, 62 pp, rank 1,141, 14 ratings.
- *The Pregnant Wife He Introduced As Staff*, Mag Carlise, 189 pp, rank 2,249, 7 ratings.
- *Curvy Teacher and the President*, Tessa Knox, 92 pp, rank 2,336, 330 ratings.
- *My Husband Neglected Me, Left Me Drowning...*, A.W. Riva, 154 pp, rank 2,219.

Several of these are brand new with under 20 ratings and still rank under 2,500, so a new name can land here. All six are betrayal-template titles, so they prove the length and the openness but not the heat or the non-betrayal plot.

**Heat the category expects:** Not measured. I have titles, not interiors. The betrayal serials are plot-led and I would guess they run about 2-4, but that is a guess. Heat 3-4 is unlikely to run hotter than the list. The risk is the opposite: the heat norm may be lower than the brief, and I cannot confirm from the scans.

**Series:** Second Chances has the lowest series share of the 12 (22 of 50), so a standalone-feeling opener fits. The scans do not say how many follow-ups readers expect. Betray Me Twice (1) and the weekly betrayal serials show that rapid follow-ups are how the short books charting here are run.

**Main risk:** Competing with the rapid-release content operations on their home ground. The template titles have price and volume advantages (weekly releases, 900+ page "bundles"), and readers who search these categories may be looking for the template, not for a non-betrayal book. The open counts (34 recent indie, 18 under 300) partly reflect that flood, not demand for new voices.

**Commercial signal:** The openness counts above; five short indie in Second Chances and seven in Later in Life; one Reddit thread on mature heroines (undated, via the web pass, one signal only).

### Angle A2: Alien or monster romance novella with an older heroine and a consent-forward bond

**The gap it fills:** The category proves the length and has the shortest-book culture of any romance category, but the lists run hot (heat 4-5) and the heroine is nearly always a young captive. The web pass lists readers wanting older protagonists and non-fae creatures, and "gentler" takes on romantasy (Writer's Digest, June 2025, an author prediction, not survey data). The gap is a heat-4 version with a competent, mature heroine and a bond that does not depend on coercion.

**Premise in one sentence:** Tamsin Reyes, 41, a xenobiologist stranded on a frozen moon after a survey-ship crash, must winter with the reclusive native hunter who pulled her from the wreck, and she has one rescue window before the thaw to decide whether the ship's signal is worth answering. If she answers, she loses him. If she doesn't, she may lose everyone else from the crew.

**Named short indie books proving the length:**

- *Captured Mate*, Morganna Williams, 139 pp, rank 2,352, 46 ratings.

**Heat the category expects:** Mostly heat 4-5 ("Knotted by", "Daddy", orc and alien mates). Heat 3-4 fits only at the top of the brief's range. The list runs hotter, so a heat-3 book may read as under-delivering to this audience. Aim for 4.

**Main risk:** Heat and trope mismatch. A packaging-clean heat-4 book in a category that sells fated-mate explicitness may get lukewarm reviews from the core audience. Also the web pass cites K-lytics reporting "sci-fi romance" among declining sub-markets (the percentages in that report looked like a data quirk, so treat as unverified), and the Oct 26 SF romance #25 sales depth (~73/day) is modest.

**Commercial signal:** Eight short indie in SF Romance and six in First Contact on the Oct 26 charting list; Ice Planet Barbarians still at rank 461 after 11 years; reader wishes for non-fae creatures and older protagonists in the web pass (author prediction).

### Angle A3: Romantic suspense, protection-plot, linked-couples series

**The gap it fills:** Open-door romantic suspense is a good-selling category (#25 about 214/day) with moderate openness, but the scan finds almost no short books in it. A tight 30k romantic-suspense opener with a fixed deadline gives the genre's main appeal (heat plus danger) at a length that readers can finish in one evening. This is the thinnest evidence of the four for the "short" part.

**Premise in one sentence:** Beth Calloway, a ferry-line deckhand, saw a smuggling drop she was not meant to see, and the US Marshal sent to protect her is stuck with her on an island where the ferry will not run for ten days. If she testifies she lives, and if the man she is falling for is the leak she does not.

**Amazon categories and numbers (Oct 26):** Romance/Romantic Suspense: 31 indie, 15 recent, 6 under 300, 1 short, best short rank 391, median 393 pp, #25 about 214/day (rank 415). Multicultural & Interracial (Oct 27) is a possible second shelf but sells weakly (see B2).

**Named short indie books:**
- *House of Peril* (Bow Street Duchess Mystery), Cara Devlin, 162 pp, rank 391, 68 ratings, Historical Mystery #1 and Romantic Suspense #19. It is a historical cozy mystery series entry, so it shows the length can chart in this category but not that open-door suspense can.
- *Absaroka Ambush*, Millie Vaughn, 193 pp, rank 987, 13 ratings, small-town romantic suspense, charting in Clean & Wholesome #10. It is clean, not open-door.
- No short open-door romantic suspense book appears on the charting list. I state that plainly: the length is untested at heat 3-4 in this category.

**Heat the category expects:** Heat 3-4 is native. Of all the neutral categories, this is the one where the brief's heat level is the norm and not a compromise. Several scenes alternating with danger beats suits the "each scene changes the relationship" rule.

**Series:** Romantic suspense is usually sold as linked couples sharing a world. The scan did not record series share for this category. A book-2 hook is natural (the colleague, the sibling, or the unresolved smuggling network).

**Main risk:** The length. 6 of 31 indie are under 300 ratings, which is moderate openness, but only one short book is on the list and it is not open-door. A 30k book would be about a quarter of the category's 393-page median and could get "too short for the price" reviews. The web pass's 2021 commenter complaint that 125-170 pp novellas cost about as much as full novels is the same worry.

**Commercial signal:** Sales depth is the best of the four (about 214/day at #25); 15 recent indie; Cara Devlin cross-listing at #19; the web pass's Draft2Digital note that "action-packed athletic billionaires" (suspense blended with another hook) are trending in preorders (a trade-press item, not a sales figure).

## Set A ranking and why

1. **A1, second-chance midlife.** Best openness (18-20 low-review indie, 34-38 recent), most short books (5 and 7), shortest medians (296 and 316 pp), tolerant heat. Weak points: heat unknown, and the template flood.
2. **A2, alien/monster older-heroine.** Strongest proof that unknown names chart at this length (8 short indie in SF Romance, several with under 100 ratings). Loses a place because the heat runs hotter and sales depth is lower (~73/day).
3. **A3, romantic suspense.** Best heat fit and best sales depth (~214/day), but there is no short open-door example, so the length is a hypothesis.
4. **A4, gothic-dark.** Seasonal inflation, the hottest heat norm, and the most competition.

Not proposed, with reasons: Mafia and Billionaires (above), Sports, Workplace, Small Town, New Adult, Alpha Male, Western (series-heavy, 0 short indie, 1-5 low-review indie authors each in the Oct 27 scan), Contemporary and Enemies to Lovers (closed per Oct 26 scan), Clean (brief requires open door).

# SET B: Author-fit angles (the three April niches)

*Labelled author-fit. These come from the April playbook, not from market data. They are evaluated on the same evidence as Set A so the author can compare. All three premises keep heat at 3-4 and the packaging PG-13.*

**What the evidence does and does not show, for all three.** Supply is near zero: the Oct 27 scan found one South Asian-authored book in all 600 slots (Swati MH's *In Another Lifetime*, #17 in Multicultural, traditionally published, 170 ratings), and no self-published South Asian romance reached any top 50 in either round. The web pass found only about 20 authors on Romance.io's "south asian / desi romance" list with about 5,183 ratings combined (and the tag is loose; the top count belongs to a YA mystery author), and one prolific indie (Roma N. Singh, 19 books by May 2025, KU-exclusive, fast releases). Zero supply is not the same as demand. The scans measure what sells, not what is missing, and nothing in them shows readers searching for these books. A gap with no rows in the data could be a gap or could be an absence of buyers. The relevant question is whether the categories these books would list in admit a new indie author at novella length, and the answers are below.

### Angle B1: South Asian romantic suspense (ranked 1 of 3 in Set B)

**The gap it fills:** A romantic-suspense protagonist and setting that the category does not currently show. The April playbook argued the thriller gap (only about 11% of Kindle thriller bestsellers are indie).

**Premise in one sentence:** Meera Rao, a court interpreter in Houston, hears a recorded call that implicates her sister's husband in a payroll-fraud ring, and the FBI agent running the case is the only person who can protect her family without destroying it. If she cooperates her sister's household falls, and if she stays silent she is a witness to a crime in progress.

**Amazon categories and numbers:**
- Romantic Suspense (Oct 26): 31 indie, 15 recent, 6 under 300, 1 short, median 393 pp, #25 about 214/day.
- Multicultural & Interracial (Oct 27): 31 indie, 16 recent, 11 under 300, 6 short, median 336 pp, #25 at Kindle rank 2,922 (about 40/day). 32 of 50 in series, 7 book 1s.

**Named short indie books:** In Romantic Suspense, *House of Peril* (162 pp, historical cozy, cross-listed, 68 ratings) and *Absaroka Ambush* (193 pp, clean). In Multicultural, the short books are omegaverse and BWWM shorts, not suspense (Oct 27 scan, point 4). No short open-door South Asian suspense exists to cite.

**Heat the category expects:** Romantic Suspense is natively 3-4, which fits the brief. Multicultural shorts run hot (omegaverse).

**Series:** Linked-couples series is the norm; a sister or cousin or colleague gives a book-2 hook. The scans do not record follow-up counts.

**Evidence frankly:** The category sells (214/day) and takes some new names (6 under 300), so it admits an indie but not obviously a novella. A second shelf in Multicultural has the most low-review indie authors of any spicy category (11) but sells about 40/day at #25, so it adds visibility cheaply but little volume. That "second shelf" mechanism is my inference, not something the scan tested.

**Main risk:** Same as A3 (short length unproven at this heat), plus discoverability: no reader signal in the scans that South Asian setting adds demand or that the audience searches for it.

**Where it falls inside Set A:** Same category as A3, so it falls just below A3. Rank in the combined order: 4th.

### Angle B2: South Asian dark contemporary (ranked 2 of 3 in Set B)

**The gap it fills:** A dark, morally gray romance set inside a diaspora family business, which sidesteps Mafia and Bratva. April called it a blue ocean.

**Premise in one sentence:** Zara Mehta, 28, a bookkeeper whose father owes a debt to the jewellery-trade broker who financed his shop, trades two years of her ledgers for his freedom to the broker's heir, Kabir, a controlled and ruthless operator, and learns the debt was built to bring her to him. If she walks she loses her father's shop and any claim on the truth, and if she stays she may fall for the man who engineered her.

**Amazon categories and numbers:**
- Romance/Dark (Oct 26): 40 indie, 37 recent, 11 under 300, 2 short, best short rank 819, #25 about 158/day.
- Mafia (Oct 27) if the family business reads as crime: 31 indie, 28 recent, 3 under 300, 1 short, median 387 pp, #25 at rank 295 (the best sales in either scan, and the least open).
- Multicultural & Interracial: see B1.

**Named short indie books:** *The Bratva's Curvy Girl* (Imani Jay, 153 pp, rank 819, 250 ratings, Dark #39) and *Knife Thrower* (Arden Hart, 199 pp, rank 1,038, 57 ratings, Dark #46). Both are heat 5 or close to it.

**Heat the category expects:** Heat 4-5 with menace. At 3-4 this would be at the mild end of the shelf.

**Series:** Required; Mafia and Dark are series-first.

**Evidence frankly:** Dark is the most open of the three niches' categories (37 recent, 11 under 300) and has real sales. But the two charting short books are heat 5, and the Mafia numbers (3 under 300, 1 short) say the biggest-selling neighbour is closed to a first novella. Nothing shows a gap in demand.

**Main risk:** Heat and category mismatch (a heat-3-4 dark book fits neither the dark audience nor the clean-ish buyer), plus the Mafia-adjacent trope fatigue.

**Where it falls inside Set A:** Same category as A4, so just below A4. Combined rank: 6th.

### Angle B3: South Asian historical romantasy (ranked 3 of 3 in Set B)

**The gap it fills:** Historical or mythic romance with a South Asian setting and a magic system. The web pass found only older traditionally published historical titles (Indu Sundaresan's *The Twentieth Wife*) and no 2025-26 self-published example. The Book Riot trend note (2025) calls for new creatures and settings beyond fae.

If she fails the river floods the capital, and if she succeeds the treaty, and the husband who has become hers, ends.

**Amazon categories and numbers:**
- Historical Romance (Oct 27): 3 indie of 50, all 3 recent and all 3 under 300 ratings, 1 short, median 348 pp, #25 at rank 1,178 (just under ~100/day by the calculator anchors; my reading). 28 of 50 in series, 10 book 1s. The 3 may be small presses.

**Evidence frankly:** The supply gap is real; the door is not. ## What this means for a heat-3-4 novella from a new pen name

No angle in either set escapes the pattern by itself. The two angles that come closest (A1, A2) list in categories where unknown names with short books already chart, but the proof books are betrayal serials or heat-4-5 series entries. The honest reading of the evidence is:

- A single 30k heat-3-4 book has no direct comp that is charting anywhere in the data.
- Short books chart when they are a series entry in a rapid-release run. The lever is a planned three-book launch (about 90k words), not the choice of niche.
- Heat 3-4 is the natural fit only in Romantic Suspense (A3, B1). In the categories where short books chart, the list runs equal or hotter.

**Cross-cutting risk, not a ranking factor:** the brief's AI-prose warnings (KDP disclosure at upload, no US copyright on model-written prose, readers who keep boycott lists) apply to every angle and are the author's open decision.

## What would change the ranking

1. **Review mining on 4-7 named comps, particularly Tied by Treachery, Promises He Broke, Captured Mate, The Alien's Mate, and one Romantic Suspense comp.** If 2- and 3-star reviews in A1's shelf mostly say "too short, no story" or "too hot / not hot enough", that moves A1 and A2 up or down more than any category count. It would also settle the open question about the heat norm in Later in Life and Second Chances, which the scans could not show.
2. **A Kindle+ page-read estimate for a 30k book.** The web pass's arithmetic is about 100-120 pages read, roughly $0.40-0.50 per full read at about $0.0042 per page (my arithmetic, not a sourced figure), so a novella earns mostly as a series starter. If the author can check how many follow-ups the top short books in A1 and A2 have and how fast they were released, that fixes how many books the launch plan needs, and it decides whether any angle is viable without a three-book plan.
3. **A rescan in a non-Halloween week of Gothic, Dark, Paranormal and Monster/SF Romance.** The Oct 26 file warns that October inflates Gothic, monster and Halloween romance. If A4 and A2's short-book counts fall sharply, A2 and A4 drop. If they hold, A4 could move above A3.

Smaller check: whether the three Historical Romance indie books (all under 300 ratings) are self-published. If they are, B3 is less closed than it looks, though still a 3-book sample.

## Deep check update (October 27, 2026, after review mining)

The orchestrator mined 2-, 3- and 5-star Amazon reviews for six comps per angle on A1 and A3 (`research/raw/review-notes-a1.md`, `research/raw/review-notes-a3.md`), and comp-reader wrote `research/comps-angle-A1.md` and `research/comps-angle-A3.md`. What changed:

**A1 — heat norm settled, and it narrows the angle.** The short books charting in Second Chances and Later in Life are plot-first betrayal dramas. Their reviewers argue about angst, grovel and forgiveness speed; none mention heat either way. The explicitly steamy over-40 second-chance books do exist (Silver Seconds, 40 and (No Longer) Fighting It) with a few hundred ratings each, spice rated by fans at about 3 of 5, and complaints about flat chemistry and poor proofreading. So the category is open, but the open-door slice of it is smaller than the counts implied. The recurring complaints across A1 comps: make-up too fast with no grovel (five reviewers); pacing sag and repetition in the long comp (five); heavy swearing (three). Heat 3 fits; heat 4 is untested. Heat alone will not sell here.

**A3 — heat is native, complaints are design-fixable, length unproven.** Nobody on this shelf complains about heat being present; they complain about heat replacing plot. Recurring complaints: heroine makes senseless choices in danger (eight-plus reviewers); suspense collapses or turns absurd in the second half (three books); heat replaces plot (two books); blurb oversold the darkness or the romance payoff (three books). Every one of these is addressed by the A3 premise as written: competent heroine, fixed ten-day deadline, suspect inside the cast, honest PG-13 blurb.
