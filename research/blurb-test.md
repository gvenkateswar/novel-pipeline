# Reader Test Blurbs

*Drafts for testing real reader interest before writing. Each angle has a back-cover blurb and a one-line hook. The blurbs apply what the comp research found readers want: fast starts, real stakes, clear tone.*

---

## Angle 1 — The Anniversary Rite (horror)

**Hook:** *One year after her father's death, she's the only one left to feed his ghost. Something else is hungry too.*

**Blurb:**

Lakshmi Iyer spends her nights in a New Jersey hospice, sitting with the dying so they don't die alone. She hasn't spoken to her father in eleven years. She didn't go back for his funeral.

Now his first death anniversary has come, and there is no son, no brother, no nephew to perform the rite. The priest in the half-empty village in Palakkad, Kerala, says it must be done anyway. Otherwise her father's spirit will wander hungry.

So Lakshmi goes back to the narrow house where she grew up, to make the rice offerings and say the words she never learned. But the crows won't touch the food. The house remembers things she has forgotten. And with each step of the ritual, it becomes clearer that the rite isn't keeping her father at peace.

It's keeping something else inside.

*A one-sitting ghost novella of grief, duty, and a ritual that must not be broken.*

*(Content notes: death, family estrangement, body horror.)*

---

## Angle 2 — The Voice in the Bronze (historical fantasy)

**Hook:** *One pour of molten bronze. One chance. And the metal has started to speak.*

**Blurb:**

Thanjavur, 1010 CE. The great temple is nearly finished, and every foundry in the Chola kingdom wants the honor of casting its icons.

Aruvi has spent her whole life in her father's workshop, shaping wax, packing clay, learning the fire. But women don't pour bronze, and her father's hands are failing. When the dowager queen commissions a dancing goddess, to be cast in a single unbroken pour before the festival moon, Aruvi has to do in secret what she has never been allowed to do in the open.

Then she hears it. A voice in the cooling metal names who will sit on the throne after the king. Rival casters want her pour to crack. A court spy wants to know what the bronze has said. And Aruvi has seven days to decide whether to finish the icon, or melt the prophecy down before anyone else can hear it.

*A standalone historical fantasy novella of fire, craft, and the Chola court.*

---

## Angle 3 — Second Helping (romance)

**Hook:** *Six hundred guests. Two families. One wedding. And the two people running it are absolutely not falling in love.*

**Blurb:**

Vidya Raman has fed every Tamil wedding in Edison, New Jersey, for twenty years. Since her husband died, she has also fed her grief, her mother-in-law's opinions, and a temple kitchen that runs on her alone.

Harpreet Gill keeps the books for half the businesses on Oak Tree Road, and he has just audited Vidya's. Divorced, careful, and allergic to fuss, he is the last man she wants in her kitchen.

Then the wedding planner quits three weeks before the Raman–Gill wedding: her niece, his nephew, a Tamil-Punjabi ceremony, and six hundred guests. Somebody has to run it. Both families have decided it should be them.

Between flower garlands, two sets of rituals, and aunties keeping score, Vidya and Harpreet find themselves arguing over menus, budgets, and whose family is worse. Then they find themselves laughing. Then they find out that both families see this wedding as a test of whether either of them gets to be happy again.

*A warm, funny, grown-up romance novella about second chances. No third-act breakup.*

## How to test

These are ways you could get a real read on interest. All of them involve posting publicly or spending money, so they need your go-ahead. None of them has been done yet.

1. **Free, about 1 week:** Post the three hooks (or the blurbs) as a poll in a reader community. Ask "which would you read first?" Good places include r/horrorlit, r/Fantasy, and r/RomanceBooks. Each subreddit has rules on self-promotion, so read them first or ask the moderators.
2. **Free, about 1 week:** Send the blurbs to 10–20 readers you know who read these genres. Ask them to rank the three and say why.
3. **About $50–100, about 2 weeks:** Make a simple placeholder cover for the top two and run a small Facebook or Instagram ad for each that leads to a sign-up page. Compare cost per click. This is the most reliable signal, but it needs covers and a landing page.
