# Comparison: Neutral Niches vs. The Anniversary Rite

*October 26, 2026. Built only from the neutral round (`market-analysis.md`, `amazon-scan.md`, `niche-proposals.md` in this folder), plus the earlier comp research on angle 1 where noted. Sales figures are rough estimates from Kindlepreneur's rank calculator.*

## The main finding

Across 55 categories, **short self-published books chart in two situations: explicit romance, or one entry in a long series released quickly.** Standalone short books barely appear, apart from a small group of horror titles. So the deciding question is less "which genre" and more **"are you writing one book, or the first of several?"**

## How angle 1 holds up

- **On sales evidence, angle 1 sits in the same group as the neutral standalone-horror pick.** Both are standalone, both have the weakest commercial case, and both have proof that short horror can chart (*Fervor*, *The Haar*).
- **It has one edge over the neutral horror pick.** It lists in Occult & Supernatural, which has more readers (~92/day at #25) than Cosmic Horror (~4/day). But that category is also much harder for a new indie author to break into.
- **Its other strength is the craft advantage from your background:** accurate, specific cultural detail that readers of similar books praise and punish. That is real, but it is a writing advantage, not market evidence.
- **The neutral data favors series-based, low-heat books for a commercial start.** Clean later-in-life romance and historical cozy mystery are the most open categories with real sales, and both have short books charting now.

## Ways to decide

1. **If the first book is mainly about learning to write and finishing a novella:** angle 1 is a fine choice. It's a standalone with no commitment to follow-ups, and you'd write it with an insider's accuracy. Expect modest sales.
2. **If the first book should start earning:** pick a series-based niche (later-in-life romance or historical cozy mystery) and plan books 2–4 before publishing book 1.
3. **A middle path (labeled author-fit, not market evidence):** the historical cozy mystery niche doesn't fix a setting. The charting examples are Regency London and the 1920s Mediterranean. A cozy series could use a setting you know well. The market evidence supports the genre and the series model, not any particular setting, so the setting would be your choice and your risk.
