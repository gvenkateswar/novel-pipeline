## What readers loved

Themes from the 5-star reviews (Amazon, via review notes; paraphrased):

1. **Speed and one-sitting reads.** Tied by Treachery's fans praise a fast read with an emotional opening. Curvy Teacher fans praise speed and heat.
2. **A clear emotional payoff.** "True love wins" endings (Tied by Treachery) and the emotional payoff of Until I Remember were named by fans.
3. **Banter and a small-town cast.** Until I Remember's readers singled out the banter after the amnesia wake-up and the supporting cast.
4. **Visible midlife representation.** Silver Seconds' fans say, in effect, "finally a 40+ romance" (paraphrase), and rate the spice at about 3 of 5. Fans of 40 and (No Longer) Fighting It like found family and a hero who turns out to be more than expected.
5. **Inherited-business setup and a hero with a hidden agenda** (two ARC reviewers on Silver Seconds), which is close to A1's restaurant-lease frame.
6. **A possessive, devoted hero** (Knox shorts), a trope-level signal rather than a midlife one.

What not to lose: pace, an emotional opening, a hero who is clearly devoted, and a payoff that lands. Heat was never named as the reason a book was loved except in the Knox shorts.

## What disappointed them

Ranked by how often raised. Only items with three or more reviewers are marked recurring.

1. **Make-up came too fast; not enough angst or grovel. RECURRING (4 reviewers on Tied by Treachery, plus 1 on Promises He Broke on quick forgiveness).** Reviewers said the blurb promised a bigger betrayal than the book delivered, the hero was decent right away, and they wanted to see him earn it. Source: Amazon, Tied by Treachery 2-3 star reviews and Promises He Broke 2-star, via review notes.
2. **Pacing sag and repetition in the long version. RECURRING (5 reviewers on Until I Remember).** Strong start, then "same thing over and over", too much talking, too little happening, could have been shorter. Source: Amazon, Until I Remember 2-3 star.
3. **Heavy swearing. RECURRING (3 reviewers on Until I Remember; two said it made them stop).** Source: Amazon, Until I Remember 2-3 star.
4. **Flat chemistry between over-40 leads. BORDERLINE.** Silver Seconds had five critical reviews and the notes give this as their one theme ("underwhelming", "too much or too little" per chapter), but also say two of the five were ARC reviewers who liked the setup. The exact number raising chemistry is not confirmed, so I cannot call it recurring. Treat as a strong lead to verify. Source: Amazon, Silver Seconds.

Not recurring (fewer than three reviewers), reported for completeness only:
- Blurb used different character names than the book, spoiling a twist (1, Tied by Treachery).
- Very short and "quick", docked for depth (2, Tied by Treachery).
- Fake-marriage reason (the grandmother) did not hold up (2, Until I Remember).
- Couple fell in love before the reader knew enough of them (2, Until I Remember).

Absent: no reviewer in the notes complained that heat was too high or too low.

## The opening

1. **Make the reconciliation cost something, without a betrayal.** Complaint 1 shows this shelf's readers want grovel and earned forgiveness. A1's walked-away-at-24 history gives a real wound with no cheating. Build a grudge the hero acts on for at least half the book, and make the heroine's return the thing he must forgive as well as the reverse. Keeps readers who want angst while avoiding the neglected-wife template.
2. **Use the novella length as the answer to the sag.** Complaint 2 punished a 302-page book for repetition. A 30k book with one deadline (sixty days to reopen) and each scene moving the relationship avoids it. Do not pad to full-novel length.
3. **Set a language rule.** Complaint 3: swearing made several readers stop. Put a profanity ceiling in the bible's World rules (for example, occasional mild swearing, no heavy use in dialogue or narration).
4. **Write chemistry on the page, in the first half.** The Silver Seconds lead (borderline, verify) says over-40 readers want convincing attraction between the leads, not a description of it. The brief's heat rule (every scene changes the relationship) serves this, and early banter in the Until I Remember vein is what fans praised.
5. **Honest blurb, and a blurb that sells the real conflict.** Complaint 1 was partly a blurb promise the book did not keep. Write the blurb after the draft, promise only the grudge and deadline the book delivers, and keep character names consistent between blurb and text. Also proofread hard: one reviewer sank a good story on copy errors (single report, but cheap to prevent).

## Heat norm verdict

The evidence shows the heat level on this shelf is not a point of contention. Betrayal-template shorts (Tied by Treachery, Promises He Broke) are judged on angst, forgiveness speed and blurb honesty; nobody in the notes praised or criticised their heat, which suggests it is incidental, roughly 2-3. The only explicit rating is for Silver Seconds, where fans put the spice at about 3 of 5 and asked for chemistry and clean editing. The Knox shorts fans reward heat, but that is an instalove short, not this shelf.

A heat-3-4 novella fits. Heat 3 matches the "steamy over 40" readers and should draw no complaint. Heat 4 sits slightly above the one measured norm and is untested: no review showed readers objecting to it, but none showed them asking for it either. The risk is not that the heat is too high, it is that heat alone will not sell the book: the reviews reward emotional stakes, pacing and chemistry. Caveat: the sample is small (27 critical reviews, 12 on one book), and the open-door midlife readership is smaller than the betrayal-template one.
