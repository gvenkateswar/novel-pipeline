## Chapter 01: Cleared to Bowl

**What happened:** Ellen's first day as owner of Kettle Lanes (Tuesday, Dec 1). She relabels the switches flight-strip style. Denise arrives at 4:30 and is skeptical. At 5:15 Ray knocks holding Arlene's key and won't hand it over. Ellen invites him to wait inside, and he stays on the vestibule mat. She calls the only locksmith (Gagnon & Son) and Danny books a rekey of the three exterior doors for 8 a.m. Wednesday. At league start Ellen reads pairings over the PA and the league heckles her in fake pilot-speak. Hollis has her throw the ceremonial first ball. It lob-fouls, jumps into lane 6, and clears Ray's deadwood. Verna rules "re-rack, re-bowl," and Ray scores 7. At 8:10–8:11 lane 7's pinsetter dies (the table stops 12 inches above the deck, a burned smell). Ellen cuts its breaker, inspects it from the catwalk, and asks Ray to fix it; he says "Not my alley." She refunds the three paying lane 7 bowlers. At close Denise shows her a photo of the pool sheet; the March 1 square is empty and "Ray won't buy one." Ray waits in his truck until Ellen's car starts.

**What changed:** Ellen owns the alley, knows the deadline and Ray's right of first refusal, and has lost money on night one. Ray has refused to fix lane 7. The rekey is booked.

**Who knows what:**
- Ellen knows about the pool, that Ray holds no square, and that he was Arlene's mechanic.
- Ray knows Ellen recorded lane 7's failure on her phone (he didn't see the video).
- Denise has seen Ellen refund lineage. Verna has noted the refund in the minutes.

**Objects / details introduced:**
- Flight-strip labels on the lane-light switches (format: lane / time / LG / count / remarks).
- The pegboard strip board with the TUES LEAGUE card and the LOCKSMITH / 0800 card.
- Arlene's rotary phone on the office desk, and the Ambrose directory with a Rotary wheel on the cover.
- Ellen first saw the alley on a Sunday in September (FOR SALE BY OWNER sign; watched Arlene run a birthday party for two hours).
- Lineage is $7.50 a head for three strings.
- Danny suspects an old Corbin lock on the bulkhead.
- Arlene's rooster recipe-box card file, with a MECHANIC / R.G. card that Ellen keeps in her vest pocket.
- Ray's key ring with the orange paper tag. The breaker labeled PINSETTER 7 in green ink is now off.
- Ellen's phone video of lane 7's failure.
- The trophy case: LINDA GAGNON MEMORIAL trophy and the 187 house-record card, Feb 1998.
- The neon sign's dead K.
- Lane 3's dead spot on the approach and its cracked settee.
- The house balls on the rack behind lane 8, and the spare rack by lane 1.
- The corkboard of lost mittens in the vestibule.
- Lanes 5 and 6 are Gagnon & Son and the Ambrose Fire Auxiliary.
- Tuesday league teams: the Legion, Lakeside Plumbing, Ambrose Pharmacy, Taylor's Marina.

**ATC phrases used (5 of 6):** say again, cleared to bowl, stand by, unable, negative. "The voice" used once.

**Ends on:** about 11 p.m. Tuesday. Ellen locks up; the front lock still takes Ray's key until 8 a.m. Ray's pickup leaves the lot right after hers starts, toward town.

## Chapter 02: Right of First Refusal

**What happened:** Wednesday, Dec 2, at the store. Ray cuts a plow driver's key while the Bench (Normie and two others) narrates. The pool sheet hangs behind the register: DEC 1 and MARCH 1 are empty, and Normie offers Ray the March 1 square, which he declines. At 7:30 Ellen comes in for salt (Kettle Lanes account, her cell number added) and plays the lane 7 video. Ray diagnoses a slipping clutch with a glazed facing; Bowl-Mor clutches aren't made anymore. He tells her to keep the breaker off and that a spare hangs on a nail in her pit (third from left, his tape, salvaged in 2011). She writes it on a card instead of reading it back.

At 8 a.m. Ray rekeys all three doors (two Schlage five-pin; the old Corbin bulkhead is replaced with a new cylinder) and hands her 3 rings of 3 keys. His old key with the orange tag no longer turns. Kneeling at the front door, he offers to buy her out at exactly what she paid and take over Arlene's note. Kate calls mid-offer. Ellen refuses ("Unable"): a federal statute ended her career, this is the first thing she's chosen, and she won't sell on day two. Ray: "Then I'll see you March first."

That evening at the store, Ray tells Danny a "Fella I knew in Tilton" story (the neighbor's furnace, shared wall). He texts Ellen asking to fix lane 7 for Thursday league, and she replies "2100. Back door. I'll unlock it." At 9 p.m. she lets him in and leaves; she doesn't give him a key. He finds the M slot empty in the card file, touches the trophy glass, replaces the clutch, and tests it (seven-second cycles). He leaves the old clutch on her desk tagged in her strip format: "07 / 2340 / MX / 1 / CLUTCH — SLIPPING. REPLACED." Thursday, 6:05 a.m.: she texts a photo with her added line "REMARKS: INVOICE TO E. DALY." Ray replies, "I didn't fix it for you. Thursday league's got a match."

**What changed:** Lane 7 works. Ellen has refused the buyout, which ends Act 1. Ray has helped her with her knowledge and at her invitation. A billing relationship is implied but not agreed.

**Who knows what:**
- Ellen: the clutch diagnosis; that Ray fixed lane 7; that he still won't buy a pool square (implied).
- Ray: Ellen's age, her retirement story, that Kate calls every morning after her shift, Ellen's cell number.
- Danny: Ray is "a yes." The Bench knows about the offer, but not the refusal.

**Objects / details introduced:**
- Ray's bank commitment letter on an SBA loan, kept folded behind his driver's license and dated 8 days after Arlene's handshake.
- The pool sheet behind the store register, with extra rows added and a pen in the cup.
- Ledoux Plowing's account.
- The Thursday league includes the Highway Department and the Lakeview Diner (on lane 7 this week).
- The old Corbin cylinder, now gone.
- The new keys: 3 rings of 3, front/back/bulkhead. Graphite in the bulkhead.
- Ray's old orange-tag key, which opens nothing.
- The salvaged spare clutch, now installed. The old clutch is tagged on Ellen's desk.
- Linda's ball is in locker 14. The orange settees.
- The stool with the cracked green cushion beside Ray's at the store (Linda's).
- The steel door by lane 10 sticks.
- Ellen's car starts on the second try.

**Ends on:** Thursday, Dec 3, about 6:10 a.m. Ray at the store, cutting a key, having sent "Thursday league's got a match."

## Chapter 03: Deadwood

**What happened:** First week of December, at Granite Lakes Savings Bank. Judy circles the bank's two numbers: $30,000 net operating income for Dec 1–Feb 28, and $25,000 cash on Feb 28. She explains them (the winter is the year; the new loan costs about $24K a year, plus a cushion). She warns, "Everybody passes the cash test in December. People fail it in February." Ellen orders the appraisal and environmental review the same day and opens a second account named HELD. NOT MINE. for meals tax and the league prize fund. Ray brings two cartons of Bowl-Mor parts from a closed alley past Plymouth; Ellen pays by check. They agree on per-call invoices. He writes the price of a key cut for the lane 7 repair; she crosses it out and writes a fair rate. She gives him one back-door key and pins up the R.G. — AFTER HOURS card (DATE / TIME IN / TIME OUT / LANE / REASON). He reads back the rule ("Back door. After close. Write it on the card. Every time."). He says he'll keep the machines running for "whoever has this building on March second." She shows him the $5,400 glow-night work order out of her own cash. A fixture is planned over the trophy-case wall.

Thursday, Dec 10: the electrician installs black lights. Denise makes Ellen fill out a league sanction card ("so you're legal if they're ever short"). She tells Ellen she can't bowl and the league won't respect that, and points to Ray as the teacher. Ellen moves two Wednesday office parties to 5 p.m. and marks them PER D. LAVOIE. Under the full black light, Linda Gagnon's house-record card glows white in the trophy case.

Friday, Dec 11: glow night, 52 bowlers, a waiting list. Lane 3's table runs late at 9:40; Ellen shuts it off and comps the birthday party fries. Denise texts Ray. At 11:20 he logs in on the card (12/11, 2320, lane 03, TABLE LATE) and tests lane 3. His glowing tape strips line the pit wall ("Nobody told me the tape did that"). His price for the lessons: the championship stays as always, last Saturday in February, all ten lanes, the Linda Gagnon Memorial, "Nothing glowing." Ellen: "You have my word. The championship stays as it is, the last Saturday in February, all ten lanes, unless the numbers say otherwise." The sweep covers the end of her sentence, and Ray is counting the cycle. She chooses not to ask for a readback and shakes his hand.

Mon Dec 14, Wed 16, Fri 18, 7 a.m. lessons on lanes 5–6. She lob-fouls three times and writes down the misses. Ray: "Nobody's ever bowled a 300. Best anybody's done is 245." He gives her the first touch: he turns her wrist and lowers her hand six inches, "Low. Let the lane have it." "The wood stays on the lane. You play what's left." (use 1 of 3). She misses the spread eagle Monday. Wednesday she leaves the clipboard in the car. Friday she uses the deadwood to clear the leave.

Saturday, Dec 19, 11:40 p.m.: Denise reads the R.G. card aloud (glow night lane 3; Tue lane 9 belt; Thu lane 3 again, underlined; Fri lane 6 "Glow light bracket. Not mine. Checked anyway."). The store pool has a new later row, and Ray still won't buy a square (Denise offered him March 1 at cost). The appraiser came Wednesday and studied the boiler without writing anything. Ellen's tape: NOI $5,600, cash $34,300, 88% of a straight-line pace ($6,333). December had the parties and holiday weeks; January has none booked. League nights (Tue and Thu) are the thinnest columns, and she doesn't know why. She pins JAN / RECONCILE / ARLENE / LEAGUE and a strip 02/27 / 1800 / LGM / 10 with REMARKS blank.

**What changed:** The bank's targets are explicit, and Ellen is behind on income. Ray has a key, logs his visits, and is paid per call. Glow nights exist on Fridays and Saturdays. Ellen is learning to bowl from Ray. The conditional promise has been made, and Ray didn't hear the tail.

**Who knows what:**
- Ellen: the full promise, including that Ray didn't say it back (she chose not to ask). League nights earn the least, cause unknown.
- Ray: thinks the promise is "the championship stays as it is, last Saturday in February, all ten lanes." He knows about the glow-night spend.
- Denise: has seen the R.G. card and the strip handwriting. She knows Ray is teaching Ellen and that he still refuses a pool square.
- Judy: the appraisal and environmental review are ordered. She knows of the HELD account (she approves it in Ch 10).

**Objects / details introduced:**
- Judy's circled sheet. Her ice-fishing photo (11 lb 4 oz lake trout on a certified scale). The loon calendar. The drive-up tube.
- HELD. NOT MINE. account.
- Gagnon & Son sales-slip pad. Ray's carpenter's pencil. The per-call invoice at Ellen's rate. "Ms." correction.
- R.G. — AFTER HOURS card on the pegboard by the pit door.
- The back-door key on Ray's ring, beside Arlene's orange-tag brass key (which opens nothing).
- Electrician's work order, $5,400: black lights, LED lane lighting, sound. Six fixtures, one over the trophy-case wall.

**Ends on:** Saturday, Dec 19, about midnight. Ellen alone at the desk, the championship strip pinned at the far end of the board with REMARKS blank.

## Chapter 04: Ground Stop

**POV:** Ray.

**What happened:** It is Thursday, January 7, during a nor'easter, on the Thursday league night.
- **The plow:** Hollis's plow jams crossways at the foot of the drive (the angle ram hose on the left side failed). Ellen pays him to keep her lot open on league nights. Kettle Road is closed, the town pulled its trucks at 8:30, and nobody tows tonight. Ray heard it from lane 6 and came down with a shovel from the vestibule. Ellen declares that nobody leaves.
- **The derby:** Hollis's derby is the last weekend of February, the same weekend as the championship, with about 1,200 people in a good year. His poster is in Ellen's vestibule.
- **Ground stop:** At 9:20 Ellen counts 36 people, calls in on the rotary phone ("We'll stay put"), and announces a ground stop over the PA. She sets the sleeping places: married couples on the 3/4 settees, Highway Department on 7/8, Lakeview Diner on 5/6, everyone else on 1, 2 and 9, Ray and Hollis on 10 by the pit door. She runs a marathon at the usual rates. Sleeping and the phone are free, and the house adds a set amount per string to the league prize fund. Verna rules under Article Six that the strings aren't league play and logs the house money as a gift.
- **The pushback:** A Highway Department man (22 years of Thursdays, a son at home with a sixteen-year-old sitter) refuses to pay. Two men sit with him and Denise voids their strings, so nobody bowls on lane 8 that night. Ray doesn't buy a string. Denise grumbles about the cost of the free coffee. Ray's story about Merle Tardif of Gilford (a 4WD stuck on a snowbank in the '78 blizzard) keeps a Diner bowler from driving.
- **The office argument:** Ray says Arlene opened the alley free for three days in February 1978 and "never charged the league for a lot of things," and Ellen glances at the ledgers. Denise trips a breaker (the popcorn machine against the space heater) and resets it at the panel by the shoe rack. Ellen asks what he'd do if it were his. Ray: open it free, eat the cost. Ellen: "On whose money?" Ray: "Mine. Bank letter says so." The league will bowl there a year from now, and Pine Ridge, 40 minutes away, never charged them for a snowstorm. Ellen has no answer. Denise interrupts because the Highway Department has taken apart lane 8's ball return.
- **The outage:** At 1:52 a.m., with 19 inches down, a pine takes out the lines on Kettle Road. The lanes go dark mid-string (the last ball is on lane 9), the boiler burner and circulator stop, and the exit signs run on battery. Ellen: "Everyone in this building is a guest." Ray hand-sets lane 5. Its table stopped high, he props it with a 2x4 and switches its breaker off, and Ellen tapes HANDS IN PIT over the switch. She insists on coming into the pit ("It's my pit"). Bowlers call "Ball!" and Denise throws early, so a pin flies over Ellen's head. Someone asks for a "fella who bought a bowling alley" story, and Ray says, "Don't know him." A pin lands in Ray's lap, and Ellen says, "Traffic, twelve o'clock, one mile." He laughs until he has to brace himself.
- **Linda's 187:** In February 1998, on a Thursday, lane 5's table quit in the ninth frame of Linda's string. Ray set her tenth by hand from the pit and heard the 187 without seeing it. He has never told anyone he didn't see it.
- **Ellen's last day:** A day shift, the month she turned 56. She signed off at 3:00 and handed the headset to a man she'd trained. There was a sheet cake, marble, with her name spelled right.
- **Morning:** At 5:40 a.m. Verna adjourns at 5:41. Ellen's pencil tally on a score sheet shows the season's NOI at $11,800 (it was $5,600 on Dec 19). The utility estimates power back Saturday evening, so Ellen marks the Friday glow and Saturday strips CLOSED. Ray sees the 02/27 LGM strip. He has been counting the cars in her lot from the store's back window on Fridays since mid-December and has told no one. He says to rebook the eleven parties free; Ellen says no ($2,640 lost) and starts calling the Friday party list.
- **Dawn:** With 22 inches down, Ray and Hollis dig out the blade. Ray's lender email extends the commitment letter through March 31. He asked for it on Dec 14, the morning of her first lesson. Hollis: "She know?" Ray: "She knows I've got one." Hollis: "You going home now?" There's no answer.

**What changed:** Ellen lost two of her best nights and $2,640 in parties. NOI is $11,800. She lost the argument with Ray and lost goodwill with part of the Thursday league (the lane 8 sit-out). Ray's bank commitment now runs to March 31. Ray and Ellen shared the pit and their two private stories.

**Who knows what:**
- Ellen: Ray's 187 secret, that he set Linda's tenth by hand and didn't see it. She knows Arlene "never charged the league for a lot of things" (it points at the ledgers). She does not know about the extension or that Ray asked for it the day of her first lesson.
- Ray: Ellen's last-day story and the marble cake. He knows her NOI ($11,800) and the cancelled parties. He has watched her lot on Fridays. He holds the extension.
- Hollis: knows about Ray's extension. The derby and the championship share a weekend.
- The Thursday league: some resent being charged. Verna's ruling makes the house contribution a gift.

**Objects / details introduced:**
- Hollis: plows Ellen's lot on league nights; a green waterproof notebook and golf pencil (snow totals 11, 19, 22); a scanner; the derby poster in the vestibule.
- The sign reads ETTLE LANES from the drive, as it has for seven years.
- Ray's habit of fixing hinges with a dime.
- The breaker panel by the shoe rack; the popcorn machine and space heater can't both be on.
- Lane 5: HANDS IN PIT tape over its breaker; the 2x4 prop; the ten gray spots.
- Lane 8's ball return was half disassembled by the Highway Department (Ray needs to look at it).
- Pine Ridge, a rival alley 40 minutes away.
- Merle Tardif of Gilford (fella story).
- The '78 blizzard free weekend (Arlene).
- Verna's Article Six ruling, recorded in the minutes; a motion of thanks is pending.
- $2,640 in party revenue lost (11 parties).

**ATC phrases used (2 of 3):** "ground stop," "Traffic, twelve o'clock, one mile." The league's "Kettle Tower, Highway Two" mock doesn't count.

**Ends on:** Friday, Jan 8, at dawn at the foot of Ellen's drive. Hollis asks Ray, "You going home now?" Ray doesn't answer.
