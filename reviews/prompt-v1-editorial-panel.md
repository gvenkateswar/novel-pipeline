# External review prompt (seven-role panel)

Paste this into ChatGPT, Gemini or another tool, and attach the clean manuscript file. Fill in the bracketed parts from the story bible. Check every fact against the bible before sending: on Book 1 the prompt said "four kisses" when the book has three, and reviewers flagged the mismatch.

```
I'm sharing the complete draft of a novella, attached as "[FILE NAME]". I want an independent, honest evaluation. Please don't be polite at the expense of being useful.

## The goal
This is a test of whether a team of AI agents, directed by one person, can produce a commercially viable novella for Amazon Kindle self-publishing. I set the niche, premise and rules and approved each stage. Agents drafted, checked continuity, line-edited, and simulated readers. I want to know: is this good enough to publish and sell in its niche as it stands, and what would most improve it?

## Why this book
- Niche: [NICHE], and why it was chosen: [MARKET REASON]
- Reader complaints we tried to answer: [LIST]
- Our answers: [LEADS WITH AGES AS STATED ON THE PAGE, STAKES, TEXTURE, HEAT LEVEL WITH EXACT KISS/SCENE COUNT]
- Format: [CHAPTERS], about [WORD COUNT] words, [SERIES PLAN], target price [PRICE]

## How to evaluate
Read the whole manuscript first. If you can't read all of it, say exactly where you stopped before giving any opinion. Count words with a tool if you can; don't estimate.

Then run a panel. Play each role in turn, as separate people, each giving its own verdict without seeing the others. Quote the text for every major point.
1. The target reader: [PERSONA]. Where would she skim or stop? What star rating, and write her review.
2. The acquisitions editor at a publisher in this niche: acquire or not, and what must change first?
3. The developmental editor: structure, pacing, character and romance arcs, energy 1–5 per chapter, whether the climax belongs to the protagonist, whether the ending is earned.
4. The line editor: prose, voice, dialogue distinctiveness, repeated phrases and habits (with counts).
5. The fact and continuity checker: [DOMAIN FACTS TO CHECK], plus contradictions across chapters.
6. The skeptical reader who suspects AI-written fiction: where it reads machine-made, and where it reads human.
7. The Kindle market strategist: title, categories, comps, price, blurb draft, cover direction; publish as is, revise first, or rework?

## After the panel
- Where the roles agreed and disagreed.
- An overall score out of 10 for publishability in this niche, with reasons.
- The top 5 revisions in priority order, each with a concrete example fix (a rewritten line or a specific scene change).
- One thing the book does well that a revision must not lose.

Be specific and quote the text. A clear "not ready" is more useful than a soft "promising." Judge it as a book a reader would pay for.
```
