Revision by revision: what the panel asked for, what the text now does
1. VAC WK: fixed, completely. This was the book's one broken promise. Now Judy plants it in Chapter 3, in the bank scene, as a question Ellen cannot answer: "You've got school vacation week at the end of it, our week, the last one. What have you got booked against it?" / "Nothing yet." Chapter 5's ledger scene repeats the exposure: "February had school vacation week, and nothing booked against it yet." Chapter 8 shows the build: five strips, "1200–1700 / VAC WK / ALL LANES / SOLD... She had built it herself since then: kids' afternoons, family strings by the hour, and a block for the derby families. She had sold it on the derby's phone tree, and every family had paid ahead." And the money bridge the notes reader claims is missing is on the page in the same scene: "Eighteen hundred dollars short on Judy's first line, with the Open still to run. Twelve hundred and fifty short on her second." The arithmetic now reconciles end to end: through Feb 20, $24,300, plus vacation week $3,900, is $28,200, exactly $1,800 under the bar, and the Open's $2,650 closes it to $30,850. The cheat is gone. What remains is the book's best-designed money thread.
2. The bank climax: fixed. Two moves did it. First, Judy's December scene now pre-defines the test: "That means on deposit here by close of business that day. Or taken in by then and documented, with register reports dated by then and a deposit slip." The Chapter 10 dispute is therefore a fair fight under a stated rule, not a technicality invented at the finish. Second, Ellen argues it herself, on speakerphone, with paper: "The report closes at four-forty on the twenty-seventh... Gross less refunds is what went in the safe. This morning's slip is the same figure, to the dollar." And the HELD speech is hers now, ending on the line that used to be Judy's report: "Not one dollar crossed over." Judy corroborates ("To the dollar") instead of rescuing. The butterscotch count is gone; in its place Ellen takes one from the bowl "for later," the first she has touched in two visits. Small, human, and it inverts the old tell. One residue: the underwriter still folds in a single line ("I'll take it") after one speech. It is procedurally earned now, but one more pushback from him would give the scene a second turn. Optional, not required.
3. Ray's final: fixed, and better than the panel specified. He bowls Linda's ball on the page: "His first ball ran low along the right boards and took seven... His second took two of the three left standing, and his third tipped the last one over backward into the pit." We get his string score against hers ("Ellen finished the string at sixty-one. Ray finished it at 109"), the ritual ("Between balls he wiped the ball on his pant leg, two strokes, the same two every time"), and the tell: "He sat down on the settee with Linda's ball in his lap and both hands over it. The right one shook once, the way Ellen's had shaken on the drop light that morning, and went still." Denise's roster spot is planted in Chapter 1, in her first scene: "Thursdays I anchor the Lakeview Diner on top of it." Verna even mirrors it in Chapter 9, noting the Diner "also shows three bowlers on the lanes, and its anchor behind the register." The final now works as written.
4. The tics: cut hard, measured. Draft 1 → Draft 2, counted on the text: "Mrs. Daly" 7→3. "I'm aware" 7→3. "Didn't/without looking up" 9→1. Sign buzzing 8→2. Hollis's cap business 6→1. The skeptic's deeper metric did not move at all: mean sentence length 12.0 words in both drafts, sentences of five words or fewer 23.1% → 23.5%, paragraph cadence essentially identical. The revision edited vocabulary, not rhythm. That is the main reason the score stops at 7.5.
5. Ellen's age and "frame": both fixed. Chapter 1 now anchors her in Hollis's first scene: "She was fifty-eight now, two years into a retirement she hadn't chosen. The cake had been marble." Fifty-eight is on the page, the two-year gap is accounted for, and the blurb can now say what your brief always said. The terminology pass is total: "frame" falls 19→6, and all six remaining uses are literal (the 187's picture frame, a doorframe, "You were going to frame it"); "box" rises 28→45, including Ray's own narration of the 187, now "the ninth box of Linda's string." I still lean Cleared to Bowl for search and accuracy, but this is branding now, not error.
