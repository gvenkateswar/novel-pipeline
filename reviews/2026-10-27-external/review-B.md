Still no acquisition as it stands; a revise-and-resubmit with a short, fixable letter. Reading the actual text softens two of the notes-based charges and hardens one.

Hardened: the climax problem is worse than the notes suggested, because in Chapter 10 Ellen's financial fate is decided by Judy's phone call to an underwriter Ellen has never met, while Ellen waits outside the glass counting the butterscotches: "There were nineteen butterscotch candies in the bowl. The editor's note writes itself: your heroine's defining trait is that she acts; at the decisive moment she is benched, and the woman who saves her is her banker, using Ellen's HELD account discipline as evidence. The evidence is hers. The action is not.
Against the market: later-in-life clean romance that sells (the Hideaway Lake pattern) runs on domestic space, food, female friendship and family traffic. This book has exactly one sustained female-friendship presence (Denise, who is superb and largely confined to the counter), one daughter who appears in Chapter 2 as a phone call and Chapter 9 as staff, and no scene of the leads eating together in 33,000 words. That is a positioning choice with a cost, not a flaw in itself. Priced and blurbed as cozy romance it will disappoint the core buyer; positioned as small-town fiction with a romance, it stands out.
